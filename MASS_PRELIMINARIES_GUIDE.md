# Mass Preliminaries in the Sehling Church Orders

This guide covers the parts of the evangelical Mass that are neither Ordinary nor Proper, in the
church orders of the sixteenth century printed in Emil Sehling's *Die evangelischen
Kirchenordnungen des XVI. Jahrhunderts*. It asks:

- **Opening versicles and preliminaries.** What stood before the Introit, and into what families
  do the orders fall?
- **The exhortation.** Where does the exhortation to the communicants fall, and what does it
  say?
- **The *Confiteor*, *Misereatur*, *Indulgentiam* and absolution.** Where are they? Does the
  priest say them alone, with his ministrants or sexton, or with the people? Are they said
  privately before Mass, at the beginning of Mass, or in the communion rite? Are the old texts
  kept or new ones written? Is the absolution optative, declaratory or indicative, and how does
  it compare with the modern Common Service?
- **Before and after the service.** Do the vesting prayers survive? Are the *Benedicite* and Ps
  150 appointed after Mass, and who says them? Are there prayers before and after the sermon, on
  entering or leaving church, or for the communicants?

**How the guide is laid out.**
- §1 summarizes the findings, and §2 sets out the scope and sources.
- §3 covers the opening versicles and preliminaries, by family.
- §4 covers the confession and absolution: placement, the chancel party, old and new texts, the
  form of the absolution, and the descent of the Common Service's confession.
- §5 covers the exhortation to the communicants.
- §6 covers what comes before and after the service: vesting, the priest's private prayers, the
  *Benedicite* and Ps 150, prayers about the sermon, and prayers on entering church and at
  communion.
- §7 is a table by order, and §8 a concordance of every order quoted.

The Canon, the Words of Institution and the prayers around communion are treated in
[`CANON_IN_GERMAN_MASSES.md`](CANON_IN_GERMAN_MASSES.md). Several texts printed there are only
cross-referenced here: Kantz 1522, Döber's exhortation, the Kiel Mass and Bugenhagen's
preparatory prayer.

**Conventions**

- **Quotations.** Every quotation is Sehling's text as it stands in the `eko.db` database built
  from the digitized edition.
  - Footnote numbers, sigla, folio markers, the editor's dates in square brackets and
    interleaved apparatus are left out.
  - Sehling's own supplied letters in square brackets are kept, as are "[!]" and his other
    signs.
  - Spelling and punctuation are as printed. A few plain OCR errors are corrected.
  - "[…]" marks an omission.
  - Every quotation has been checked against the database text, allowing for OCR noise: at least
    90 per cent of its words must be found there in order. Its page has been checked against the
    edition's page markers.
  - Each quotation is preceded by an HTML comment, invisible when rendered, that names the
    `eko.db` document it was checked against.
- **Translations.** The English is formal-equivalence, in the idiom of the Authorized Version:
  - Word order and clause structure are kept where English allows.
  - Stock terms are rendered the same way throughout. *Beicht* is "confession". *Absolution* is
    "absolution". *Vermanung* is "exhortation". *Kirchendiener* is "minister of the church".
    *Küster*, *köster*, *custos* and *opperman* are "sexton" (or "custos").
  - Words the translation supplies are in square brackets.
- **Texts from outside Sehling.** The English of the Common Service and later American Lutheran
  books, given in §4.5 for comparison, is not set as a quotation and has not been checked
  against the corpus.
- **Citations.** Citations are in the form "Sehling volume, page".
  - The half-volumes of the edition are written 6/1, 6/2, 7/1, 7/2.1, 7/2.2, 17/1, 17/2, 19/1,
    19/2, 20/1 and 20/2, following the digitized volumes.
  - Each order is named by place, title and date as they appear in Sehling's running heads.
    These sometimes differ from the database's record headings.

## 1. Summary of findings

1. **The Roman preparation was broken up.** No evangelical order keeps the whole of the priest's
   preparation: *Introibo*, Ps 43 *Iudica me*, *Confiteor*, *Misereatur*, *Indulgentiam* and
   *Aufer a nobis*. *Introibo* and *Iudica* disappear everywhere. *Adiutorium nostrum* (Ps
   124:8) is the one versicle that survives widely. The German missals' *Confitemini Domino
   quoniam bonus* survives in Döber and Mecklenburg 1540 (§3.1).
2. **The preliminaries fall into six families** (§3.1):
   - **A.** The priest's private *Confiteor* at the altar during the Introit
     (Brandenburg-Nürnberg, Brandenburg, Pfalz-Neuburg, Hohenlohe, Regensburg, Amberg,
     Buxtehude, Schleswig-Holstein).
   - **B.** Bugenhagen's kneeling prayer with the sexton: the Our Father and *Domine, non
     secundum* (Wolfenbüttel, Hildesheim).
   - **C.** The public confession of Döber (Nürnberg 1525), carried north to Lippe, Kiel and
     Mecklenburg and back south to Pfalz-Zweibrücken and Pfalz-Veldenz.
   - **D.** The medieval *Offene Schuld* of the preaching service moved into the Mass (Kantz,
     Volprecht, Naumburg, Nördlingen).
   - **E.** The Strasbourg and Reformed opening: confession and comfortable word (Strasbourg,
     Palatinate 1563).
   - **F.** A hymn, a German psalm, or *Veni Sancte Spiritus* with its versicle and collect, the
     last a recasting of the medieval *accessus* as a preparation of the congregation (Luther
     1526, Amberg 1544, Verden 1606, Transylvania 1547).
3. **The confession stands in four places** (§4.1): privately before the Introit; publicly
   before the Introit; after the sermon (Bugenhagen 1528, Amberg 1550, the Palatinate); and in
   the communion rite after the exhortation (Kantz, Württemberg 1536, Nürnberg after 1569).
   Mecklenburg 1552 and Nördlingen 1544 have it twice.
4. **The chancel party** (§4.2):
   - **Families A and B.** The priest prays alone or with his ministrants (Brandenburg 1540,
     Pfalz-Neuburg), or with the second ministrant in dialogue (Regensburg). In the north the
     sexton is the server: he kneels with the priest (Bugenhagen, Mecklenburg 1540), says the
     *Misereatur* (Kiel), or reads the absolution (Kiel, Hadeln).
   - **Family C.** In Mecklenburg 1552 and Pfalz-Zweibrücken 1557 "another minister or the
     custos" kneels beside the priest and says the prayer for forgiveness, and the choir answers
     the versicle.
   - **The people** keep silence and confess in their hearts (Mecklenburg), repeat the words
     after the priest (Döber, Bugenhagen's pulpit confession, Amberg's weekday prayer), or
     answer "Ja" (Palatinate 1563).
5. **Before Mass.** The priest's *Confiteor* is said at the altar, not in the sacristy. The
   communicant's real preparation is private confession on the Saturday or before the service.
   The Brandenburg and Pfalz-Neuburg orders forbid a "gemeine beicht" (common confession) in its
   place (§4.6).
6. **No Roman text is kept in public.** The Latin *Confiteor* survives only as the priest's
   private prayer, unprinted (Hohenlohe: "das reine Confiteor lateinisch"). Every public
   confession is a new text, in five families of wording (§4.3). The *Misereatur* survives by
   name at Kiel; as a prayer for "us" in Döber; and as the second minister's collect in
   Mecklenburg 1552. The *Indulgentiam* survives in Kantz's "Der allmechtig, barmherzig Gott
   vergeb euch euer sünd!" and in Döber's "Der almechtich, barmherzig Got hat sich unser
   erbarmet".
7. **Five kinds of absolution** (§4.4):
   - **optative** ("wöl sich unser erbarmen");
   - **declaration of grace** in the first person plural ("hat sich unser erbarmet … Das
     verleihe uns Got allen");
   - **indicative** ("sag euch frei, ledig und los"; "vorgebe ich euch"; "entbind ich euch");
   - **conditional declaration** with retention (Strasbourg, Palatinate 1563);
   - **none**, only a word of comfort (Bugenhagen 1528).

   At the beginning of Mass the declaration of grace prevails; in the communion rite, the
   indicative.
8. **The Common Service descends from Mecklenburg** (§4.5). The Common Service's preparation is
   Döber's 1525 sequence by way of Mecklenburg 1552 and Pfalz-Zweibrücken 1557. That sequence is
   the address "Beloved in the Lord", *Adiutorium*, the confession "by nature sinful and
   unclean", the prayer "O most merciful God", and the declaration "Almighty God … hath had
   mercy upon us … Grant this, O Lord, unto us all". The prayer "O most merciful God" was the
   second minister's *Misereatur*; the declaration is a declaration of grace, not an indicative
   absolution. The indicative form of later American books ("Upon this your confession … I
   forgive you all your sins") has its counterpart in Naumburg 1537.
9. **The exhortation** (§5) falls in four places:
   - after the sermon, from the pulpit or altar (Luther 1526, left free);
   - at the altar before the Verba (Brandenburg-Nürnberg 1533, moved there at Luther's
     suggestion; Württemberg);
   - in place of the Preface (Bugenhagen) or after the Sanctus (Mecklenburg 1552);
   - after the Our Father (Nürnberg 1524–1525, Osiander's original place).

   There are six families of text: Luther's paraphrase of the Our Father; Osiander and
   Brandenburg-Nürnberg; Württemberg; Bugenhagen; Mecklenburg 1552, which contains an
   absolution; and the Reformed Palatinate, with the fencing of the table. The principal texts
   are given in full.
10. **No vesting prayers** (§6.1). None of the prayers in the Lutheran Missal Project's *Vesting
    Prayers* occurs in any order: the preparatory psalms, the antiphon *Ne reminiscaris*, the
    *preces* and collects, and the prayers at the washing of hands and at each vestment.
    Vestments are "frei" (indifferent). The priest vests in silence, at Hadeln during the *Te
    Deum* "nicht alse nödig" (not as needful). The *Stufengebet* alone of the medieval priest's
    three preparations survives. The *accessus* survives only as the congregation's *Veni Sancte
    Spiritus*.
11. **After Mass** (§6.3). The *Benedicite* and Ps 150 are never appointed after Mass. They
    occur only as the canticle and psalms of Sunday Lauds, sung by the schoolboys under the
    cantor or by the canons (Pomerania 1535, Schleswig-Holstein 1542, Ansbach 1533). The
    priest's private thanksgiving survives only at Hildesheim 1544 and in Schleswig-Holstein
    1542: he unvests during the closing hymn, kneels, and "danket Godde hemelick vor sick
    sülvest" (thanketh God secretly for himself), with no text given.
12. **Sermon, church door and communion** (§§6.4–6.5):
    - The sermon is framed by a bidding to prayer, the Our Father or a hymn of the Holy Ghost,
      and the general prayer after it. All are led by the preacher, with the people praying and
      the schoolboys singing.
    - No order prints a prayer for the worshipper on entering or leaving church, or private
      prayers for the communicant. Their place is taken by the opening hymn "wann man in die
      Kirche kompt" (Sayn 1590), the prayers the minister says for the communicants
      (Pfalz-Veldenz 1574), and the hymns during the distribution.
    - The nearest thing to a hymnal devotion is the Pfalz-Zweibrücken *Gesangbuch* of 1571/1587,
      which prints the Mecklenburg confession and absolution for the people's use "vor der
      Predig am Sontag" (before the sermon on Sunday).

## 2. Scope, sources and method

**Scope.** The guide covers the evangelical (Lutheran, Upper German and Reformed) orders printed
in Sehling's volumes 1–24. It deals with what the orders set before the Introit, the confession
and absolution wherever they fall, the exhortation to the communicants, and the prayers before
and after the service. It does not treat:
- the Ordinary and Propers (see [`PROPERS_GUIDE.md`](PROPERS_GUIDE.md));
- the collects (see [`COLLECTS_GUIDE.md`](COLLECTS_GUIDE.md));
- the general prayer (see [`GENERAL_PRAYERS_GUIDE.md`](GENERAL_PRAYERS_GUIDE.md));
- the Canon and the communion devotions (see
  [`CANON_IN_GERMAN_MASSES.md`](CANON_IN_GERMAN_MASSES.md));
- private confession (see [`EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md`](EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md) §17).

**Sources.** The quotations come from the `eko.db` database. Candidate passages were found by
searching the whole corpus for:
- the Latin incipits *Confiteor*, *Misereatur*, *Indulgentiam*, *Introibo*, *Adiutorium*,
  *Confitemini*, *Ne reminiscaris*, *Quam dilecta*, *Benedicite* and *Laudate*;
- the German and Low German words for open confession and absolution (*offene beicht*, *offene
  schuld*, *gemeine beicht*, *bicht*, *absolutio*);
- the words for the exhortation (*vermanung*, *vormaninge*, *exhortatio*, *ermanung*), together
  with the address formulas ("Mein allerliebsten", "Lieben freunde", "Myne allerlevesten", "Ir
  geliebten");
- the words for vesting, the sacristy and the preparation (*ornat*, *messgewand*, *angekledet*,
  *praeparatio ad missam*, *Rüsthandlung*);
- prayers before and after the sermon, on entering church, and before and after communion.

Each hit was read in context. Editor's notes that identify a source or a medieval model are
quoted where they bear on the question.

**Method and limits.**
- **Pages.** Each order is cited by Sehling's running head. Pages were checked against the page
  markers in the database.
- **Dates.** Some orders are dated differently in the database's record headings. Hadeln is
  printed by Sehling as the "Hadelner Kirchenordnung von 1526" within a record headed 1590.
  Verden's order is the *Kirchenordnung* of 1606 within a record headed 1612. Döber's Mass of
  1525 lies within the record of the Nürnberg parish churches of 1524.
- **Coverage.** The texts of the exhortation families are reproduced in full for their principal
  witnesses only; the followers listed in §5.1 were identified by their incipits and have not
  all been collated.
- **Negative findings.** That a form "does not occur" (vesting prayers, *Ne reminiscaris*,
  prayers on entering church) means that searches of the whole corpus for its Latin incipits and
  German equivalents found nothing. OCR damage can hide a passage, so such findings are strong
  but not absolute.

## 3. Opening versicles and preliminaries

### 3.1 What the Roman Mass had, and what became of it

**The medieval preparation.** Before the Introit, the Roman Mass of the later Middle Ages had
three layers of preparation for the priest:
1. **The *accessus*** (Akzess): a private office of psalms and prayers said before Mass.
2. **The *praeparatio ad missam*** in the sacristy: the washing of hands and a prayer at the
   putting on of each vestment.
3. **The prayers at the foot of the altar** (*Stufengebet*), said by the priest and server in
   dialogue:
   - the invocation, and *Introibo ad altare Dei* with Ps 43 *Iudica me*;
   - *Adiutorium nostrum in nomine Domini* (Ps 124:8);
   - the *Confiteor*, said first by the priest and then by the server;
   - the *Misereatur* ("May almighty God have mercy upon thee …"), said by each for the other;
   - the *Indulgentiam* ("May the almighty and merciful Lord grant us pardon, absolution and
     remission of our sins");
   - the versicles *Deus, tu conversus*, and the prayers *Aufer a nobis* and *Oramus te*.

Many German diocesan missals also had the versicle *Confitemini Domino quoniam bonus* / *Quoniam
in saeculum misericordia eius* (Ps 118:1) before the *Confiteor*.

**What became of it.** No evangelical order in Sehling keeps the whole of this, and none keeps
*Introibo* or *Iudica me*. What survives falls into the families set out below:

| Family | Form | Who says it | Where | Orders (§) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | The priest's own *Confiteor* (or "what his devotion bids") at the altar, while the choir sings the Introit | The priest, sometimes with a ministrant or a fellow minister | Before or during the Introit | Brandenburg-Nürnberg 1533, Brandenburg 1540, Pfalz-Neuburg 1543, Hohenlohe 1553, Regensburg 1553, Amberg 1550, Buxtehude 1526, Nördlingen 1544, Schleswig-Holstein 1542 (3.2) |
| B | Bugenhagen's kneeling prayer: Our Father and the tract *Domine, non secundum* | The priest and the sexton, alternately | During the opening psalm | Wolfenbüttel 1543, Hildesheim 1544 (3.3) |
| C | A public confession in the people's hearing, with *Adiutorium* and *Confitemini*, a recast *Misereatur* and a declaration of grace | The priest kneeling, with the sexton or a second minister; the people silently or repeating | At the very beginning of Mass | Döber 1525, Lippe [1525–1538], Kiel [after 1526], Mecklenburg 1540 and 1552, Pfalz-Zweibrücken 1557, Pfalz-Veldenz 1574, Hadeln (3.4) |
| D | The *Offene Schuld* moved into the Mass, with an indicative absolution | The priest to the people | Before the Introit, or before the Sacrament | Kantz 1522, Volprecht 1524, Naumburg 1537, Nördlingen 1544 (3.5) |
| E | The Upper German and Reformed opening of the preaching service: confession of sins and a comfortable word | The minister for the congregation | At the beginning of the service, or after the sermon | Strasbourg 1537, Palatinate 1563, Wertheim 1524 (3.6; and §4) |
| F | A hymn, a German psalm, or *Veni Sancte Spiritus* with its versicle and collect | Choir, schoolboys or congregation; the priest the collect | Before the Introit | Luther 1526, Amberg 1544, Verden 1606, Transylvania 1547, Buxtehude 1526, Mecklenburg villages, Sayn 1590 (3.7) |

**The versicles that survive.**
- *Adiutorium nostrum* (Ps 124:8) is the one Roman versicle that survives widely: Kantz 1522,
  Döber 1525, Lippe, Mecklenburg 1540 and 1552, Pfalz-Zweibrücken 1557 and Poullain's French
  liturgy at Frankfurt (Sehling 9, p. 563) all have it.
- *Confitemini Domino quoniam bonus* (Ps 118:1) survives in Döber, Lippe and Mecklenburg 1540,
  but is dropped in Mecklenburg 1552.
- In Verden 1606 the versicle is *Emitte Spiritum tuum* (Ps 104:30), which belongs with *Veni
  Sancte Spiritus*.

### 3.2 Family A: the priest's own *Confiteor* at the altar

The commonest form in the south and in Brandenburg is a private preparation of the priest. He
comes to the altar in his vestments, kneels and says the *Confiteor* or some other prayer of his
own while the choir sings the Introit. The people take no part, and the text is not printed. The
model is the Brandenburg-Nürnberg order. **Brandenburg-Nürnberg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1533**
(Sehling 11, p. 188):

<!-- doc 270 -->
> Ordnung der meß, wie die gehalten soll werden. Zum ersten, wenn der Priester zum altar kumbt,
> mag er das Confiteor oder, was ine sein andacht erinnert, sprechen, darnach den introitum
> lesen, doch das er aus der heiligen schrift genummen sei. Dieweil sollen die schuler, wo man
> schul hat, den introitum auch singen lateinisch. Wo man aber als in dörfern zu solichem
> lateinischen gesang nicht leut hette, soll man ein christenlich teutsch gesang nach
> gelegenheit jedes orts singen.

Order of the Mass, how the same shall be held. First, when the priest cometh to the altar, he
may say the Confiteor, or what his devotion putteth him in mind of, and thereafter read the
Introit, provided that it be taken out of the holy Scripture. Meanwhile the scholars, where
there is a school, shall also sing the Introit in Latin. But where, as in villages, there were
not people for such Latin singing, a Christian German hymn shall be sung, according to the
occasion of each place.

The Mark Brandenburg adds the ministrants. **Brandenburg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1540**
(Sehling 3, pp. 67–68):

<!-- doc 1746 -->
> Erstlich sol der priester, so die mess helt, samt seinen ministranten in iren gewönlichen
> kirchenornaten, nach gewonheit einer jeden kirchen zu dem altar gehen, anfenglich das
> confiteor sprechen; darnach soll der gewönlich introitus, folgend das kyrieeleison, nach
> gelegenheit, darauf das gloria in excelsis, welches sampt dem et in terra zu dem kyrie
> gehöret, gesungen werden

First, the priest that holdeth the Mass, together with his ministrants, in their accustomed
church ornaments, according to the custom of each church, shall go to the altar and at the
beginning say the Confiteor. Thereafter shall be sung the accustomed Introit, then the Kyrie
eleison, as occasion serveth, and upon that the Gloria in excelsis, which together with the Et
in terra belongeth to the Kyrie.

Pfalz-Neuburg copies Brandenburg, but makes the priest kneel and allows a penitential psalm
instead. **Pfalz-Neuburg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1543** (Sehling 13, p. 70):

<!-- doc 386 -->
> Erstlich soll der priester, so die meß halten will, sampt seinen ministranten, wo und wann man
> dieselben zu gebrauchen pflegt, in iren gewonlichen kirchenornaten nach gewonheit einer jeden
> kirchen zu dem altar gehn und anfenglich darvor kniend das Confiteor oder ein feinen bußpsalm
> sprechen.

First, the priest that will hold the Mass, together with his ministrants (where and when they
are wont to be used), shall go to the altar in their accustomed church ornaments, according to
the custom of each church, and at the beginning, kneeling before it, say the Confiteor or a
goodly penitential psalm.

Hohenlohe insists on the Latin text of the Missal. **Hohenlohe, *Kirchenordnung*, 1553**
(Sehling 15, p. 68):

<!-- doc 539 -->
> Zum ersten soll der pfarrherr in seinem ornat und kirchengewand vor deim altar kommen,
> niderknyen und das reine Confiteor lateinisch betten. Indes soll der schulmaynster sampt den
> schulern denn introitum lateinisch singen de tempore, aber allein die, so der heyligen
> geschrift gemeß sind.

First, the parson shall come before the altar in his ornament and church vesture, kneel down,
and pray the pure Confiteor in Latin. Meanwhile the schoolmaster with the scholars shall sing
the Introit *de tempore* in Latin, but only those that are agreeable to the holy Scripture.

**Editor's note** (p. 68). "Confiteor ist das Sündenbekenntnis als Teil des Stufengebetes … Hier
ist das Stück aus der Messe gemeint, während in der Brand. KO 1533 (Sehling 11, 188) nur ein
entsprechendes Stück gefordert war." (The Confiteor is the confession of sins that is part of
the prayers at the foot of the altar. Here the piece from the Mass itself is meant, whereas the
Brandenburg-Nürnberg order of 1533 required only a corresponding piece.) What "reine" (pure) is
meant to exclude is not said; presumably the invocation of the Virgin and the saints.

At Regensburg the *Confiteor* is said in dialogue with the second of two ministrants.
**Regensburg, *Kirchenordnung* under Justus Jonas, 1553** (Sehling 13, pp. 419–420):

<!-- doc 440 -->
> so kombt der priester (das ist der minister, welcher, wie obstehet, wochner ist) sambt zwaien
> ministranten oder diaconen fur den altar und […] betet also mit dem einen ministranten (der
> dazumal secundus ist) das Confiteor kniend. Unterdes singt der chor den introitum de tempore
> lateinisch und die Kyrie eleison zu dreien malen.

then cometh the priest (that is, the minister who, as is said above, is hebdomadary) together
with two ministrants or deacons before the altar, and prayeth thus with the one ministrant (who
is for that time the second) the Confiteor kneeling. Meanwhile the choir singeth the Introit *de
tempore* in Latin and the Kyrie eleison three times.

At Amberg the choir sings an antiphon of the season or *Veni Sancte* over the priest's
*Confiteor*, and the priest then sings a collect. **Amberg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1550**
(Sehling 13, p. 285):

<!-- doc 411 -->
> Die meß würd bei uns schier durchaus wie von alters herkommen mit gewonlicher kleidung,
> liechtern, gesengen und anderen ornaten aufs zierlichist gehalten; den erstlich betet der
> briester vor dem altar knieend das Confiteor. Unter disem singet der chor Haec est dies oder
> Veni, Sancte und der briester darauf Oremus und collectam. Darauf singet der chorus den
> introitum von der dominica und fürfallenden ordenlichen festen.

The Mass is held with us almost throughout as it hath come down of old, with the accustomed
vesture, lights, chants and other ornaments, most seemly; for first the priest prayeth the
Confiteor kneeling before the altar. During this the choir singeth *Haec est dies* or *Veni
Sancte*, and the priest thereupon *Oremus* and the collect. Thereupon the choir singeth the
Introit of the Sunday and of the ordinary feasts that fall.

**Editor's note** (p. 285): "Wahrscheinlich nicht das im Wechsel mit dem Ministranten
gesprochene der Messe, sondern wie auch 1533 (Sehling 11, 188) ein stilles Gebet." (Probably not
the *Confiteor* of the Mass said in alternation with the ministrant, but, as also in 1533, a
silent prayer.)

In the north the same private prayer appears at Buxtehude. **Buxtehude, *Buxtehuder Agende*,
1526** (Sehling 7/1, p. 74):

<!-- doc 2082 -->
> Under dem introitu schall der preister, mit gewontliken missengewande ahngetagen, vor dat
> altar gahn unde vor des altares vote syn gebedt und confessie dohn.

During the Introit the priest, clad in the accustomed Mass vestment, shall go before the altar
and at the altar's foot make his prayer and confession.

In Schleswig-Holstein 1542 the priest falls on his knees before the altar and reads the
*Confiteor* "by sick" (to himself), with an intercession for the preachers, the king and the
duchies
(Sehling 23, p. 89; quoted in full in [`CANON_IN_GERMAN_MASSES.md`](CANON_IN_GERMAN_MASSES.md)
§15.3).

**Nördlingen 1544: the private and the public confession side by side.** Kaspar Löner's order
keeps both. The celebrant first receives absolution from a fellow minister on his own
*Confiteor*, while two choirs, the school and the people, sing *Kum, heiliger Geist* or *Nun
bitten wir* in alternation. Then he turns to the people and says the open confession and the
absolution for them. **Nördlingen, *Kirchenordnung* of Kaspar Löner, 1544**
(Sehling 12, pp. 311–312):

<!-- doc 372 -->
> An hohen festen soll der pfarrer selbs das hohe ampt halten, an den andern aber und suntagen
> der alte pfarrer in seiner geburlichen kleidung fur den alter kumen, alda die absolution von
> einem seiner mitdiener empfahen auf sein Confiteor hin. Indes sollen zwen chore - einer der
> schulen, der ander des volks - an hohen festen singen umb einander Kum, hailiger Geist, alle
> drei gesetze aus. An andern festen aber und sonntagen Nun bitten wir den Heiligen Gaist mit
> allen seinen gesetzen. Wen das aus ist, sol der celebrant sich zu dem volk wenden oder, so man
> je wollt im chor bleiben, […] herausgen fur den alter, alldau das Confiteor oder offne beicht
> dem volk fursprechen und darauf die absolution.

On high feasts the pastor himself shall hold the high office, but on the others and on Sundays
the old pastor shall come before the altar in his due vesture, and there receive the absolution
from one of his fellow ministers upon his Confiteor. Meanwhile two choirs, the one of the
school, the other of the people, shall on high feasts sing *Kum, hailiger Geist* in turn, all
three stanzas out; but on other feasts and Sundays *Nun bitten wir den Heiligen Gaist* with all
its stanzas. When that is out, the celebrant shall turn to the people, or, if they would needs
remain in the choir, come out before the altar, and there say before the people the Confiteor or
open confession, and thereupon the absolution.

> Hiemit ist also eine nur für den Geistlichen und seinen Ministranten bestimmte Rüsthandlung
> gemeint wie Brandenburg 1540 (Sehling 3, 67f.) und Pfalz-Neuburg 1542 […] oder - auf den
> Geistlichen beschränkt — Brandenburg-Nürnberg 1533 (Sehling 11, 188) und in V. Dietrichs
> Agendbüchlein 1543 (Sehling 11, 495). Ob sie sich nur auf das Confiteor und das Misereatur

What is meant here, then, is a preparation appointed only for the clergyman and his ministrant,
as in Brandenburg 1540 and Pfalz-Neuburg 1542, or, restricted to the clergyman, in
Brandenburg-Nürnberg 1533 and in V. Dietrich's *Agendbüchlein* 1543. Whether it refers only to
the Confiteor and the Misereatur together with the Indulgentiam in the narrowest sense, or
whether the whole prayers at the foot of the altar of the Mass are meant (which, moreover, are
not contained in the missals before 1570, but must have been known from elsewhere), cannot be
decided.

On the open confession to the people (p. 312):

<!-- doc 372 -->
> Die Offene Schuld ist hier ein allgemein gottesdienstlicher Rüstgottesdienst für die Gemeinde
> wie etwa auch bei Volprecht (Sehling 11, 39f.) und Döber (Sehling 11, 51), nicht ein Ersatz

The Open Confession is here a general preparatory service of worship for the congregation, as
also with Volprecht and Döber; not a substitute for confession, or its repetition, as with Kantz
or in Nürnberg.

**What family A shows.**
- **No text.** The orders do not print the *Confiteor* they mean. Brandenburg-Nürnberg leaves
  the choice to the priest's "andacht"; Hohenlohe alone demands the "pure" Latin *Confiteor* of
  the Missal.
- **Who.** The priest alone (Brandenburg-Nürnberg, Hohenlohe, Amberg, Buxtehude,
  Schleswig-Holstein), with his ministrants (Brandenburg 1540, Pfalz-Neuburg), with the second
  ministrant in dialogue (Regensburg), or with a fellow minister who absolves him (Nördlingen).
- **When.** At the altar, kneeling, during the Introit or the opening hymn. It is never said in
  the sacristy, and never before the bells.
- **The people** do not hear it. It is the evangelical remnant of the *Stufengebet*.

### 3.3 Family B: Bugenhagen's kneeling prayer with the sexton

Bugenhagen's later orders replace the *Confiteor* with the Our Father and the tract *Domine, non
secundum peccata nostra* (Ps 103:10; Ps 79:8–9). The priest kneels with the sexton and prays
"vor sick und vor dat volk und vor alle nött der christenheit" (for himself and for the people
and for all the need of Christendom). The sexton answers each versicle. This is the form in
Braunschweig-Wolfenbüttel 1543 (Sehling 6/1, pp. 54–55) and Hildesheim 1544
(Sehling 7/2.1, p. 852). Both texts, with translations and the full versicles, are in
[`CANON_IN_GERMAN_MASSES.md`](CANON_IN_GERMAN_MASSES.md) §§15.1–15.2.

**Note.**
- The prayer is said aloud but at the altar, during the opening German psalm, so that in
  practice the people would not have followed it.
- The versicle *Domine, ne memineris iniquitatum nostrarum antiquarum* (Ps 79:8) is the nearest
  thing in the corpus to the antiphon *Ne reminiscaris* of the Missal's preparation (Tobit 3:3),
  which does not occur (§6.2).

### 3.4 Family C: Döber and the Mecklenburg public confession

**The form.** This is the most important family, because it is the source of the modern Lutheran
confession of sins (§4.5). It keeps the shape of the Roman *Stufengebet* but speaks it aloud, in
German, in the people's hearing:
1. an address to the people: "My dearly beloved in God, open your hearts …";
2. the priest kneels; *Adiutorium nostrum* and *Confitemini Domino*;
3. a new confession in the first person singular ("I, poor sinful man …"), said by the priest
   for all;
4. a prayer for forgiveness in the plural, in the place of the *Misereatur*;
5. a declaration of God's mercy, in the place of the *Indulgentiam*, ending "Das verleihe uns
   Got allen" (God grant this unto us all).

It begins in Nürnberg with Andreas Döber's Mass for the New Hospital. **Nürnberg, Andreas Döber,
*Von der evangelischen meß … im Newen Spital*, 1525** (Sehling 11, p. 51):

<!-- doc 249 -->
> Mit ersten, wenn der priester über altar kumbt und den kelch aus dem sack geton hat, kert er
> sich zum volk und spricht laut, wie hernach volgt (auch alle wort, die in der ganzen meß
> sind), so das er doch verstentlich sei den zuhörern: Mein aller liebsten in Got, eroffent eur
> herzen und last uns Got unser sünd bekennen, und sprecht mir nach mit herzlicher begird im
> namen des Vaters und des Suns und des Heilgen Geists! Amen. In dem kniet der priester nider
> für den altar und spricht fein lanksam wie hernach volgt. Unser hilf sei im namen des Herren,
> der erschaffen hat himel und erden! Bekennet dem Herren; dann er ist gut und sein
> barmherzigkeit ist ewich. Und ich armer, sündiger mensch bekenn Got dem allmechtigen, meinem
> schöpfer und erlöser, das ich nit allein gesündiget hab mit gedanken, worten oder werken
> sonder auch in sünden empfangen und geborn, also das alle mein natur und wesen vor seiner
> gerechtigkeit streflich und verdamlich ist. Darumb fleuhe ich zu seiner grundlosen
> barmherzigkeit, such und bite genad. Herr, bis genedich mir armen sünder! Der barmherzig Got
> wöl sich unser erbarmen und uns unsere sünd verzeihen und den Heiligen Geist geben, […] das
> wir durch in seinen götlichen willen erfüllen und das ewig leben empfangen. Amen. Die
> absolution. Spricht der priester: Neigt eur herz zu Got! Der almechtich, barmherzig Got hat
> sich unser erbarmet und seinen einigen Sun für unser sünd in tod geben und umb seinet willen
> uns verzihen, auch allen den, die an seinen heiligen namen glauben, Gottes kinder zu werden
> gewalt geben und den Heiligen Geist verheißen. Wer glaubt und getauft würt, der sol selig
> sein. Das verleihe uns Got allen. Amen. Und einem jglichen geschehe nach seinem glauben! In
> dem, do er spricht: Bit Got für mich, desselben gleichen will ich auch tun! stehet er auf für
> den altar und spricht für das volk (auch mag das volk mit im beten) also: Kum, Heiliger Geist,
> erfülle die herzen deiner glaubigen und entzünde in ihnen das feuer deiner götlichen lieb, der
> du durch mancherlei zungen hast gesammelt die völker der ganzen welt in einigkeit des
> glaubens! Got sei gelobet!

First, when the priest cometh to the altar and hath taken the chalice out of the bag, he turneth
to the people and speaketh aloud, as hereafter followeth (and so also all the words that are in
the whole Mass), so that he may yet be understood of the hearers: My dearly beloved in God, open
your hearts, and let us confess our sins unto God, and say after me with hearty desire, in the
name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Ghost! Amen. Therewith the priest kneeleth
down before the altar and saith full slowly as hereafter followeth. Our help be in the name of
the Lord, who hath made heaven and earth! Confess unto the Lord, for he is good, and his mercy
is everlasting. And I, poor sinful man, confess unto God almighty, my Creator and Redeemer, that
I have sinned not only in thoughts, words or works, but was also conceived and born in sins, so
that all my nature and being is before his righteousness punishable and damnable. Therefore I
flee unto his bottomless mercy, seek and pray for grace. Lord, be merciful to me a poor sinner!
The merciful God vouchsafe to have mercy upon us, and forgive us our sins, and give us the Holy
Ghost, that through him we may fulfil his divine will and receive everlasting life. Amen. The
absolution. The priest saith: Incline your heart to God! The almighty, merciful God hath had
mercy upon us, and hath given his only Son unto death for our sins, and for his sake forgiven
us; and to all them also that believe on his holy name he hath given power to become the
children of God, and hath promised the Holy Ghost. He that believeth and is baptized shall be
saved. God grant this unto us all. Amen. And unto every one be it done according to his faith!
As he saith: Pray God for me, the same will I also do for you! he standeth up before the altar
and saith for the people (and the people also may pray with him) thus: Come, Holy Ghost, fill
the hearts of thy faithful and kindle in them the fire of thy divine love, who through divers
tongues hast gathered the peoples of the whole world in the unity of the faith! God be praised!

**Note.**
- **The Roman skeleton.** *Adiutorium* and *Confitemini* are the versicles of the German
  missals. "Der barmherzig Got wöl sich unser erbarmen …" is the *Misereatur*, now in the plural
  ("unser", us) and said by the priest for priest and people together. "Der almechtich,
  barmherzig Got hat sich unser erbarmet …" stands where the *Indulgentiam* stood, and its
  opening words echo it.
- **The *Orate fratres*.** "Bit Got für mich, desselben gleichen will ich auch tun" is the Roman
  request for the people's prayers, moved here from the offertory.
- **The absolution** is a declaration in the perfect tense ("hat sich unser erbarmet", hath had
  mercy) that ends in a wish ("Das verleihe uns Got allen", God grant this unto us all). It is
  neither the indicative "I absolve you" nor the optative *Misereatur*; see §4.4.
- **Sehling's editor** notes that the *Form und Ordnung einer christlichen meß*
  (Sehling 11, pp. 56 ff.) takes over this beginning, headed "Das Confiteor".

The Low German Mass of Lippe follows Döber. The choir sings the Introit or *Veni Sancte
Spiritus* while the priest reads the confession. **Lippe, *Deutsche Messe*, [c. 1525–1538]**
(Sehling 22, p. 565):

<!-- doc 1549 -->
> Thom ersten lest de prester den confiteor. De confiteor Unse hulpe sy in dem namen des heren,

> barmharticheit ys ewich. Unde ik arme sündige mynsche bekenne Gade dem almechtigen, mynen
> schepper und vorloser, dat ik nicht allene ghesündiget hebbe myt dancken, worden edder
> wercken, sunder ok yn sünden entfangen und gebaren byn, also dat all myne natür und wesent vor

> barmharticheit, soke und bydde gnade. Here, wes my armen sünder barmhartich. De absolutio De
> almechtige, barmhertige Godt, de vor unse sünde synen eynygen sone in den doet ghegeven

First the priest readeth the Confiteor. The Confiteor. Our help be in the name of the Lord, who
hath made heaven and earth. Confess unto the Lord, for he is good, and his mercy is everlasting.
And I, poor sinful man, confess unto God almighty, my Creator and Redeemer, that I have sinned
not only in thoughts, words or works, but was also conceived and born in sins, so that all my
nature and being is before his mercy punishable and damnable; therefore I flee unto his
bottomless mercy, seek and pray for grace. Lord, be merciful to me a poor sinner. The
absolution. The almighty, merciful God, who for our sins hath given his only Son unto death,
vouchsafe to have mercy upon us, and for his sake forgive us our sins, and grant the Holy Ghost
unto all that believe on him, that through him we may fulfil his divine will and receive
everlasting life, amen. […] But while the priest readeth the Confiteor, the choir singeth the
Introit of the Mass.

**Note.** Here the optative prayer ("wolde syck unser erbarmen", vouchsafe to have mercy) is
itself headed "De absolutio". Döber's declaration is missing.

The Kiel Mass takes Döber's address and confession, but its absolution is Kantz's indicative
form, and it is read by the sexton. **Schleswig-Holstein, *Deutsche Messe* (Kiel), [after
1526]** (Sehling 23, p. 55):

> De Confiteor Myne alderlevesten yn Godt, opent iwe harte unde latet uns Gade unße sunde

> des hilgen gestes. Amen. Ick arme, sündige mynsche bokenne my Gade, dem almechtigen, myneme

The Confiteor. My dearly beloved in God, open your hearts and let us confess our sins unto God,
and say after me with desirous heart: In the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy
Ghost. Amen. I, poor sinful man, confess myself unto God almighty, my Creator and Redeemer, that
I have sinned not only in thoughts, words or works, but was also conceived and born in sins, so
that all my nature and being is before his righteousness punishable and damnable. Therefore have
I refuge unto his bottomless mercy, seek and desire grace: Lord, be merciful to me a poor
sinner. The merciful God vouchsafe to have mercy upon us, and forgive us our sins, and grant the
Holy Ghost, that through him we may fulfil his divine will and receive everlasting life. Amen.

There follow the *Misereatur*, read by minister and sexton, and an absolution read by the
sexton: "Unde yck, uth bevele unßes heren Jesu Christi, segge jwe … frigh, leddich unde loß alle
jwe sunde" (And I, by the commandment of our Lord Jesus Christ, say unto you … free, quit and
loosed of all your sins; Sehling 23, p. 55; full text in
[`CANON_IN_GERMAN_MASSES.md`](CANON_IN_GERMAN_MASSES.md) §11.1). "Ick hebbe thoflucht" (I have
refuge) is the reading that Mecklenburg and the later orders keep.

**Mecklenburg 1540: the country Mass.** The Low German *Ordeninge der misse* puts the confession
at the head of every communion Mass. The sexton, "or another Christian", kneels with the priest
and says the prayer for forgiveness. **Mecklenburg, *Ordeninge der misse*, 1540/1545**
(Sehling 5, pp. 150–151):

Mass or communion. The same shall be begun, as heretofore ordained and in use in this land, with

for the Lord Jesus Christ's sake. Lord, be merciful to me a poor sinner. The other minister
answereth this prayer: O almighty, merciful God, who hast given thine only-begotten Son unto
death for us, vouchsafe to have mercy upon us, and for the sake of the same thy beloved Son
forgive us all our sins; and also give us thy Holy Ghost, who may kindle and increase in us true
knowledge of thy divine being and will, and thereto true obedience toward thee; to the end that
we may obtain everlasting life through thy grace, for the Lord Jesus Christ's sake, amen. The
priest saith hereafter this absolution: The almighty, merciful God hath had mercy upon us,
forgiveth us truly all our sins for the sake of his dear Son, whom for our sakes he hath given
unto death, and hath given power to become the children of God to all that believe on his name,
giveth us moreover his Holy Ghost; he that believeth and is baptized shall be saved. God grant
this unto us all, amen. While this confession, prayer and absolution is spoken, the whole church
shall be still and hear it, and also with the priest likewise confess, pray, and take the
absolution to heart, learn it well, and often say the like before God. After this the choir
singeth the Introit *de dominica* or *de festo*.

**Note.**
- **"wie vorhin in diesem lande geordnet"** points back to 1540.
- **What changes from 1540.**
  - The *Confitemini* versicle is dropped. The choir, not the sexton, answers *Adiutorium*.
  - The confession turns from the third person ("bekenne gade", confess unto God) to the second
    ("bekenne fur dir, o allmechtiger gott", confess before thee, O almighty God).
  - It adds "von natur sündig und unrein" (by nature sinful and unclean) and "Ich hab aber
    zuflucht zu deiner grundlosen barmherzigkeit" (But I have refuge unto thy bottomless mercy).
  - The second minister's prayer becomes a collect with a relative clause ("der du …") and asks
    for "wares erkentnis … dazu waren gehorsam" (true knowledge … and true obedience).
  - The absolution gains "vergibt uns warhaftiglich alle unsere sünd" (forgiveth us truly all
    our sins), in the present tense.
- **The people.** In 1540 and 1552 the people are told to keep silence, confess with the priest
  in their hearts, and learn the text. They do not say it aloud, despite the "Sprecht mir nach"
  (say after me) of the address. The address keeps the phrase from Döber.
- **Pfalz-Zweibrücken** copies 1552 word for word (below).

**Pfalz-Zweibrücken 1557 and Pfalz-Veldenz 1574: the Mecklenburg form in the Palatinate.** The
Zweibrücken order prints the 1552 form whole, after an opening hymn to the Holy Ghost sung
"until the church gathers". **Pfalz-Zweibrücken, *Kirchenordnung*, 1557** (Sehling 18, p. 187):

<!-- doc 967 -->
> So denn das Abendmal Christi auff ein Sontag oder andern Feirtag in der Kirchen zu halten
> fürgenommen wirdt, soll anfengklich das gesang, Komm heyliger Geyst etc., Nun bitten wir den
> heyligen Geyst etc. oder sonst ein Teutscher Psalm oder geystlich lied, sonderlich der zeyt
> gemeß, gesungen werden, biß die Kirch zusammen kombt. […] Denn kniet der Priester nider für
> dem Altar, und ein ander Kirchendiener oder Custos neben im, und spricht der Priester mit
> lauter stimme: Unser hilffe stehet im namen des Herrn. Antwort der Chor: Der geschaffen hat
> himel und erden.

When, then, the Supper of Christ is purposed to be held in the church on a Sunday or other holy
day, there shall first be sung the hymn *Komm heyliger Geyst*, etc., *Nun bitten wir den
heyligen Geyst*, etc., or else a German psalm or spiritual song, especially one agreeable to the
season, until the church cometh together. […] Then the priest kneeleth down before the altar,
and another minister of the church or the custos beside him, and the priest saith with a loud
voice: Our help standeth in the name of the Lord. The choir answereth: Who hath made heaven and
earth.

**Editor's notes** (p. 187). The section from here to the Words of Institution is "aus KO
Mecklenburg 1552". The Zweibrücken hymnal of 1571/1587 reprints it under the heading "Beicht,
Gebett und Absolutio vor der Predig am Sontag" (Confession, prayer and absolution before the
sermon on Sunday), that is, as the opening of every Sunday service, communion or not.

Pfalz-Veldenz takes over the Zweibrücken text with one change that matters: the second minister
has gone, and the minister says the confession, the prayer for forgiveness and the absolution
himself, kneeling for the first two and standing for the third. **Pfalz-Veldenz,
*Kirchenordnung*, 1574** (Sehling 18, pp. 498–499):

<!-- doc 1011 -->
> Wann man, das Abendtmahl zuhalten, bey einander versamlet ist, soll erstlich der Tisch des
> herrn, wölchen man sonnst den altar nennet, mit einem rainen, weissen thuch gedeckhet, mit
> Brodt und Wein beraith sein, als dann fahet man an zu singen, knüehendt: Nun bitten wir den
> heiligen Geist. Oder: Khom, heiliger Geist etc. Darauff vermahnet der Pfarrer zur bekantnus
> der sünden und hertzlichem gebett umb verzeyhung derselbigen, ungefarlich mit solchen wortten:
> Meine aller liebsten in dem herrn, lasset uns Gott unnsere Sünde bekhennen und umb vergebung
> in dem Namen unnsers herrn Jesu Christi bitten, Eröffnet derhalben ewere hertzen und sprecht
> mir nach mit hertzlichen begehren zu Gott im glauben an den herrn Christum durch den heiligen
> Geist. […] Dann kniehet der dhiener nider und spricht dem volckh für mit lautter Stimme: Ich
> armer sündiger Mensch bekhenn vor dir, o allmechtiger Gott, meinem Schöpffer und Erlöser, das
> ich leider vil gesündiget hab, nicht allein mit gedanckhen, wortten und werckhen, sonder das
> ich von Natur sündig und unrein bin, in sünden Empfangen und geborn. Ich hab aber zuflucht zu
> deiner grundtlosen Barmhertzigkheit, suche und begehre gnade umb des herrn Jesu Christi
> willen; herr, sey gnedig mir armen sünder. Allmechtiger, barmhertziger Gott, der du deinen
> Eingebornen Sohn für uns in den Todt gegeben hast, wöllest dich unnser erbarmen unnd umb
> desselbigen, deines geliebten Sohns, willen uns all unnser Sünden verzeyhen, auch deinen
> heiligen Geist geben, der in uns wahre erkantnus deines göttlichen wesens unnd willens, darzu
> wahren gehorsam gegen dir anzünde und vermehre, uff das wir diß unnser leben nach allem deinem
> wolgefallen hinbringen und hernach das ewige leben durch dein gnadt umb des herrn Jesu Christi
> willen erlangen etc. Absolutio würdt gesprochen von dem dhiener, wann er auffgestanden: Der
> allmechtig, barmhertzig Gott hat sich unnser erbarmet, vergibt uns warhafftig all unnsere
> sünde und Missethat umb seines geliebten Sohns willen, den er umb unsert willen hat in den
> todt gegeben, und hat macht geben, khinder Gottes zuwerden, allen, die an seinen Namen
> glauben, gibt unns darzu seinen heiligen Geist, der uns erleucht und in alle warheit leitte,
> das wir seelig werden, das verleyhe uns Gott allen, durch Jesum Christum, unnsern hailandt.
> Amen.

When they are assembled together to hold the Supper, the table of the Lord, which is otherwise
called the altar, shall first be covered with a clean white cloth and be made ready with bread
and wine; then they begin to sing, kneeling: *Nun bitten wir den heiligen Geist*, or *Khom,
heiliger Geist*, etc. Thereupon the pastor exhorteth to the confession of sins and to hearty
prayer for the forgiveness of the same, about with such words: My dearly beloved in the Lord,
let us confess our sins unto God and pray for forgiveness in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ;
open therefore your hearts and say after me with hearty desire unto God, in faith in the Lord
Christ, through the Holy Ghost. […] Then the minister kneeleth down and saith before the people
with a loud voice: I, poor sinful man, confess before thee, O almighty God, my Creator and
Redeemer, that I have, alas, sinned much, not only in thoughts, words and works, but that I am
by nature sinful and unclean, conceived and born in sins. But I have refuge unto thy bottomless
mercy, seek and desire grace for the Lord Jesus Christ's sake; Lord, be merciful to me a poor
sinner. Almighty, merciful God, who hast given thine only-begotten Son unto death for us,
vouchsafe to have mercy upon us, and for the sake of the same, thy beloved Son, forgive us all
our sins; also give thy Holy Ghost, who may kindle and increase in us true knowledge of thy
divine being and will, and thereto true obedience toward thee, to the end that we may pass this
our life according to all thy good pleasure, and hereafter obtain everlasting life through thy
grace, for the Lord Jesus Christ's sake, etc. The absolution is spoken by the minister when he
hath stood up: The almighty, merciful God hath had mercy upon us, forgiveth us truly all our
sins and misdeeds for the sake of his beloved Son, whom for our sakes he hath given unto death,
and hath given power to become the children of God to all that believe on his name, giveth us
moreover his Holy Ghost, who enlighteneth us and leadeth us into all truth, that we may be
saved; God grant this unto us all, through Jesus Christ, our Saviour. Amen.

**Hadeln: the confession sung, the sexton absolving.** The Hadeln order, which Sehling dates
1526, has the priest vest during the *Te Deum* at the end of Matins and kneel for *Veni Sancte
Spiritus*. He then sings the confession on the same tone, and the sexton answers and reads the
absolution. **Hadeln, *Kirchenordnung* "von 1526"** (Sehling 5, pp. 467–468):

<!-- doc 1955 -->
> Under des dat men singet Te Deum, schal sick de prester kleden mit gewantlickem missgewant,
> nicht alse nödig, sundern als ein düchtig erbarlich zirath, und nicht darinne de saligkeit
> soken. Des gelicken schal men ein edder twe wasslichte ansticken (dewile idt des heren
> aventmahl ist), und up dem altare brennen laten, bet de misse uthe is. Wenn denn de collecta
> der metten gesungen ist, so schall de prester demödig up de knee sitten gahn, und singen mit
> dem chore Veni sancte spiritus latinisch edder dudesch. Finita collecta blifft de prester
> sitten, und hevet lude mit demsulvigen thono de confession an, und bi ehme de köster
> antwordet, und list de absolution. Alss den singet men einen psalmum vor dem Introitu de
> dominica edder trinitate edder de Spiritu sancto vel pasche, edder wat der schrift nicht tho
> weddern ist.

While the Te Deum is being sung, the priest shall clothe himself with the accustomed Mass
vestment, not as needful, but as a seemly and honest ornament, and not seek salvation therein.
Likewise one or two wax lights shall be lit (since it is the Lord's Supper) and be let burn upon
the altar until the Mass is out. When then the collect of Matins hath been sung, the priest
shall go humbly to kneel upon his knees and sing with the choir *Veni sancte spiritus* in Latin
or German. The collect being ended, the priest remaineth kneeling and beginneth aloud the
confession on the same tone, and the sexton by him answereth and readeth the absolution. Then a
psalm is sung before the Introit, of the Sunday or of the Trinity or of the Holy Ghost or of
Easter, or whatever is not contrary to Scripture.

**Note.** One manuscript (Sehling's VIII, 65) adds the remark: "Nb. dat Confiteor mit des
costers absolution ist gefallen" (Nota bene: the Confiteor with the sexton's absolution has
fallen [away]; Sehling 5, p. 468). The sexton's absolution was given up at some later date.

### 3.5 Family D: the *Offene Schuld* moved into the Mass

**The *Offene Schuld*.** The late medieval *Offene Schuld* ("open guilt") was a general
confession, with a declaratory absolution, recited by the preacher after the sermon in the
vernacular preaching service. It was never part of the Mass. The first evangelical Masses carry
it into the Mass, as an absolution of the communicants. Sehling's editor sets out the
development under Volprecht 1524 (Sehling 11, p. 39):

<!-- doc 247 -->
> Wie schon die deutsche Messe des Kaspar Kantz in Nördlingen 1522 (Smend 74), die Straßburger
> deutsche Messe 1524 (Smend 126) und die Messe Döbers (siehe unten S. 57) nach
> mittelalterlichem Vorbild erst im Predigtgottesdienst (Surgant f. 84ff.), dann aber auch nach
> der Predigt im Meßgottesdienst (Jungmann 1, 631ff.). Bei diesem Übergang aus dem
> Predigtgottesdienst in die Messe wandelte sich aber, wie diese ihre Verwendung zeigt, die
> Offene Schuld für das Volksempfinden aus einem Predigtanhängsel in eine Rüsthandlung für den
> Sakramentsteil.

As already the German Mass of Kaspar Kantz in Nördlingen 1522, the Strasbourg German Mass 1524,
and Döber's Mass, after the medieval model, first in the preaching service, but then also after
the sermon in the Mass. In this passage out of the preaching service into the Mass, however, the
Open Confession was changed, as this use of it shows, in the feeling of the people from an
appendage of the sermon into a preparation for the Sacrament.

**Kantz, Nördlingen 1522.** The earliest form opens with an exhortation, then gives the
absolution with no confession text at all: "Der allmechtig, barmherzig Gott vergeb euch euer
sünd! Und ich, us bevelch unsers Herren Jesu Christi an statt der heiligen kirchen, sag euch
frei, ledig und los aller euer sünden …" (The almighty and merciful God forgive you your sins!
And I, by the commandment of our Lord Jesus Christ, in the stead of the holy Church, pronounce
you free, quit and loosed of all your sins …; Sehling 12, p. 285). Sehling's editor calls it a
fusion of the *Indulgentiam* with the absolution of private confession. The full text, with
*Adiutorium* and the recast *Orate fratres*, is in
[`CANON_IN_GERMAN_MASSES.md`](CANON_IN_GERMAN_MASSES.md) §2.1.

**Volprecht, Nürnberg 1524.** The prior of the Augustinians at Nürnberg says an *offene beicht*
"anstatt des Confiteor" before the Introit, addressed to God and to "brothers and sisters". The
absolution that follows is headed "Absolutio, das Misereatur". **Nürnberg, *Deutsche Messe* of
the prior Volprecht, 1524** (Sehling 11, pp. 39–40):

<!-- doc 247 -->
> Wenn der briester will lesen ein teutsche meß, spricht er vor die offene beicht anstatt des
> Confiteor: Ich armer, elender, sündiger maensch bekenn mich Gott meinem himlischen Vater und
> meinem Herren Jesu Christo meinem seligmacher, euch brüder und schwester und der ganzen
> christlichen versamlung, das ich leider oft und dick wieder Gott, meinen Herrn, gesündigt hab
> mit unglauben und mißtrauen, in nit geliebt uber alle ding und meinen nechsten als mich
> selbes. Das ist mir vest und ser laid von grund meines herzens. O Herr Gott, allmechtiger
> Vater, ich armer sunder ermahn dich deiner gnadenreichen zusagung und versprechung, da du
> verheist vergebung der sund durch das blut deines Suns Jesu Christi, der fur uns gestorben ist
> und sein blut zu vergebung der sund fur uns vergossen hat. Derselbig mein Herr […] Jesus
> Christus hat auch durch sein heiligen mund gesprochen, wo zwen oder drei in seinern namen
> versamelt sein, da sei er in irer mitte, und, was sie von dir in seinem namen bitten, das
> sollen sie gewert sein. So bitten wir verzeihung unser sund in seinen namen. Herr Gott, sprich
> zu uns: Nach deinem glauben geschech dir! Gee hin in frid! Sundige nit meer! Deine sund sein
> dir vergeben, verziehen und nachgelassen. Absolutio, das Misereatur: Mein lieber bruder und
> schwester, Gott hat sich unser erbarmet und hat uns verzihen alle unser sund und will uns
> geben das ewig leben. Amen.

When the priest will read a German Mass, he saith beforehand the open confession instead of the
Confiteor: I, poor, wretched, sinful man, confess myself unto God my heavenly Father and unto my
Lord Jesus Christ my Saviour, unto you, brothers and sisters, and unto the whole Christian
congregation, that I have, alas, often and much sinned against God my Lord with unbelief and
mistrust, not loved him above all things, and my neighbour as myself. That is sore and grievous
unto me from the bottom of my heart. O Lord God, almighty Father, I, a poor sinner, put thee in
mind of thy gracious pledge and promise, wherein thou promisest forgiveness of sins through the
blood of thy Son Jesus Christ, who died for us and shed his blood for us for the forgiveness of
sins. The same my Lord Jesus Christ hath also spoken by his holy mouth: where two or three are
gathered together in his name, there is he in the midst of them; and what they ask of thee in
his name, that shall be granted them. So we pray for the pardon of our sins in his name. Lord
God, say unto us: According to thy faith be it unto thee! Go in peace! Sin no more! Thy sins are
forgiven, pardoned and remitted thee. Absolution, the Misereatur: My dear brother and sister,
God hath had mercy upon us, and hath pardoned us all our sins, and will give us everlasting
life. Amen.

**Note.**
- **"euch brüder und schwester"** keeps the *Confiteor*'s "et vobis, fratres" (and to you,
  brethren), with the saints left out.
- **The absolution** is in the first person plural ("unser", us), so the priest includes
  himself; it is a declaration, not a sentence of loosing.

**Naumburg 1537: an indicative absolution at the start of Mass.** At St. Wenzel's in Naumburg
the choir sings *Kom heiliger geist herre gott*, and the priest then reads "das confiteor zum
volk". It is a bidding to prayer, a confession said by all, and an absolution in the first
person singular. **Naumburg, *Kirchen-Ordnung* for St. Wenzel's, 1537** (Sehling 2, pp. 77–78):

<!-- doc 1219 -->
> Darauf list der priester das confiteor zum volk wie volget. Lieben freunde, dieweil wir itzunt
> alle in dem dienst gottes des almechtigen zu hören sein heiliges gotliches wort vorsamlet
> sein, so wollen wir erstlichen den herrn umb seine gnade anrufen und bitten, das er uns unsere
> herzen zu dem warhaftigem Vorstand seines gütlichen Worts eröffnen wolle […] Dieweil wir aber
> alle sunder sein, so last uns erstlichen unsere sunde bekennen und dieselben gott dem
> almechtigen abbitten und sprecht. Ich armer sunder bekenne dir o almechtiger gott und
> barmherziger vater fur dieser ganzen gemein alle meine sunde und missethat, dardurch ich dich
> so mannichfeltig zu zorn vorursacht und bit dich durch deine grundlose barmherzigkeit, und
> durch das bittere leiden und sterben deines lieben sohns JESU CHRISTI du wollest gnedig sein
> mir armen sundern. Absolutio. Auf solch euer bekentnus aus bevel des herrn Jesu Christi anstat
> der heiligen christlichen kirchen vorgebe ich euch all eure sunde im namen des vaters und des
> sons und des heiligen geistes. Amen. Der fried des herrn Jesu Christi sei mit euch allen.
> Amen. Hierauf singt der ganze chor das kyrie eleison

Thereupon the priest readeth the Confiteor to the people, as followeth. Dear friends, forasmuch
as we are now all gathered in the service of God the almighty to hear his holy, godly word, we
will first call upon the Lord for his grace and pray him that he would open our hearts to the
true understanding of his divine word […] But forasmuch as we are all sinners, let us first
confess our sins and beg pardon for the same of God the almighty, and say: I, poor sinner,
confess unto thee, O almighty God and merciful Father, before this whole congregation all my
sins and misdeeds, whereby I have so manifoldly provoked thee to wrath; and I pray thee, through
thy bottomless mercy and through the bitter passion and death of thy dear Son JESUS CHRIST, that
thou wouldest be gracious to me, poor sinner. Absolution. Upon this your confession, by the
command of the Lord Jesus Christ, in the stead of the holy Christian Church, I forgive you all
your sins, in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Ghost. Amen. The peace of
the Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen. Thereupon the whole choir singeth the Kyrie
eleison.

**Note.** "Auf solch euer bekentnus … anstat der heiligen christlichen kirchen vorgebe ich euch
all eure sunde" is the formula of private confession, used here over the whole congregation.
Compare the Brandenburg and Pfalz-Neuburg prohibitions of a "gemeine beicht" (§4.6).

### 3.6 Family E: the Upper German and Reformed opening

In Strasbourg and the Reformed orders the service begins with the minister's call to confession,
a prayer of confession in the first person plural, and a comfortable word as absolution. This is
the *Offene Schuld* of the preaching service, kept at its head. **Strasbourg, *Agende*,
1537/1541** (Sehling 20/1, p. 270):

<!-- doc 1302 -->
> Das Confiteor oder offene schuld: Bekennen Gott, dem Herren, und verjehen mitt mir ein jedes
> seine sünd und missethat: Almechtiger, ewiger Got und vatter, wir bekennen und verjehen dir,
> das wir inn ungerechtigkeiten empfangen, voller sünd und ubertrettung sein inn allem unserm
> leben, als die, so deinem wort nit volkomen glauben noch deinen heiligen gebotten nach kommen.
> Wir bitten dich, sihe an dein güte, und umb deins namens willen seie genedig und verzeihe
> unserer missethat, die leider gros ist. Ein absolution oder trostspruch, I. Timothei I: Das
> ist gewislich war unnd ein theur werdes wort: Christus Jesus ist kommen inn die welt, die
> sünder selig zu machen. Ein jedes bekenne sich in warheit mit dem H. Paulo in seinem hertzen
> und glaube inn Christum, so versprich ich euch in seinem namen verzeihung aller ewer sünden
> und sage euch derselbigen los auff erden, das ir iren auch im himel los seien in ewigkeit,
> Amen. Etwan nimet er andere sprüch, die uns der verzeihung der sünden unnd bezalung Christi
> für unsere sünd getrüsten, Als: Joannis am III.: Also hat Got die welt geliebt, das er seinen
> einigen Sun gab, auff das alle, die an in glauben, nit verloren werden, sondern das ewig leben
> haben.

The Confiteor or open confession: Confess unto God the Lord, and acknowledge with me, every one,
his sins and misdeeds: Almighty, everlasting God and Father, we confess and acknowledge unto
thee that we were conceived in iniquities, and are full of sin and transgression in all our
life, as they that neither believe thy word perfectly nor follow thy holy commandments. We pray
thee, look upon thy goodness, and for thy name's sake be gracious and pardon our misdeed, which,
alas, is great. An absolution or comfortable word, 1 Timothy 1: This is a true saying and a
precious word: Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners. Let every one confess himself
in truth with Saint Paul in his heart, and believe in Christ; so I promise you in his name the
pardon of all your sins, and pronounce you loosed of the same on earth, that ye may be loosed of
them also in heaven for ever, Amen. Sometimes he taketh other sayings that comfort us with the
pardon of sins and Christ's payment for our sins, as John 3: God so loved the world, that he
gave his only Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.

**Note.**
- **The name.** "Das Confiteor oder offene schuld": in Strasbourg the open confession *is* the
  *Confiteor*.
- **The absolution** is conditional on each one's confession and faith ("Ein jedes bekenne sich
  … und glaube … so versprich ich euch"). Further comfortable words follow: John 3:35–36, Acts
  10:43 and 1 John 2:1–2 (p. 271).
- **A second Confiteor** follows: "Almechtiger, ewiger Gott und vatter, wir bekennen und
  verjehen, das wir leider in sünden empfangen und geboren sind unnd daher geneigt zu allem
  argen unnd treg zu allem guten …" (Almighty, everlasting God and Father, we confess and
  acknowledge that we were, alas, conceived and born in sins, and therefore inclined to all evil
  and slothful to all good …; p. 271). The Palatinate editor names this "other Confiteor" among
  the sources of the Palatinate prayer below.

**The Palatinate 1563.** The Reformed order of Elector Frederick III opens the Sunday morning
service with a prayer of confession before the sermon, and puts the confession, absolution and
retention after the sermon (§4.4). The prayer before the sermon begins **Palatinate,
*Kirchenordnung*, 1563** (Sehling 14, p. 388):

<!-- doc 504 -->
> Für der predig, insonderheyt an den Son- und feyertagen morgens und an bettagen, soll diß
> nachvolgend gebet dem volck fürgesprochen werden, in welchem die christlich gemein des
> menschhchen elendts außtrücklich erinnert und die heilsame gnade Gottes begert wirdt, auf daß
> die hertzen zur demut bereit werden und das wort der gnaden desto begierlicher annemen. Gnad,
> fried und barmhertzigkeyt etc. Himmlischer vater, ewiger und barmhertziger Gott, wir bekennen
> und verjehen für deiner göttlichen majestet, daß wir arme, elende sünder seind, empfangen und
> geboren in aller boßheyt und verderbnuß, geneigt zu allem bösen, unnütz zu einigem guten, und
> daß wir mit unserm sündlichen leben ohne underlaß deine heilige gebot ubertreten

Before the sermon, especially on Sundays and holy days in the morning, and on days of prayer,
this following prayer shall be spoken before the people, wherein the Christian congregation is
expressly reminded of man's misery, and the wholesome grace of God is desired, to the end that
hearts may be made ready unto humility and receive the word of grace the more desirously. Grace,
peace and mercy, etc. Heavenly Father, eternal and merciful God, we confess and acknowledge
before thy divine majesty that we are poor, wretched sinners, conceived and born in all
wickedness and corruption, inclined to all evil, unprofitable to any good, and that with our
sinful life we transgress thy holy commandments without ceasing

**Note.**
- **The opening** is the apostolic greeting "Gnad, fried und barmhertzigkeyt" (Grace, peace and
  mercy) in the place of the versicles. The prayer ends with the Our Father (p. 389).
- **The editor** traces the prayer to Geneva 1563, Frankfurt 1555 and "Straßburg … (das andere
  Confiteor)", the second Strasbourg confession above.
- **Poullain and Micron.** The French stranger church at Frankfurt opens with *Adiutorium
  nostrum in nomine Domini* and a *Confessio peccatorum*, "Domine Deus, Pater aeterne …"
  (Sehling 9, p. 563). Micron's Dutch order for London, also used at Emden, has an open
  confession with a conditional declaration (Sehling 7/1, p. 602).

**Wertheim 1524: the confession after the sermon.** The earliest report of a Mass after the
sermon is Franz Kolb's letter to Luther. **Wertheim, Franz Kolb to Luther, 27 August 1524**
(Sehling 11, p. 707):

<!-- doc 322 -->
> primum convenimus omnes ad concionem; facta concione, statim omissis ornatibus huc usque
> usitatis, paratis pane et vino, flexis ante altare genubus, alta et teutonica voce dico
> Confiteor brevissimum, deinde Pater noster, pro tertio Credo in Deum; statim surgo, consecro
> panem nudis Christi verbis et calicem, et sine omni adhortatione communico fratrem meum
> capellanum cum aliis fratribus et sororibus.

First we all come together to the sermon; the sermon being done, straightway, the ornaments
hitherto used being laid aside, bread and wine being made ready, with knees bowed before the
altar, I say with a loud voice and in German a very short Confiteor, then the Pater noster,
thirdly the Credo in Deum; straightway I rise, consecrate the bread with the bare words of
Christ, and the cup, and without any exhortation communicate my brother the chaplain with the
other brethren and sisters.

### 3.7 Family F: a hymn, a psalm or *Veni Sancte Spiritus* at the beginning

**Luther.** The *Formula missae* (1523) begins with the Introit. The *Deutsche Messe* (1526)
begins with a German hymn or psalm, followed directly by the Kyrie. **Wittenberg, Luther,
*Deudsche messe und ordnung gottis diensts*, 1526** (Sehling 1, p. 14):

<!-- doc 3 -->
> Zum anfang aber singen wir ein geistlich lied oder einen deudschen psalmen in primo tono auf
> die weise wie folget. „Ich will den Herrn loben alle zeit.“ (Psalm 34, 1 —23). Darauf kyrie
> eleison, auch im selben thon, drei mal und nicht neun mal

But at the beginning we sing a spiritual song or a German psalm in the first tone, after the
manner as followeth: "I will bless the Lord at all times" (Psalm 34:1–23). Thereupon Kyrie
eleison, also in the same tone, three times and not nine times.

Luther's Mass has no confession and no versicles before the Introit or the opening psalm.

**The Holy Ghost before the Introit.** Several orders recast the medieval *accessus* as a
congregational preparation of the Holy Ghost. At the Amberg hospital church the schoolmaster and
boys sing *Veni Sancte*, and the priest sings a German collect with the versicle. **Amberg,
*Ordnung der kirchen* … *im spital*, 1544** (Sehling 13, p. 282):

<!-- doc 410 -->
> Zum ersten, soe der priester in seinem habitu nach altem gebrauch der kirchen uber den altar
> tritt, singt unser schulmeister mit den knaben das lateinisch Veni, Sancte..., darauf der
> priester ein deutsche collecten sampt dem versikel, umb gnad des Hailigen Gaistes bittende.
> Nachmals singt der chor den introitum missae de S[ancta] Trinitate oder sonst de tempore

First, when the priest in his habit, according to the old use of the church, goeth up to the
altar, our schoolmaster singeth with the boys the Latin *Veni, Sancte …*; thereupon the priest a
German collect together with the versicle, praying for the grace of the Holy Ghost. Afterward
the choir singeth the Introit of the Mass of the Holy Trinity, or else *de tempore*.

**Editor's note** (p. 282). This is "eine Rüsthandlung für die Gemeinde anstatt des
Stufengebetes (mit dem Confiteor)" (a preparation for the congregation in place of the prayers
at the foot of the altar, with the Confiteor), probably a form of the *accessus*. The note is
quoted in full in §6.1. The collect is presumably the Pentecost collect *Deus, qui corda
fidelium*, and the versicle *Cor mundum crea in me, Deus*, which Luther used at his ordinations
(WA 38, 424).

At Verden the choir sings *Veni Sancte Spiritus*, the priest sings *Emitte Spiritum tuum*, and
the collect *Deus, qui corda fidelium* follows. **Verden, *Kirchenordnung*, 1606**
(Sehling 7/1, pp. 155–156):

<!-- doc 2090 -->
> Erstlich, wann der priester oder pfarrer vor den altar getretten, so soll der chor die
> antiphen Veni, sancte Spiritus anfahen. Darnach soll der priester singen: Emitte Spiritum et
> creabuntur. Der chor soll antworten: Et renovabis faciem terrae […] Ferner soll der priester
> singen: Oremus. Deus, qui corda fidelium. Darauf soll der introitus de tempore, Kyrie eleison,
> Gloria in excelsis Deo, item Et in terra pax gesungen werden

First, when the priest or pastor hath stepped before the altar, the choir shall begin the
antiphon *Veni, sancte Spiritus*. Thereafter the priest shall sing: *Emitte Spiritum et
creabuntur*. The choir shall answer: *Et renovabis faciem terrae* […] Further the priest shall
sing: *Oremus. Deus, qui corda fidelium*. Thereupon shall be sung the Introit *de tempore*,
Kyrie eleison, Gloria in excelsis Deo, and Et in terra pax.

**Editor's note** (p. 155): "Unsere Liturgie bis zum Introitus ist eine 'praeparatio ad missam'.
Im späteren Mittelalter hatte man aus dem privaten priesterlichen Vorbereitungsoffizium (Akzeß)
manchmal eine Hore der Kleriker und gleichsam ein 'officium de Spiritu sancto' gestaltet" (Our
liturgy up to the Introit is a *praeparatio ad missam*. In the later Middle Ages the private
preparatory office of the priest, the *accessus*, had sometimes been made into an hour of the
clergy, as it were an "office of the Holy Ghost").

The same pattern stands in other orders:
- **Kantz 1522** and **Döber 1525**: *Veni Sancte Spiritus*, said by the priest for the people
  after the absolution (§3.4).
- **Buxtehude 1526**: on weekdays a German psalm before the Introit, "alse Kum, hilliger Geist"
  (Sehling 7/1, p. 74).
- **Naumburg 1537**, **Nördlingen 1544**, **Pfalz-Zweibrücken 1557** and **Pfalz-Veldenz 1574**:
  *Kom heiliger geist herre gott* or *Nun bitten wir den heiligen Geist*, sung by the choir or
  congregation, at Pfalz-Veldenz kneeling.
- **Hadeln**: *Veni sancte spiritus*, priest kneeling with the choir (§3.4).
- **Hof 1592**: *Veni Sancte Spiritus* "ante introitum" at the Monday office in the monastery
  church, sung in three parts (Sehling 11, p. 464).

**The Benedictus at Hermannstadt.** The Transylvanian order sings Zechariah's canticle in German
before the Introit on Sundays, in place of the abolished procession. **Transylvania,
*Kirchenordnung*, 1547, Latin version** (Sehling 24, p. 223):

<!-- doc 1669 -->
> Dominicis diebus pro abrogato circuitu, ante Introitum, canitur Germanicum canticum Zachariae:
> Benedictus Dominus aut si quibus magis tunc placeant litaniae, tempore Paschali: Salve festa
> dies. Aliis diebus festis haec praetermittimus, initium missae sumentes ab introitu de tempore
> aut alio simili, si non repugnet scripturae.

On Sundays, in place of the abolished procession, before the Introit, there is sung in German
the canticle of Zacharias, *Benedictus Dominus*; or, if the litany please any better at that
time; in Eastertide, *Salve festa dies*. On other feast days we leave these out, taking the
beginning of the Mass from the Introit *de tempore* or another like it, if it be not contrary to
Scripture.

**In the villages.** Where there was no Latin choir, a German psalm or hymn stood in place of
the Introit:
- Mecklenburg 1540: "Up den dörperen schal men stedes einen düdeschen psalm singen vor den
  introitum" (in the villages a German psalm shall always be sung before the Introit; Sehling 5,
  p. 151).
- Brandenburg-Nürnberg 1533: "ein christenlich teutsch gesang" (§3.2).
- Sayn 1590: "Erstlich, wann man in die Kirche kompt, soll man einen Teutschen Psalm singen.
  Darnach soll der Pfarrherr ein Collect lesen" (First, when one cometh into the church, a
  German psalm shall be sung; thereafter the pastor shall read a collect; Sehling 19/1, p. 412).

## 4. Confession and absolution: what the orders did with the *Confiteor*

### 4.1 Where it falls

The confession of sins stands in four different places in the orders, and a few orders have it
in two:

| Place | Form | Orders |
|---|---|---|
| **Before the Introit, privately** | The priest's *Confiteor* or other prayer at the altar, during the Introit | Brandenburg-Nürnberg 1533, Brandenburg 1540, Pfalz-Neuburg 1543, Hohenlohe 1553, Regensburg 1553, Amberg 1550, Buxtehude 1526, Schleswig-Holstein 1542, Nördlingen 1544; Bugenhagen's kneeling prayer at Wolfenbüttel 1543 and Hildesheim 1544 (§§3.2–3.3) |
| **Before the Introit, publicly** | Address, confession and absolution in the people's hearing | Volprecht 1524, Döber 1525, Lippe, Kiel, Mecklenburg 1540 and 1552, Naumburg 1537, Nördlingen 1544, Pfalz-Zweibrücken 1557, Pfalz-Veldenz 1574, Hadeln; Strasbourg 1537; Palatinate 1563 (prayer before the sermon) (§§3.4–3.6) |
| **After the sermon** | Open confession from the pulpit, or at the altar before the Sacrament | Bugenhagen, Brunswick 1528; Wertheim 1524 (§3.6); Kurpfalz 1556; Palatinate 1563; Amberg 1550 (§4.4) |
| **In the communion rite** | Confession and absolution after the exhortation, before the Verba | Kantz 1522; Württemberg 1536 and its followers; Nürnberg after 1569; Mecklenburg 1552 (the absolution inside the second exhortation) (§§4.4, 5) |

**Mecklenburg 1552** has it twice: the public confession at the beginning of every Mass (§3.4),
and an absolution inside the exhortation before communion (§5.6). **Nördlingen 1544** has it
twice at the beginning: the celebrant's own *Confiteor*, absolved by a fellow minister, and then
the open confession to the people (§3.2).

**The Palatinate** moves it. Kurpfalz 1556 sets the order "die predigt, offen beicht und
gemeines gebet" (the sermon, open confession and common prayer; Sehling 14, p. 165). In 1563 the
confession and absolution follow the Sunday morning sermon, and the Supper has its own
examination by questions (§4.4).

### 4.2 Who says it, and does the chancel party pray it together?

- **The priest alone.** In family A the priest says the *Confiteor* by himself at the altar,
  silently or "by sick" (Brandenburg-Nürnberg 1533, Hohenlohe 1553, Amberg 1550,
  Schleswig-Holstein 1542).
- **The priest and the ministrants.** Brandenburg 1540 and Pfalz-Neuburg 1543 have the priest go
  to the altar "samt seinen ministranten". At Regensburg 1553 the priest prays the *Confiteor*
  kneeling with "dem einen ministranten (der dazumal secundus ist)", that is, in the Roman
  dialogue with the server.
- **The priest and the sexton.** In the north the sexton is the server:
  - Bugenhagen's prayer is said by the priest kneeling "mit dem köster" (Wolfenbüttel) or "mit
    dem oppermanne" (Hildesheim), who answers each versicle.
  - In Mecklenburg 1540 the sexton "edder ein ander christen" (or another Christian) kneels with
    the priest and says the prayer for forgiveness.
  - At Kiel the sexton reads the *Misereatur* and the absolution, and the priest says "Amen".
  - At Hadeln the sexton answers the priest's sung confession and reads the absolution. One
    manuscript notes that this "ist gefallen" (has been given up).
- **The priest and a second minister.** Mecklenburg 1552 and Pfalz-Zweibrücken 1557: "ein ander
  kirchendiener oder custos" (another minister of the church, or the custos) kneels beside the
  priest; "der ander diener" says the prayer for forgiveness; the choir answers *Adiutorium*. At
  Nördlingen 1544 a fellow minister absolves the celebrant.
- **The people.** The people take one of four parts:
  - They say it **after the priest**: "sprecht mir nach" (say after me; Döber 1525) and "spreket
    de bicht mit my" (say the confession with me; Bugenhagen 1528).
  - They **confess silently** with him: Mecklenburg 1540 and 1552, "sol die ganze kirch stille
    sein … auch mit dem priester also bekennen" (the whole church shall be still … and also
    confess likewise with the priest).
  - They **confess in their hearts**: Strasbourg 1537, "Ein jedes bekenne sich … in seinem
    hertzen" (let every one confess himself … in his heart).
  - They **answer "Ja"** to questions: Palatinate 1563 (§4.4).

**Is it done privately before Mass?** In two senses, yes:
- **The priest.** His *Confiteor* (family A) is private, but it is said at the altar at the
  beginning of Mass, not in the sacristy beforehand.
- **The communicants.** The usual Lutheran preparation was not a general confession at all, but
  each communicant's private confession, examination and absolution on the Saturday or before
  the service. The general confession was never allowed to replace it in Brandenburg (§4.6; the
  private rite is treated in [`EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md`](EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md)
  §17).

### 4.3 Old texts or new?

**The Latin *Confiteor*.** No evangelical order in Sehling prints the Roman *Confiteor*. Where
it is kept it is the priest's private prayer, and the text is assumed (§3.2). Hohenlohe 1553
wants "das reine Confiteor lateinisch" (the pure Confiteor in Latin); Brandenburg-Nürnberg
allows "das Confiteor oder, was ine sein andacht erinnert" (the Confiteor, or what his devotion
puts him in mind of).

**New confessions.** Every public confession in the corpus is a new text. There are five
families of wording:

| Family | Incipit | Orders |
|---|---|---|
| Döber–Mecklenburg | "Und ich armer, sündiger mensch bekenn Got dem allmechtigen, meinem schöpfer und erlöser, das ich nit allein gesündiget hab mit gedanken, worten oder werken …" | Döber 1525, Lippe, Kiel, Mecklenburg 1540, 1552, Pfalz-Zweibrücken 1557, Pfalz-Veldenz 1574 |
| Volprecht | "Ich armer, elender, sündiger maensch bekenn mich Gott meinem himlischen Vater … euch brüder und schwester …" | Volprecht 1524 |
| Naumburg | "Ich armer sunder bekenne dir o almechtiger gott und barmherziger vater fur dieser ganzen gemein alle meine sunde und missethat …" | Naumburg 1537 |
| Württemberg | "Ich armer sünder bekenn mich Gott, meinem himelischen vatter, das ich leider schwerlich und manigfalt gesündet hab, nit allein mit eusserlichen, groben sünden, sonder vil mer mit innerlicher angeborner plindtheit …" | Württemberg 1536; Kurpfalz 1556; Palatinate 1563 (in the second person, "für dir, meinem Gott und schöpfer") |
| Strasbourg | "Almechtiger, ewiger Got und vatter, wir bekennen und verjehen dir, das wir inn ungerechtigkeiten empfangen …" | Strasbourg 1537; Palatinate 1563 (prayer before the sermon); Poullain; Micron |

Bugenhagen's Brunswick confession of 1528 is a sixth, unique form: a confession against the
Creed and the two tables of the law, with a pause for each to name his own sin (§4.4).

**The confession's two points.** The Döber family confesses actual sin "mit gedanken, worten
oder werken" (in thoughts, words or works: the *cogitatione, verbo et opere* of the *Confiteor*)
*and* original sin, "in sünden empfangen und geborn" (conceived and born in sins). Württemberg
confesses "innerlicher angeborner plindtheit, unglauben, zweifelung" (inward inborn blindness,
unbelief, doubt). Both make original sin and unbelief the matter of the confession.

**What became of the *Misereatur*.** The Roman *Misereatur tui omnipotens Deus* ("Almighty God
have mercy upon thee") is said by the server for the priest and by the priest for the server. In
the orders:
- **Kept by name.** At Kiel: "Minister legit: Myseriator. De coster list: Miseriator" (the
  minister reads the Misereatur; the sexton reads the Misereatur). The text is not given.
- **Recast as a prayer for us all.** Döber: "Der barmherzig Got wöl sich unser erbarmen …" (The
  merciful God vouchsafe to have mercy upon us …). Lippe heads this same prayer "De absolutio".
- **Given to the second minister.** In Mecklenburg 1540 "De dener antwerdet dit gebet" (the
  minister answereth this prayer), as the server answered the priest's *Confiteor* with the
  *Misereatur*. In 1552 it becomes a full collect, "O allmechtiger barmherziger gott, der du
  deinen eingebornen son für uns in den tod gegeben hast, wollest dich unser erbarmen …" (O
  almighty, merciful God, who hast given thine only-begotten Son unto death for us, vouchsafe to
  have mercy upon us …).
- **The name given to a declaration.** Volprecht heads his absolution "Absolutio, das
  Misereatur" (§3.5).

**What became of the *Indulgentiam*.** The Roman *Indulgentiam, absolutionem et remissionem
peccatorum nostrorum tribuat nobis omnipotens et misericors Dominus* is a wish in the first
person plural. Two orders keep its opening words:
- **Kantz 1522**: "Der allmechtig, barmherzig Gott vergeb euch euer sünd!" (The almighty and
  merciful God forgive you your sins!), in the second person, followed by the indicative
  absolution.
- **Döber 1525**: "Der almechtich, barmherzig Got hat sich unser erbarmet …" (The almighty,
  merciful God hath had mercy upon us …), recast as a declaration in the perfect tense.

### 4.4 Optative, declaratory or indicative?

The absolutions fall into five types. The modern Common Service declaration of grace is type 2.

| Type | Formula | Orders |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Optative** (a wish or prayer) | "Der barmherzig Got wöl sich unser erbarmen und uns unsere sünd verzeihen …" (vouchsafe to have mercy upon us and forgive us) | Döber 1525 (the *Misereatur*); Lippe and Kiel; Mecklenburg 1540; Mecklenburg 1552 and Pfalz-Zweibrücken 1557 (as the second minister's prayer) |
| **2. Declaration of grace** (first person plural, no "I") | "Der almechtich, barmherzig Got hat sich unser erbarmet … Das verleihe uns Got allen" (hath had mercy upon us … God grant this unto us all) | Döber 1525; Mecklenburg 1540 and 1552; Pfalz-Zweibrücken 1557; Pfalz-Veldenz 1574; Volprecht 1524 ("Gott hat sich unser erbarmet") |
| **3. Indicative** (the minister, by Christ's command, looses) | "ich, us bevelch unsers Herren Jesu Christi an statt der heiligen kirchen, sag euch frei, ledig und los" (I, by the commandment of our Lord Jesus Christ, in the stead of the holy Church, pronounce you free, quit and loosed) | Kantz 1522; Kiel (read by the sexton); Naumburg 1537; Württemberg 1536; Nürnberg after 1569; Mecklenburg 1552 (in the exhortation) |
| **4. Conditional declaration, with retention** | "Sovil nun euer sein, die … denselbigen … verkündige ich … Soviel aber under euch seind, die noch einen gefallen haben an iren sünden …" (As many of you as … to them I declare … But as many among you as still have pleasure in their sins …) | Strasbourg 1537 (without retention); Micron; Palatinate 1563 |
| **5. No absolution**, only a word of comfort | "Jesus Christus is unse salicheit ewichlick" (Jesus Christ is our salvation for ever) | Bugenhagen, Brunswick 1528; Palatinate 1563 (Supper examination) |

**Type 2 and type 3.** The declaration of grace (type 2) is the commonest form at the
*beginning* of the Mass. The indicative (type 3) is the commonest form in the *communion rite*,
where it comes out of private confession. The orders do not argue over the difference.
Württemberg 1536 offers both, and in the second person singular.

**Württemberg 1536: the open confession and three absolutions.** After the exhortation the
pastor says the confession for all, then one of three absolutions; the congregation then sings
the Our Father "als ein offenlich beicht" (as a public confession). **Württemberg,
*Kirchenordnung*, 1536** (Sehling 16, pp. 108–109):

<!-- doc 651 -->
> Nach der vermanung soll er inen vorsagen die offenliche beicht unnd nachvolgends die
> offenliche absolution, ungevarlich auff nachvolgende weis: Ich armer sünder bekenn mich Gott,
> meinem himelischen vatter, das ich leider schwerlich und manigfalt gesündet hab, nit allein
> mit eusserlichen, groben sünden, sonder vil mer mit innerlicher angeborner plindtheit,
> unglauben, zweifelung, kleinmütigkeit, ungedult, hochfart, bösen lüsten, heimlichen neyd unnd
> hass, mißvergünstig etc., wie das mein Herr Gott an mich erkendt und ich leider so
> volnkommenlich nit erkennen kan, also rewen sie mich und seind mir leid, beger gnad von Gott.
> Absolutio: Der allmechtig Got hat sich dein erbarmet und durch verdienst des allerheiligsten
> leidens, sterbens und aufferstehns unsers Herren Jesu Christi, seines geliebten suns, vergibt
> er dir all dein sünd, unnd ich, als ein verordneter diener der Christenlichen kirchen,
> verkündige dir aus bevelch unsers Herren Jesu Christi solche vergebung aller deiner sünd in
> namen des vatters und des suns und des heyligen geists, Amen. Gee hin im frid, dir geschech,
> wie du glaubest. Oder also: Der allmechtig und barmhertzig Gott vergibt dir dein sünd, und
> ich, aus bevelch unnsers Herren Jesu Christi, an stat der heiligen kirchen, sag dich frey,
> ledig und los aller deiner sünd in namen des vatters und des suns unnd des heiligen geists,
> Amen, Gee hin unnd sündige nicht mer, sonder besser dich one underlas; das helff dir Gott.
> Oder also: In der einsatzung des predig ampts des heiligen Evangelions hat Jesus Christus zu
> seinen Aposteln gesagt: Wer euch höret, der höret mich, und welchen ir die sünde erlassen, den
> seind sie erlassen, unnd welchen ir sie behaltet, den sind sie behalten. Aus vermög diser wort
> und bevelch Christi verkündige ich euch, das alle ewere sünd durch Jesum Christum gebüsset
> sein und erlasse euch als ein ordenlicher diener des heiligen Evangelions aller ewer mißthat
> in dem namen des vatters, des suns und des heiligen geists, Amen. Auff das singe die kirch das
> vatter unser teutsch, dieweil es ein sonderlich hertzlich gebett und auch darzu ein offenlich
> beicht ist.

After the exhortation he shall say before them the public confession, and afterward the public
absolution, about after this manner: I, poor sinner, confess myself unto God my heavenly Father,
that I have, alas, grievously and manifoldly sinned, not only with outward, gross sins, but much
more with inward, inborn blindness, unbelief, doubting, faint-heartedness, impatience, pride,
evil lusts, secret envy and hatred, grudging, etc., as my Lord God knoweth of me, and I, alas,
cannot so perfectly know; therefore they repent me and are grievous unto me; I desire grace of
God. Absolution: The almighty God hath had mercy upon thee, and through the merit of the most
holy passion, death and resurrection of our Lord Jesus Christ, his beloved Son, he forgiveth
thee all thy sins; and I, as an appointed minister of the Christian Church, declare unto thee,
by the command of our Lord Jesus Christ, such forgiveness of all thy sins, in the name of the
Father and of the Son and of the Holy Ghost, Amen. Go in peace; be it unto thee as thou
believest. Or thus: The almighty and merciful God forgiveth thee thy sin, and I, by the command
of our Lord Jesus Christ, in the stead of the holy Church, pronounce thee free, quit and loosed
of all thy sins, in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Ghost, Amen. Go, and
sin no more, but amend thyself without ceasing; God help thee thereto. Or thus: In the
institution of the preaching office of the holy Gospel, Jesus Christ said to his apostles: He
that heareth you heareth me; and whose soever sins ye remit, they are remitted, and whose soever
ye retain, they are retained. By virtue of these words and command of Christ I declare unto you
that all your sins are atoned through Jesus Christ, and I remit you, as an ordained minister of
the holy Gospel, all your misdeeds, in the name of the Father, of the Son and of the Holy Ghost,
Amen. Thereupon let the church sing the Our Father in German, for it is a singularly hearty
prayer, and therewith also a public confession.

**Note.**
- **The second person singular.** The first two absolutions are in the singular ("dir", thee),
  as in private confession. Sehling's editor refers both to the absolution of private confession
  in Brandenburg-Nürnberg 1533 (Sehling 11, p. 187). Only the third is in the plural.
- **The confession** passes, almost word for word, into the Kurpfalz order of 1556 and from it
  into the Reformed Palatinate order of 1563 (below).
- **The Our Father** sung after the absolution is called "ein offenlich beicht" because of its
  fifth petition.

**Nürnberg after 1569: the *Offene Schuld* appended to the exhortation.** Veit Dietrich's
*Agendbüchlein*, in the editions from 1569, ends the exhortation with an invitation to confess
and an absolution that is both conditional and indicative. **Nürnberg, Veit Dietrich,
*Agendbüchlein*, edition of 1569, from Sehling's apparatus** (Sehling 11, p. 498):

<!-- doc 297 -->
> Und dieweil wir alle gesündiget haben und der genaden Gottes bedörfen, so demütiget eure
> herzen vor Gott dem Herren, bekennet eure sünde und gebrechen mit herzlicher lieb und begirde
> seiner götlichen gnaden und hilf, mit festem glauben und vertrauen auf sein genedig zusagen
> und vergebt von herzen ein jeder seinem nechsten, auf das euer himlischer Vater eure sünd und
> missetaten auch vergeb! So ir solches tut, entbind ich euch an stat der heiligen christlichen
> kirchen und aus befelch und zusagen unsers Herren Jesu Christi, da er sprach: „Weme ir die
> sünde vergebt, dem sein sie vergeben“ (Joh. 20, 23), von allen euren sünden im namen des
> Vaters und des Sons und des Heiligen Geistes. Amen.

And forasmuch as we have all sinned and have need of the grace of God, humble your hearts before
God the Lord, confess your sins and infirmities with hearty love and desire of his divine grace
and help, with firm faith and trust in his gracious promise, and forgive from the heart every
one his neighbour, that your heavenly Father may also forgive your sins and misdeeds! If ye do
this, I loose you, in the stead of the holy Christian Church and by the command and promise of
our Lord Jesus Christ, when he said, "Whose soever sins ye remit, they are remitted unto them"
(John 20:23), from all your sins, in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy
Ghost. Amen.

1524/25 on the model of the medieval preaching service). Osiander left it out when the
exhortation was taken into the church order of 1533; "auf den Wunsch der Gemeinde" (at the
congregation's wish) it was quickly taken back, and "Osiander führte jetzt gegen sie einen
heißen, aber vergeblichen Kampf" (Osiander now waged a hot but vain battle against it). The
wording disputed in 1533 came from Wenzel Linck.

**What the comparison shows.**
- **The order** of the Common Service preparation (address, *Adiutorium*, a second versicle,
  confession, prayer for forgiveness, declaration of grace) is Döber's order of 1525, by way of
  Mecklenburg.
- **The prayer for forgiveness** ("O most merciful God …") is the old *Misereatur*. In
  Mecklenburg 1552 it belonged to the *second minister* or custos kneeling beside the priest.
  That is the server's place in the Roman *Stufengebet*.
- **The declaration of grace** ("Almighty God … hath had mercy upon us … Grant this, O Lord,
  unto us all") is type 2 (§4.4): God's mercy declared in the third person, with no "I absolve",
  and closed with a wish. It is not an indicative absolution, and not the optative *Misereatur*
  either. Its final wish goes back to Döber's "Das verleihe uns Got allen".
- **The confession** passes from the priest's "Ich armer sündiger mensch" to "we poor sinners".
  In the sixteenth-century orders the priest says it alone while the people keep silence and
  confess in their hearts; the Common Service gives it to the congregation.
- **The second versicle.** The Common Service's second versicle (Ps 32:5) is not in Sehling's
  Mecklenburg texts. Its place is taken in Döber and Mecklenburg 1540 by *Confitemini Domino*.

**The indicative absolution in American Lutheran books.** The other common form in American
Lutheran books, "Upon this your confession, I, by virtue of my office, as a called and ordained
servant of the Word, announce the grace of God unto all of you, and in the stead and by the
command of my Lord Jesus Christ I forgive you all your sins …", has its sixteenth-century
counterpart in Naumburg 1537: "Auf solch euer bekentnus aus bevel des herrn Jesu Christi anstat
der heiligen christlichen kirchen vorgebe ich euch all eure sunde" (§3.5). Its confession is
also close to the one that goes with this absolution in those books: "O almighty God, merciful
Father, I, a poor, miserable sinner, confess unto Thee all my sins and iniquities … and I pray
Thee of Thy boundless mercy and for the sake of the holy, innocent, bitter sufferings and death
of Thy beloved Son, Jesus Christ, to be gracious and merciful to me". Compare Naumburg's "bit
dich durch deine grundlose barmherzigkeit, und durch das bittere leiden und sterben deines
lieben sohns JESU CHRISTI du wollest gnedig sein mir armen sundern". Württemberg's "als ein
verordneter diener der Christenlichen kirchen" (as an appointed minister of the Christian
Church) is the sixteenth-century form of "a called and ordained servant of the Word".

### 4.6 General confession against private confession

The Brandenburg-Nürnberg tradition was wary of the general confession. The Brandenburg order
forbids the people to replace private confession with a confession said together. **Brandenburg,
*Kirchenordnung*, 1540** (Sehling 3, p. 60):

<!-- doc 1746 -->
> Desgleichen der misbrauch, so an etlichen orten furgenomen, das auf einen haufen etliche
> personen zugleich ein gemeine beicht thun und offentliche absolution empfahen, und es denn
> dabei wenden lassen, sol nicht gestattet, sondern ein jeder in sonderheit notturftiglich
> gehört und absolvirt werden. Und auf das es nu ordenlich und recht zugehe und alle misbreuch
> soviel möglich verhütet werden, sol man dem volk ansagen, wer das heilig sacrament wolle
> empfahen, das er sich des abends zuvor oder wo es ferre des wegs oder anderer zufell halben
> nicht sein kond, des morgens vor der messzeit dem pfarrer […] anzeige

Likewise the abuse that hath been taken up in some places, that several persons together in a
crowd make a common confession and receive a public absolution, and let it rest there, shall not
be suffered; but every one shall be heard and absolved severally, as need requireth. And that it
may now go orderly and rightly, and all abuses be prevented as far as possible, the people shall
be told that whoso will receive the holy sacrament shall [announce himself] the evening before,
or, where that could not be by reason of the distance of the way or other chances, in the
morning before the time of Mass, to the pastor […].

Pfalz-Neuburg 1543 repeats the rule: the pastors shall "nicht gestatten, das ir vil miteinander
ein gemeine beicht tuen, sonder ein jeder sol für sich selbst sein beicht tun und absolution
begern" (not suffer many of them to make a common confession together, but every one shall make
his confession for himself and desire absolution; Sehling 13, p. 57).

**The point of the prohibition.** It is not aimed at a confession in the Mass. Brandenburg 1540
itself has the priest's *Confiteor* at the altar (§3.2). It is aimed at groups of communicants
confessing together on the Saturday and taking that for private confession. In the Nürnberg
tradition, the general absolution in the Mass was itself contested in 1533 (§4.4). Where both
exist, as in Mecklenburg, the public confession at the start of the Mass is a preparation for
all, and the private confession on the Saturday is the condition of admission to communion.

## 5. The exhortation to the communicants

### 5.1 Where it falls, and what it is for

**What it is.** Every Lutheran order with a communion rite has an exhortation (*Vermahnung*,
*vormaninge*, *exhortatio*) addressed to the communicants. Bugenhagen calls it "de rechte
prefatie, dat is eyne vohrrede" (the true Preface, that is, a foreword; Brunswick 1528, Sehling
6/1, p. 440). It does four things:
- it teaches what the Sacrament is;
- it calls the communicants to examine themselves (1 Cor 11:28);
- it rehearses or paraphrases the Words of Institution;
- it calls for thanksgiving and love of the neighbour.

It never replaces the Words of Institution, which are always sung or said after it. In Nürnberg,
where the exhortation itself rehearsed the Verba, Sehling notes that once the Verba were sung
aloud the exhortation "doubled" them.

**Placement.** The exhortation stands in four places:

| Place | Orders |
|---|---|
| **After the sermon, in the pulpit or at the altar, before the Our Father and Verba** | Luther, *Deutsche Messe* 1526 (pulpit or altar left free); Saxony 1539 and 1580; Mansfeld 1562; Grubenhagen 1581 |
| **At the altar after the sermon, before the Verba** | Brandenburg-Nürnberg 1533 (moved there "auf Luthers Anregung"); Württemberg 1536 and its family; Pfalz-Neuburg 1543; Hohenlohe; Waldeck 1556; Verden 1606; Palatinate 1563 (at the table) |
| **At the altar after the sermon, before the Preface** (as its "true" form) | Bugenhagen, Brunswick 1528, Hamburg 1529, Wolfenbüttel 1543, Hildesheim 1544; Kantz 1522 |
| **After the Preface and Sanctus, before the Our Father and Verba** | Mecklenburg 1552 ("so man zeit hat", if there is time) |
| **After the Verba and Our Father, before communion** | Nürnberg 1524, Döber 1525, *Form und Ordnung* 1525 (Osiander's original place) |

with 1524, to before the beginning of the sacramental part goes back to Luther's suggestion in
his *Deutsche Messe*).

### 5.2 Luther, *Deutsche Messe*, 1526: the paraphrase of the Our Father and the admonition

Luther joins a paraphrase of the Our Father to a short admonition on the testament, and leaves
it free whether it is read from the pulpit or at the altar. **Wittenberg, Luther, *Deudsche
messe und ordnung gottis diensts*, 1526** (Sehling 1, p. 15):

<!-- doc 3 -->
> Nach der predigt sol folgen eine offentliche paraphrasis des vater unsers und vermanung an die
> so zum sacrament gehen wollen, auf die oder besser weise, wie folget: Lieben freunde Christi,
> weil wir hie versamlet sind in dem namen des hern, sein heiliges testament zu empfahen, so
> vermane ich euch aufs erste, das ihr eur herze zu got erhebt, mit mir zu beten das vater
> unser, wie uns Christus unser herr geleret und erhorung trostlich zugesagt hat. Das gott unser
> vater im himel uns seine elende kinder auf erden barmherziglich ansehen wolte und gnade
> verleihen, das sein heiliger name unter uns und in aller welt geheiliget werde durch reine,
> rechtschaffne lere seines worts und durch brunstige liebe unsers lebens, wolte gnediglich
> abwenden alle falsche lere und boses leben, darin sein werder name gelestert und geschendet
> wird. Das auch sein reich zu kome und gemehret werde, alle sunder, verblendte und vom teufel
> in sein reich gefangen zur erkentnis des rechten glaubens an Jhesum Christ, seinen son,
> bringen und die zal der christen gross machen. Das wir auch mit seim geist gesterkt werden,
> seinen willen zu thun und zu leiden, beide im leben und sterben, im guten und bosen, allzeit
> unsern willen brechen, opfern und todten. Wolt uns auch unser teglich brod geben, fur geiz und
> sorge des bauchs behueten, sondern uns alles guts gnug zu ihm versehen lassen. Wolt auch uns
> unser schuld vergeben, wie wir denn unsern schuldigern vergeben, das unser herz ein sicher
> frolich gewissen fur ihm habe und fur keiner sunde uns nimmer furchten noch erschrecken. Wolt
> uns nicht ein furen in anfechtunge, sondern helfe uns durch seinen geist das fleisch zwingen,
> die welt mit ihrem wesen verachten und den teufel mit allen seinen tücken uberwinden. Und zu
> letzt uns wolt erlosen von allem ubel, beide leiblich und geistlich, zeitlich und ewiglich.
> Wilche das alles mit ernste begeren, sprechen von herzen: ‘Amen’, on allen zweifel glaubend,
> es sei ja und erhoret im himel, wie uns Christus zusagt: 'Was ihr bittet, gleubt, das ihrs
> haben werdet, so sols geschehen'. Amen. Zum andern vermane ich euch in Christo, das ihr mit
> rechtem glauben des testaments Christi warnehmet und allermeist die wort, darinnen uns
> Christus sein leib und blut zur vergebung schenkt, im herzen feste fasset, das ihr gedenkt und
> dankt der grundlosen liebe, die er uns bewisen hat, da er uns durch sein blut von gots zorn,
> sund, todt und helle erloset hat, und darauf eusserlich das brod und wein, das ist seinen leib
> und blut, zur sicherung und pfand zu euch nemet. Dem nach wollen wir in seinem namen und aus
> seinem befehl durch seine eigene wort das testament also handeln und brauchen. Ob man aber
> solche paraphrasin und vermanung wolle auf der canzel flux auf die predigt thun oder fur dem
> altar, las ich frei eim iglichen seine wilkore. Es sihet, als habens die alten bis her auf der
> cantzel gethan, daher noch blieben ist, das man auf der cantzel gemein gebet thut oder das
> vater unser fur spricht. Aber die vermanung zu einer offentlichen beicht worden ist.

After the sermon shall follow a public paraphrase of the Our Father and an admonition to them
that will go to the sacrament, after this or a better manner, as followeth: Dear friends of
Christ, forasmuch as we are here gathered together in the name of the Lord to receive his holy
testament, I admonish you first that ye lift up your heart to God, to pray with me the Our
Father, as Christ our Lord hath taught us and hath comfortably promised us a hearing. That God
our Father in heaven would mercifully look upon us his wretched children on earth, and grant
grace that his holy name may be hallowed among us and in all the world, through the pure and
upright teaching of his word and through the fervent love of our life; would graciously turn
away all false teaching and evil life, whereby his worthy name is blasphemed and put to shame.
That his kingdom also may come and be increased; that he would bring all sinners, the blinded,
and them that are taken captive by the devil in his kingdom, to the knowledge of the right faith
in Jesus Christ his Son, and make the number of Christians great. That we also may be
strengthened with his Spirit to do and to suffer his will, both in life and in death, in good
and in evil, always breaking, offering up and slaying our own will. That he would also give us
our daily bread, keep us from covetousness and care for the belly, but let us look to him for
enough of all good things. That he would also forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors,
that our heart may have a sure and joyful conscience before him, and never fear nor be afraid of
any sin. That he would not lead us into temptation, but help us by his Spirit to subdue the
flesh, despise the world with all its ways, and overcome the devil with all his wiles. And
lastly, that he would deliver us from all evil, both bodily and spiritual, temporal and eternal.
Whoso earnestly desire all this, let them say from the heart: "Amen", believing without all
doubt that it is yea and heard in heaven, as Christ promiseth us: "What ye ask, believe that ye
shall have it, and it shall be done." Amen. Secondly, I admonish you in Christ that ye give heed
with right faith to the testament of Christ, and above all hold fast in your heart the words
wherein Christ giveth us his body and blood for forgiveness; that ye remember and give thanks
for the bottomless love which he hath shown us, when he redeemed us by his blood from God's
wrath, sin, death and hell; and thereupon take outwardly unto you the bread and wine, that is,
his body and blood, for assurance and pledge. Accordingly we will, in his name and by his
command, through his own words, so handle and use the testament. But whether this paraphrase and
admonition be done in the pulpit straight after the sermon, or before the altar, I leave free to
every one's choice. It seemeth that the ancients have done it hitherto in the pulpit, whence it
hath still remained that the common prayer is made in the pulpit, or the Our Father said before
[the people]. But the admonition hath become a public confession.

**Note.**
- **The two parts.** The paraphrase is a prayer, said by the minister, to which the people
  answer "Amen". The admonition proper is the second paragraph.
- **The pulpit.** Luther's last sentences give the history: the medieval pulpit office (*Prone*)
  had a bidding prayer, the Our Father, and an admonition that "zu einer offentlichen beicht
  worden ist" (has become a public confession), the *Offene Schuld* of §3.5.
- **The followers.** Albertine Saxony 1580 heads the text "Vermanung zum gebet vor der
  communion" and has it read "für dem altar" (Sehling 1, p. 368). Mansfeld 1562 makes it
  optional: "das sie brauchen möge, wer gerne wil" (that whoever will may use it), and allows it
  to be dropped in towns when the singing and organ make the service too long
  (Sehling 2, p. 227).

### 5.3 Osiander and Brandenburg-Nürnberg, 1524–1533

**Osiander's exhortation.** It was written for the Nürnberg parish churches in 1524 and read
after the Verba and the Our Father. Döber's text of 1525 is printed with a translation in
[`CANON_IN_GERMAN_MASSES.md`](CANON_IN_GERMAN_MASSES.md) §8.7.

**The 1533 text.** The Brandenburg-Nürnberg order of 1533 revised it and moved it before the
Verba. This revised text is the most widely copied exhortation in the corpus.
**Brandenburg-Nürnberg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1533** (Sehling 11, pp. 195–196):

<!-- doc 270 -->
> Ordnung des Herren abentmals. Wann der priester das abentmal des Herren (so er anderst
> comunicanten hat) halten will, soll er von stund an nachfolgende ermanung zum volk tun und
> dann die wort des nachtmals, wo die meß gesungen würdet, ungeferlich auf nachfolgende weise
> und nach den hernach gesetzten noten singen, oder, so er die meß liset, mit lauten
> verstendigen worten lesen, damit die umbsteenden dieselben hören mögen. Volget die ermanung.
> Ir allerliebsten in Got, dieweil wir jetzo das heilig abentmal unsern Herren Jesu Christi
> wöllen bedenken und halten, darin er uns sein flaisch zu einer göttlichen […] speis und sein
> blut zu einem trank, den glauben darmit zu sterken, gegeben hat, sollen wir pillich mit großen

> sünde bekennen, Gottes zorn und den tod förchten und nach der gerechtigkeit hungerig und
> dürstig sind. So wir aber uns selbs prüfen und ein jeder in sein aigen gewissen geen, wie uns

> umb unser sünden willen mensch worden, auf das er das gesetz und allen willen Gottes für uns

> für uns und zu unser erledigung auf sich neme und erlitte. Und das wir das je festiglich
> glaubten und durch den glauben frölich in seinem willen möchten leben, nam er nach dem

> zeugnus gib ich euch mein leib zur speis. Desgleichen nam er auch den kelch und sprach: Nemet
> hin und trinket aus disem alle! Das ist der kelch des neuen testaments in meinem blut, das für
> euch und für vil vergossen wird zu vergebung der sünde. So oft ir das tut solt, ir mein darbei
> gedenken. Das ist: dieweil ich mich euer angenummen und euer sünde auf mich geladen hab, will
> ich mich selbs für die sünde in tod opfern, mein blut vergießen, […] gnad und vergebung der
> sünde erwerben und also ein neu testament aufrichten, darinnen die sünde vergeben und ewig
> nicht mer soll gedacht werden. Des zu einem gewisen anzaigen und zeugnus gib ich euch mein
> blut zu trinken. Wer nun also von disem brot isset und von disem kelch trinket, auch disen
> worten, die er von Christo höret, und disen zaichen, die er von Christo empfahet, festiglich
> glaubt, der bleibt in dem Herrn Christo und Christus in ime und wird ewiglich leben. Darbei
> sollen wir nun sein gedenken und seinen tod verkündigen, nemlich das er für unser sünde sei
> gestorben und zu unser rechtfertigung wider auferstanden, und ime darumb danksagen, ein jeder
> sein kreuz auf sich nemen und ime nachfolgen und nach seinem gepot einander lieben, wie er uns

Order of the Lord's Supper. When the priest will hold the Supper of the Lord (if he have
communicants at all), he shall straightway make the following exhortation to the people, and
then sing the words of the Supper, where the Mass is sung, about after the following manner and
according to the notes set down hereafter; or, if he readeth the Mass, read them with loud and
intelligible words, that they that stand about may hear them. The exhortation followeth. Dearly
beloved in God, forasmuch as we will now consider and keep the holy Supper of our Lord Jesus
Christ, wherein he hath given us his flesh for a divine meat and his blood for a drink,
therewith to strengthen faith, we ought rightly with great diligence every one to examine
himself, as the holy Paul exhorteth us; for this holy sacrament is given for a special comfort
and strength to poor troubled consciences, which confess their sins, fear God's wrath and death,
and are hungry and thirsty after righteousness. But when we examine ourselves, and every one
goeth into his own conscience, as the holy Paul teacheth us, we shall surely find nothing else
than all manner of horrible sins, and death, which we have deserved with sin, and yet can in no
wise help ourselves out thereof. Therefore hath our dear Lord Jesus Christ had mercy upon us,
and for our sins' sake is become man, that he might fulfil the law and all the will of God for
us and for our good, and take upon himself and suffer death and all that we had deserved with
our sins, for us and for our deliverance. And that we might steadfastly believe this, and
through faith live joyfully in his will, he took after the supper the bread, gave thanks, brake
it, and said: Take and eat! This is my body, which is given for you. That is: that I am become
man, and all that I do and suffer, is all your own, done for you and for your good. For a sure
token and witness thereof I give you my body for meat. Likewise he took also the cup and said:
Take and drink ye all of this! This is the cup of the new testament in my blood, which is shed
for you and for many for the forgiveness of sins. As oft as ye do this, ye shall remember me
thereby. That is: forasmuch as I have taken your cause upon me and laden your sins upon myself,
I will offer myself unto death for sin, shed my blood, obtain grace and forgiveness of sins, and
so set up a new testament, wherein sins are forgiven and shall be remembered no more for ever.
For a sure token and witness thereof I give you my blood to drink. Whosoever now eateth thus of
this bread and drinketh of this cup, and firmly believeth these words which he heareth from
Christ, and these signs which he receiveth from Christ, he dwelleth in the Lord Christ and
Christ in him, and shall live for ever. Hereby shall we now remember him and show forth his
death, namely that he died for our sins and rose again for our justification, and give him
thanks therefor, every one take up his cross and follow him, and according to his commandment
love one another, as he hath loved us: for we are all one bread and one body, forasmuch as we
are all partakers of one bread and drink of one cup.

**Note.**
- **What 1533 changes in Osiander's text.** It adds "den glauben darmit zu sterken" (therewith
  to strengthen faith) and the "trost und sterk … den armen betrübten gewissen" (comfort and
  strength for poor troubled consciences). The Nürnberg closing wish "Das verleihe uns Got
  allen, das wirs wirdiglich empfahen" (God grant us all this, that we may receive it worthily)
  is dropped.
- **No confession or absolution.** The 1533 text has none. The Nürnberg editions of 1569 and
  later append the *Offene Schuld* with an absolution (§4.4).

### 5.4 Württemberg, 1536: Brandenburg-Nürnberg expanded

Württemberg 1536 takes the Brandenburg-Nürnberg text and expands it. It adds Augustine's
sacraments as "sichtbarliche wort" (visible words), a stronger statement of the presence ("mein
waren, wesenlichen leib", my true, essential body), and the Didache's figure of the grains and
grapes. **Württemberg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1536** (Sehling 16, pp. 107–108):

<!-- doc 651 -->
> Wann nun der pfarrherr also zu dem altar kompt, wend er sich zu dem volck und thu ein kurze
> ermanung an diejhenigen, so dann zu dem hochwirdigen Sacrament gehn wöllen, aus dem eylfften
> Capitel der ersten an die Corinthier oder sonst ungevarlich auff nachvolgende weis und form:
> Ir allerliebsten in Christo Jesu, dieweil wir yetzund das gnadreich abentmal unsers liebsten
> heylands begeen und halten werden, Darinnen er uns sein warhafftigen leib zu einer speis unnd
> sein eigen blut zu einem dranck, den glauben damit zu stercken, gegeben hat, Sollen wir
> billich mit grossem fleis und inprünstiger andacht uns selbs, wie Sant […] Paulus vermanet,
> brüffen, Dann dieweil die armen, blöden gewissen eben sowol durch die gnadenreichen sacrament,
> die da sichtbarliche wort von Sant Augustin genant werden, als durch das wort, so zu dem er
> eingehet, vermittelst dem heiligen geyst getröst und gesterckt mögen werden, so ist das
> gnadenreich nachtmal zu einem sondern trost und stercke den betrübten geistern und zerschlagen
> hertzen geben […] Dann zu gleicherweis, wie aus vil börlin zusamen gekeltert ein wein unnd ein
> tranck fleußt unnd sich in einander menget, und aus vil kernlin ein meel gemalet, ein brot und
> kuch gebachen wirdt, also sollen wir alle, so durch den glauben Christo in geleibt sein, durch
> brüderliche lieb umb Christus, unsers liebsten heilands willen, der unns zuvor so hoch geliebt
> hat, alle ein leibe, tranck, kuchen und brot werden unnd solchs gegeneinander nit allein mit
> lären worten, so nit von hertzen gehn, sonder durch wercken und warheit, wie Johannis leeret,
> on allen trug, trewlich gegeneinander beweisen, Amen.

When now the pastor so cometh to the altar, let him turn to the people and make a short
exhortation to them that will go to the most worthy Sacrament, out of the eleventh chapter of
the first [epistle] to the Corinthians, or else about after the following manner and form:
Dearly beloved in Christ Jesus, forasmuch as we will now celebrate and keep the gracious Supper
of our dearest Saviour, wherein he hath given us his true body for a meat and his own blood for
a drink, therewith to strengthen faith, we ought rightly with great diligence and fervent
devotion to examine ourselves, as Saint Paul exhorteth; for forasmuch as poor feeble consciences
may be comforted and strengthened, by means of the Holy Ghost, as well through the gracious
sacraments, which are called by Saint Augustine visible words, as through the word, so far as it
entereth in, therefore the gracious Supper is given for a special comfort and strength to
troubled spirits and broken hearts […] For in like manner as out of many grapes pressed together
one wine and one drink floweth and is mingled together, and out of many grains one meal is
ground, and one bread and cake is baked, so shall we all, that are incorporated into Christ by
faith, through brotherly love, for the sake of Christ our dearest Saviour, who first loved us so
highly, become all one body, drink, cake and bread, and show the same faithfully one toward
another, not only with empty words that come not from the heart, but in deeds and in truth, as
John teacheth, without all guile, Amen.

**Note.** After this exhortation come the open confession, one of three absolutions, and the Our

1553, into the Palatinate, Augsburg, Strasbourg and the Rhenish counties (§5.1).

Exhortation or admonition before the altar concerning the sacrament, to the communicants. My
dearly beloved, it is continually set before us by the preaching of the Gospel of Christ that we
of ourselves are ignorant, poor and lost sinners. And forasmuch as we of ourselves are no more
than flesh and blood, therefore we can by no understanding or power of ours loose ourselves out
of the strict judgment of God and from the power of the devil, wherein we are fallen through the
transgression of the commandments and of the will of God. So God hath known our impotence better
than we, and hath given for us, as a gracious Father, his only-begotten Son Jesus Christ, that
we might be enlightened through his Gospel, and redeemed from our sins through his death, and
through him be children of God, eternally saved, if we believed it. This he letteth be preached
to us continually: whoso believeth it, he hath surely eternal life; upon such faith and unto
such salvation are we also baptized; therein shall we abide continually; so abide we in Christ
and Christ in us. So eat we continually without ceasing, spiritually with faith, the body of
Christ and drink his blood; that is, we are incorporated into Christ, that we become one with
him, in that we believe that he hath given his body into death for us and shed his blood for us
on the cross; thereupon we rely for salvation against all false teaching, all sin, temptation
and need. Out of which benefit of Christ we also learn what love and patience we shall exercise
toward our neighbour, yea, toward our enemy. What would we more? Yet that we might not forget
nor grow slack (as, alas, we do) in such faith in the incarnation and death of Christ, he hath
also commanded us a special remembrance or showing forth of his death, as oft as we will, that
we shall also in the outward sacrament, hidden from reason, known to faith alone out of the word
of Christ, eat and drink his body and blood; that we may in no wise doubt that his death and
shedding of blood on the cross is our sure salvation; thereof shall we sing, read, preach, hear,
as we do in the Mass, and afterward also speak thereof and show it forth among one another, for
our comfort and for the salvation of many, according to the command of Christ: This do in
remembrance of me. Whoso now will worthily eat and drink this sacrament, he shall do two things:
he shall believe what Christ saith, and do what he commandeth. He saith: This is my body, which
is given for you. This is my blood, which is shed for you for the forgiveness of sins. That
shall ye believe. But he commandeth: Take and eat. Drink ye all thereout, and remember me. That
shall ye do according to his gracious word and command. Amen.

**Note.**
- **"Twe dink"** (two things), believing and doing, is the whole doctrine of worthy reception.
- **Two kinds of eating.** "So ete wy stedes … geistlick mitdeme loven" (so eat we continually …
  spiritually with faith) is the spiritual eating of John 6. The Sacrament is given in addition,
  "imme uthwendigen sacramente" (in the outward sacrament), so that we "jo nicht twivelen
  scholen" (may in no wise doubt).
- **The followers.** Wolfenbüttel 1543 and Hildesheim 1544 repeat it word for word, in their own
  Low German spelling. Lüneburg 1564 and Verden 1606 offer it as a second form beside
  Brandenburg-Nürnberg.

### 5.6 Mecklenburg, 1552: the exhortation with an absolution

The Mecklenburg order makes the exhortation a short summary of repentance and faith. It ends
with a conditional, indicative absolution and a prayer said in the first person. **Mecklenburg,
*Kirchenordnung*, 1552** (Sehling 5, pp. 202–203):

<!-- doc 1922 -->
> Vermanung: Absolutio, danksagung und gebet vor der communio. Lieben freunde, ir als
> christliche menschen, die ir zuvor bericht habt von gott, von gottes ernstlichen willen und
> gesetz, von der menschlichen natur erschaffung, und ernach von unser sünde, und von der
> erlösung durch den heiland Jesum Christum, und von allen artickeln des glaubens, und
> warhaftiglich die selbigen gleubet, erscheinet allhie und komet zu diesem hohen trost, den uns
> der herr Christus in niessung seines leibs und bluts gibet, nu wisset ir den eid des
> allmechtigen ewigen gottes, darin er spricht, so war ich lebe, ich will nicht, das der sünder
> sterbe, sondern das er bekeret werde und das leben habe. Diesen eid hat gott mit seines
> eingebornen sons Jesu Christi blut, tod und ufferstehung bekreftiget, und ist beides darin
> gefasset, die bekerung und vergebung der sünden. […] Derhalben allen, die also hie erscheinen,
> die sich zu gott bekeren, und fur gottes zorn wider ire sünd erschrecken, und gleuben, das
> inen um des herrn Christi willen ire sünd vergeben werden, und haben fursatz von sünden wider
> gewissen abzulassen, denen verkünde ich vergebung der sünden, laut der wort Christi, Welchen
> ir die sünde vergebet , denen sind sie vergeben, derhalben aus befehl des herrn Christi sprich
> ich euch diese absolutio, das euch eure sünd vergeben sind, um des herrn Christi willen, und
> diese stimme des evangelii solt ir annemen, und am herrn Christo warhaftigen trost haben, und
> forthin im glauben und gutem gewissen gott gehorsam sein. Und zum zeugnis, das euch selb diese
> gnad geschenkt und adplicirt wird, solt ir den leib und das blut des herrn Jesu Christi
> empfahen, und wissen, das der herr Christus am creuz ein opfer fur euch gewesen ist, und hat
> seinen leib fur euch geben und sein blut fur euch vergossen, und das der herr Christus euch zu
> seinen gliedmassen machet und wil in euch kreftig sein. Hie solt ir auch dem allmechtigen
> gott, vater unsers herrn Jesu Christi, und dem herrn Jesu Christo fur solche grosse gnaden
> danken und trost und freude am herrn Christo haben, denn er wil bei euch sein, euch regiern
> und bewaren, ir solt auch hiebei euer herzlich gebet thun fur gemeine kirchen und herrschaft
> fur euch und fur eure kindlin. Allmechtiger warhaftiger gott, ewiger und einiger vater unsers
> heilands Jesu Christi, samt deinem einigen son Jesu Christo und heiligen geist, erschaffer
> aller creaturen, der du weise, warhaftig, gütig, gerecht, keusch und richter bist und zürnest
> wider die sünd, ich bekenne, das ich leider viel sünde an mir habe, und habe dazu viel sünde
> wissentlich und unwissentlich gethan, und ist mir herzlich leid, das ich dich warhaftigen gott
> erzürnet hab, und bitte dich, du wollest mir durch deine grosse barmherzigkeit um deines
> allerliebsten sons Jesu Christi willen alle meine sünde vergeben

Exhortation: absolution, thanksgiving and prayer before the communion. Dear friends, ye that as
Christian men have been instructed beforehand concerning God, concerning God's earnest will and
law, concerning the creation of man's nature, and thereafter concerning our sin, and concerning
the redemption through the Saviour Jesus Christ, and concerning all the articles of the faith,
and truly believe the same, appear here and come to this high comfort which the Lord Christ
giveth us in the eating of his body and blood. Now ye know the oath of the almighty, eternal
God, wherein he saith: As I live, I will not the death of the sinner, but that he be converted
and have life. This oath God hath confirmed with the blood, death and resurrection of his
only-begotten Son Jesus Christ, and both are comprehended therein, conversion and forgiveness of
sins. […] Therefore unto all that so appear here, that turn to God, and are terrified before
God's wrath against their sin, and believe that their sins are forgiven them for the Lord
Christ's sake, and have the purpose to leave off sins against conscience, unto them I declare
forgiveness of sins, according to the words of Christ, Whose soever sins ye remit, they are
remitted unto them; therefore by the command of the Lord Christ I speak unto you this
absolution, that your sins are forgiven you for the Lord Christ's sake; and this voice of the
Gospel ye shall receive, and have true comfort in the Lord Christ, and henceforth be obedient to
God in faith and good conscience. And for a witness that this grace is given and applied to you
yourselves, ye shall receive the body and the blood of the Lord Jesus Christ, and know that the
Lord Christ hath been a sacrifice for you on the cross, and hath given his body for you and shed
his blood for you, and that the Lord Christ maketh you his members and will be mighty in you.
Here ye shall also give thanks to the almighty God, the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, and to
the Lord Jesus Christ, for such great graces, and have comfort and joy in the Lord Christ, for
he will be with you, govern and preserve you; ye shall also here make your hearty prayer for the
common Church and government, for yourselves and for your little children. Almighty, true God,
eternal and only Father of our Saviour Jesus Christ, together with thine only Son Jesus Christ
and the Holy Ghost, Creator of all creatures, who art wise, true, good, just, chaste and a
judge, and art wroth against sin: I confess that I, alas, have much sin in me, and have moreover
done much sin, knowingly and unknowingly, and it is heartily grievous unto me that I have
angered thee, the true God; and I pray thee that thou wouldest forgive me all my sins through
thy great mercy, for the sake of thy dearest Son Jesus Christ

**Note.**
- **The absolution** is indicative ("sprich ich euch diese absolutio", I speak unto you this
  absolution) but given only to a defined class ("allen, die …", unto all that …). It is type 3
  and type 4 at once (§4.4).
- **The prayer that follows** addresses God by his attributes ("weise, warhaftig, gütig,
  gerecht, keusch und richter", wise, true, good, just, chaste and a judge). It is in the first
  person, a prayer for each communicant to make his own, not a public confession.
- **The followers.** Pfalz-Zweibrücken 1557 (Sehling 18, p. 189), Nassau-Weilburg 1576
  (Sehling 10, p. 264) and Sayn 1590 print it under the same heading.

### 5.7 The Palatinate, 1563: the Reformed form with examination and fencing

The Palatinate exhortation is read "bey dem tisch" (at the table) after the sermon and the
Sunday prayer. It begins with 1 Cor 11:23–29 and goes on to self-examination, the "fencing of
the table" and the comfort of weak consciences. **Palatinate, *Kirchenordnung*, 1563**
(Sehling 14, pp. 383–384):

<!-- doc 504 -->
> Und gleich nach geschehener predig und sontagsgebet, wie daniden vermeldet wirdt, ehe dann man
> singt, soll der diener des worts diese nachvolgende vermanung bey dem tisch, da man das
> nachtmal halten wil, verstendtlich, außtrücklich und ernstlich fürlesen. Form, das heilige
> abendmal zu halten. Ir geliebten in dem herrn Jesu Christo, höret an die wort der einsatzung
> deß heiligen abendmals unsers herrn Jesu Christi, welche uns beschreibet der heilig apostel
> Paulus in der ersten epistel an die Corint. am XI. capitel […] Die nun also gesinnet sein, die
> wil Gott gewißlich zu gnaden annemen und für würdige tischgenossen seins sons Jesu Christi
> erkennen. Dargegen aber, die dieses zeugnuß in irem hertzen nicht empfinden, die essen und
> trincken inen selbst das gericht. […] Diese alle, so lang sie in solchen lastern beharren,
> sollen gedencken und sich diser speiß, welche Christus allein seinen gläubigen verordnet hat,
> enthalten, auf daß nit ir gericht und verdamnuß desto schwerer werde. Diß aber wirdt uns nicht
> fürgehalten, lieben christen, die zerschlagen hertzen der gläubigen kleinmütig zu machen, als
> ob niemands zum abendmal des herrn gehen möchte, dann die one alle sünde weren. Denn wir
> kommen nicht zu diesem abendmal, damit zu bezeugen, daß wir volkommen und gerecht seind in uns
> selbst, sonder dargegen, weil wir unser leben ausserhalb uns in Jesu Christo suchen, bekennen
> wir, daß wir mitten in dem todt ligen.

And straightway after the sermon and the Sunday prayer has been done, as is set down below,
before there is singing, the minister of the word shall read this following exhortation at the
table where the Supper is to be held, intelligibly, distinctly and earnestly. Form for holding
the holy Supper. Ye beloved in the Lord Jesus Christ, hear the words of the institution of the
holy Supper of our Lord Jesus Christ, which the holy apostle Paul describeth to us in the first
epistle to the Corinthians, in the eleventh chapter […] They now that are so minded, them God
will surely receive into grace and acknowledge for worthy table-companions of his Son Jesus
Christ. But on the other hand, they that feel not this witness in their heart eat and drink
judgment to themselves. […] All these, so long as they persevere in such vices, shall consider
and abstain from this meat, which Christ hath ordained for his faithful alone, lest their
judgment and damnation be the heavier. But this is not set before us, dear Christians, to make
the broken hearts of the faithful faint-hearted, as though none might go to the Supper of the
Lord but they that were without all sin. For we come not to this Supper to bear witness thereby
that we are perfect and righteous in ourselves, but on the contrary, because we seek our life
outside ourselves in Jesus Christ, we confess that we lie in the midst of death.

**Note.**
- **The list of the excluded** (omitted above) runs from idolaters, those who invoke the saints
  and worship images, and those who bless cattle, through blasphemers, rebels, perjurers, the
  disobedient, killers and the quarrelsome, to adulterers, drunkards, thieves, usurers and
  gamblers.
- **The editor's source notes** give Geneva 1563 almost word for word for the opening, and
  London 1565 for the threefold examination.
- **The examination.** On communion days the preparatory service (§4.4) asks the three
  questions, answered "Ja".

## 6. Before and after the service

### 6.1 Vesting prayers

**No vesting prayers survive.** None of the evangelical orders in Sehling prints or appoints a
vesting prayer, or any of the other prayers in the Lutheran Missal Project's *Vesting Prayers*:
- the psalms of preparation (84, 85, 86, 116, 130) with the antiphon *Ne reminiscaris*;
- the Kyrie, Our Father and *preces*;
- the collects;
- the prayers before vesting, at the washing of the hands, and at the amice, alb, cincture,
  maniple, stole, tunicle, chasuble and mitre.

The searches covered the Latin incipits (*Ne reminiscaris*, *Quam dilecta*, *Benedixisti*,
*Inclina, Domine*, *Credidi*, *De profundis* in a preparatory setting), *praeparatio ad missam*,
*amictus*, *indue me*, and the German and Low German words for vesting. The psalms occur only in
the Office and the Introits.

The only mention of the medieval prayers is an editor's note. Sehling's editor, on the Amberg
hospital order of 1544, distinguishes the three preparations of the priest before Mass.
**Amberg, *Ordnung der kirchen* … *im spital*, 1544, editor's note** (Sehling 13, p. 282):

<!-- doc 410 -->
> Was hier in diesem beachtenswerten Versuch als Rüsthandlung für die Gemeinde anstatt des
> Stufengebetes (mit dem Confiteor) als einer Rüsthandlung des zelebrierenden Geistlichen vor
> den Introitus gesetzt ist, ist wahrscheinlich eine Form des Akzesses. Dieser ist die erste der
> drei vorgeschriebenen Rüsthandlungen des Geistlichen. Diese war nicht - wie die auf ihn
> folgende Praeparatio ad missam (u.a. mit den Gebeten bei Anlegung der einzelnen Bestandteile
> der Meßkleidung) und die dann schon in der Messe selbst in Gestalt des Stufengebetes - im
> Meßbuch ihrer Form nach genau vorgeschrieben, konnte daher örtlich und persönlich durchaus
> verschieden sein und auch die Form einer Andacht, mit der Priester sich gemeinsam auf die
> Feier der Messe vorbereiteten, annehmen

What is here set before the Introit, in this noteworthy attempt, as a preparation for the
congregation instead of the prayers at the foot of the altar (with the Confiteor), which are a
preparation of the celebrating clergyman, is probably a form of the *accessus*. This is the
first of the three prescribed preparations of the clergyman. It was not, like the *Praeparatio
ad missam* that followed it (with, among other things, the prayers at the putting on of the
several parts of the Mass vesture), and that which then came within the Mass itself in the form
of the prayers at the foot of the altar, exactly prescribed in its form in the Missal, and could
therefore differ quite widely by place and person, and could also take the form of a devotion
with which priests prepared themselves together for the celebration of Mass.

**What the orders do say about vesting.** The orders mention vesting only as a rubric, and only
to say that it signifies nothing. The vestments themselves are indifferent. **Brandenburg,
*Kirchenordnung*, 1540** (Sehling 3, p. 65):

<!-- doc 1746 -->
> Die andern zusetz eusserlicher leiblicher ding, als messgewand, altardeck, silbern und gülden
> gefess, lichter etc. sein aller ding frei, geben und nemen dem glauben und gewissen nichts.
> Darum dieweil sie vorhin verhanden und gezeuget sein, sol man sie behalten und brauchen,
> sonderlich die kleider darum, das die diener der kirchen nicht alweg in iren eigen kleidern
> also gestalt sein, das sie darinnen tapfer und ehrlich der gemein mochten dienen.

The other additions of outward bodily things, as Mass vestment, altar cloth, silver and golden
vessels, lights, etc., are altogether free; they give and take nothing to faith and conscience.
Therefore, since they were there before and have been provided, they shall be kept and used,
especially the vestments, because the ministers of the church are not always so furnished in
their own clothes that they might serve the congregation therein worthily and honourably.

The rubrics on vesting say no more than this:
- **The moment of vesting.** At Hadeln the priest vests during the *Te Deum* at the end of
  Matins, "nicht alse nödig, sundern als ein düchtig erbarlich zirath, und nicht darinne de
  saligkeit soken" (not as needful, but as a seemly and honest ornament, and not seeking
  salvation therein; §3.4). In Mecklenburg 1540 the priest turns to the people "wenn he sick
  hefft angekledet" (when he hath vested himself; §3.4). In both, vesting is done in silence, in
  the church or sacristy, with no prayer.
- **The *Stufengebet***, the third of the medieval preparations, is the only one that survives
  (§3.2–3.4). The *accessus* survives, recast, only as the congregation's *Veni Sancte Spiritus*
  (§3.7).

### 6.2 The psalms and the *Ne reminiscaris*

None of the five preparatory psalms of the Missal's *praeparatio* is appointed as a private
preparation of the minister, and the antiphon *Ne reminiscaris* (Tobit 3:3) does not occur.
- **The nearest thing** is Bugenhagen's *Domine, ne memineris iniquitatum nostrarum antiquarum*
  (Ps 79:8), said with the sexton at the altar step (§3.3). It is the same plea for the
  forgetting of old sins, but taken from the Lenten tract.
- **Penitential psalms.** Pfalz-Neuburg 1543 allows "ein feinen bußpsalm" (a goodly penitential
  psalm) in place of the *Confiteor* (§3.2). Ps 130 *De profundis* appears as a hymn, Luther's
  *Aus tiefer Not*, sung as the Introit in Lippe (§3.4) and often elsewhere.
- **The psalms themselves** occur in the corpus only as Introits, Office psalms, or proof texts.
  Ps 84 *Quam dilecta* is the dedication psalm at Hof 1592 (Sehling 11, p. 422), and Lüneburg
  1564 cites it in defence of public worship (Sehling 6/1, p. 541).

### 6.3 After the Mass

**The priest's private thanksgiving.** The medieval *gratiarum actio post missam* (the canticle
*Benedicite*, Ps 150 *Laudate Dominum in sanctis eius*, and collects, said by the priest while
unvesting) survives in only one form. In Bugenhagen's Hildesheim order of 1544 and the
Schleswig-Holstein order of 1542, the priest unvests during the closing hymn, kneels again
before the altar, and "danket Godde hemelick vor sick sülvest" (thanketh God secretly for
himself; Sehling 7/2.1, p. 855; Sehling 23, p. 91). No text is given. Both are quoted with
translations in [`CANON_IN_GERMAN_MASSES.md`](CANON_IN_GERMAN_MASSES.md) §§15.2–15.3.

**The Benedicite and Ps 150.** Neither is appointed after Mass in any evangelical order. Both
occur only as the canticle and psalm of **Sunday Lauds**, sung in the choir by the schoolboys or
the canons. Pomerania prescribes the weekday canticles of Lauds after the *Te Deum*.
**Pomerania, *Pia ordinatio caeremoniarum*, 1535** (Sehling 4, p. 349):

<!-- doc 1857 -->
> Post tertium responsorium, quod cum gloria patri etc. canitur, sequatur te deum etc. Mox
> cantor incipiat aliquam antiphonam, secundum cuius tonum canatur tantum unus ex psalmis
> prophetalibus, quae cantica vocantur. — Dominica die: Benedicite omnia etc. -— Feria secunda:
> Confitebor tibi, domine, quoniam iratus etc. — Feria tertia: Ego dixi etc. —- Feria quarta:
> Exultavit etc. — Feria quinta: Cantemus etc. — Feria sexta: Domine, audivi etc. — Sabbato:
> Audite coeli etc.

After the third responsory, which is sung with the Gloria Patri, etc., let the Te Deum follow,
etc. Next let the cantor begin some antiphon, according to whose tone there shall be sung only
one of the prophetic psalms, which are called canticles: on the Lord's Day, *Benedicite omnia*,
etc.; on Monday, *Confitebor tibi, Domine, quoniam iratus*, etc.; on Tuesday, *Ego dixi*, etc.;
on Wednesday, *Exultavit*, etc.; on Thursday, *Cantemus*, etc.; on Friday, *Domine, audivi*,
etc.; on Saturday, *Audite coeli*, etc.

The Schleswig-Holstein order of 1542 has the same scheme in Low German, "des Sondages:
Benedicite Omnia etc." (on Sundays: *Benedicite omnia*; Sehling 23, p. 141). At the Ansbach
collegiate churches Sunday Lauds keeps both the *Benedicite* and the *Laudate* psalms.
**Brandenburg-Ansbach, *Ordnung singens und lesens bei den Stiften*, 1533**
(Sehling 11, p. 313):

<!-- doc 276 -->
> Am sontag singen sie das Te Deum laudamus. Darnach die psalmen Dominus regnavit, Jubilate Deo
> omnis terra, Deus Deus meus, Benedicite omnia opera, Laudate Dominum de coelis mit der
> antiphona Indutus est, Benedictus Dominus etc., wie man sunst gewonlich am sontag helt.

On Sunday they sing the Te Deum laudamus; thereafter the psalms *Dominus regnavit*, *Jubilate
Deo omnis terra*, *Deus, Deus meus*, *Benedicite omnia opera*, *Laudate Dominum de coelis*, with
the antiphon *Indutus est*; the *Benedictus Dominus*, etc., as is otherwise customarily held on
Sunday.

**Who sings them.** At Ansbach, "sie": the canons and vicars of the collegiate foundations. In
Pomerania and Schleswig-Holstein, the cantor and the schoolboys in the choir. The people are not
present at Lauds. The *Laudate* psalms (Pss 148–150) are sung under the one heading "Laudate
Dominum de coelis".

**What the orders do appoint after Mass.** In place of the priest's private thanksgiving, the
evangelical Mass ends with a public thanksgiving by all:
- a postcommunion collect, usually Luther's "Wir danken dir, allmächtiger Herr Gott";
- the Aaronic blessing;
- a hymn or psalm sung by the schoolboys and people while the communicants return, or at the
  very end. At Hildesheim it is "einen düdeschen, korten psalm" (a short German psalm) begun by
  the schoolmaster.

In Kantz 1522 and in Nürnberg the *Nunc dimittis* is sung after communion. The collects and
thanksgiving prayers are treated in [`COLLECTS_GUIDE.md`](COLLECTS_GUIDE.md), and the hymns in
[`HYMN_PRACTICE_GUIDE.md`](HYMN_PRACTICE_GUIDE.md).

### 6.4 Prayers before and after the sermon

**The pattern.** Every Lutheran order surrounds the sermon with prayer. The preacher calls the
people to prayer before he begins, and they pray, usually the Our Father in silence or aloud, or
sing a hymn to the Holy Ghost. At the end of the sermon he calls them again to thanksgiving and
to intercession for the Church, the government and all need, and leads the general prayer. The
Lüneburg order puts it most fully. **Lüneburg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1564**
(Sehling 6/1, pp. 544–545):

<!-- doc 2003 -->
> Aber vor der predigt hat der priester das volk zum christlichen gebet zu vermanen, das der
> liebe Gott ihnen seinen heiligen Geist geben wolle, sein heiliges wort nützbarlich zu handeln
> […] und in das herze zu fassen, das sie ihr sündlich leben bessern, trost und sterkung ihres
> glaubens erlangen und hinfurt christlich und seliglich leben mögen. Darauf sol man ein Vater
> unser sprechen oder singen: Nu bitten wir den heiligen Geist. Umb die Weihnachten: Ein
> kindelein so löbelich etc. Umb die Ostern: Christ ist erstanden etc. Zum ende der predigt sol
> das volk abermal zum gebet und danksagung gegen Gott vermanet werden und samptlich vor
> erhaltung der kirchen Gottes und rechter lere, darzu der treuen warhaftigen lerer und das Gott
> treue arbeiter in seine ernte senden wolle, für die oberkeit, für zeitlichen friede und
> gewechs der früchte und in summa für die not der ganzen christenheit und sonderbarer personen,
> die des christlichen gebets begeren

But before the sermon the priest is to exhort the people to Christian prayer, that the dear God
would give them his Holy Ghost, to handle his holy word profitably and to take it into the
heart, that they may amend their sinful life, obtain comfort and strengthening of their faith,
and henceforth live christianly and blessedly. Thereupon an Our Father shall be said, or *Nu
bitten wir den heiligen Geist* sung; about Christmas, *Ein kindelein so löbelich*, etc.; about
Easter, *Christ ist erstanden*, etc. At the end of the sermon the people shall again be exhorted
to prayer and thanksgiving toward God, and [shall pray] together for the preservation of the
Church of God and right doctrine, and thereto of faithful, true teachers, and that God would
send faithful labourers into his harvest; for the government, for temporal peace and the
increase of the fruits; and in sum for the need of all Christendom and of particular persons
that desire the Christian prayer.

**Other orders.**
- **Luther 1526**: "das man auf der cantzel gemein gebet thut oder das vater unser fur spricht"
  (that the common prayer is made in the pulpit, or the Our Father said before [the people]),
  remains from the old pulpit office (§5.2).
- **Mecklenburg 1552**: at the end of the sermon the people are "zum gebet und danksagung …
  vermanet" (exhorted to prayer and thanksgiving), and the preacher is to bid them "das sie in
  der kirchen bei der communio bleiben" (that they remain in the church at the communion)
  (Sehling 5, p. 198). Lüneburg copies it.
- **Hadeln**: the preacher "schall thom ersten dat volk vormahnen tho vlitiger anropinge der
  hulpe gades" (shall first exhort the people to diligent calling upon the help of God). After
  the sermon he again exhorts to prayer, recites the Small Catechism, and prays for the prince,
  the preachers "und alle nothsaken" (and all needs) (Sehling 5, p. 468).
- **Buxtehude 1526**: the chaplains "scholen … alle ore predige mit gesange, Godt umme syne
  gnade to bidden, ahnfangen" (shall begin all their sermons with a hymn, to pray God for his
  grace; Sehling 7/1, p. 72).
- **Hanau-Lichtenberg 1573**, weekday sermons: "fur der Predig eynen Psalmen singen. Dem folg
  ein Vater unser und die Predig. Letzlich beschließ die Predig mit dem Gesang, Gebet und Segen"
  (before the sermon sing a psalm; then follow an Our Father and the sermon; lastly close the
  sermon with hymn, prayer and blessing; Sehling 20/2, p. 53). In the villages without a school
  the pastor sings a German psalm, then "erzele er die offne Beicht, Absolution und Epistel"
  (let him recite the open confession, absolution and epistle; ibid.).
- **Palatinate 1563**: the prayer of confession before the sermon, ending in the Our Father,
  said by the minister (§3.6); the confession, absolution and the long general prayer after it
  (§4.4).

The pulpit prayers, the greeting and the notices are treated more fully in
[`SERMONS_GUIDE.md`](SERMONS_GUIDE.md) §10.

**Who prays.** The preacher bids and leads; the congregation prays the Our Father with him or
silently, and answers "Amen". The schoolboys and choir sing the hymn. No order gives a private
prayer for the preacher to say before going up into the pulpit. Pfalz-Veldenz 1574 has him begin
the sermon "mitt dem gebett, wie breuchlich" (with the prayer, as is customary; Sehling 18, p.
499), and leaves the text to custom.

### 6.5 On entering and leaving church; the communicants' own prayers

**On entering church.** No order in the corpus prints or appoints a prayer for the worshipper to
say on entering or leaving the church. The modern hymnal's "prayer on entering church" has no
sixteenth-century church-order counterpart. What the orders appoint "when one comes into church"
is a hymn:
- **Sayn 1590**: "Erstlich, wann man in die Kirche kompt, soll man einen Teutschen Psalm singen.
  Darnach soll der Pfarrherr ein Collect lesen" (First, when one cometh into the church, a
  German psalm shall be sung; thereafter the pastor shall read a collect; Sehling 19/1, p. 412).
- **Pfalz-Zweibrücken 1557**: an opening hymn of the Holy Ghost is sung "biß die Kirch zusammen
  kombt" (until the church cometh together; §3.4).
- **Amberg 1550**, weekday morning prayer: the priest begins *Deus in adiutorium*, and the choir
  sings one or two Latin psalms "bis das volk zusammenkumbt" (until the people come together;
  Sehling 13, p. 287).

The orders regulate behaviour instead: the people are not to stand about in the churchyard or
run in and out during the sermon. The village ordinance of Obereisenheim (Castell) of 1579 wants
each to go into church when the bell is rung, to "Gottes wort hören, der predigt auswarten und
sein gebet mit einer ganzen gemeind ruhig und still volbringen" (hear God's word, wait upon the
sermon, and finish his prayer with the whole congregation quietly and still; Sehling 11, p.
688).

**The communicants' prayers.** The orders print no private prayers for the communicant to say
before or after receiving. What the communicant does is set out in three ways:
1. **Private preparation** by confession and examination on the Saturday (§4.6).
2. **Prayers said for them by the minister**:
   - Pfalz-Veldenz 1574 has a "Gebett vor dem Abendmal", read by the minister to the people
     after the absolution. It asks "du wöllest uns allen, die wir von disem deinem heiligen
     abendtmal essen unnd trinckhen werden, die seelige gemeinschafft deines leibs und bluts
     gnediglich schenckhen" (that thou wouldest graciously give unto all of us that shall eat
     and drink of this thy holy Supper the blessed communion of thy body and blood; Sehling 18,
     p. 499).
   - Micron's Dutch order has a "Gebett vor dem nachtmal" (prayer before the Supper), said by
     the minister from the pulpit while all kneel (Sehling 7/1, p. 630).
   - Ysenburg 1588 prints more than one prayer after communion, all collects of the minister
     (Sehling 10, p. 627).
3. **Hymns sung during the distribution.** *Jesus Christus unser Heiland*, *Gott sei gelobet*,
   the German *Agnus Dei*: the hymns are the people's prayer during communion. Mecklenburg 1552
   tells the communicants "ir solt auch hiebei euer herzlich gebet thun" (ye shall also here
   make your hearty prayer), and gives the prayer in the first person, to be made their own
   (§5.6).

**The hymnal.** The nearest thing to the modern hymnal's printed devotions is the
Pfalz-Zweibrücken *Gesangbuch* of 1571/1587. It reprints the Mecklenburg confession, prayer and
absolution as "Beicht, Gebett und Absolutio vor der Predig am Sontag" (Confession, prayer and
absolution before the sermon on Sunday), so that the people had the text in their hands
(Sehling 18, p. 187, apparatus).

**A note on "Benedicite".** In the household sections of the orders, "Benedicite" and "Gratias"
are the grace before and after meat of Luther's Small Catechism ("Aller augen warten auff dich,
Herr" and "Dancket dem Herrn, dann er ist freundlich"). It is not the canticle of the three
children. Pfalz-Zweibrücken 1557 heads it "Wie ein Haußvater sein Gesinde soll leren, das
Benedicite und Gratias sprechen" (How a householder shall teach his household to say the
Benedicite and Gratias; Sehling 18, p. 180).
