# Confessional Subscription in the Sehling Church Orders

This guide covers how the evangelical church orders of the sixteenth and early seventeenth
centuries bound people to the confessions, as printed in Emil Sehling's *Die evangelischen
Kirchenordnungen des XVI. Jahrhunderts*. It asks five questions:

- Which documents did each order name as its rule of doctrine, its *corpus doctrinae* or *norma
  doctrinae*? Was it Scripture alone, the Augsburg Confession, the Apology, the Smalcald
  Articles, Luther's catechisms, Melanchthon's *Loci* and *Corpus doctrinae*, a territorial
  corpus, the Formula of Concord or the Book of Concord, or a local confession?
- How was a minister bound to these documents? By a list of books to buy, a question at the
  visitation, an examination, a written *Revers*, a sworn oath, a "Yea" at ordination, or a
  subscription with his own hand?
- How did this differ from one territory to another, and how did it change over time? The main
  stages are the 1530s, the Interim years, the age of the territorial *corpora doctrinae* in the
  1560s and 1570s, the Formula of Concord campaign of 1577–1580, and the refusals and Reformed
  reorientations that followed.
- Were lay people ever bound in the same way? This covers the profession and vow at
  confirmation, admission to communion, godparents, admission as a burgher, and magistrates and
  other officials.
- What did the orders say about refusers and dissenters?

The findings are summarised in §1, and the method and its cautions are described in §2. §3 is a
glossary of the documents the orders name. §4 sets out the forms of binding. §§5–16 go through
the territories region by region, each in date order. §17 covers the laity. §18 is a table by
order, and §19 a concordance of every order quoted.

**Conventions**

- **Quotations.** Every quotation is Sehling's text as it stands in the `eko.db` database built
  from the digitized edition.
  - Footnote numbers, sigla, folio markers and interleaved apparatus are left out.
  - Sehling's own supplied words in square brackets are kept, as are "[!]", "[sic]" and his
    other signs.
  - Spelling and punctuation are as printed, including plain OCR errors (such as "Gorpus" for
    "Corpus" or "heiser" for "keiser").
  - "[…]" marks an omission.
  - Every quotation has been checked against the database text, allowing for OCR noise: at least
    90 per cent of its words must be found there in order. Its page has been checked against the
    edition's page markers.
  - Each quotation is preceded by an HTML comment, invisible when rendered, that names the
    `eko.db` document it was checked against.
- **Translations.** The English is formal-equivalence, in the idiom of the Authorized Version:
  - Word order and clause structure are kept where English allows.
  - Stock terms are rendered the same way throughout. *Corpus doctrinae* and *norma doctrinae*
    are left in Latin. *Bekenntnis* is "confession". *Kirchendiener* is "minister of the
    church". *Pfarrer*, *Pfarrherr* is "pastor". *unterschreiben*, *subscribieren* is
    "subscribe". *geloben* is "vow" and *schwören* is "swear". *mit handgebender Treue* is "with
    fealty given by the hand". *Verstand* is "sense" or "understanding".
  - Book titles are given in their usual English form ("the Augsburg Confession", "the Smalcald
    Articles").
  - Words the translation supplies are in square brackets.
- **Abbreviations.** CA = Augsburg Confession (1530); CA *variata* = Melanchthon's altered
  edition of 1540; Apol = Apology of the Augsburg Confession; SA = Smalcald Articles; SC and LC
  = Luther's Small and Large Catechisms; FC = Formula of Concord (1577); BoC = Book of Concord
  (1580); *CPhil* = *Corpus doctrinae Philippicum* (1560); KO = *Kirchenordnung*, church order.
- **Citations.** Citations are in the form "Sehling volume, page".
  - The half-volumes of the edition are written 6/1, 6/2, 7/1, 7/2.1, 7/2.2, 17/1, 17/2, 19/1,
    19/2, 20/1 and 20/2, following the digitized volumes.
  - Each order is named by place, title and date as they appear in Sehling's running heads.
    These sometimes differ from the database's record headings (see §2.3).
    - Where a passage is Sehling's own introduction or apparatus, not the text of an order, this
      is said.
- **Traditions.** Most of the orders cited are Lutheran, and they are not marked. Every order of
  another tradition is marked where it is cited, by its name in brackets after the order, as
  "Kurpfalz 1563 (Reformed)". The traditions follow the inventory in
  [`CHURCH_ORDERS_GUIDE.md`](CHURCH_ORDERS_GUIDE.md), §3:
  - **Reformed**: the Swiss, Calvinist and Heidelberg churches, among them the German
    territories that turned Reformed after 1560 and the French and Dutch stranger churches;
  - **mediating**: orders that stood between the Lutheran and the Reformed: the Upper German
    cities of Bucer's circle before the Interim, Philip of Hesse's church, Hermann von Wied's
    Cologne order, and the Philippist churches of Bremen and Colmar;
  - **Reformed–Lutheran treaty**: the East Frisian settlements of 1595 and 1599.
  - The mark follows the order cited, not the territory, since a territory could change its
    tradition: Kurpfalz is Lutheran under Ottheinrich (1556) and Ludwig VI (1576–1583) and
    Reformed under Frederick III and from 1583; Bremen is Lutheran to 1561, mediating
    (Philippist) from 1556 to 1575 and Reformed from 1582; Hesse is mediating until the
    division, after which Hessen-Kassel turned Reformed and Hessen-Darmstadt stayed Lutheran.
  - Orders made under or for the Augsburg Interim of 1548 are marked as Interim orders
    (`CHURCH_ORDERS_GUIDE.md`, §4).
  - "Philippist" in this guide names a party within the Lutheran churches. Only the Philippist
    churches that the inventory counts as mediating (Bremen and Colmar) are marked.

## 1. Summary of findings

### 1.1 The main findings

**1. There was no single confessional standard until 1580, and not everywhere after it.** Every
territory had a rule of doctrine, but what that rule held varied a great deal. In outline it
moved through five stages:

- **To about 1540: Scripture and the prince.** The earliest oaths bound a pastor to preach "the
  holy gospel and word of God" purely, together with obedience to his lord.
  Brandenburg-Ansbach's priest's oath of 1528 is the model (§14.1). Heilbronn's council swore in
  1530 to stand together in matters "of the gospel" (§17.5). Confessions are not yet named.
- **About 1540 to 1560: the Augsburg Confession and Melanchthon.** The CA and its Apology become
  the measure of preaching. The orders add Melanchthon's *Loci communes* as the textbook from
  which ministers are examined. This is so in Albertine Saxony in 1540 and 1557 (§5.1),
  Württemberg in 1547 (§13.1), Pomerania (§7.4) and Strasbourg's examination of 1549, issued
  under the Interim (§15.1). The norm is still given chiefly as a list of books a parish must
  buy.
- **The 1560s and 1570s: territorial *corpora doctrinae*.** Princes and cities gathered their
  own bodies of doctrine, which pastors had to own, follow and often subscribe. These were the
  Philippist *Corpus doctrinae* of 1560 (adopted in Electoral Saxony, Kurland, Brieg, Oldenburg,
  Bremen and Hesse, the last two mediating), the Pomeranian, the Prussian (1567), the
  Thuringian, the Wolfenbüttel *Corpus Julium* (1576), the Brunswick city corpus (1571), the
  Ansbach-Nürnberg *norma doctrinae* (1573) and the Hohenlohe corpus (1578). Some local
  confessions also served as corpora: Mansfeld 1565, the Lüneburg Articles of 1561, Danzig's
  *Notel* of 1562, the Strasbourg Concord of 1563 and Ravensburg's local "formula concordiae" of
  1578.
- **1577 to about 1600: the Formula and the Book of Concord.** The FC was put to every minister
  for signature in Electoral Saxony, Brandenburg, Württemberg, Mecklenburg, Henneberg, Mansfeld,
  Pfalz-Neuburg, the Upper Palatinate, Ansbach and many Swabian and Franconian cities, and it
  was written into church orders such as Hoya's (1581). Later orders made signing the BoC a
  condition of admission to office (§§5.3, 6.2, 9, 13, 14). Ordination questions took the FC
  into the vow itself, answered with "Yea" (Henneberg 1582, Waldeck, Frankfurt 1589 and
  Strasbourg 1598).
- **The refusers and the Reformed turn.** Some refused the FC outright: Anhalt (§6.6), Hesse
  (mediating), Danzig, Nürnberg and its neighbours, Gottorp and the Danish lands, Colmar and
  Bremen (both mediating). Pomerania kept its own corpus and took only three points of the FC
  against the Calvinists, in
  1593. Some ceased to require it when they turned Reformed: Kurpfalz after 1583 and the Upper
        Palatinate in 1585. Others kept a pre-1577 norm or replaced the FC with a local formula,
        such as Hohenlohe's *Gründlicher Bericht* of 1603–1605. Where the Reformed took over,
        the CA *variata* or Scripture alone became the stated norm. The CA was understood in the
        Frankfurt Recess and Naumburg sense, and the Heidelberg Catechism replaced Luther's.

**2. The canon grew by accretion.** The usual sequence of additions to Scripture and the three
creeds was:

- the CA, from 1530;
- the Apology;
- the SA, named increasingly from the 1550s, often "as signed at Smalcald 1537";
- Luther's catechisms;
- then either Melanchthon's corpus or a territorial corpus;
- and finally the FC or the whole BoC.

Many orders show this growth inside their own textual history. Ansbach's 1528 oath carries
apparatus variants for 1570, 1573 and 1588 (§14.1). The Hohenlohe KO of 1578 gained a
handwritten "7. Die formulam concordiae" (§13.3). The Kurpfalz formula of 1556 was rewritten in
1580 and 1586 (§12.1).

**3. "Unaltered" and "in its true sense" are the signs of party.**

- *Gnesio-Lutheran or FC party.* Adjectives such as *unverändert*, *ungeändert*, *uralt*, *wie
  anno 30 übergeben*, "in its right, true sense" and "as explained in the Apology, the SA and
  the catechisms" mark the Gnesio-Lutheran or FC party. Examples are Wolfenbüttel 1569 (§9.1),
  Verden 1606, Osnabrück 1610 and Hoya 1581.
- *Philippist or Reformed party.* Phrases such as "as repeated at Frankfurt and Naumburg", the
  *Corpus doctrinae Philippi* and the *Repetitio* (*Confessio Saxonica*) mark the Philippist or
  Reformed party. Examples are Bremen 1572 (mediating), the Upper Palatinate 1585 (Reformed) and
  Kurland 1570 (Lutheran).

**4. The form of binding hardened.** The forms run from a book list, through a question at the
visitation and a promise, to a sworn oath and a signature in one's own hand. In the oldest
orders the confession appears as books a parish must buy (Ernestine Saxony 1554, Albertine
Saxony 1557). Later orders demand:

- a public confession at admission (Ernestine Saxony 1569, Strasbourg from 1562);
- a written promise or *Revers* (Nassau-Diez 1564);
- an oath on the confessions (Ansbach, Peine/Gottorp 1574, Wimpfen, Hessen-Darmstadt 1617,
  Transylvania);
- subscription "with his own hand" (Electoral Saxony 1580, Regensburg 1572/88, Hildesheim 1593,
  Wimpfen).

The Kurpfalz (Lutheran and Reformed in turn) and Pfalz-Neuburg *Bestallungen* bind "with fealty
given by the hand in the stead of a bodily sworn oath" (§12.1). §4 gives the forms in full.

**5. Subscription reached beyond the clergy, but not to the common people.** Superintendents,
consistorial assessors and professors were bound as a matter of course. So in some cities were
burgomasters, syndics, physicians and secretaries (Hildesheim 1593), the chamberlain and council
(Regensburg 1572/88), and schoolmasters in many places. **No order requires ordinary lay people
to subscribe the CA or the FC.** The laity were bound in other ways (§17):

- by the catechism examination before first communion and before marriage;
- by a public profession and vow at confirmation, chiefly in Hesse from 1539 and Strasbourg
  (both mediating), the Reformed refugee churches and Electoral Saxony's confirmation of the
  baptismal faith;
- by the confession and exploration required before each communion (Schweinfurt 1543, after the
  CA itself);
- by rules for godparents and for admission as a burgher.

The Reformed orders alone required an explicit "confession of the Reformed doctrine" before the Supper (Nassau-Dillenburg 1582, Reformed, §17.2).

## 2. Scope, sources and method

### 2.1 What was searched

The whole of `eko.db` was searched for the vocabulary of confessional obligation:

- the names of the confessions, in German and Latin and with their OCR variants (*augspurgische
  confession*, *apologia*, *schmalkaldische artikel*, *catechismus Lutheri*, *formula
  concordiae*, *concordienbuch*, *corpus doctrinae*, *norma doctrinae*, *loci communes*,
  *repetitio*);
- the verbs of binding (*unterschreiben*, *subscribieren*, *schwören*, *geloben*, *angeloben*,
  *verpflichten*, *zusagen*, *Revers*, *Eid*, *Pflicht*, *iuramentum*, *subscribo*, *promitto*);
- the occasions of binding: ordination, examination, vocation, *Bestallung*, visitation, synod,
  admission to the consistory, and appointment as schoolmaster, superintendent or assessor.

Every hit was read in context. Each was sorted by whether it was the text of an order, Sehling's
introduction or a footnote. For the laity the search covered confirmation, first communion,
admission to the Supper (*exploration*, *verhör*), godparents (*gevattern*, *paten*,
*susceptores*), admission as burgher (*bürger*, *hintersäß*), and oaths of councils and
officials.

### 2.2 What the guide does not do

- It does not give the full text of each confession, nor the history of the FC's making. It
  records only what the church orders themselves say.
- It does not list every signature. The great lists of subscribers in the archives are beyond
  the edition. Sehling sometimes reports their numbers, for example 444 signatures in
  Mecklenburg in 1577 (Sehling 5, pp. 142–143), and these are noted where he does.
- **A book list is not a subscription.** Many orders tell a parish to buy the CA, the *Loci* or
  a *Corpus doctrinae*, and tell the pastor to read it. That makes the book a standard of
  preaching, but it is not an oath or a signature. Where an order lists books only, this is
  said.
- **A territorial norm is not always a personal obligation.** A preface may say that "our
  churches" hold the CA without saying how a pastor was bound to it. The guide marks which kind
  of statement each quotation is.

### 2.3 Cautions

- **Sehling's introductions and apparatus.** Several of the best statements about subscription,
  such as the Hildesheim decision of 1593 or the Anhalt refusal of the FC (Anhalt was still
  Lutheran), are found only in Sehling's introductions or footnotes, which quote or summarise
  archival sources. They are marked as such.
- **Apparatus variants.** Some orders were reissued, and the editors print the later wording in
  the apparatus. Examples are the Ansbach priest's oath (1528, with forms of 1570, 1573 and
  1588), the Hohenlohe KO of 1578 (with a handwritten addition naming the FC) and the Kurpfalz
  KO of 1556 (with variants of 1577). These are quoted as variants with their dates.
- **Record headings.** The database's record headings sometimes name the first order in a volume
  section rather than the order on the page. So document 1844 is headed "Diese erste Danziger
  Ordnung … 1557", but its p. 234 is the Thorn KO of 1575, and its p. 210 the Danzig KO of 1612.
  Each order is named here from Sehling's running head. Where the running head is itself
  misleading, this is said (as at Anhalt, p. 579, where a passage of the 1588 *Instructio* runs
  onto a page headed with the next order).
- **OCR errors.** OCR errors are quoted as printed. Thus Calenberg's "Gorpus doctrinae Julium"
  is printed as such, and the Kassel KO of 1539 (mediating) carries the running head "1589".

### 2.4 How to read the regional sections

Each regional section goes through its orders in date order. For each it says:

- **what the norm was**: which documents the order names, in what order, and with what
  qualifiers ("unaltered", "as delivered in 1530", "in its true sense");
- **who was bound**: ordinands, pastors, superintendents, schoolmasters, assessors, councils;
- **how they were bound**: book list, visitation question, examination, promise, *Revers*, oath,
  ordination "Yea", or subscription.

The table in §18 gathers these by order.

## 3. The documents the orders name

This glossary gives the documents in the form the orders name them. Where an order's wording
identifies a particular edition or reading (the *invariata* or the *variata*, the first print of
the Apology), this is noted. For dates and contents the standard editions are the
*Bekenntnisschriften der evangelisch-lutherischen Kirche* (BSLK, BSELK), which the editors of
the later volumes cite in their footnotes.

### 3.1 The ancient creeds

- **The three creeds** (*drei Hauptsymbola*, *symbola oecumenica*): the Apostles', the Nicene
  and the Athanasian. They come first after Scripture in almost every list.
- **The four councils** (Nicaea, Constantinople, Ephesus and Chalcedon). These are named in
  Kurland (1570), the Katzenelnbogen pastors' oath (1598, mediating, together with Leo's Tome to
  Flavian), Strasbourg (1598) and the Upper Palatinate (1585, Reformed). In Hessen-Kassel and
  the Upper Palatinate they are aimed against the ubiquity doctrine.

### 3.2 The Lutheran confessions

- **Augsburg Confession (CA), 1530.** Named in nearly every order from the 1530s on. The
  qualifiers matter:
  - *wie anno 30 übergeben*, *ungeändert*, *unverändert*, *uralt*, *rein, unverfälscht*,
    *invariata*, "the first edition";
  - against "as repeated at Frankfurt and Naumburg" (§12.2) and the *variata* of 1540. Ansbach
    (1573) names both "the last" and "the first edition" as signed at Naumburg in 1561 (§14.1).
- **Apology of the CA (Apol).** Usually paired with the CA. Goslar names it "in the first print"
  (§9.6). Anhalt dates the CA and the Apology "anno 30 und 33" (§6.6).
- **Smalcald Articles (SA), 1537.** Often "written by Dr Luther for the council at Mantua, and
  subscribed at Smalcald" (Albertine Saxony 1580, Ernestine Saxony 1569).
- **Luther's Small and Large Catechisms (SC, LC).** Hoya calls them "the laymen's Bible" (§9.5).
- **Wittenberg Concord, 1536.** Named in Pfalz-Neuburg (1564) and in Osnabrück (1610), which
  calls it "*Formula Concordiae*, anno 1536 aufgerichtet". This should not be confused with the
  FC of 1577.
- **Formula of Concord (FC), 1577, and the Book of Concord (BoC), 1580.** The FC is also called
  the *Bergisches Buch*, the *einigungs und erklerungs notel* (Henneberg), the *einigungs
  formul* (Henneberg, Waldeck) and the *Concordibuch*. The BoC is the whole corpus printed in
  1580.
- **Erfurt Apology of the BoC, 1582.** Signed with the FC in Hildesheim (§10.1).

### 3.3 Melanchthon and the Philippist corpora

- ***Loci communes*.** Melanchthon's doctrinal handbook. It was the examination textbook in
  Albertine Saxony (1540), Württemberg (*Loci recentiores*, 1547), Pomerania, Nassau-Diez (1564)
  and Ansbach (1573).
- ***Repetitio* or *Confessio Saxonica*, 1551.** Written for the Council of Trent. Albertine
  Saxony (1557), Ansbach (1573) and Bremen (1570, mediating) name it.
- ***Examen ordinandorum*** (Melanchthon's examination of ordinands, 1552) and the
  ***Responsiones* to the Bavarian articles**.
- ***Corpus doctrinae Philippicum* (or *Misnicum*), 1560.** Melanchthon's collected doctrinal
  works, printed by Vögelin in Leipzig. It was official in Electoral Saxony until 1574 and was
  adopted in Kurland (1570), Silesia (Brieg 1592), Oldenburg (1573), Bremen (1570–1575) and
  Hesse (1571), both mediating, and Pfalz-Zweibrücken.

### 3.4 Territorial corpora and local confessions

- ***Corpus doctrinae Pomeranicum*** (1564): Melanchthon's works with Luther's catechisms and

- ***Corpus doctrinae Prutenicum*** (1567), the Prussian corpus (§7.2).
- ***Corpus doctrinae Thuringicum*** (1570), and the Weimar *Confutationsbuch* (1558/59) against
  the sects, which Ernestine Saxony made binding in 1569 (§6.1).
- ***Corpus doctrinae Julium*** (Wolfenbüttel, 1576), signed in Calenberg-Göttingen (§9.4).
- ***Corpus doctrinae Wilhelminum*** (Lüneburg, 1576). This is named only in Sehling's
  introduction (Sehling 6/1, pp. 487–489).
- **The Brunswick city corpus** (1571) (§9.2).
- **The Lüneburg Articles** (1561), against the Philippists. Rostock (1573) names them, and
  Brunswick (1571) condemns what they condemn.
- **The confession of Lübeck, Hamburg and Lüneburg against the Interim** (1548). Part of the
  Lüneburg city corpus (1575).
- **The Lower Saxon Confession** (1571). In Goslar's corpus (1591/95).
- **The Mansfeld confession** (1565) (§6.3).
- **The Danzig *Notel*** (1562). The city's own formula, signed by its clergy (§7.3).
- **The Ansbach-Nürnberg *Norma doctrinae*** (1573) (§14.1).
- **The Strasbourg Concord** (1563) (§15).
- **The Ravensburg "formula concordiae"** (1578, renewed 1617), a local formula of the city's
  preachers (§13.5).
- **The Württemberg Confession** (*Confessio Virtembergica*), presented at Trent in 1552
  (§13.1).
- **The Hohenlohe *Gründlicher Bericht*** (1603/05), which took the place of the FC (§13.3).
- **The Confessio Anhaltina** and the Anhalt Supper confession of 1585. These are named in
  Sehling's introduction (§6.6).

### 3.5 Reformed documents

- **The Heidelberg Catechism** (1563, Reformed), which replaced Luther's catechism in the
  Reformed Kurpfalz *Bestallung* of 1586 (§12.1).
- **The CA as "repeated" at Frankfurt (1558) and Naumburg (1561)**: the CA read in the *variata*

  mediating).
- **"The confession of the Reformed doctrine"** required for admission to the Supper in
  Nassau-Dillenburg (1582, Reformed) (§17.2).

## 4. The forms of binding

The orders bind ministers to their confessions in seven ways. They range from the weakest, a
list of books, to the strongest, an oath confirmed by one's own signature. Most orders use
several of them at once.

1. **The book list.** The parish must buy the named books, keep them in the inventory, and the
   pastor must read them. Examples are Ernestine Saxony 1554, Albertine Saxony 1540 and 1557,
   Pomerania 1563/69 and Brieg 1592 (§§5.1, 6.1, 7.1, 7.4). It sets a standard of preaching but
   requires no personal act.
2. **The visitation question.** The visitor asks the pastor whether he holds the named
   confessions as his *norma doctrinae* and renounces all contrary opinions. Examples are
   Henneberg 1574, Magdeburg 1583 and Nassau-Weilburg 1576 (§§6.2, 6.4, 11.5).
3. **The examination.** The candidate is examined from the *Loci*, the CA or the corpus.
   Examples are Württemberg 1547, Strasbourg 1549 and Ansbach 1594, where he must first have
   read the whole BoC (§§13.1, 15.1, 14.1).
4. **The promise, *Revers* or *Bestallung*.** The minister promises in writing or by handshake
   to teach according to the named books. Examples:
   - the Nassau-Diez *Revers* (1564, §11.4);
   - the Pomeranian synodal formula "*per dexteram societatis*" (1574, §7.4);
   - the Kurpfalz *Bestallungen* "with fealty given by the hand in the stead of a bodily sworn
     oath" (1580–1601, Lutheran in 1580 and Reformed from 1586, §12.1).
5. **The oath.** The minister swears by God's name to the named books. Examples:
   - Ansbach, *glob und schwer* (1528 and later, §14.1);
   - the Peine and Gottorp preachers' oath (1574, §§8.4, 10.1);
   - the Wimpfen *iuramentum* (after 1577, §13.5);
   - Colmar (1575, §15.3);
   - Hessen-Darmstadt (1617, §11.1);
   - the Transylvanian superintendent (1601, §16). The Hohenlohe oath of 1588 swears to the
     church order rather than to named confessions (§13.3).
6. **The ordination vow.** At the laying on of hands the ordinand is asked whether he will
   direct his faith and confession by the named books and answers "Yea". This is so in Henneberg
   1582, Waldeck, Frankfurt 1589 and Strasbourg 1598 (§§6.2, 11.2, 11.3, 15.2). In Mecklenburg
   in 1552 the promise is to remain in "the pure Christian doctrine which he hath confessed in
   this hearing", and no book is named (§8.1).
7. **Subscription.** The minister signs the corpus or the FC "with his own hand". Examples:
   - Albertine Saxony 1580 (§5.3);
   - Brunswick 1571 (§9.2);
   - Regensburg 1572/88 (§14.3);
   - Strasbourg "*corde et manu*" 1577 (§15.2);
   - Hildesheim 1593 (§10.1);
   - Soest 1590 (§10.7);
   - Schwäbisch Hall 1614/15 (§13.5);
   - Wimpfen's *Notula subscriptionis* (§13.5);
   - Pfalz-Zweibrücken's Latin "*mente atque manu subscribo*" of 1564 (§12.4).

**Who was bound.** Pastors and preachers come first. The orders regularly add:

- deacons, chaplains and schoolmasters (Hildesheim, Kurpfalz 1601 (Reformed), Hessen-Darmstadt);
- superintendents (Wild- und Rheingrafschaft 1576, Transylvania 1601);
- consistorial presidents and assessors (Ernestine Saxony 1569, Prussia 1584, Ansbach 1594);
- professors (Hessen-Darmstadt 1617);
- in cities, the council itself (Regensburg 1572), and burgomasters, syndics, physicians and
  secretaries (Hildesheim 1593).

For the laity see §17.

## 5. Albertine (Electoral) Saxony

Albertine Saxony moved further than any other territory: from the CA and Melanchthon's *Loci*
(1540), through the *Corpus doctrinae Philippicum* (official from 1566 by Sehling's account,
Sehling 1, pp. 124–125), to the Book of Concord. In 1580 a signature to the BoC was the
condition of admission to every parish.

### 5.1 The CA and the *Loci* as a book list (1540, 1557)

The visitors' general instruction of 1540 tells pastors to preach nothing but what agrees with
the CA and Apology, and to buy the books by which they will be measured. **Albertine Saxony,
*Gemeiner bericht der visitatorn an die pfarrer und dorfschaften*, 1540** (Sehling 1, p. 285):

<!-- doc 32 -->
> Und letzlich nichts anders lehren, denn das der confession und apologia keiserlicher mai. zu
> Augspurg uberantwortet, und bevorn dem heiligen evangelio gemes ist; darzu wil von nöten sein,
> das sie ihnen keufen die biblien und dieselbe confession etc. locos communes d. Philip.,
> postillam Lutheri und andere christliche reine bücher, und darinnen vleissig studiren.

And lastly let them teach nothing else than that which is agreeable to the confession and
apology delivered to his imperial majesty at Augsburg, and above all to the holy gospel; whereto
it will be needful that they buy for themselves the Bible and the same confession, etc., the
*Loci communes* of Dr Philip, Luther's postil, and other Christian pure books, and study
diligently therein.

By 1557 the parish was to buy the *Repetitio* (*Confessio Saxonica*) of 1551 with the CA.
**Albertine Saxony, *General-Artikel und gemeiner Bericht*, 8 May 1557** (Sehling 1, p. 325):

<!-- doc 37 -->
> Bei den kirchen-vätern sol er anhalten, das sie von der kirchen-einkommen eine deutsche
> biblia, augspurgische confession, und deren obgemelte repetition, agenda, auch die deutsche
> locos communes erkaufen, die bei der pfarr bleiben und in das inventarium vorzeichnet werden,
> dieselbe sol er und der cüster fleissig zu lesen schuldig sein.

He shall urge the churchwardens that they buy out of the church's income a German Bible, the
Augsburg Confession and the repetition thereof above mentioned, the agenda, and also the German
*Loci communes*, which shall remain with the parish and be written into the inventory; and the
same shall he and the sexton be bound diligently to read.

The norm here is wholly Melanchthonian. The CA is read with its *Repetitio* and the *Loci*; the
Smalcald Articles are not named.

### 5.2 The *Corpus doctrinae Philippicum* (1560–1574)

The 1560 *Corpus doctrinae* (§3.3) was the official norm in Electoral Saxony from 1566 until the
fall of the Philippists in 1574 (Sehling's introduction, Sehling 1, pp. 124–125). Outside Saxony
it was named as a norm in Kurland, Silesia, Oldenburg, Bremen and Hesse, and Melanchthon's
*Loci* in Nassau (§§7.1, 8.2, 10.4, 10.5, 11.1, 11.4).

### 5.3 The Book of Concord as the condition of office (1580)

Elector August's church order of 1580 makes a signature to the BoC the condition of being sent
to a parish. **Electoral Saxony, *Kirchenordnung* of Elector August, 1580** (Sehling 1, p. 380):

<!-- doc 44 -->
> Wann eine person auf gehalten examen und beschehene probpredigt zum kirchendienst tüchtig
> erkant, und durch die kirchen ördentlich vocirt und berufen ist, auch das buch der concordien
> mit eigener hand unterschrieben hat, das ihm der lehr halben zutrauen, sol ihm vor endlicher
> abfertigung auf die verordente pfarr, durch den presidenten oder directorem des consistorii
> nachfolgende erinnerung mit ernst fürgehalten werden.

When a person, after the examination held and the trial sermon done, is found fit for the
ministry of the church, and is orderly called and summoned by the church, and hath also
subscribed the Book of Concord with his own hand, so that he may be trusted as touching
doctrine, then before his final dispatch to the parish appointed the admonition following shall
be earnestly set before him by the president or director of the consistory.

The same order sets out the whole norm, in the sequence that became standard. The Smalcald
Articles are now in, and the *Corpus Philippicum* is gone (Sehling 1, p. 420):

<!-- doc 44 -->
> ist unser ernstlicher wille und meinung, das hinfüro in allen kirchen unserer churfürstenthumb
> und landen, in massen wir anfangs in der Vorrede uber diese verfaste ordnung uns erkleret,
> sich alle pfarrer und kirchendiener anders nicht, denn den biblischen prophetischen und
> apostolischen schriften und denselben nach der unverenderten confession und der darauf
> erfolgten apologia und den zu Schmalkalden durch d. Luthern geschriebenen und die daselbsten
> versandete fürneme theologen approbirten und unterschriebenen artikeln, so dem concilio zu
> Mantua uberantwortet werden sollen, auch beiden christlichen catechismis d. Luthers und der
> jüngst darauf zu Torgau von allen streitigen artikeln gestelter und hernach aus aller andern
> kirchen eingebrachtem bedenken verbesserter einhelliger declaration und christlichen erklerung
> durchaus gemess verhalten und denselben zuwider weder heimlich noch offentlich, nichts leren
> sollen.

It is our earnest will and meaning that henceforth in all the churches of our electorate and
lands, as we declared at the beginning in the preface to this ordinance, all pastors and
ministers of the church shall in all things behave themselves according to none other than the
Biblical, prophetic and apostolic Scriptures; and after them according to the unaltered
confession and the apology that followed thereon; and the articles written by Dr Luther at
Smalcald, approved and subscribed by the chief theologians there assembled, which were to have
been delivered to the council at Mantua; and also both Christian catechisms of Dr Luther; and
the unanimous declaration and Christian explanation of all the articles in controversy, lately
drawn up thereupon at Torgau and afterwards amended from the judgments brought in from all other
churches; and shall teach nothing contrary to the same, neither secretly nor openly.

The FC is not named by its title. It is described as the declaration made at Torgau and amended
from the judgments of the other churches. The same order's teaching on confirmation is quoted in
§17.1.

## 6. Ernestine Saxony, Henneberg, the Harz counties, Magdeburg, Halberstadt and Anhalt

The Ernestine dukes were the Gnesio-Lutheran counterweight to Albertine Saxony. Their norm adds
the Smalcald Articles early, and in 1569 the Weimar *Confutationsbuch* against the "sects".
Around them the smaller territories of central Germany (Henneberg, Mansfeld, the archbishopric
of Magdeburg, Halberstadt) followed with local confessions and, after 1577, with the FC. Anhalt
alone refused it.

### 6.1 Ernestine Saxony: from the book list to the public confession (1554, 1569)

The 1554 instruction puts the Smalcald Articles beside the CA in the parish's library.
**Ernestine Saxony, *Instruction Johans Friderichen des mittlern, Johans Wilhelm und Johans
Friderichen d. j.*, 1554** (Sehling 1, p. 227):

<!-- doc 21 -->
> so sollen die visitatores mit den kirchvetern iedes orts vorfugen, das sie von dem kirchen
> oder anderm gemainen gut eine deutsche biblien und doctor Luthers hauspostil, Augspurgische
> confession, Schmalkaldische artikel, so Lutherus uf das concilium zu Mantua anno 38, zusampt
> der unterrichtung der visitatorn welche auch letzlich anno 38 ausgangen, kaufen, und
> dieselbigen erblichen zu der pfar widemen, in das pfar inventarium vorzeichen

the visitors shall order with the churchwardens of every place that they buy out of the church's
or other common goods a German Bible and Doctor Luther's house postil, the Augsburg Confession,
the Smalcald Articles, [which] Luther [made] for the council at Mantua in the year 38, together
with the Instruction of the Visitors, which likewise came forth lastly in the year 38; and endow
them in perpetuity to the parish, and write them into the parish inventory.

By 1569 no one could sit in the consistory at Jena unless he openly confessed the norm and
condemned the errors named in the ducal *Confutationes* of 1558. **Ernestine Saxony, *Ordnung
und reformation ecclesiastici consistorii zu Jena*, 1569** (Sehling 1, p. 234):

<!-- doc 24 -->
> so ist unser gemüht und meinung, und wollen das kein person daran angenommen, noch geduldet
> werde, die sei dann eines christlichen erbarn wandels und bekenne sich offentlich und klar zu
> der h. schrift, des alten und neuen testaments, den dreien symbolis, apostolico, niceno und
> Athanasii, der augspurgischen confession, wie die anno 30. keiser Carol dem fünften, von
> unserm herren grosvater, und genedigen herrn vater herzog Johansen, und herzog Johans
> Fridrichen zu Sachsen, churfürsten, hochlöblicher christlicher gedechtnus, und etlich andern
> fürsten und stedten, ubergeben, sampt deren apologia, auch den artikeln, welche durch
> obgenanten d. Martin Luther seliger, auf das concilium zu Mantua gestellt, […] und
> widersprochen dargegen allen alten und neuen secten und corruptelen, so gottes reinen wort zu
> wider, und sonderlich denen die in unsern fürstlichen confutationibus, nach gehabtem synodo zu
> Weimar im 1558. jar im druck ausgangen, begriffen sind.

it is our mind and meaning, and we will, that no person be received thereto nor suffered, except
he be of a Christian and honest conversation, and confess himself openly and clearly to the holy
Scripture of the Old and New Testaments; to the three creeds, the Apostolic, the Nicene and
Athanasius's; to the Augsburg Confession, as it was delivered in the year 30 to the emperor
Charles the Fifth by our lord grandfather and gracious lord father, Duke John and Duke John
Frederick of Saxony, electors of most laudable Christian memory, and certain other princes and
cities; together with the apology thereof; and also to the articles which the aforenamed Dr
Martin Luther of blessed memory set forth for the council at Mantua […]; and except he gainsay
all old and new sects and corruptions which are contrary to God's pure word, and especially
those that are comprehended in our princely confutations, which came forth in print after the
synod held at Weimar in the year 1558.

This is the first order in this guide to make a **negative** confession part of the obligation.
The minister must not only hold the books but condemn, by name, the errors a particular book
condemns. Brunswick (1571, §9.2), Regensburg (1567, §14.3) and Hessen-Darmstadt (1617, §11.1) do
the same. In 1580 the Ernestine pastors were told to read the coming Book of Concord, on which
they would be examined (Sehling 1, p. 254).

### 6.2 Henneberg: the visitation question, subscription and the ordination vow (1574–1582)

The Henneberg visitation of 1574/77 asks every pastor whether he holds the confessions as his
*norma doctrinae*. **Henneberg, *Visitations- und Consistorial-Ordnung*, 1574, 1577**
(Sehling 2, p. 291):

<!-- doc 1247 -->
> Jedes orts pfarrherr ist erstlich zu befragen, ob sich derselbe mit seinem glauben, lehr und
> bekäntniss zu h. göttl. schrift, den drei haupt symbolis, augspurgischer confession, apologia,
> schmalkaldischen artikeln und catechismo Lutheri durchaus in ihrem rechten, natürlichen,
> klaren verstande gelassen beipflicht, und pro norma doctrinae halten, auch dabei beständig
> verharren, hingegen aber alle widrigen meinungen absagen wolle.

The pastor of every place is first to be asked whether he, with his faith, doctrine and
confession, assenteth wholly to the holy divine Scripture, the three chief creeds, the Augsburg
Confession, the Apology, the Smalcald Articles and Luther's catechism, in their right, natural
and plain sense, and will hold them *pro norma doctrinae* and continue steadfastly therein, and
on the other hand renounce all contrary opinions.

The church order of 1582 recalls that Henneberg's ministers had always been "appointed,
examined, ordained and made to subscribe" on these books, and lately also on the FC.
**Henneberg, *Des durchlauchtigen hochgebornen fürsten und herrn, herrn Georg Ernsten, kirchen
ordnung*, 1582** (Sehling 2, p. 300):

<!-- doc 1247 -->
> solche bücher und schriften jederzeit fur unserer kirchen normam gehalten, unsere
> kirchendiener auch darauf bestellen, examiniren, ordiniren, und unterschreiben lassen, […] wie
> wir uns denn auch, sampt den kirchendienern unserer fürstlichen grafschaft, hiebevor neulich
> auf eben dieselbige normam in unterschreibung der gemeinen einungs und erklerungs notel,
> sonsten die formula concordiae genant, als die aus solchem grund hergeflossen, gleichsfals
> referiret und gezogen.

[we have] at all times held such books and writings for the norm of our churches, and have also
caused our ministers of the church to be appointed, examined, ordained, and to subscribe
thereupon […]; as we also, together with the ministers of the church of our princely county,
have lately referred and drawn ourselves likewise to this same norm in the subscription of the
common note of union and declaration, otherwise called the Formula of Concord, as that which
hath flowed from the same ground.

The same order puts the whole norm, the FC included, into the ordination itself. The ordinand
answers "Yea" before hands are laid on him (Sehling 2, p. 324):

<!-- doc 1247 -->
> Wollet ihr nu, wie euch nach lenge aus gottes wort vorgehalten, auch in demselbigen euch in
> euerm ampt weiter mass gegeben, halten, besonders aber euren glauben und bekentnus richten
> nach gottes wort, wie dasselbige in prophetischer und apostolischer heiliger schrift
> verfasset, desgleichen nach den dreien haupt symbolis, auch hieraus hergenomener,
> wolgegründter, warer augspurgischen confession, apologia, schmalkaldischen artikeln, kleinem
> und grossem catechismo Lutheri, sampt jetziger christlichen einigungs formul, so saget es
> hiermit gott und seiner heiligen christlichen kirchen, auch uns von ampts wegen, zu. Antwort:
> Ja. So befehlen wir euch das ampt des heiligen evangeliums, in dem namen gottes des vaters,
> sons, und heiligen geistes, amen. Imponite manus.

Will ye now keep that which hath been set before you at length out of God's word, and the
further measure given you therein for your office; but especially direct your faith and
confession according to God's word, as the same is contained in the prophetic and apostolic holy
Scripture, and likewise according to the three chief creeds, and the true, well-grounded
Augsburg Confession taken therefrom, the Apology, the Smalcald Articles, Luther's Small and
Large Catechism, together with the present Christian formula of union? Then promise it hereby to
God and his holy Christian church, and to us by virtue of our office. Answer: Yea. So we commit
unto you the office of the holy gospel, in the name of God the Father, the Son and the Holy
Ghost, amen. *Lay on hands.*

Waldeck copied this question word for word (§11.2).

### 6.3 Mansfeld: the county's own confession of 1565 and the Book of Concord (1580, 1586)

Mansfeld's corpus included its own confession of 1565. **Mansfeld, *Kirchen-agenda für die
prediger der grafschaft Mansfeld*, 1580** (Sehling 2, p. 215):

<!-- doc 1241 -->
> Was aber gleichwol die lere anlanget, so bezeugen wir auch hiemit kürzlich und offentlich, das
> wir durch gottes gnade bei der waren und richtigen einhelligkeit der rechtgleubigen
> christlichen kirchen gottes bestendiglich verharren, wie dieselbige in gottes des allmechtigen
> unwandelbaren worte der heiligen schrift, als in den büchern der heiligen propheten und
> aposteln, in den allgemeinen symbolis apostolico, Athanasiano, und Niceno, in der ersten
> reinen, unverenderten augspurgischen confession, wie die anno 1530. weiland keiser Carolo dem
> fünften etc. zu Augspurg ubergeben, derselben apologia, in den schmalkaldischen artikeln, den
> beiden catechismis, grossen und kleinen, und in den andern schriften des teuren mannes gottes
> Lutheri, auch in unsern, in offenem druck, sonderlich des 1565. jars, ausgegangenen
> bekentnissen, begriffen und verfasset ist.

But as touching doctrine, we testify also hereby briefly and openly that by God's grace we
continue steadfastly in the true and right unanimity of the orthodox Christian church of God, as
the same is comprehended and set down in the unchangeable word of Almighty God, the holy
Scripture, that is, in the books of the holy prophets and apostles; in the universal creeds, the
Apostolic, the Athanasian and the Nicene; in the first, pure, unaltered Augsburg Confession, as
it was delivered at Augsburg in the year 1530 to the late emperor Charles the Fifth, etc.; in
the Apology thereof; in the Smalcald Articles; in the two catechisms, the Large and the Small;
and in the other writings of the dear man of God, Luther; and also in our confessions that have
come forth in open print, especially those of the year 1565.

The consistory order of 1586 then sets the BoC beside the county's own confession as the rule by
which disputes are decided. **Mansfeld, *Gräflich mansfeldische geistliche consistorial
ordnung*, 1586** (Sehling 2, p. 201):

<!-- doc 1240 -->
> so soll derselbige aus der h. bibel, den schriften der propheten und aposteln, aus den
> symbolis und erkäntnüssen der wahren kirchen, aus der augspurgischenconfession und apologia,
> aus d. Luthers schriften und catechismis, aus unserer kirchen anno 1565 ausgegangenen
> bekäntnüss geortert und entschieden werden. Weil auch das anno 1585 in druck ausgegangene
> concordien buch jeztgedachter und erzehlter norma einstimmet, und wir dasselbige in allen
> unsern kirchen durch die ganze grafschaft haben deponiren, niederlegen und den pfarr herren
> darnach zu lehren befehlen lassen,

then shall the same be discussed and decided out of the holy Bible, the writings of the prophets
and apostles, out of the creeds and confessions of the true church, out of the Augsburg
Confession and Apology, out of Dr Luther's writings and catechisms, [and] out of our church's
confession that came forth in the year 1565. And forasmuch as the Book of Concord that came
forth in print in the year 1585 [variant: 1580] agreeth with this norm just now named and
recounted, and we have caused the same to be deposited and laid up in all our churches
throughout the whole county, and the pastors to be commanded to teach thereafter,

The consistory was to admit no one who "will not be at one with the others" in this norm.

### 6.4 Archbishopric of Magdeburg: the visitation question (1583)

The archbishopric's visitation instruction of 1583 calls the confessions "the right *corpus
doctrinae*", and makes the FC rest on them. **Archbishopric of Magdeburg, *Instruktion zur
Visitation*, 25 May 1583** (Sehling 2, p. 419):

<!-- doc 1256 -->
> Welche gemeine offentliche schriften und confessiones neben den prophetischen und
> apostolischen schriften und den dreien haupt symbolis das rechte corpus doctrinae, das ist
> summarischer inhalt, furbilt und richtschnur der reinen lehr sei, ob er seine lehr in allen
> seinen offentlichen predigten, auch in bericht bei den schwachgleubigen und kranken, vormuge
> prophetischer und apostolischer schriften, den dreien haupt symbolis, der augspurgischen
> unvorenderten confession, welche Carolo quinto anno etc. 30 ubergeben, derselben apologia,
> schmalkaldischen artikeln, grossen und kleinen catechismos Lutheri, darauf sich die anno etc.
> 80 publicirte formula concordiae grundet, fur sich selbst halte und gleube und seiner
> bevohlenen kirchen vortrage und lehre.

Which common public writings and confessions, beside the prophetic and apostolic Scriptures and
the three chief creeds, are the right *corpus doctrinae*, that is, the summary content, pattern
and rule of pure doctrine. Whether he himself holdeth and believeth, and propoundeth and
teacheth to the church committed to him, his doctrine in all his public sermons, and also in
instructing the weak in faith and the sick, according to the prophetic and apostolic Scriptures,
the three chief creeds, the unaltered Augsburg Confession which was delivered to Charles the
Fifth in the year 30, the Apology thereof, the Smalcald Articles, and Luther's Large and Small
Catechisms, upon which the Formula of Concord published in the year 80 is grounded.

### 6.5 Halberstadt: the Brunswick corpus (1588)

Halberstadt, ruled by the Wolfenbüttel dukes as administrators, takes over the *Corpus
doctrinae* printed in the Brunswick church order. **Bishopric of Halberstadt, *Instruktion für
die Visitation*, 8 August 1588** (Sehling 2, pp. 468–469):

<!-- doc 1259 -->
> darneben die biblia, augsburgische confession, derselben apologia, schmalkaldische articul,
> catechismos Lutheri, wie auch das corpus doctrinae in der braunschweigischen kirchen ordnung
> nicht allein fleissig zu lesen und in kopf zu fassen, sondern auch nach derselben richtschnur
> und regul ihre predigten anzustellen,

and besides, not only diligently to read and get into their heads the Bible, the Augsburg
Confession, the Apology thereof, the Smalcald Articles, Luther's catechisms, and also the
*corpus doctrinae* in the Brunswick church order, but also to order their sermons according to
the rule and line thereof.

### 6.6 Anhalt: the refusal

Anhalt never signed the FC. By Sehling's account, Prince Joachim Ernst "could not be moved" to
sign it. From 1578 Anhalt's ordinands were no longer sent to Wittenberg but ordained at Zerbst
(Sehling 2, pp. 525, 528–529). After his death the visitation instruction of 1588 still named a
norm without the FC. It dates the CA and the Apology "anno 30 und 33" and names the late Prince
Wolfgang's signature to them. **Anhalt, *Instructio commissariorum in potestate executionis der
vorgenommenen Visitation*, 21 September 1588**
(Sehling 2, p. 579, a page that bears the running head of the next order):

<!-- doc 1262 -->
> damit sie einige normam haben christlicher lehre und bekenntnüss, thun wir ihnen darzu
> folgende bücher benennen, als die heilige biblia des alten und neuen testaments, die drei
> haupt symbola, apostolicum, Nicaenum et Athanasianum, die augspurgische confession und
> derselben apologia, wie die anno 30 und 33 heiser Carln den fünften allerhochlöblichster,
> christlicher gedächtnüss übergeben und in offenen druck ausgangen, welche auch weiland der
> hochgeborne fürst herr Wolfgang, fürst zu Anhalt etc. hochlöblicher christseligster
> gedächtnüss etc. unser geliebter vater mit eigener hand neben andern chur und fürsten und
> ständen unterschrieben, die schmalkaldischen artikul von d. Martino Luthero anno 37
> verfertiget, und itzt gemeldes herren doctoris klein und grossen catechismos, welches sie pro
> confessione und norma sanctorum verborum halten und dabei in christlicher einfalt bleiben,

that they may have one norm of Christian doctrine and confession, we name to them for that
purpose the following books: namely the holy Bible of the Old and New Testaments; the three
chief creeds, the Apostolic, the Nicene and the Athanasian; the Augsburg Confession and the
Apology thereof, as they were delivered in the years 30 and 33 to the emperor Charles the Fifth
of most laudable Christian memory, and came forth in open print, which also the late high-born
prince, Lord Wolfgang, prince of Anhalt, etc., of most laudable and blessed Christian memory,
etc., subscribed with his own hand beside other electors, princes and estates; the Smalcald
Articles made by Dr Martin Luther in the year 37; and the said doctor's Small and Large
Catechisms; which they shall hold *pro confessione* and as the norm of sound words, and abide
therein in Christian simplicity.

The canon here is that of the 1570s, before the FC: CA, Apol, SA and the catechisms. It is held
"*pro confessione*", not signed.

## 7. Silesia, Prussia, the Polish cities and Pomerania

The eastern territories kept to the norms they had made before the FC. Brieg in Silesia still
named the *Corpus Philippicum* in 1592. Prussia had its own *Corpus doctrinae* of 1567. The
cities of Royal Prussia had a corpus confirmed by the Polish king, and Danzig a city formula,
the *Notel*. Pomerania had a Melanchthonian corpus of its own. None of the orders quoted here
makes the FC a condition of office, and Danzig forbade it to be "canonised".

### 7.1 Silesia: Brieg (1592)

The Brieg church order of 1592 names, beside the CA and Apology, Luther's writings, the *Corpus
doctrinae Philippi* and the Mecklenburg agenda. **Brieg, *Kirchenordnung für Brieg*, 1592**
(Sehling 3, pp. 444–445):

<!-- doc 1809 -->
> Die lehre betreffend: Soll diese rein ohne irrige meinung, wie sie in den prophetischen und
> apostolischen schriften und approbirten symbolis, deren grund und inhalt in der Augspurgischen
> confession und ihrer apologia, den scriptis Lutheri, corpore doctrinae Philippi, der
> Mecklenburg, agenda, und was hiemit stimmet, begriffen, geführet und wie viel der zeit
> gepflanzet werden,

Touching doctrine: this shall be carried on pure, without erroneous opinion, as it is
comprehended in the prophetic and apostolic Scriptures and the approved creeds, whose ground and
content [is set down] in the Augsburg Confession and its Apology, in the writings of Luther, the
*corpus doctrinae* of Philip, the Mecklenburg agenda, and whatsoever agreeth herewith; and [so
shall it be] planted, so far as the time [alloweth].

A later chapter of the same order, headed *De corpore doctrinae*, tells every preacher what to
read. The database files this page under the next territory, Jägerndorf, but it is the end of
the Brieg order (Sehling 3, p. 448):

<!-- doc 1810 -->
> De corpore doctrinae. Es soll ein jeder praedicant in städten und dörfern neben der Heil,
> bibel haben und fleissig lesen das Corpus doctrinae Philippi Melanchtonis, die postillen des
> herrn Lutheri, die Thomos Lutheri und Philippi, auch anderer gelehrter leute fügliche scripta.

*Of the corpus doctrinae.* Every preacher in towns and villages shall have, beside the holy
Bible, and diligently read, the *Corpus doctrinae* of Philip Melanchthon, the postils of Master
Luther, the Tomes of Luther and of Philip, and also the fitting writings of other learned men.

This is a book list, not a subscription.

### 7.2 Ducal Prussia: the *Corpus doctrinae Prutenicum* (1567) and the assessors' oath (1584)

Prussia's corpus of 1567 was made after the Osiandrian controversy. Pastors are to keep strictly
to it. **Prussia, *Von Erwehlung der beiden bischoff*, 1568** (Sehling 4, p. 111):

<!-- doc 1833 -->
> Darinnen sollen sie gestracks bleiben bei dem Corpore Doctrinae, wie dasselbige in diesem
> fürstenthumb anno 1567 aus den prophetischen und apostolischen schriften, den alten symbolis,
> auch der lehre Lutheri zusamen getragen und publiziret ist, und sollen sie sonderlich den
> heiligen catechismum Lutheri gar fleissig dem volk einbilden.

Therein they shall abide strictly by the *Corpus doctrinae*, as the same was gathered together
and published in this duchy in the year 1567 out of the prophetic and apostolic Scriptures, the
ancient creeds, and also the doctrine of Luther; and they shall especially imprint the holy
catechism of Luther very diligently upon the people.

The consistory order of 1584 binds its president and assessors by oath to "our church's
unanimous Christian confession", without naming its parts. **Prussia, *Consistorialordnung*,
1584** (Sehling 4, p. 125):

<!-- doc 1834 -->
> Eid des präsidenten und der assessorum. Ich schwöre, dass ich in allen und jeden dieses
> konsistorii vorfallenden sachen, beneben den andern hiezu verordneten assessorn, getreulich
> und fleissig nach meinem besten verstand und vermögen rathen, bedenken, suchen und befördern
> helfen wolle, was dem seelig machenden göttlichen worte, unserer kirche einhelligen
> christlichen bekenntniss, der ehrbarkeit, und beschriebenen rechten gemäss,

*Oath of the president and of the assessors.* I swear that in all and every matter coming before
this consistory I will, together with the other assessors appointed thereto, faithfully and
diligently, according to my best understanding and ability, counsel, consider, seek and help to
further that which is agreeable to the saving divine word, to our church's unanimous Christian
confession, to honesty, and to the written laws,

### 7.3 Thorn, Elbing and Danzig: a royal corpus and the city *Notel* (1575, 1586, 1612)

The three great cities of Royal Prussia needed the king's leave for their religion. Thorn's
church order names the corpus that King Sigismund Augustus had allowed them: Luther's two
catechisms and the CA, "given both to teachers and hearers". **Thorn, *Kirchenordnung für
Thorn*, 1575** (Sehling 4, p. 234):

<!-- doc 1844 -->
> Darumb gedachte obrickeit fur nützlich und nöthig geachtet, ihren kirchen ein gewisses corpus
> doctrinae, aus den schriften der h. propheten und aposteln zusammengetragen, furzulegen,
> nemlich den kleinen und grossen catechismum Lutheri und die confessionem Augustanam, welche
> auch Sigismundus Augustus, könig in Polen, hochlöbl. und seel. gedächtniss, allergnädigster
> herr den dreien städten Thorn, Elbing und Dantzig auf ihr demütiges und emsiges ansuchen
> allergnädigst erlaubet und zugelassen hat, darumb auch beide den lehrern und zuhörern
> mitgegeben worden, über gedachtem corpore doctrinae vest zuhalten und nichts daran zuendern,

Therefore the said magistrates have held it profitable and needful to set before their churches
a certain *corpus doctrinae*, gathered out of the writings of the holy prophets and apostles,
namely Luther's Small and Large Catechism and the Augsburg Confession; which also Sigismund
Augustus, king of Poland, of most laudable and blessed memory, [our] most gracious lord, most
graciously permitted and allowed to the three cities of Thorn, Elbing and Danzig upon their
humble and earnest suit; wherefore it hath also been given both to the teachers and to the
hearers, to hold fast by the said *corpus doctrinae* and to change nothing therein.

Danzig had besides its own formula, the *Notel* of 1562, signed by its clergy. By Sehling's
account it remained the city's confessional basis until 1793 (Sehling 4, p. 172). When the FC
came, the council forbade it. **Danzig, *Decret des Senats*, 23 October 1586**
(Sehling 4, p. 192):

<!-- doc 1844 -->
> sich keiner art und form zu reden gebrauchen sollen, die wieder die augspurgische confession
> und notel streiten. Des sollen auch weder publice noch privatim des Calvini, Zwinglii
> schriften, das concordienbuch und dergleichen buecher, von imands der herrn predicanten
> canonisiret oder imgeringsten defendiret, vielweniger irkeinerley streitschriften von ihnen in
> dieser stadt oder kirchen eingefueret werden.

[they] shall use no manner nor form of speech that striveth against the Augsburg Confession and
the *Notel*. Neither shall the writings of Calvin [or] Zwingli, the Book of Concord and the like
books be canonised, or in the least defended, by any of the lords preachers, either publicly or
privately; much less shall any controversial writings whatsoever be brought by them into this
city or church.

The Book of Concord is here set beside Calvin and Zwingli as a party book. In 1612 the norm was
still the CA of 1530 and the *Notel*, which each preacher had signed. **Danzig, *Kirchenordnung
für Danzig*, 1612** (Sehling 4, p. 210):

<!-- doc 1844 -->
> Dieser vier predicanten lehr und predigtampt belangend, seind sie semptlich pflichtschuldig,
> ihrer bestellung nach bei der einmal erkannten und bekanten wahrheit gotlichs worts nach
> inhalt. der prophetischen und apostolischen schriften, der einmal von den protestierenden
> stenden keiser Carole quinto anno 1530 zu Augspurg übergebenen confession, und der von ihnen
> selbst unterschriebenen der stadt formula concordiae oder notel zu lehren zu predigen und die
> hochwürdigen sacramenta zu administrieren.

As touching the doctrine and preaching office of these four preachers, they are all bound in
duty, according to their appointment, to teach, to preach and to administer the most worthy
sacraments according to the truth of God's word once known and confessed, after the content of
the prophetic and apostolic Scriptures, of the confession delivered once for all by the
protesting estates to the emperor Charles the Fifth at Augsburg in the year 1530, and of the
city's formula of concord or *Notel*, subscribed by themselves.

Here "formula concordiae" means Danzig's own *Notel*, not the FC of 1577.

### 7.4 Pomerania: the Melanchthonian corpus (1563/69) and the synodal handshake (1574)

The Pomeranian order refers its churches to Luther's writings and to Melanchthon's *Loci* and
Apology. It requires every parish to buy the book in which Melanchthon's works are printed
"together with the catechisms and confession of Luther", the *Corpus doctrinae Pomeranicum*.
**Pomerania, *Kerckenordeninge*, 1569** (Sehling 4, p. 382):

<!-- doc 1862 -->
> Dise unde andere artikel der christlicken lere schölen in den kercken unser lande rein unde
> vlitich geleret unde mer erkleret werden, in dem vorstande, darin se hen unde wedder van
> doctore Martino Luthero in sinen schritten, unde van Philippo Melanthone in den locis
> communibus unde in der apologien der augsborgischen confession utgelecht unde vorvatet sint,
> darup de pamerischen kercken sick refereren unde to merer declaration erbeden. Unde dewile
> dorch den ehrwerdigen Philippum Melanthonem de vörnemesten artickel unser waren christliken
> lere des evangelii mit raht Martini Lutheri seligen unde anderer vörnemen theologen in etlicke
> böker ganz richtich, godtsalich unde christlick begrepen unde vorvatet, ock allrede tovörn in
> ein boeck samt den catechismis unde confession Lutheri, up unsen bevel unde vorordeninge
> tosamende gedrücket sind, willen, ordenen unde gebeden wi hir mit, dat in einer iedern
> parkercken in unsen landen, in steden effte dörpern, vörgedachte böke, […] schölen geköfft
> unde vorwaret unde van den kerckendenern vlitich gelesen werden.

These and other articles of Christian doctrine shall be purely and diligently taught in the
churches of our lands, and further explained, in the sense in which they are expounded and set
down here and there by Doctor Martin Luther in his writings, and by Philip Melanchthon in the
*Loci communes* and in the Apology of the Augsburg Confession; whereunto the Pomeranian churches
refer themselves, and offer [themselves] to further declaration. And forasmuch as the chief
articles of our true Christian doctrine of the gospel have been comprehended and set down by the
reverend Philip Melanchthon, with the counsel of Martin Luther of blessed memory and other chief
theologians, in certain books, very rightly, godly and Christianly, and have already before been
printed together into one book, together with the catechisms and confession of Luther, at our
command and ordinance: we will, ordain and command herewith that in every parish church in our
lands, in towns or villages, the aforesaid books […] shall be bought and kept, and diligently
read by the ministers of the church.

The personal bond came at the synod, by a handshake modelled on Galatians 2:9. The minister
promises obedience to the superintendent and synod "in retaining the godly consensus of heavenly
doctrine". No confession is named. **Pomerania, *Statuta synodica*, 1574** (Sehling 4, p. 492):

<!-- doc 1868 -->
> Formula, Obedientiae et fidelitatis pastorum et omnium ministrorum ecclesiae, praestanda in
> synodo, data dextera societatis, qua et apostoli inter se sunt usi. Gal. 2. vers. 9. Ego N. N.
> promitto, per dexteram societatis, obedientiam et fidelitatem reverendo superintendenti
> nostro, et venerabili synodo N. in retinendo pio consensu doctrinae coelestis, in toto
> ministerio rite peragendo, in regendis moribus vitae, in tuenda conjunctione et concordia, et
> in rebus omnibus, juxta statuta synodi, ordinationem ecclesiasticam et agendam. Quae omnia
> fideliter bona […] conscientia praestabo, pro posse et nosse, et falsus frater non ero,

*Form of the obedience and fidelity of the pastors and all ministers of the church, to be
performed in the synod, the right hand of fellowship being given, which the apostles also used
among themselves, Gal. 2, verse 9.* I, N. N., promise by the right hand of fellowship obedience
and fidelity to our reverend superintendent and to the venerable synod of N., in retaining the
godly consensus of heavenly doctrine, in rightly performing the whole ministry, in governing the
manners of life, in maintaining union and concord, and in all things according to the statutes
of the synod, the church order and the agenda. All which I will perform faithfully with a good
[…] conscience, as far as I am able and know, and will not be a false brother.

Pomerania never adopted the FC as such. By Sehling's account a ducal mandate of 10 May 1593
accepted only "the synodal decision in three chief points out of the *Formula Concordiae*
against the Calvinists" (Sehling 4, p. 321).

## 8. Mecklenburg, Kurland, Lauenburg and Schleswig-Holstein

The Baltic territories show the widest spread of all:

- **Mecklenburg** signed the FC in 1577.
- **Kurland** bound its preachers to the Wittenberg *Corpus Philippicum* in 1570.
- **Lauenburg** (1585) named the FC as an alternative ("or").
- **Gottorp**, under the Danish king's lead, rejected the Concord. It bound its preachers by
  oath to the CA, Apology, SA and catechisms in 1574, and then in 1607 to Scripture alone.

### 8.1 Mecklenburg and Rostock (1552, 1573)

Mecklenburg's church order of 1552 was the work of Melanchthon's circle, and its examination was
Melanchthon's *Examen ordinandorum*. The ordinand's promise names no book. He promises to remain
in the doctrine he has just confessed in his examination. **Mecklenburg, *Kirchenordnung*,
1552** (Sehling 5, p. 192):

<!-- doc 1922 -->
> Nach dieser vermanung sol eine christliche zusage von im genomen werden. Nemlich, das er in
> diesem heiligen amt, mit gottes furcht, glauben und anrufung zu gott dienen wolle, und wolle
> züchtiglich leben und vleissig lernen. Item, das er in der reinen christlichen lere, die er in
> dieser verhör bekant hat, und die durch gottes gnad in diesen kirchen eintrechtiglich
> geprediget wird, mit gottes hülf bestendig bleiben wolle, und wolle im amt treue und vleissig
> sein. Darauf spricht der ordinandus, er wolle durch gottes gnad dieses treulich thun und
> halten.

After this admonition a Christian promise shall be taken of him: namely, that he will serve God
in this holy office with the fear of God, with faith and calling upon God, and will live soberly
and learn diligently. Item, that he will by God's help abide steadfastly in the pure Christian
doctrine which he hath confessed in this hearing, and which by God's grace is preached with one
accord in these churches, and will be faithful and diligent in the office. Whereupon the
ordinand saith that by God's grace he will faithfully do and keep this.

Twenty years later the Rostock ministerium wrote a full list into its statutes. It includes the
Mecklenburg church order, the Lüneburg Articles of 1561 against the Philippists, and the FC,
"which they approved by their subscription in 1577". **Rostock, *Statuta et decreta*, 1573**
(Sehling 5, p. 285):

<!-- doc 1934 -->
> I. Ut amplectatur sanam incorruptamque doctrinam caelestem, traditam in scriptis prophetarum
> apostolorum et evangelistarum, in ea sententia quae expressa est in symbolis, in confessione
> Augustae exhibita Carolo V. imperatori anno M. D. XXX. ejusque apologia, in Smalcaldicis
> articulis, in libro legum ceremonialium hujus ducatus, ut vocant, megapolensis, in catechismis
> majori et minori, caeterisque divini Lutheri scriptis omnibus, in articulis Luneburgensibus et
> in libro concordiae seu consensionis inter docentes in ecclesiis doctrinam caelestem a doct.
> Martino Luthero instauratam amplectentibus, quem anno M. D. LXXVII. (sed leges conditae an.
> 73) subscriptione sua comprobarunt.

I. That he embrace the sound and uncorrupt heavenly doctrine delivered in the writings of the
prophets, apostles and evangelists, in that sense which is expressed in the creeds; in the
confession exhibited at Augsburg to the emperor Charles V in the year 1530, and the apology
thereof; in the Smalcald Articles; in the book of the ceremonial laws of this duchy, the
Mecklenburg [book], as they call it; in the Large and Small Catechisms, and all the other
writings of the divine Luther; in the Lüneburg Articles; and in the book of concord or agreement
among those who teach in the churches and embrace the heavenly doctrine restored by Doctor
Martin Luther, which in the year 1577 they approved by their subscription (but the laws were
made in the year 73).

The editor's parenthesis shows that the FC was added to a statute of 1573. By Sehling's account,
444 Mecklenburg ministers signed the FC in 1577 (Sehling 5, pp. 142–143).

### 8.2 Kurland: the Wittenberg *Corpus doctrinae* (1570)

Kurland's church order of 1570 binds its new preachers to the CA and Apology, the creeds, the
"oldest, unfalsified councils", the SA and "the whole Wittenberg *corpus doctrinae*" of 1560.
**Kurland, *Kurländische Kirchenordnung*, 1570** (Sehling 5, p. 70):

<!-- doc 1907 -->
> Wenn nun die neuen prediger also introducirt und zum lehramte bestetiget worden seind, sollen
> sie sich höchstes vermügens befleissigen, das allein seligmachende wort gottes, biblischer,
> das ist prophetischer und apostolischer schrift, den rechten verstand des gesetzes und
> evangelii, nach der Augspurgischen confession und apologia, woran wir nicht zweifeln, sonder
> die gewissheit haben, das sie die rechte, einige und warhaftige lehre der prophetischen und
> apostolischen zeugnusse sei, der dreien Symbolen, eltisten, unvorfelschten concilien,
> Schmalkaldischen artickeln und dem ganzen Wittenbergischen corpore doctrinae von dem seligen
> herrn Philippo Melanthone anno 1560 vice cygneae cantionis in den druck verfertigt, ihrer
> gemeine, vermüge derselbigen richtigen lehr auf Christi bevelich ohne scheu fürhalten,

When now the new preachers have been thus introduced and confirmed in the office of teaching,
they shall endeavour to the utmost of their power to set before their congregation without fear,
at Christ's command and according to the right doctrine thereof, the only saving word of God of
the Biblical, that is, of the prophetic and apostolic Scripture, [and] the right understanding
of the law and the gospel; according to the Augsburg Confession and Apology (whereof we doubt
not, but have the certainty, that it is the right, only and true doctrine of the prophetic and
apostolic testimony), the three creeds, the oldest unfalsified councils, the Smalcald Articles,
and the whole Wittenberg *corpus doctrinae* made ready for the press by the late Master Philip
Melanchthon in the year 1560, in the stead of a swan-song.

The Philippist corpus stands here beside the Smalcald Articles, a combination that the FC party
would not allow after 1574.

### 8.3 Lauenburg: the FC as an alternative (1585)

Lauenburg's church order of 1585 makes each pastor promise to teach according to the creeds, the
"ancient and unaltered" CA, the Apology, the SA, Luther's writings and catechisms, "or the
*formula concordiae*, touching the points of doctrine in dispute". **Lauenburg,
*Kirchenordnung*, 1585** (Sehling 5, p. 402):

<!-- doc 1953 -->
> Zum andern, dass er nichts, denn was gott in seinem heiligen beschriebenen worte der bibel
> offenbart hat, lehren und predigen solle, nach erklerunge der dreien vorgenanten symbolorum
> ecclesiae, der uhralten und ungeanderten Augspurgischen confession, apologia, schmalcaldischen
> artikeln, der schriften D. Martini Lutheri und cathechismi gross und kleins, oder formulae
> concordiae, belangend die streitige lehr puncta, und derwegen diese bücher fleissigen und alle
> tage durchsehe, evolvire, und ihm bekandt mache,

Secondly, that he shall teach and preach nothing but what God hath revealed in his holy written
word of the Bible, according to the explanation of the three aforenamed creeds of the church,
the ancient and unaltered Augsburg Confession, the Apology, the Smalcald Articles, the writings
of Dr Martin Luther, and the Large and Small Catechism, or the *formula concordiae* as touching
the points of doctrine in dispute; and that he therefore diligently look through these books
every day, turn them over, and make himself acquainted with them.

The FC is not a further book in a list. It is the authorised explanation of the older books
where they are disputed.

### 8.4 Schleswig-Holstein (Gottorp): an oath instead of the Concord (1574, 1607)

The Danish king and the dukes in Schleswig-Holstein would not take the Torgau Book or the Bergen
Book. By Sehling's account, the Gottorp theologians asked instead for a new edition of the
*Corpus Philippicum*, with the SA and Luther's catechisms added (Sehling 23, p. 298). Instead of
the Concord, Superintendent Paul von Eitzen drew up a preacher's oath against the Calvinists,
centred on the Lord's Supper (Sehling 23, p. 309). **Schleswig-Holstein-Gottorp, *Predigereid*,
1574** (Sehling 23, p. 365; Low German):

<!-- doc 1619 -->
> gelieck wo dusse lehre vam hochwerdigen sacrament is erkleret in der Augsborgischen
> Confession, Apologia, Schmalkaldischen Artickelen und beiden Catechismis des hilligen lehrers
> und vaders Martini Lutheri, welchers böker, schriffte und bekentnisse ick ock in dussem
> artickel holde und gelöve recht und warhafftig. Und by dussem minem ede vorplichte my, dorch
> de hulpe und gnade Gottes, desulve wäre, einfoldige lehre vam hilligen aventmahl miner
> befahlen gemeine getruwliken, unvorfelschet und unverändert vorthoholden und tho leren beth an
> minen ende.

even as this doctrine of the most worthy sacrament is declared in the Augsburg Confession, the
Apology, the Smalcald Articles and both Catechisms of the holy teacher and father Martin Luther;
which books, writings and confessions I also hold and believe in this article to be right and
true. And by this my oath I bind myself, through the help and grace of God, faithfully to set
forth and to teach the same true and simple doctrine of the holy Supper to the congregation
committed to me, unfalsified and unaltered, until my end.

The same oath was used in the duke's pledged district of Peine, in the bishopric of Hildesheim
(§10.1).

In 1607 Duke Johann Adolf abolished the oath and put a new one in its place.
**Schleswig-Holstein-Gottorp, *Mitteilung über den Erlaß eines neuen Predigereides*, 4 January
1607** (Sehling 23, p. 415):

<!-- doc 1628 -->
> Nachdem wir aus hochwichtigen, bewegenden uhrsachen den bißhero in gebrauch gehaltenen
> priester eydt verendern lassen, wie ihr aus anliegender formul zu vornehmen, also, daß nun
> hinfortan nach dieser neuen eydt formul die ordinanden den priesterlichen eidt schwehren und
> der vorige eyd cassiret unnd auffgehoben seyn soll,

Forasmuch as we, for very weighty and moving causes, have caused the priest's oath hitherto held
in use to be altered, as ye may perceive from the form enclosed, so that henceforth the
ordinands shall swear the priestly oath after this new form of oath, and the former oath shall
be cassated and abolished,

The new oath names no confession at all, only "the prophetic and apostolic Scriptures".
**Schleswig-Holstein-Gottorp, *Neuer Predigereid*, 4 January 1607** (Sehling 23, p. 416):

<!-- doc 1628 -->
> Ich, N.N., schwere zu Gott, daß ich mein anbefohlen Lehrambt mit hbgsten Trewen und
> gepuerenten Eiver ahne fleischliche Adfecten fhuren, das lauter, reine Wortt Gottes
> unvervelscht, wie solches in den Prophetischen unnd Apostolischen Schrifften begriffen, ohne
> menschliche, verfuhrische Anhenge und Opinion lehren unnd predigen,

I, N.N., swear to God that I will conduct the office of teaching committed to me with the
highest faithfulness and due zeal, without carnal affections; [and] teach and preach the clear,
pure word of God unfalsified, as the same is contained in the prophetic and apostolic
Scriptures, without human, seductive additions and opinions;

The change from 1574 to 1607 is the plainest case in the corpus of a confessional oath being
withdrawn. A Lutheran oath against the Calvinists gave way to a Scripture-only oath under a
prince of Reformed sympathies.

## 9. Braunschweig-Wolfenbüttel, Lüneburg, Calenberg-Göttingen, Hoya and Goslar

Lower Saxony was the home ground of Chemnitz and Mörlin. It produced the most precise statements
of the norm: the CA understood "as explained" by the Apology, the SA and Luther's catechism. It
also produced the densest set of local corpora:

- the Lüneburg Articles of 1561;
- the Brunswick city corpus of 1571;
- the Lower Saxon Confession of 1571;
- the Wolfenbüttel *Corpus Julium* and the Lüneburg *Corpus Wilhelminum*, both of 1576.

Subscription to the corpus was the condition of admission in the cities as well as in the
duchies.

### 9.1 Braunschweig-Wolfenbüttel: the CA "in the sense" of the Apology, the SA and the catechism (1569)

Duke Julius's church order of 1569 defines the *corpus doctrinae* and adds a "declaration" to
shut out those who shelter under the name of the CA. **Braunschweig-Wolfenbüttel,
*Kirchenordnung* of Duke Julius, 1569** (Sehling 6/1, pp. 91–92):

<!-- doc 1974 -->
> So ist nun diß die summa, daß das corpus doctrinae, die form und das fürbild der reinen lehr,
> wie dieselbig in den kirchen dieses fürstenthumbs hinfuro geprediget und gelehret soll werden,
> sein soll die heilige, prophetische und apostolische schrift in dem einfeltigen, wahren,
> eigentlichen, gesunden verstand, wie die schrift sich selbs erkleret, und wie derselbig
> verstand auß und nach Gottes wort kurzlich zusammengezogen ist in den alten, bewehreten
> symbolis apostolico, nicaeno, athanasiano und in den artickeln der augspürgischen confession.
> Weil aber auch leider in diesen letzten betrübten zeiten der welt etliche rotten und sekten
> ihre corruptelas unter dem nahmen der augspürgischen confession zu bedecken und zu beschönen
> sich understehen […] muß diese declaration deutlich dabey gesetzt werden, das wir die
> augspürgische confession annhemen, verstehen und behalten in dem verstand, wie sie in der
> erfolgten und angeheften apologia, nachmals in den schmalkaldischen artickeln und entlich im
> catechismo und andern schriften Lutheri auß Gottes wort expliciert und verkleret worden ist.

This then is the sum: that the *corpus doctrinae*, the form and pattern of pure doctrine, as the
same shall henceforth be preached and taught in the churches of this principality, shall be the
holy, prophetic and apostolic Scripture, in the simple, true, proper and sound sense, as
Scripture expoundeth itself, and as the same sense is briefly drawn together out of and
according to God's word in the ancient approved creeds, the Apostolic, the Nicene and the
Athanasian, and in the articles of the Augsburg Confession. But forasmuch as, alas, in these
last troubled times of the world certain factions and sects take upon them to cover and colour
their corruptions under the name of the Augsburg Confession […], this declaration must be
plainly set thereby: that we accept, understand and hold the Augsburg Confession in the sense in
which it hath been expounded and declared out of God's word in the Apology that followed and is
annexed thereto, afterwards in the Smalcald Articles, and lastly in the catechism and other
writings of Luther.

The editors' footnote at "the name of the Augsburg Confession" points to the *variata* of 1540.
This formula, the CA read through the Apology, the SA and the catechism, was taken up almost
word for word in Verden in 1606 (§10.2). It is also the rule by which the FC itself reads the
CA.

### 9.2 Brunswick city: articles to be subscribed (1571)

The city of Brunswick's ministerium put to every new minister a set of Latin articles for
subscription. They name the city's *corpus doctrinae* and condemn whatever the Lüneburg Articles
condemn. **Brunswick, *Articuli, qui subscribendi proponuntur illis qui ad ministerium in hac
ecclesia recipiuntur*, 1571** (Sehling 6/1, p. 471):

<!-- doc 1987 -->
> Articuli, qui subscribendi proponuntur illis qui ad ministerium in hac ecclesia recipiuntur.
> […] Formam purae doctrinae et sanorum verborum sicut in scriptis propheticis et apostolicis et
> probatis symbolis explicata et in confessione augustana et apologia eiusdem sicut anno 1530
> exhibita et anno 1531 edita sunt atque in articulis schmalcaldicis et catechismis Lutheri
> declarata sunt fideliter et constanter nobiscum profiteatur et retineat. Hoc enim est corpus
> doctrinae huius ecclesiae. § 2. Corruptelas cum hoc corpore doctrinae pugnantes sive olim
> damnatae sive recens exortae sint sive imposterum oriantur non amplectatur nec spargat sed
> fugiat et damnet praecipue vero quae in articulis lyneburgicis damnatae sunt.

Articles which are set forth to be subscribed by those who are received into the ministry in
this church. […] [§ 1.] That he faithfully and constantly profess and retain with us the form of
pure doctrine and of sound words, as it is set forth in the prophetic and apostolic writings and
the approved creeds, and in the Augsburg Confession and the Apology thereof, as they were
exhibited in the year 1530 and published in the year 1531, and as it is declared in the Smalcald
Articles and Luther's catechisms. For this is the *corpus doctrinae* of this church. § 2. That
he embrace not nor spread the corruptions that fight with this *corpus doctrinae*, whether they
were condemned of old or have lately arisen or shall arise hereafter, but flee and condemn them;
and especially those that are condemned in the Lüneburg Articles.

The city's order for calling preachers, of the same year, sets the subscription in the
procedure. The new man subscribes and vows before he is admitted to the "colloquy", the city's
assembly of ministers. **Brunswick, *Vokationsordnung für die Prediger*, 1571**
(Sehling 6/1, p. 457):

<!-- doc 1985 -->
> Zum siebenden: Wen nach diesem allen die vocatio volzogen, so soll die angenommen person durch
> einen erbaren rath und kastenherren des weichbildes dem colloquio presentiert und darselbst
> mit ihme gehandelt werden, das ehr dem corpori doctrinae dieser loblichen kirchen
> unterschreibe und angelobe, das ehr sich der wolhergebrachten christlichen ordnunge des
> colloquii uhterwerfe, alsdan soli ehr vor ein membrum des colloquii auf und angenommen werden.

Seventhly: when after all this the call hath been completed, the person accepted shall be
presented to the colloquy by an honourable council and the treasurers of the ward, and there it
shall be dealt with him that he subscribe the *corpus doctrinae* of this laudable church, and
vow that he will submit himself to the well-established Christian order of the colloquy; and
then shall he be received and accepted as a member of the colloquy.

By Sehling's account, the city council later sided against the duke over the signing of the FC
(Sehling 6/1, p. 341).

### 9.3 Lüneburg: the duchy's confession (1564) and the city's corpus (1575)

The Lüneburg church order of 1564 states the duchy's doctrine as a confession of the prince, in
its preface. It names the creeds, Luther's catechism, and the CA and Apology of 1530.
**Principality of Lüneburg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1564** (Sehling 6/1, p. 534):

<!-- doc 2003 -->
> sondern wir bekennen, das wir die lere vorrecht halten, die in unserm fürstenthumb geprediget
> wird, die Gott selbs hat durch seinen Son Jhesum Christum geoffenbaret und die in der
> propheten und aposteln schrift gefasset in dem verstande, der in den symbolis apostolico,
> niceno und Athanasii, auch in D. Martini Lutheri seliger catechismo, auch in der confession
> und apologia, welche der römi. kei. maj. zu Augspurg anno etc. 1530 uberantwortet, verfasset
> und erholet ist. Und gedenken, bey solcher erkanten lere mit göttlicher hülf bestendig zu
> bleiben,

but we confess that we hold for right the doctrine that is preached in our principality, which
God himself hath revealed through his Son Jesus Christ, and which is contained in the writings
of the prophets and apostles, in the sense that is set down and repeated in the creeds, the
Apostolic, the Nicene and Athanasius's, and also in the catechism of Dr Martin Luther of blessed
memory, and also in the confession and apology which were delivered to the Roman imperial
majesty at Augsburg in the year 1530. And we purpose by God's help to abide steadfastly by such
acknowledged doctrine.

The city of Lüneburg had its own corpus. Besides the usual books it held the confession of
Lübeck, Hamburg and Lüneburg against the Interim of 1548. Each ordinand had to write his own
confession, which was examined against it. **Lüneburg city, *Kirchenordnung der Stadt Lüneburg*,
1575** (Sehling 6/1, p. 654):

<!-- doc 2017 -->
> so befhelt ehr ihnen ihre bekantnus von den vornembsten und nottwendigsten artickeln der
> kirchenlehre, die da mit den schriften der heiligen propheten, Christi und der apostolen mit
> den dreyen symbolis apostolico, niceno und Athanassi, mit der confession, so rö[mische]
> kay[serliche] ma[]este]tt Carolo V. anno etc. 30 auf dem reichstage zu Augspurg von etlichen
> des heiligen römischen reichs stenden ubergeben, sampt der apologia, so kurz darauf gestellet,
> mit den smalchaldischen artickeln, catechismis Lutheri und dieser dreyer erbaren stette
> Lubeck, Hamburg und Luneburg bekantnusse, so anno 1548 wider das interim im druck ausgangen,
> welche scripta vor das corpus oder fundamentum doctrinae huius ecclesiae gehalten werden,
> durchaus ubereinstimme, mit dem furderligsten schriftlich zu vorfassende und uberantwortende.

then he [the superintendent] commandeth them with all speed to set down in writing and deliver
their confession of the chiefest and most necessary articles of the church's doctrine, which
[must] agree throughout with the writings of the holy prophets, of Christ and of the apostles;
with the three creeds, the Apostolic, the Nicene and Athanasius's; with the confession which was
delivered in the year 30 by certain estates of the holy Roman Empire to the Roman imperial
majesty Charles V at the diet at Augsburg, together with the Apology that was set forth shortly
after; with the Smalcald Articles, Luther's catechisms, and the confession of these three
honourable cities, Lübeck, Hamburg and Lüneburg, which came forth in print in the year 1548
against the Interim; which writings are held for the corpus or foundation of the doctrine of
this church.

In 1576 the duchy received Chemnitz's *Corpus doctrinae Wilhelminum*. By Sehling's account it
was still signed with the church order of 1643 (Sehling 6/1, pp. 487–489).

### 9.4 Calenberg-Göttingen: the *Corpus Julium* (1601) and Göttingen's own order (1568)

When the Wolfenbüttel dukes inherited Calenberg-Göttingen, its four great towns came under their
corpus. A preacher presented for a town where the duke was patron had to subscribe the ducal
church order and the *Corpus doctrinae Julium*, and then the town's own church order.
**Calenberg-Göttingen, *Gandersheimer Landtagsabschied*, 1601** (Sehling 6/2, p. 894):

<!-- doc 2042 -->
> auch, wann die praesentirte person in lehr und leben genugsamb qualificirt befunden und die
> fürstliche kirchenordnunge und Gorpus doctrinae Julium unterschrieben, sol dieselbige person
> dem rath und ministerio alda zur probpredigt, und da die vocatio aus erheblichen ursachen
> nicht abgeschlagen wirdet, zugleich zur immission und subscription ihrer sonderbaren vor
> vielen jahren aufgerichteten kirchenordnunge,

and when the person presented hath been found sufficiently qualified in doctrine and life, and
hath subscribed the princely church order and the *Corpus doctrinae Julium*, the same person
shall be sent over to the council and ministry there for the trial sermon, and, if the call be
not refused for weighty causes, at the same time for induction and for subscription of their
particular church order set up many years ago.

Göttingen's own order had made the norm in 1568. It runs: the CA of 1530, the Apology, the SA,
Luther's catechism and "our old church order of the city of Göttingen, reviewed and approved by
Luther". **Göttingen, *Christliche und in Gottes wort … gegründte kirchenordnung der stadt
Göttingen*, 1568** (Sehling 6/2, p. 916):

<!-- doc 2044 -->
> daß man, wie in annemung des evangelii, anno 30 allhie auß Gottes genediger heymsuchung
> geschehen, auch forthin gedenke einhelliglich und einfeltiglich zu bleiben bey der rechten
> augspurgischen confession, wie dieselbige anno 30 der rhömischen keyserlichen majestat von
> protestierenden stenden uberantwortet, auch eiusdem apologia, schmalkaldischen artickeln,
> catechismo Lutheri und der alten von Luthero ubersehener und approbierter unser, der stadt
> Göttingen, kirchenordnung, wie dieselbige gründlichen genommen auß den schriften der propheten
> und aposteln und in Lutheri büchern weiter erkleret werden, und alles, das denselbigen
> entgegen, es habe namen, wie es wölle, es komme her, von wem es wölle, solle verworfen werden,

that, as was done here in the year 30 at the receiving of the gospel through God's gracious
visitation, so henceforth also [they] purpose to abide with one accord and in simplicity by the
right Augsburg Confession, as the same was delivered in the year 30 by the protesting estates to
the Roman imperial majesty, and by the Apology thereof, the Smalcald Articles, Luther's
catechism, and our old church order of the city of Göttingen, reviewed and approved by Luther,
as the same are grounded thoroughly on the writings of the prophets and apostles and further
explained in Luther's books; and that all that is contrary to the same, be its name what it
will, and come it from whom it will, shall be rejected.

### 9.5 Hoya: the FC in the church order (1581)

The county of Hoya's church order of 1581 sets the FC at the end of the list. It calls Luther's
catechisms "the laymen's Bible". **Hoya, *Kirchenordnung*, 1581** (Sehling 6/2, p. 1131):

<!-- doc 2064 -->
> das sie in allen ihren predigten und Lehren durchaus den prophetischen und apostolischen
> schriften altes und neues testaments, den dreyen allgemeinen heuptsymbolen, als dem
> apostolico, nicaeno und athanasiano, der reinen, ungeenderten augspurgischen confession, so
> keyser Carolo dem V. zu Augspurg anno 30 in der grossen reichsversamlung ubergeben, sampt
> derselben apologia und articulen zu Schmalkalden, anno 37 gestellet, und von den fürnembsten
> theologen damals unterschrieben worden, den catechismis, grossen und kleinen, des herrn D.
> Martini Lutheri, als der leyen bibel, darinnen alLes kurz begriffen, was in heiliger schrift
> weitleuftig gehandelt und einem fromen, gottseligen Christen zu seiner seligkeit zu wissen von
> nöten ist, und auch der formulae concordiae, das ist, der christlichen, wiederholten,
> einmütigen bekentnis der churfürsten, fürsten und stende augspurgischer confession, anno 80
> voilzogen und publiciert, sich sollen gleichförmig und einhellig gemeß verhaiten.

that in all their sermons and teachings they shall behave themselves throughout conformably and
with one accord according to the prophetic and apostolic writings of the Old and New Testaments;
the three universal chief creeds, namely the Apostolic, the Nicene and the Athanasian; the pure,
unaltered Augsburg Confession, which was delivered to the emperor Charles V at Augsburg in the
year 30 in the great diet of the empire, together with the Apology thereof, and the Articles set
forth at Smalcald in the year 37 and subscribed at that time by the chief theologians; the
catechisms, Large and Small, of Master Dr Martin Luther, as the laymen's Bible, wherein is
briefly comprehended all that is handled at large in holy Scripture, and is needful for a godly,
devout Christian to know for his salvation; and also the *formula concordiae*, that is, the
Christian, repeated, unanimous confession of the electors, princes and estates of the Augsburg
Confession, completed and published in the year 80.

The order goes on to threaten anyone found not to hold by "these writings, as the right rule …
the only touchstone".

### 9.6 Goslar: a city corpus signed "with our own hands" (1591/95)

The imperial city of Goslar adopted its corpus on 9 November 1591 and signed the list in 1595.
The list is notable for three things:

- it names the Apology "as it first came forth in print";
- it includes the Lower Saxon Confession of 1571 "in its right Saxon sense";
- it holds the FC "in its right Saxon sense" as signed by the council and ministry in 1577.

No minister or schoolmaster is to be admitted unless he has signed. **Goslar, *Verzeichnis der
in Goslar gültigen Lehrschriften*, 11 May 1595** (Sehling 7/2.2, pp. 339–340):

<!-- doc 2208 -->
> Wir, ein gantzes ministerium der kirchen und gemeinde Gottes der kayserlichen freyen reiches
> stadt Gosslar, thun kund und bekennen mit dieser unser eigen unterschrifft, das wir im jar
> nach Christi, unsers herrn, seligen geburt 1591, am 9. tage Novembris, in beysein unser
> christlichen obergkeit, […] diese nachfolgende bucher, schriffte und bekentnisse zu einem
> Corpore doctrinae auffund angenomen haben, […] Zum funfften bekennen wir uns auch zu der
> Apologia derselbigen Auspurgischen Confession, wie sie zum ersten mal im druck ist ausgangen.
> […] Zum siebenden bekennen wir uns auch zu der Sechsischen Kirchen Confession in irem rechten
> Sechsischen verstande, wie sie anno Christi 1571 einhelliglich mit der Sechsischen kirchen
> subscription und unterschrifft in den druck gegeben worden. […] Zum achten halten wir uns auch
> stet und feste an die Formulam concordiae unser evangelischen kirchen in irem rechten
> Sechsischen verstande, wie sie auff- und angenomen, auch eintregtiglich unterschrieben ist
> worden, insonderheit auch von einem ehrbaren rath und ministerio alhie zu Goslar anno Christi
> 1577. […] Es sol auch in kunfftiger zeit niemand zu einem ministro ecclesiae oder schueldiener
> auff- und angenomen oder darinnen geduldet werden, er habe den so wol alse wir diesem itzt
> genanten, unserm Corpori doctrinae sich mit eigener hand unterschrieben, […] Zu mehrer uhrkund
> der warheit haben wir uns mit eigenen hemden unterschrieben.

We, the whole ministry of the church and congregation of God of the imperial free city of
Goslar, do make known and confess with this our own subscription that in the year after the
blessed birth of Christ our Lord 1591, on the 9th day of November, in the presence of our
Christian magistrates […], we accepted and received these books, writings and confessions
following for a *corpus doctrinae*, […] Fifthly, we confess ourselves also to the Apology of the
same Augsburg Confession, as it came forth in print the first time. […] Seventhly, we confess
ourselves also to the Confession of the Saxon Churches in its right Saxon sense, as it was given
to the press in the year of Christ 1571 with one accord, with the subscription and signature of
the Saxon churches. […] Eighthly, we hold ourselves also steadfastly and firmly to the *Formula
concordiae* of our evangelical churches in its right Saxon sense, as it hath been received and
accepted, and also subscribed with one accord, and especially also by an honourable council and
ministry here at Goslar in the year of Christ 1577. […] Neither shall anyone in time to come be
received and accepted as a minister of the church or schoolmaster, or be suffered therein,
unless he, as well as we, have subscribed this our *corpus doctrinae* now named with his own
hand, […] For the greater witness of the truth we have subscribed with our own hands.

"*hemden*" ("shirts") is the OCR's reading of *henden*, "hands".

## 10. Hildesheim, Verden, Osnabrück, Oldenburg, Bremen, East Frisia and Soest

The north-west was confessionally divided. The Lutheran cities and counties (Hildesheim, Verden,
Osnabrück, Oldenburg, Soest) held to the FC, or reached it late. Bremen went over to the
Philippist and then the Reformed side. East Frisia settled its quarrel in 1599 by naming nothing
but "the Augsburg Confession".

### 10.1 Hildesheim: the Peine oath (1574/89) and the subscription of the city's officials (1593)

The ducal oath used in Gottorp (§8.4) was also used in Peine, a district of the bishopric of
Hildesheim held in pledge by the dukes of Schleswig-Holstein. It is printed here in High German,
sworn "by the holy name of God". The first two articles bind to Scripture and the creeds and are
aimed at the Calvinist Christology. **Peine, *Eid und obligation der prediger im herzogtumb
Schleswiegk wie auch in ihren fürstl. gn. pfandgericht und ampt Peina*, [1574/89]**
(Sehling 7/2.1, p. 787):

<!-- doc 2130 -->
> 1. Ich N. prediger des h. evangelii, ordentlicherweise durch den willen Gottes geeschet und
> berufen, schwere bei dem h. namen Gotteß und meines erlösers Jesu Christi, daß ich die
> göttliche, biblische schrift in allen punkten, artikeln und worten halte und gläube warhaftig.
> 2. Zum anderen, daß ich auch daß h. apostolische symbolum und die anderen waren symbola, so
> von der h. allgemeinen christl. kirchen seind approbiret und angenomen, halte und geläube
> warhaftig in allen artikeln unseres christlichen glaubenß nach dem richtschnur göttliches
> wortß und also auch in dem artikel von der unzertrenlichen vereinigung der zweien naturen,
> göttlicher und menschlicher, in der einigen ungeteileten person Christi, welche unzertrenliche
> vereinigung keine zeit noch stunde in ewigkeit kan auflosen oder teilen.

1. I, N., preacher of the holy gospel, orderly called and summoned by the will of God, swear by
   the holy name of God and of my Redeemer Jesus Christ that I hold and believe the divine,
   Biblical Scripture to be true in all points, articles and words. 2. Secondly, that I also
   hold and believe the holy Apostolic Creed and the other true creeds, which have been approved
   and received by the holy universal Christian church, to be true in all articles of our
   Christian faith according to the rule of God's word; and so also in the article of the
   inseparable union of the two natures, divine and human, in the one undivided person of
   Christ, which inseparable union no time nor hour can dissolve or divide in eternity.

Its third and fourth articles go on to the CA with the Apology, the SA and the catechisms, and
to the Lord's Supper "as declared in the Augsburg Confession, Apology, Smalcald Articles and
both Catechisms" (Sehling 7/2.1, p. 788).

The city of Hildesheim had signed the FC on 10 April 1580 (Sehling's apparatus, Sehling 7/2.1,
p. 909). In 1593 its government extended the subscription to the city's lay officials. The
council's decision is known only from Sehling's introduction, which quotes it from the council's
records. **Hildesheim, council decision on the subscription of the *Formula Concordiae*, 30
August 1593, quoted in Sehling's introduction** (Sehling 7/2.1, p. 817):

<!-- doc 2133 -->
> Als am 18. Junii neigst verschienen die lobliche sambtregierunge dero stadt Hildesheim sich
> einhellich beraten, das der herr superintendens, die hern burgermeister, syndici, physici,
> secretarien, wie auch alle prediger und schuldiener beider stette die Formulam Concordiae und
> deren Apologiam mit ihrer subscription corroboriren solten, so ist demnach sollicher
> radtschlag erneuert und nochmalig von der loblichen regierunge geschlossen, das von ihnen und
> ihren successorn allermassen, wie der her superintendens den anfank gemacht, ein jeder ohne
> allen anhank zu sollicher Formula Concordiae und deren Apologia mit eigner hand sich bekennen
> und dieselbe unterschreiben solle. Actum den 30. Augusti anno etc. 93.

Whereas on the 18th of June last past the laudable joint government of the city of Hildesheim
took counsel with one accord that the lord superintendent, the lords burgomasters, the syndics,
the physicians, the secretaries, and also all preachers and schoolmasters of both towns should
confirm the *Formula Concordiae* and the Apology thereof with their subscription; therefore the
said resolution hath been renewed and once more concluded by the laudable government: that by
them and their successors, every one without any reservation, even as the lord superintendent
hath made the beginning, shall confess himself to the said *Formula Concordiae* and the Apology
thereof with his own hand, and subscribe the same. Done the 30th of August in the year, etc.,
93.

The "Apology" is the Erfurt Apology of the BoC (1582). Sehling adds that the last signature
under the Hildesheim Concord dates from 1824. The city's school order binds every teacher in the
same way. **Hildesheim, *Schulordnung*, 1574/1618** (Sehling 7/2.1, p. 909):

<!-- doc 2138 -->
> Und weil zu dem ende die Formula Concordiae zusampt derselben Apologij bei unß angenommen, so
> sollen alle unser collegen nicht allein zur subscription solcher bucher verknupft und
> verbun[den]

And forasmuch as to that end the *Formula Concordiae*, together with the Apology thereof, hath

### 10.2 Verden: the CA "in the sense" of the Apology, the SA and the catechisms (1606)

The Verden church order of 1606 takes over the Wolfenbüttel "declaration" of 1569 (§9.1) almost
word for word. **Bishopric of Verden, *Kirchenordnung*, 1606** (Sehling 7/1, p. 149):

<!-- doc 2088 -->
> so soll und muß dabey die declaration in acht genomen werden, das wir die Augßburgische
> Confession und derselben lehr und inhalt annehmen und behalten in dem verstand, wie sie in der
> darauf erfolgeten Apologia und dan ferner in den Smalcaldischen Articulen und endlich im

in the Large and Small Catechism of Luther, and the other books and writings of this dear and

### 10.3 Osnabrück: the Wittenberg Concord and the Book of Concord (1610)

The city of Osnabrück's ordinance for its superintendent and preachers names, between the
Apology and the SA, "the *Formula Concordiae* set up in the year 1536", that is, the Wittenberg
Concord on the Supper. It ends with the Book of Concord. **Osnabrück, *Superintendenten- und
Predigerordnung*, 1610** (Sehling 7/1, pp. 296–297):

<!-- doc 2102 -->
> Daß sie nemblich und furirst in ihren pfarkirchen reyne und gesunde lehre führen nach dem
> corpore doctrinae alter, unveranderter Außpurgischer Confession, wie die der römischen
> kay[serlich]en may[este]tt Carolo quinto, hochsähligsten angedenkens, ufm reichstage zu
> Außpurgh anno tausentfunfhundertunddreißig uberreicht worden, dazu wir und unser gemaind unß
> bekennen, in dem verstand, wie sie in der darauf erfolgten Apologia, item Formula Concordiae,
> anno 1536 aufgerichtet, wie auch denn Smalkaldischen Articulen und im Großen und Kleinen
> Catechismo Lutheri, dan auch in Libro Concordiae oder christlichen wiederholeten einmutigen
> bekentnuß der weltlichen chur-, fursten und stette außgelägt und erkleret worden.

That namely and first of all they conduct pure and sound doctrine in their parish churches
according to the *corpus doctrinae* of the old, unaltered Augsburg Confession, as it was
delivered at the diet at Augsburg in the year fifteen hundred and thirty to the Roman imperial
majesty Charles the Fifth, of most blessed memory, whereunto we and our congregation confess
ourselves; in the sense in which it hath been expounded and explained in the Apology that
followed thereon, item [in] the *Formula Concordiae* set up in the year 1536, as also in the
Smalcald Articles and in Luther's Large and Small Catechism, and also in the Book of Concord, or

The confession here is made by the council ("we and our congregation"). The duty is laid on the
preachers.

### 10.4 Oldenburg: the *Corpus Philippicum* (1573), then the FC (1586–1609)

Oldenburg's church order of 1573 adds to the Lutheran confessions the books "inserted and
incorporated" in Melanchthon's *Corpus doctrinae*. **Oldenburg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1573**
(Sehling 7/2.1, pp. 987–988):

<!-- doc 2147 -->
> Die ganze christliche lere ist und heist gesetz und evangelium, vorfasset in den biblischen,
> prophetischen und apostolischen schriften, die wir nennen das Alte und Neue Testament, derer
> kurze summa und begriff stehet in den dreien symbolis: Apostolico, Niceno et Athanasiano, und
> in den artikeln der Augspurgischen Confession und derselben Apologia, Smalcaldischen Artikeln,
> Catechismus Lutheri und darauf erfolgten und angeheften büchern, so dem Corpori Doctrinae, vom
> herrn Philippo seligen geschrieben, inseriert und einverleibet seind, als da sind die
> repetition der Augsspurgischen Confession, Loci communes, Examen der ordinanden und
> wideriegung der papistischen bairischen artikel. Diese lehr und bücher sollen die pastores
> selbst wissen und haben und nach denselben die christliche lere dem volk verstendlich,
> ordentlich und ungefelscht fürtragen

The whole Christian doctrine is and is called law and gospel, set down in the Biblical,
prophetic and apostolic writings which we call the Old and New Testament, whereof the short sum
and compass standeth in the three creeds, the Apostolic, the Nicene and the Athanasian; and in
the articles of the Augsburg Confession and the Apology thereof, the Smalcald Articles, Luther's
catechism, and the books following thereon and annexed, which are inserted and incorporated in
the *Corpus doctrinae* written by Master Philip of blessed memory, such as are the repetition of
the Augsburg Confession, the *Loci communes*, the Examination of the Ordinands, and the
refutation of the papistical Bavarian articles. This doctrine and these books shall the pastors
themselves know and have, and according to them set forth the Christian doctrine to the people
understandably, orderly and unfalsified.

The FC came in by way of the *Bestallungen*, the letters of appointment. Sehling's apparatus
quotes them:

- 1574 (Hamelmann): Scripture, the CA, the catechism and the church order.
- 1586 (Johannes Judex): adds the FC.
- 1608 (Superintendent Gottfried Schlüter): the full later norm, with a clause against the
  Calvinists. **Oldenburg, *Bestallung* of Superintendent Gottfried Schlüter, 1608, quoted in
  Sehling's apparatus** (Sehling 7/2.1, p. 964):

<!-- doc 2142 -->
> Gottes heiliges seligmachentes wort nach den biblischen schrieften Alten und Neuen Testaments,
> darauf gegrundten, unverfalschten im jahr 1530 auf dem großen reichstage zue Auspurgh kaiser
> Carln dem funften uberreichten und der Formulae Concordiae einverleibten Auspurgischen
> Confession, denen darauf erfolgten Schmalkaldischen Articuln, dem Großen und Kleinen
> Catechismo Lutheri, der Formulae Concordiae und unßer kirchenordnung rein und lauter ohne
> einige corruptelen lehren,

[that he shall] teach God's holy saving word purely and clearly, without any corruptions,
according to the Biblical writings of the Old and New Testaments, the Augsburg Confession
grounded thereon, unfalsified, delivered in the year 1530 at the great diet at Augsburg to the
emperor Charles the Fifth and incorporated in the *Formula Concordiae*, the Smalcald Articles
that followed thereon, Luther's Large and Small Catechism, the *Formula Concordiae*, and our
church order,

The phrase "the CA … incorporated in the FC" identifies the *invariata* by the text printed in
the BoC. The visitation articles of 1609 ask each pastor to confess the FC and the church order
and to "condemn the Calvinist doctrine of election, of the person of the Lord Christ and of the
most worthy sacraments" (Sehling 7/2.1, p. 1175).

### 10.5 Bremen: the *Corpus Philippicum* and the road to the Reformed (1570–1575)

The Bremen texts of these years are mediating (Philippist) orders; the city's Reformed orders begin in 1582.


Bremen's ministerium (mediating) agreed in 1572 on a "simple confession", by which all future
ministers were to be bound. It names Scripture, the creeds, the CA and the *Corpus doctrinae* of
Melanchthon, "which the Electoral Saxon and other churches have received". **Bremen, *Einigung
des geistlichen Ministeriums über die in Bremen gültige Lehre*, 1572** (mediating; Sehling 7/2.2, p. 548):

<!-- doc 2226 -->
> sind wir auff folgende einfältige bekändtnuß (darnach sich auch alle kirchendiener, so noch
> hinforder anzunehmen sein, umb jetztgedachten uhrsachen willen zu verhalten hetten) einig
> worden: Alß daß wir für allen dingen uns zur heiligen schrifft und hernach zu den christlichen
> Symbolen, Augustana confessione und dem Corpore doctrinae christianae Philippi Melanthonis (so
> die churfurstliche Sachsische und andere kirchen angenommen und im rechten verstande nach
> Gottes wort sich darauff beruffen) bekennet haben und noch bekennen. Ja, daß wir auch dem
> Vehrdischen abscheide, so weit derselbige neben dem Corpore doctrinae mit Gottes wort richtig
> und gleichformig übereinstimmet […]

we have agreed upon the following simple confession (whereby all ministers of the church who are
yet to be received hereafter should also have to govern themselves, for the causes now
mentioned): namely, that above all things we have confessed and do still confess ourselves to
the holy Scripture, and after it to the Christian creeds, the *Augustana confessio*, and the
*Corpus doctrinae christianae* of Philip Melanchthon (which the Electoral Saxon and other
churches have received, and appeal thereto in its right sense according to God's word). Yea,
that we also [hold] to the Verden recess, so far as the same, together with the *Corpus
doctrinae*, agreeth rightly and conformably with God's word […]

The call of Markus Mening in 1570 adds the Apology and the *Confessio Saxonica*
(Sehling 7/2.2, p. 544). The settlement of 1575 adds the Frankfurt Recess of 1558 (p. 566). None
of these Bremen texts names the SA. This is the Philippist canon, and from it the city went over
to the Reformed confession.

### 10.6 East Frisia: "the Augsburg Confession" and nothing more (1593, 1599)

In East Frisia, Lutheran and Reformed congregations lived side by side. Count Edzard II's church
order for Marienhafe (1593) is fully Lutheran. It names the "unfalsified" CA and Apology, the
catechisms "as the laymen's Bible" and the FC of 1580, in words close to Hoya's (§9.5). **East
Frisia, *Marienhafer Kirchenordnung*, 1593** (Sehling 7/1, p. 684):

<!-- doc 2119 -->
> so wollen wir allen unsern pastorn, predigern und kirchendienern ernstlich hiemyt aufehrlegt
> und bevolen haben, das sie yn allen ihren predigten, religionshandel und -gesprech durchaus
> den prophetischen und apostohschen geschriften, yn der heiligen bibel verfasst, sampt den
> dreien symbolis: Apostolico, Nicaeno et Athanasii, der ungevalschten Auspurgischen Confession
> und derselben Apologia, den catecismus, großen und kleinen, Lutheri (als der leyen bibel) und
> der Formulae Concordiae, anno 1580 wiederholet, volzogen und gepublicieret, sich gleichformich
> und einhellich gemaes verhalten sollen etc.

so we will that it be earnestly laid upon and commanded hereby to all our pastors, preachers and
ministers of the church, that in all their sermons, religious dealings and conversations they
behave themselves throughout conformably and with one accord according to the prophetic and
apostolic writings set down in the holy Bible, together with the three creeds, the Apostolic,
the Nicene and Athanasius's; the unfalsified Augsburg Confession and the Apology thereof;
Luther's catechisms, Large and Small (as the laymen's Bible); and the *Formula Concordiae*,
repeated, completed and published in the year 1580, etc.

The treaty between the count and the estates in 1599, a Reformed–Lutheran treaty, named only the
CA. The editors' footnote at this point discusses the *variata*. The treaty goes on to admit
that, from the beginning of the Reformation, both "opinions" about the CA's sense had been
preached and suffered in the land (Sehling 7/1, p. 422). **East Frisia, *Die Konkordaten*,
1599** (Reformed–Lutheran treaty; Sehling 7/1, p. 421):

<!-- doc 2110 -->
> daß in unserm lande, kirchen und schuelen, keine andere lehre dem volke solle offentlich
> vorgetragen, beschutzet oder gehandhabet werden, alß welche den prophetißchen und
> apostolißchen schriften gemeß, in die christliche bekentnuß der Augspurgißchen Confession
> zusamengetragen und vorfaßet

that in our land, churches and schools no other doctrine shall be publicly set before the
people, defended or maintained, than that which, agreeably to the prophetic and apostolic
writings, is gathered together and set down in the Christian confession of the Augsburg
Confession.

The preachers of Soest agreed on articles in 1590, "without whose subscription no one shall

themselves been examined and ordained. **Soest, *Artikel der Prediger*, 1 September 1590**
(Sehling 22, p. 480):

<!-- doc 1536 -->
> ohn wellicher articul subscription hinfurder niemandt in unnser ministerium soll zugelassen
> werden, unnd sindt die articule diese nachfolgende: 1. Unnser corpus doctrinae ist unnd soll
> sein die heilige bibel, prophetische unnd apostolische schrifft, die drey bewehrten symbola,
> als Apostolicum, Nicenum unnd Athanasii, die Augspurgische Confession unnd derselben Apologia,
> wie dieselbige anno funffzehenhundert unnd dreissigk rom. key. majestet ist uberandtwortet
> worden, die Schmalkaldischen Articul, der kleine unnd grosse catechismus Lutheri unnd Formula
> Concordiae, auff wellichs corpus doctrinae wir examinirt, ordinirt unnd in das heilige
> predigampt getreten sein, dabey wir auch biß in unnsere gruben vormittelst gottlicher hulff
> wollen bestendig beharren.

without the subscription of which articles no one shall henceforth be admitted into our

holy Bible, the prophetic and apostolic Scripture; the three approved creeds, namely the
Apostolic, the Nicene and Athanasius's; the Augsburg Confession and the Apology thereof, as the
same was delivered in the year fifteen hundred and thirty to the Roman imperial majesty; the
Smalcald Articles; the Small and Large Catechism of Luther; and the *Formula Concordiae*: upon
which *corpus doctrinae* we have been examined and ordained and have entered into the holy
preaching office, and whereby we will also abide steadfastly unto our graves, by means of God's

Soest was biconfessional in other respects. In 1583 it admitted godparents of both religions
(§17.4).

## 11. Hesse, Waldeck, Frankfurt, Nassau and Ysenburg

By the editors' account the Hessian landgraves (mediating) together rejected the FC
(Sehling 8, p. 344; Sehling 9, p. 33), although the introduction to Hessen-Darmstadt speaks of
Darmstadt's signature in 1577 (Sehling 9, p. 45). After Philip's death (1567) its general synods
held to the CA, the Apology, the SA, Luther's catechism and the Wittenberg *Corpus doctrinae*.
The Agende of 1574 called the CA "the symbol of this our time". Once the landgraviate was
divided, the two halves went separate ways. Hessen-Kassel bound its pastors by oath against the
ubiquity doctrine (1598) and turned Reformed. Hessen-Darmstadt made every minister, professor
and schoolmaster swear to the unaltered CA and renounce the Calvinists (1617). The neighbouring
counties show the same spread:

- **Waldeck** took the FC into its ordination question.
- **Frankfurt** took the Book of Concord into its ordination question.
- **Nassau** went from Melanchthon's *Loci* to an explicitly Reformed order (Dillenburg 1582).
- **Nassau-Weilburg and Ysenburg** held to the unaltered CA.

### 11.1 Hesse: the synod, the Agende, and the divided landgraviate (1571–1617)

The general synod's *Abschied* of 1571 (mediating) records the pastors' undertaking to teach
according to a mixed norm: the SA beside the Wittenberg *Corpus doctrinae*. **Hesse, *Abschied*
of the general synod, 1571** (mediating; Sehling 8, p. 355):

<!-- doc 2263 -->
> und nicht allein vor sich selbst nach der heiligen gottlichen geschrift und darein begrundten
> dreien symbolis, der Augspurgischen Confession, daruf gevolgter Apologien, Schmalkaldischen
> Articulis, dem catechismo Lutheri und zu Wittenberg ausgangenem corpore doctrinae zu lehren,
> sondern auch eußersten vleiß dahin zu arbeiten, urbutig und von herzen willig, daß von andern,
> beide in schulen und kirchen, dergleichen beschehe,

[they declared themselves] ready and willing from the heart not only themselves to teach
according to the holy divine Scripture and the three creeds grounded therein, the Augsburg
Confession, the Apology following thereon, the Smalcald Articles, Luther's catechism and the
*corpus doctrinae* that came forth at Wittenberg, but also to labour with the utmost diligence
that the like be done by others, both in schools and in churches.

The Agende of 1574 (mediating) puts Scripture alone as the *norma iudicii*, with the creeds and
the CA as "the symbol of this our time". One witness of the text (B) adds the Apology and the
*Corpus doctrinae* of "P. M." (Philip Melanchthon). **Hesse, *Agende*, 1574**
(mediating; Sehling 8, p. 415):

<!-- doc 2272 -->
> so söllen in allen und jeden punkten christlicher lehr die prophetischen und apostolische
> schriften das rechte fundament, die einige norma iudicii, regel und richtschnur sein, nach
> welcher alle fürfallende streit und irrung dirimirt und hingelegt werden mögen, und nechst der
> heiligen göttlichen schrift die drei bewerte symbola Apostolicum, Nicenum und Athanasianum,
> dergleichen die Augspurgische Confession, dieser unser zeit symbolum, als die in den
> prophetischen und apostolischen schriften warhaftig fundirt und gegründet und zum eigentlichen
> und notwendigen bericht von einem jeden artikel gnugsam seind,

then in all and every point of Christian doctrine the prophetic and apostolic writings shall be
the right foundation, the only *norma iudicii*, rule and line, according to which all
controversies and errors arising may be decided and laid aside; and next to the holy divine
Scripture the three approved creeds, the Apostolic, the Nicene and the Athanasian, and likewise
the Augsburg Confession, the symbol of this our time, as being truly founded and grounded in the
prophetic and apostolic writings and sufficient for proper and needful instruction concerning
every article.

After the division, the pastors of the Lower County of Katzenelnbogen, under Hessen-Kassel
(still mediating in 1598), swore a service oath with a long Christological section. It names the
four councils and Leo's Tome alongside the CA, the Apology, the SA and the Hessian synodal
*Abschiede*, and it rejects the "newly introduced bodily ubiquity". **Hessen-Kassel, *Diensteid
der Pfarrer in der Niedergrafschaft Katzenelnbogen*, 20 February 1598**
(mediating; Sehling 9, p. 54):

<!-- doc 2287 -->
> In sonderheit aber bekennen wir uns mit mund und hertzen zu der reinen lehr von der person
> unsers hernn Jesu Christi und mit namen dero newlich eingeführter leiblicher ubiquitet und
> allenthalbenheit halben zu dem claren wort Gottes, non stolide, sed dextre intellecto, den
> dreien hauptsymbolis Apostolico, Niceno, Athanasiano, desgleichen denen vier in der kirchen
> Gottes approbirten heuptconciliis und sonderlich dem concilio Chalcedonense undt der epistola
> Leonis ad Flavianum wie auch Augustana Confessione, deren Apologia und […] Schmalcaldicis
> Articulis und darauff erfolgten Synodalabschieden etc. und wollen in crafft deren auch bei den
> gewönlichen reden bleiben

But in especial we confess ourselves with mouth and heart to the pure doctrine of the person of
our Lord Jesus Christ, and namely, as touching the newly introduced bodily ubiquity and
omnipresence, to the plain word of God, understood not stupidly but rightly; to the three chief
creeds, the Apostolic, the Nicene and the Athanasian; likewise to the four chief councils
approved in the church of God, and especially to the council of Chalcedon and the epistle of Leo
to Flavian; as also to the Augsburg Confession, the Apology thereof, and […] the Smalcald
Articles, and the synodal recesses that followed thereon, etc.; and will by virtue of the same
abide also by the customary forms of speech

The oath goes on to reject, as "monstrous and portentous propositions", the teaching that
Christ's humanity is "almighty, all-knowing, infinite and everywhere present".

Hessen-Darmstadt, which stayed Lutheran, answered with a mandate that made the unaltered CA a
sworn condition of office. It also required a negative declaration against every other
confession. **Hessen-Darmstadt, *Mandat zur Beibehaltung der Confessio Augustana*, 28 July
1617** (Sehling 9, p. 152):

<!-- doc 2287 -->
> Derowegen soll auch in kirchen und schuelen kein theologus, professor, praedicant oder
> schuelmeister ufgenommen werden, er gelobe unnd schwere dann zuevorderst, daß er anderst nicht
> lehren wölle, dann waß Gotteß wortt und erwehntter wahren unverenderttenn Augspurgischenn
> Confession gemeeß sey, sonderlich aber auch sich mit mundt und hertz in affirmativa et
> negativa erklehre, das er in confessione richtigk und rein unnd nichtt catholisch oder
> papistisch, zwinglich oder calvinisch, widerteufferisch oder andern irrigen unnd widerigen
> secten verwanth unnd zugethann sey,

Wherefore also no theologian, professor, preacher or schoolmaster shall be received in churches
and schools, except he first vow and swear that he will teach nothing else than what is
agreeable to God's word and the aforesaid true, unaltered Augsburg Confession; and especially
also declare himself with mouth and heart, *in affirmativa et negativa*, that he is right and
pure in confession, and not catholic or papist, Zwinglian or Calvinist, Anabaptist, nor related
or addicted to other erroneous and contrary sects.

> Zum andern fragen wir, ob ihr auch ewern glauben und bekentnis richten wollet nach Gottes

### 11.3 Frankfurt: the Book of Concord in the ordination vow (1589)

The imperial city of Frankfurt put the Book of Concord into the questions of its ordination
rite. The ordinand answers "Yea". **Frankfurt, *Agende*, 1589** (Sehling 9, p. 541):

<!-- doc 2332 -->
> Wollet ir aber auch mit Gottes Hülff und Gnaden bey dem klaren, hellen und außgetruckten Wort
> Gottes, so in den Schrifften Mose, der Propheten und Aposteln begriffen und newlicher zeit in
> der Augspurgischen Confession, Schmalkaldischen Articuln, beyden Catechismen Lutheri und dem
> Christlichen Concordibuch widerholet und erklärt worden, trewlich und fleissig bleiben, auch
> allen Christlichen Ordnungen dieser Franckfurtischen Kirchen trewlich nachkommen? Antwort: Ja.

But will ye also, with God's help and grace, abide faithfully and diligently by the clear,
bright and express word of God, which is contained in the writings of Moses, of the prophets and
of the apostles, and hath lately been repeated and declared in the Augsburg Confession, the
Smalcald Articles, both Catechisms of Luther and the Christian Book of Concord; and also
faithfully follow all the Christian ordinances of this church of Frankfurt? Answer: Yea.

The confessions are presented as a recent *repetition* of Scripture, not as a separate norm.

### 11.4 Nassau: from Melanchthon's *Loci* to the Reformed confession (1564–1582)

The Nassau-Diez *Revers* of 1564 names the creeds, the CA and "Philip's *Loci*". It is a promise
("*angeloben und versprechen*"), not an oath. **Nassau-Diez, *Revers der Kirchendiener*,
[1564]** (Sehling 10, p. 103):

<!-- doc 177 -->
> Puncten, welche diejenige, so zum predigampt in des wolgebornen u[nseres] g[nädigen] h[errn]
> etc. kirchen angenomen werden, angeloben und versprechen muß[en] laut der ordnung 1. Das sie
> die h[eilige] lahr nach der h. propheten und aposteln schriften in dem verstandte, wie der in
> symbolis Apostolico, Nicaeno und Athanasii, auch in der Augspurgischen Confession und locis
> Philippi ausgedruckt ist, rein und lauter treiben wollen und keine newe dogmata seen oder
> eigene und besondere formulas zureden, dardurch etwa uneinigkeitt und spaltung zwischen uns
> selbst oder ergernis bei dem volck entstehen mochte, gebrauchen,

Points which those who are received into the preaching office in the churches of the well-born,
our gracious lord, etc., must vow and promise according to the ordinance: 1. That they will set
forth the holy doctrine purely and clearly according to the writings of the holy prophets and
apostles, in the sense expressed in the creeds, the Apostolic, the Nicene and Athanasius's, and
also in the Augsburg Confession and Philip's *Loci*; and will sow no new dogmas, nor use their
own and peculiar forms of speech whereby disunity and division might arise among ourselves, or
offence among the people.

By 1582 Nassau-Dillenburg had adopted the Reformed confession (Reformed). Its church order of
that year required, before the Supper, a confession of "the Reformed doctrine" (§17.2).

### 11.5 Nassau-Weilburg and Ysenburg: the unaltered CA (1576, 1588)

Nassau-Weilburg's visitors asked each pastor whether he taught the chief articles "as they are
briefly set down in the unaltered Augsburg Confession". **Nassau-Weilburg, *Kirchenordnung*,
1574/1576** (Sehling 10, p. 306):

<!-- doc 198 -->
> Articul, darauff die Pfarrer und Kirchendiener zu befragen 1. Ob er die vornembste Articul
> unserer Christlichen Religion vermög Prophetischer und Apostolischer Schrifften, wie die in
> der ungeenderten Augspurgischen Confession, so in Anno [15]30 Keyser Carolen, dem V. dieses
> Namens, ubergeben worden, kürtzlich verfasset und in andern bewehrten Symbolis der reinen
> Evangelischen Kirchen (so der ungeenderten Augspurgischen Confession zugethan und verwandt
> seynt) widerholet werden, seiner befolhenen Gemeine fleissig und trewlich fürtrage.

Articles upon which the pastors and ministers of the church are to be asked: 1. Whether he set
forth diligently and faithfully to the congregation committed to him the chief articles of our
Christian religion according to the prophetic and apostolic writings, as they are briefly set
down in the unaltered Augsburg Confession which was delivered in the year [15]30 to the emperor
Charles, the Fifth of that name, and repeated in other approved symbols of the pure evangelical
churches (which are addicted and related to the unaltered Augsburg Confession).

The Agende of 1618 adds the Apology, the SA and the catechisms (Sehling 10, p. 233). In 1609 the
same territory told its pastors to admit, with discretion, godparents who did not hold the
unaltered CA in every point (§17.4).

The county of Ysenburg-Birstein gave its pastors a *norma doctrinae* of the creeds, the
unaltered CA, the Apology, the SA and both catechisms. **Ysenburg-Birstein, *Kirchenordnung*,
1588** (Sehling 10, p. 619):

<!-- doc 228 -->
> Erstlich sollen die Pfarrherrn und Kirchendiener eine gewisse normam Doctrinae haben, darnach
> sie sich in Erkanntnuß und Underscheidt der rechten und falschen Lehr richten mögen. Sollen
> auch nichts anders lehren, dann was der Propheten und Apostel Schrifft, den bewerthen Symbolis
> unnd der unverenderten Augspurgischen Confession, Anno 1530 uberantwort, sampt derselbigen
> Apologi, So wol auch den Schmalkaldischen Artickeln und beyden Catechismis Lutheri gemeß ist.

First, the pastors and ministers of the church shall have a certain *norma doctrinae*, whereby
they may govern themselves in knowing and distinguishing right and false doctrine. They shall
also teach nothing else than what is agreeable to the Scripture of the prophets and apostles,
the approved creeds, and the unaltered Augsburg Confession delivered in the year 1530, together
with the Apology thereof, as also to the Smalcald Articles and both Catechisms of Luther.

The FC is not named. The order goes on to tell pastors to read Luther and Philip.

## 12. The Palatinates: Kurpfalz, the Upper Palatinate, Pfalz-Neuburg, Pfalz-Zweibrücken and Sponheim

The Wittelsbach Palatinates show more clearly than anywhere else how a single binding formula
could carry different confessions in turn. The Kurpfalz *Kirchendienerbestallung* kept its
wording through four reigns while its content changed:

| Reign | Confessional content |
|---|---|
| Ottheinrich (1556) | Lutheran: CA, Apol, SA and Luther's catechism |
| Ludwig VI (1580) | Lutheran: the Book of Concord |
| Johann Casimir (1586) | Reformed: the Heidelberg Catechism, with the CA and Apol "in their right sense" |
| Friedrich IV (1601) | Reformed: bound "with fealty given by the hand in the stead of a bodily sworn oath" |

The Upper Palatinate, which had signed the FC in 1581–82, was released from it in 1585 under the
Reformed regent. Pfalz-Neuburg stayed Lutheran and signed. Pfalz-Zweibrücken, then Lutheran,
drew up its own Latin subscription in 1564, which Strasbourg also used. The Hintere Grafschaft
Sponheim took the FC as "the symbol" of the Lutheran churches in 1608.

### 12.1 Kurpfalz: one formula, four confessions (1556–1601)

Ottheinrich's church order of 1556 binds pastors to the creeds, the CA and Apology of 1530 "in
their right, unfalsified sense", the SA and Luther's catechism. **Kurpfalz, *Kirchenordnung* of
Ottheinrich, 1556** (Sehling 14, pp. 114–115):

<!-- doc 476 -->
> daß ihr in euerer lehr, predigt und gantzem kirchenampt bey der richtschnur göttlichs worts
> bleiben und davon weder zur rechten noch zur linkken abweichen, […] und in eurem lehren,
> predigen und conferieren etc. den alten bewerten symbolis Apostolico, Niceno und Athanasiano,
> der Augspurgischen Confession und deren Apologia (wie dieselb von den christlichen churfürsten
> und stendenkeyser Carolo dem fünften anno etc. 1530 in ihrem rechtem, unverfelschtem verstand,
> darauf wir uns jederzeit und noch mit mund und hertzen berufen und biß an unser ende mit
> verleihung göttlicher gnaden darbey bestendiglichen zu bleiben gedencken überantwort),
> deßgleichen bey den Schmalkäldischen Artickeln, dem Catechismo Lutheri, wie solcher diser
> unser kirchenordnung einverleibt, euch gemäß verhalten und nichts ungleichs oder widerwertigs
> einmengen oder einfüren.

that ye abide by the rule of God's word in your doctrine, preaching and whole church office, and
turn not aside therefrom to the right hand nor to the left […]; and that in your teaching,
preaching and conferring, etc., ye behave yourselves agreeably to the old approved creeds, the
Apostolic, the Nicene and the Athanasian, and to the Augsburg Confession and the Apology thereof
(as the same was delivered by the Christian electors and estates to the emperor Charles the
Fifth in the year 1530, in its right, unfalsified sense, whereunto we have at all times appealed
and do still appeal with mouth and heart, and purpose by the grant of divine grace to abide
steadfastly thereby to our end), and likewise to the Smalcald Articles and Luther's catechism as
it is incorporated in this our church order; and mingle or bring in nothing unlike or contrary.

Under Ludwig VI (1576–1583), who restored Lutheranism after his father's Reformed reign, the
formula was extended to the Book of Concord. **Kurpfalz, *Kirchendienerbestallung*, 1580**
(Sehling 14, p. 507):

<!-- doc 505 -->
> Und nachdem die streitigen religionspuncten wider das bapstthumb und die secten in der
> Augspurgischen Confession und derselbigen Apologia nach rechtem verstandt der prophetischen
> und apostolischen schriften erklährt, sollet und werdet ihr bey gedachter Confession und
> Apologia, deßgleichen bey dem von churfürstlicher Pfaltz, auch etlicher ander churfürsten,
> fürsten, grafen, herrn und ständen deß reichs, deren kirchen und schulen approbiertem
> Concordibuch bleiben und kein alte oder neue opinion oder secten, so derselbigen zuwider,
> eynführen noch vertheidingen,

And forasmuch as the points of religion in controversy against the papacy and the sects are
declared in the Augsburg Confession and the Apology thereof according to the right understanding
of the prophetic and apostolic writings, ye shall and will abide by the said Confession and
Apology, and likewise by the Book of Concord approved by the Electoral Palatinate and also by
certain other electors, princes, counts, lords and estates of the empire, [and] their churches
and schools; and shall neither bring in nor defend any old or new opinion or sect contrary
thereto.

Six years later, under the Reformed administrator Johann Casimir, the same sentence returns
without the Book of Concord. The catechism that "directs" the pastor to the CA is now the large
and small Heidelberg Catechism, as the editors note. **Kurpfalz, *Kirchendienerbestallung*,
1586** (Reformed; Sehling 14, p. 525):

<!-- doc 509 -->
> Und nachdem die streitigen religionspuncten wider das papstumb und die secten in der
> Augspurgischen Confession und derselbigen Apologia nach rechtem verstandt der prophetischen
> und apostolischen schriften erklärt, sollet und wedet ihr bey gedachter Confessio und
> Apologia, inmassen euch dahin der groß und klein catechismus, von neuem ubersehen und der
> kirchenordnung eynverleibt, weisen, endlich bleiben

And forasmuch as the points of religion in controversy against the papacy and the sects are
declared in the Augsburg Confession and the Apology thereof according to the right understanding
of the prophetic and apostolic writings, ye shall and will abide finally by the said Confession
and Apology, in such measure as the Large and Small Catechism, newly revised and incorporated in
the church order, directeth you thereto.

The 1586 text is thus a Reformed subscription to the CA, read through the Heidelberg Catechism.
By 1601 the schoolmasters' *Bestallung* was written in the first person. It ends with a pledge
"with fealty given by the hand in the stead of a bodily sworn oath", confirmed by the man's own
signature. **Kurpfalz, *Schuldienerbestallung*, 1601** (Reformed; Sehling 14, p. 593):

<!-- doc 517 -->
> Dieses alles stedt, fest und unverbruchlich zu halten, auch alles ander zu leisten, was einem
> getreuen schuldiener und gehorsamen underthanen gebüret und wol anstehet, thue ich mit
> handgebner treu an eines leiblichen geschwornen eids statt hiemit geloben und mich dessen mit
> eigner underschrift verpflichten und verbinden, getreulich und sonder gefehrde.

All this to hold steadfast, firm and inviolable, and also to perform all else that becometh and
well beseemeth a faithful schoolmaster and obedient subject, I do hereby vow with fealty given
by the hand in the stead of a bodily sworn oath, and do oblige and bind myself thereto with my
own signature, faithfully and without guile.

The same handfast pledge "in the stead of an oath" appears in Pfalz-Neuburg's *Bestallung* of
1555 (Sehling 13, p. 108).

### 12.2 The Upper Palatinate: released from the Book of Concord (1585)

The instruction of 1585 is a Reformed order.


The Upper Palatinate's clergy signed the FC in 1581–82 (Sehling's introduction, Sehling 13, p.
269). When Johann Casimir took over the regency, his instruction to the church council of 1585
defined the CA by its later repetitions at Frankfurt (1557/58) and Naumburg (1561). The editors'
footnote calls this "entirely in the sense of the so-called *Variata*". The instruction then
released the ministers from their obligation to the BoC. **Upper Palatinate,
*Kirchenratsinstruktion*, 3 March 1585** (Reformed; Sehling 13, pp. 328–329):

<!-- doc 420 -->
> dann der confession, uf dem reichstag zue Augspurg anno [15]30 keiser Carolo ubergeben, und
> angehafter apologien, in dem rechten und gesunden verstand, wie sie damals von den
> christlichen chur- und fürsten gemeint und hernacher auf etlichen underschiedlichen
> ver[…]Besamblungen - als anno [15]57 und [15]58 zu Frankfurt - und [15]61 zu Naumburg - von
> churfürsten und stenden vorbesagter augspurgischen confession repetirt, widerholet und
> ercleret, auch von deren theologis ausgefueret und verteidingt worden ist, […] Weilen man
> allein uf Gottes wort als die unteilbare richtschnur sehen und auf keine menschenschriften
> oder autoritet sich verbünden soll, so befelhen und wöllen wir, das alle unsers obigen
> fürstentumbs zu Bayern kirchen- und schuldiener ihrer des concordibuch getoner pflichten
> halber allerdings erlassen und fürbas allein zu Gottes wort und zu dem obgesetzten verbunden
> und in pflicht genomen werden.

then [according to] the confession delivered to the emperor Charles at the diet at Augsburg in
the year [15]30, and the apology annexed, in the right and sound sense, as it was then meant by
the Christian electors and princes, and afterwards at sundry assemblies (as in the years [15]57
and [15]58 at Frankfurt, and [15]61 at Naumburg) repeated, rehearsed and declared by the
electors and estates of the aforesaid Augsburg Confession, and also set forth and defended by
their theologians, […] Forasmuch as we ought to look to God's word alone as the indivisible
rule, and bind ourselves to no writings or authority of men, we command and will that all
ministers of church and school in our aforesaid principality in Bavaria be wholly released from
the duty they took upon them to the Book of Concord, and henceforth be bound and taken into duty
to God's word alone and to the [norm] set down above.

The phrase "to bind ourselves to no writings of men" is the Reformed answer to subscription as
such.

### 12.3 Pfalz-Neuburg: the Wittenberg Concord against the Calvinists (1564), the *corpus doctrinae* (1576)

Duke Wolfgang's mandate of 1564 against the Calvinists prescribed the "phrases and ways of
speaking" for the Supper. They were to be taken from the CA, the Apology, the Wittenberg Concord
of 1536, the SA and the church order. **Pfalz-Neuburg, *Mandat gegen die Kalvinisten*, 1564**
(Sehling 13, p. 148):

<!-- doc 401 -->
> so wollen und bevelhen wir hiemit ernstlich, das alle unsere kirchendiener hinfur in iren
> predigen, auch privatunderrichtungen sonderlich, wan si den handl des Herren nachtmals erklern
> sollen - nicht neu, ungewondliche phrases und weis zu reden einfuren, sonder sich der phrases
> und weis zu reden allermaist gebrauchen, so in Gottes wort, augspurgischer confession,
> derselbigen apologia und zu Wittemberg in anno [15]36 aufgerichter concordia de caena Domini,
> auch in den schmalcaldischen artikln und unserer kirchenordnung gesetzt und begriffen sein.

we will and command hereby earnestly that all our ministers of the church henceforth, in their
sermons and also in private instructions, especially when they are to explain the matter of the
Lord's Supper, bring in no new, unwonted phrases and ways of speaking, but use most of all the
phrases and ways of speaking that are set and contained in God's word, the Augsburg Confession,
the Apology thereof and the *concordia de caena Domini* set up at Wittenberg in the year [15]36,
and also in the Smalcald Articles and our church order.

By 1576 these books were called the territory's *corpus doctrinae*, with the CA as "the symbol
of the right, true church of our time". **Pfalz-Neuburg, *Generalartikel*, 1576**
(Sehling 13, p. 171):

<!-- doc 404 -->
> das dieselbige zuvorderst der heiligen schrift selbst und dann auch den dreien symbolis
> apostolico, nicaeno, Athanatiano, desgleichen auch der augspurgischen confession, wie si anno
> 1530 kaiser Carolo Quinto ubergeben, als unserer zeit der rechten, wahrhaftigen kirchen
> symbolo, derselben apologien, schmalkaldischen artikeln und catechismo Lutheri nicht zuwider,
> sonder in allweg als dem reinen corpori doctrinae gemeß gefüert und, da jemand solchem zuwider
> geleret und uf seiner gefaßten irrigen opinion verharren, im furstentumb keineswegs geduldet
> werden solle.

that the same [exposition of Scripture] be conducted first of all agreeably to holy Scripture
itself, and then also not contrary to the three creeds, the Apostolic, the Nicene and
Athanasius's, nor likewise to the Augsburg Confession as it was delivered in the year 1530 to
the emperor Charles the Fifth, as the symbol of the right, true church of our time, [nor] to the
Apology thereof, the Smalcald Articles and Luther's catechism, but in every way agreeably [to
them] as to the pure *corpus doctrinae*; and that if anyone teach contrary thereto and persist
in the erroneous opinion he hath conceived, he shall in no wise be suffered in the principality.

Pfalz-Neuburg later signed the BoC (Sehling's introduction, Sehling 13, p. 35).

### 12.4 Pfalz-Zweibrücken: "*mente atque manu subscribo*" (1564)

Pfalz-Zweibrücken's Latin *Bekenntnisverpflichtung* of 1564 is the most elaborate personal
subscription in the corpus. It is written in the first person and goes article by article:

- Scripture;
- *four* creeds: the Apostles', the Nicene, the Athanasian, and the Te Deum "thought to have
  been made by Augustine and Ambrose";
- the four councils;
- the CA of 1530, as understood by those who presented it;
- the history of its later editions;
- the SA, each with its own act of assent.

Strasbourg used the same text in 1565, and Sickingen in 1564. **Pfalz-Zweibrücken,
*Bekenntnisverpflichtung der Kirchendiener*, 1564** (Sehling 18, pp. 328–329):

<!-- doc 982 -->
> 1. Itaque amplector ex animo, omnia scripta Prophetarum et Apostolorum veteris et novi
> testamenti, quae sunt ipsum certum et indubitatum verbum Dei, […] 2. Deinde credo, amplector
> et confiteor quatuor illa catholicae ecclesiae symbola: Apostolicum, Nicenum, Athanasianum et
> quod ab Augustino atque Ambrosio confectum putatur. […] 4. […] toto pectore amplector et
> recipio eam doctrinae religionis formulam et summam, quae scripto exhibita est ab
> Illustrissimi Saxoniae Electore et aliis Principibus et duabusque civitatibus Nurnberga et
> Reutlinga Imperatori Carolo V. Augustae Vindelicorum anno 1530, quae inde Augustana confessio
> est nominata. […] Ego igitur his Smalcaldicis articulis quos veros et sacras scripturae
> conformes esse agnosco et confiteor (habent enim certa et indubitata Propheticorum et
> Apostolicorum librorum testimonia) ex animo, ut sunt κατα το ρητον positi, assentior et sine
> ulla sophistica his mente atque manu subscribo.

1. Therefore I embrace from my heart all the writings of the prophets and apostles of the Old
   and New Testaments, which are the very certain and undoubted word of God […] 2. Then I
   believe, embrace and confess those four creeds of the catholic church: the Apostolic, the
   Nicene, the Athanasian, and that which is thought to have been made by Augustine and Ambrose.
   […] 4. […] with my whole heart I embrace and receive that form and sum of the doctrine of
   religion which was exhibited in writing by the most illustrious Elector of Saxony and other
   princes, and the two cities of Nürnberg and Reutlingen, to the emperor Charles V at Augsburg
   in the year 1530, which is thence named the Augsburg Confession. […] I therefore assent from
   my heart to these Smalcald Articles, which I acknowledge and confess to be true and
   conformable to the holy Scriptures (for they have the certain and undoubted testimonies of
   the prophetic and apostolic books), as they are set down according to the letter; and without
   any sophistry I subscribe them with mind and hand.

"*κατα το ρητον*", "according to the letter", excludes any reservation. Pfalz-Zweibrücken did
not go on to the FC. By Sehling's account, Duke Johann announced the official rejection of the
Book of Concord in 1580 (Sehling 18, p. 40).

### 12.5 Sponheim: the FC as "the symbol of all pure churches" (1608)

The Hintere Grafschaft Sponheim, a condominium of Baden and the Palatine house, had made the CA,
Melanchthon's *Loci* and his *Examen* its *corpus doctrinae* in 1560 (Sehling 18, p. 636). In
1608 the visitors complained that some pastors had neither bought nor read the FC. **Hintere
Grafschaft Sponheim, *Generalartikel*, 1608** (Sehling 18, p. 671):

<!-- doc 1021 -->
> Es haben etliche pfarrer in der hindern Graveschaft Sponheim formulam concordiae nicht allein
> in ihre Bibliothecas nicht erkaufft, sondern auch zum theil nicht gelesen, welche doch nunmehr
> pro symbolo aller reinen kirchen der unverfelschten Augspurgischen Confession gehalten wirdt,
> auch weilandt H[errn] Carol, Pfalzgravens christmilter gedechtnuß, eigentlicher will gewesen,
> daß so wol s.f.gn. kindere alß auch die landtsunderthanen dahin gewiesen werden sollen.

Certain pastors in the Hinder County of Sponheim have not only not bought the *formula
concordiae* for their libraries, but also in part have not read it; which is nevertheless now
held *pro symbolo* of all pure churches of the unfalsified Augsburg Confession; and it was also
the express will of the late Lord Charles, Count Palatine, of Christian and gentle memory, that
both his princely grace's children and the subjects of the land should be directed thereto.

The article goes on to require every pastor to buy the octavo edition within a year and to
declare in writing to both chancelleries whether he assented to all its articles
(Sehling 18, pp. 671–672).

## 13. Württemberg, Hohenlohe and the Swabian cities

Württemberg had its own confession, the *Confessio Virtembergica*, which Brenz drew up for the
Council of Trent in 1552. From the 1550s it set that confession beside the CA. Its theologians,
Andreae above all, were the authors of the Concord, and in 1577 the duke ordered every minister
to sign the FC. Hohenlohe, Württemberg's neighbour, shows a different path. In 1578 it made its
own *corpus doctrinae*. In 1581, on Andreae's advice, it cut that corpus down to the CA. From
1603 it bound its ministers to a local *Gründlicher Bericht* in place of the FC. The free cities
of Swabia (Hall, Wimpfen, Ravensburg, Ulm) mostly signed. Some had their own local formula as
well.

### 13.1 Württemberg: the CA and the *Loci* (1547), the CA and "our Confession" (after 1559)

Duke Ulrich's visitation order of 1547 directs preaching to the CA and Apology, and sends the
ordinary preachers to Melanchthon's later *Loci*, "for they are examined out of the same in the
synods". **Württemberg, *Visitationsordnung*, 1547** (Sehling 16, p. 155):

<!-- doc 659 -->
> Die Leer unnd das Predig Ambt belanngenndt, sollenn sie dieselbigen alle auff die mainung unnd
> Innhallt der Augspurgischen Confession unnd darauff gevolgte Apologia richten unnd darwider
> nichts news oder widerwertigs fürgeen lassenn, unnd sonnderlich die gemeinen predicanten zu
> solhen, desgleichen auf die Locos Communes philippi recentiores weisen, dann sie in den
> Sinodis aus denselbigenn examinirt werden.

As touching doctrine and the preaching office, they shall direct them all according to the
meaning and content of the Augsburg Confession and the Apology that followed thereon, and suffer
nothing new or contrary thereto to go forth; and especially they shall direct the common
preachers to the same, and likewise to the later *Loci communes* of Philip, for they are
examined out of the same in the synods.

Under Duke Christoph the *Iuramentum und Promissio* of the ministers named two confessions: the
Augsburg and "our Confession", the Württemberg Confession of 1552. **Württemberg, *Eid der
Kirchendiener*, [after 1559]** (Sehling 16, p. 424):

<!-- doc 685 -->
> Iuramentum und Promissio der Kirchendiener Unsers Fürstenthumbs. […] Und nach dem die
> erklärung solcher Artickel, darinn man in Glaubens sachen zu diser zeit strittig, in der
> Augspurgischen und unser Confession kurtz und klar, nach anweisung des rechten, waren,
> catholischen verstands der prophetischen und apostolischen Schrifft begriffen und verfaßt
> seind, so erfordert die notturfft des Kirchenampts, das der Kirchendiener in solchen Artickuln
> sein Lehr nach der erklärung und inhallt der zweien bemelten Confessionen getrewlich
> verrichte.

*Oath and promise of the ministers of the church of our principality.* […] And forasmuch as the
explanation of such articles as are at this time in controversy in matters of faith is
comprehended and set down briefly and plainly in the Augsburg Confession and in our Confession,
according to the direction of the right, true, catholic sense of the prophetic and apostolic
Scripture, the need of the church's office requireth that the minister of the church perform his
teaching in such articles faithfully according to the explanation and content of the two said
Confessions.

### 13.2 Württemberg: the mandate to sign the FC (1577)

Duke Ludwig's mandate of 1577 sent the FC to the church and told every theologian and every
minister of church and school to sign it. The FC is described as wholly conformable to the
earlier norm, the Württemberg Confession "delivered at Trent" included. The duke notes that the
electorates of Saxony and Brandenburg had already signed. **Württemberg, *Mandat zur
Unterzeichnung der Konkordienformel*, 1577** (Sehling 16, p. 445):

<!-- doc 692 -->
> das sie zuvorderst der hailigen, göttlichen schrifft, den dreyen alten unnd bewerten Symbolis,
> der christlichen Augspurgischen Confession, derselben Apologi, auch unnser vor diser Zeit
> publicierter unnd zu Trient überraichter christlicher Confession, den Schmalkaldischen
> Articuln, auch Katechismus Lutheri durchaus gleichförmig […] unnd nunmehr vielgedachte formula
> Concordie in baiden Churfürstenthumb, Sachsen unnd Brandenburg, von allen Theologen, Kirchen-
> unnd schuldienern albereit unnderschriben wurdt. […] das wir auch in unserm Fürstenthum
> vilgemelte, hiemit uberschickte Schrifften durch alle unsere Theologen, Kürchen- unnd
> Schuldienern (zue gezeugnuß des christlichen Consensus) unnderzaichnen lassen sollen

that they are first of all wholly conformable to the holy divine Scripture, the three old and
approved creeds, the Christian Augsburg Confession, the Apology thereof, and also our Christian
Confession published before this time and delivered at Trent, the Smalcald Articles, and also
Luther's Catechism […]; and the oft-mentioned *formula Concordiae* is now already subscribed in
both electorates, Saxony and Brandenburg, by all theologians and ministers of church and school.
[…] that we also in our principality should cause the oft-mentioned writings herewith sent to be
signed by all our theologians and ministers of church and school (in witness of the Christian
consensus).

The Limpurg church order of 1610 likewise binds its ministers to the CA and the FC
(Sehling 16, p. 611).

### 13.3 Hohenlohe: an own corpus (1578), cut down (1581), and the *Gründlicher Bericht* (1603–1615)

Hohenlohe's church order of 1578 opens with a *corpus doctrinae*. The church is to hold it
"affirmatively and negatively", and so are both "teachers and hearers". **Hohenlohe,
*Kirchenordnung*, 1578** (Sehling 15, p. 260):

<!-- doc 574 -->
> bekennen sich diser grafschaft kirchen zu nachfolgenden schriften und büchern, als darinnen
> die ewige und unwandelbare göttliche warheit in allen und jeden stücken unserer warhaften,
> christlichen rehgion begriffen und erklert ist. Sollen auch dieselben beides lehrer und
> zuhörer pro corpore cloctrinae affirmative und negative halten, nembhchen: 1. Die schriften
> der heiligen propheten und apostel altes und newes testaments, fals die eiihge normam iudicii
> in allen controvershs. […] 2. Die drey alten symbola, als apostolicum, nicenum und Athanash.
> 3. Die augspurgische confession, wie sie anno etc. [15]30 keiser Carolo V. zu Augspurg auf
> clem

the churches of this county confess themselves to the writings and books following, as wherein
the eternal and unchangeable divine truth is comprehended and declared in all and every part of
our true Christian religion. And both teachers and hearers shall hold the same *pro corpore
doctrinae*, affirmatively and negatively, namely: 1. The writings of the holy prophets and
apostles of the Old and New Testament, as the only *norma iudicii* in all controversies. […] 2.
The three old creeds, namely the Apostolic, the Nicene and Athanasius's. 3. The Augsburg
Confession, as it was [delivered] in the year [15]30 to the emperor Charles V at Augsburg at the
…

The apparatus records the order's later life:

- In the printer's copy the CA is qualified "unaltered in words and meaning".
- A hand in the Stuttgart copy added "7. *Die formulam concordiae*".
- The reissue of 1688 reads "7. The *formula concordiae*, published and subscribed in the year
  1580".

The apparatus also quotes the *Verzeichnis* of 4 November 1581, in which Andreae himself advised
dropping the *Repetitio* and Melanchthon's *Loci* from the list. As the editors note, FC II had
condemned the teaching of the *Loci* on free will. The corpus was cut down to Scripture, the
creeds and the CA. **Hohenlohe, *Verzeichnis* of 4 November 1581, quoted in the apparatus to the
*Kirchenordnung* of 1578** (Sehling 15, p. 261):

<!-- doc 574 -->
> dieweil es aber mit gedachter repetition augspurgischer confession und den locis Philippi ein
> solche gelegenheit, wie im protocoll fol. 17 und 18 zu befinden, so vermeinet demnach D. Jacob
> [Andreä], zank, zwitracht, auch kunftige gefahr der khchen zu verhuetten, das ermelte bucher
> außzulaßen, und man sich in disem ersten capitel nach Gottes wort zum buch der concordien
> bekennet, darin obgesetzte reyne bucher sambt gruntlicher erklerung der strittigen articul
> begriffen. Daruff ist verabschiedt worden, das man sich nach der bibel und die 3 symbola
> simpliciter nur [!] uf die augspurgische confession, wie sie anno etc. [15]30 kayser Carolo zu
> Augspurg ubergeben in der kirchenordnung referiren und solche pro corpore doctrinae halten
> solle.

but since the matter standeth so with the said repetition of the Augsburg Confession and the
*Loci* of Philip, as is to be found in the protocol, fol. 17 and 18, Dr Jacob [Andreae] is
therefore of opinion, in order to prevent strife, discord and also future danger to the church,
that the said books be left out, and that in this first chapter [the church] confess itself,
after God's word, to the Book of Concord, wherein the aforesaid pure books are comprehended,
together with a thorough explanation of the articles in controversy. Whereupon it was resolved
that, after the Bible and the three creeds, [the church] should refer itself in the church order
simply and only [!] to the Augsburg Confession as it was delivered in the year [15]30 to the
emperor Charles at Augsburg, and hold the same *pro corpore doctrinae*.

Sehling's "[!]" marks the outcome. Andreae advised confessing the BoC, but the counts decided to
name the CA alone. The ministers' oath of 1588 binds to the church order and its ceremonies
rather than to any confession by name. **Hohenlohe, *Eid der gemeinsamen Kirchendiener*, 1588**
(Sehling 15, p. 562):

<!-- doc 608 -->
> Ir werdet geloben und schweren, das ir euer anbevolhen arnbt des heihgen ministerii bestes und
> getreues vlais auswarten, euch der graveschaft küchenordnung allerdings gemes verhalten, in
> derselben ohne sembthcher graven von Hohenloe etc. vorwissen und bevelich nichzit endern,
> weder ichtwas davon oder darzu tun,

Ye shall vow and swear that ye will wait upon the office of the holy ministry committed to you
with your best and faithful diligence; behave yourselves altogether agreeably to the church
order of the county; and change nothing therein without the foreknowledge and command of all the
counts of Hohenlohe together, etc., neither take anything therefrom nor add anything thereto;

From 1603 the counts made a local doctrinal book, the *Gründlicher Bericht* (printed 1605), the
norm in place of the FC. The partition treaties of 1609–1615 bound every minister to it by
signature, oath and *Revers*. **Hohenlohe, *Erb- und Teilungsverträge*, 1609–1615**
(Sehling 15, p. 674):

<!-- doc 633 -->
> Insonderheit aber unß mit allem vleiß angelegen sein laßen, damit in allen und jeden kirchen
> und schulen nach anleitung jungstern in anno etc. 1605 under unßer grave Wolffgangs namen
> außgangenen büchleins (so intituhrt ist: Gründlicher bericht auß heiliger, göttlicher sclnfft
> von den vornembsten articuln christlicher lehr) daß lauter und rein wort […] Gottes durch alle
> und jede dießer graveschaft kirchendiener den zuhörern und undertanen ohnverfälscht
> vorgetragen und geprediget und unnöttiger zank und spaltungen destomehr verhüettet werden.
> Subscriptiones und pflichtleistung der kirchendiener Inmaßen dan dießem allem also
> nachzukommen, alle kirchendiener und lehrer mit mund und hand zusagen, auch solche formulam
> doctrinae mit aignen handen pure underschreiben und darüber gewöhnliche leibliche aydspflicht
> neben ubergebung ihrer scln'iftlichen revers wurklichen leisten und darbey versprechen sollen,

But in especial [we will] let it be our care with all diligence that in all and every church and
school, according to the direction of the little book lately put forth in the year, etc., 1605
under the name of our Count Wolfgang (which is entitled: *A thorough report out of the holy
divine Scripture of the chiefest articles of Christian doctrine*), the clear and pure word […]
of God be set forth and preached unfalsified to the hearers and subjects by all and every
minister of the church of this county, and unnecessary strife and divisions be so much the more
prevented. *Subscriptions and duty of the ministers of the church.* Insomuch that, to fulfil all
this, all ministers of the church and teachers shall promise with mouth and hand, and also
subscribe such *formula doctrinae* purely with their own hands, and thereupon actually perform
the customary bodily oath of duty, together with the delivery of their written *Revers*, and
promise therewith

By Sehling's account, the FC was restored in Hohenlohe in 1630 (Sehling 15, pp. 17, 675–679).

### 13.4 Ulm and Limpurg

Ulm's instructions for the officials of its rural territory (1567) bound the pastors to the CA,
the Apology and Ulm's own "subscribed" confession on the Lord's Supper, "which came forth
publicly in print" (Sehling 17/2, p. 270). In 1586 the preachers reported that doctrine was
taught throughout Ulm's territory according to Scripture, the CA, the Apology, the SA "and the
Concord lately subscribed" (Sehling 17/2, p. 277).

### 13.5 Schwäbisch Hall, Wimpfen and Ravensburg

At Schwäbisch Hall, ordinands subscribed the Book of Concord together with the city's church
order. **Schwäbisch Hall, ordination, *Kirchenordnung* 1614/15** (Sehling 17/1, p. 184):

<!-- doc 763 -->
> Zum vierdten sollen die ordinierten Personen ernstlich ermahnet werden, daß sie sich beydes,
> in der Lehr unnd im Leben, unverweißlich verhalten wöllen etc. Dessen zu mehrer caution sollen
> sie dem Concordi Buoch [Konkordienbuch 1580, BSLK S. 735-1100] und diser Kirchen Ordnung
> subscribieren und underschreiben [,,und underschreiben" fehlt Entwurf 1614].

Fourthly, the ordained persons shall be earnestly admonished that they will behave themselves
without reproach both in doctrine and in life, etc. For the greater security thereof they shall
subscribe and underwrite the Book of Concord [the Book of Concord of 1580] and this church order
["and underwrite" is wanting in the draft of 1614].

The imperial city of Wimpfen bound its clergy by oath and then by a written *Notula
subscriptionis*. In it the signer confesses the CA of 1530 and the FC "with this my own
handwriting", "by virtue of the oath performed". A passage struck through in the manuscript
speaks of laying three fingers on the BoC printed at Tübingen in 1580. **Wimpfen, *Eid und
Pflichten der Geistlichen*, [after 1577]** (Sehling 17/2, p. 530):

<!-- doc 930 -->
> Notula subscriptionis Ich, N. N. von N., bekhenne mit dieser meiner aigenen hanndschrifft, die
> in anno aintausendt fünffhundert unnd dreyzig zu Augspurg kayser Carolo dem fünfften
> uberantwortter confession zusampt die Formula Concordiae mit allen ihren einverleibten puncten
> unnd articuln, khein außgenommen, in affirmativa der hayligen, göttlichen schrifft gemes unnd
> recht sein, will auch mit Gottes hilff darbey beständig (crafft gelaisten juraments) bleiben
> unnd darwider nichts heimblichs noch offentliches die zeit meines lebens zu lehren, zu
> predigen, zu thun mich understehen noch auch andern, die solches thun werden, beyfall thun.
> Actum den etc.

*Note of subscription.* I, N. N. of N., confess with this my own handwriting that the confession
delivered at Augsburg to the emperor Charles the Fifth in the year one thousand five hundred and
thirty, together with the *Formula Concordiae*, with all the points and articles incorporated in
them, none excepted, are in the affirmative agreeable to the holy divine Scripture and right;
and I will also with God's help abide steadfastly thereby (by virtue of the oath performed), and
will not undertake to teach, preach or do anything secret or open against it all the time of my
life, nor give assent to others who shall do so. Done the, etc.

Ravensburg, a city of mixed confession, had its own local "formula concordiae", drawn up by the
evangelical preachers in 1578. Every preacher signed it under the terms of his *Bestallung*, and
it was renewed in 1617. **Ravensburg, *Konkordie und Kirchenordnung*, 28 January 1578, renewal
of 1617** (Sehling 17/2, p. 500):

<!-- doc 922 -->
> Allßdann in dem verschinen fünffzehenhundert achtunndsibentzigisten jahr durch die herren
> prediger deß heyligen evangelii nach Augspurgischer Confeßion alhie zue Ravenspurg etwelcher
> darinnen angezogner motiven und ursachen wegen ein formula concordiae verfasset unnd von allen
> bißheero bestellten predigern innhallt ihrer bestallungen subscribirt worden,

Whereas in the past year fifteen hundred and seventy-eight, for certain motives and causes
alleged therein, a *formula concordiae* was drawn up by the lords preachers of the holy gospel
according to the Augsburg Confession here at Ravensburg, and hath been subscribed by all the
preachers appointed hitherto according to the tenor of their *Bestallungen*,

Here, as at Danzig (§7.3), "formula concordiae" names a local agreement among the city's own
preachers, not the FC of 1577.

## 14. Franconia and Regensburg

Franconia gives the longest single record in the corpus. The Brandenburg-Ansbach priest's oath
of 1528 survives with its later forms recorded in the apparatus, so that one text shows the
whole development:

1. In 1528, an oath to the prince to preach "the holy gospel and word of God".
2. From 1570, an oath to the creeds, the CA, the Apology, the SA and a local "formula of
   concord".
3. From 1573, a printed *norma doctrinae* shared with Nürnberg.
4. From 1588, the Book of Concord.

Nürnberg refused the FC and kept the 1573 *norma doctrinae*. By the editors' account it remained
Nürnberg's confessional standard into the nineteenth century. Its neighbours Windsheim and
Weißenburg also refused the FC (Sehling 11, pp. 486, 656, 673). The imperial city of Regensburg,
by contrast, made every minister sign the BoC with his own hand before the consistory.

### 14.1 Brandenburg-Ansbach: the priest's oath (1528, 1570, 1573, 1588)

The base text of 1528 is an oath of allegiance. It is sworn to the margrave and confirmed "by
this my own handwriting", and it binds the pastor to nothing but the gospel.
**Brandenburg-Ansbach-Kulmbach, *Der evangelische Priestereid*, 1528** (Sehling 11, p. 107):

<!-- doc 264 -->
> Ich, N., erwelter oder berufner pfarher zu N., glob und schwer dem durchleuchtigen
> hochgeborenen fürsten herrn Georgen, markgrafen zu Brandenburg..., hiemit und in kraft dieser
> meiner aigen handschrift, daß ich das heilig evangelion und wort Gottes alts und neus
> testaments lauter und clar predigen soll und will und nichts, das dawider ist,

I, N., elected or called pastor at N., vow and swear to the most serene, high-born prince, Lord
George, margrave of Brandenburg…, hereby and by virtue of this my own handwriting, that I shall
and will preach the holy gospel and word of God of the Old and New Testaments purely and
clearly, and nothing that is against it,

The editors print the later wording from the surviving sworn copies. From 1570 to 1588 the
clause reads (Sehling 11, p. 107):

<!-- doc 264 -->
> 1570 bis 1588: das heilig evangelion und wort Gottes alts und neus testaments, der apostel und
> propheten schriften, den drei hauptsymbolis, der augsburgischen confession und derselben
> apologia, den schmalkaldischen artikeln und deren nächstverschiedenen siebenzigsten jahrs
> zwischen hochgedachten meines gnädigen herrn theologen und prädikanten verglichenen formulen
> concordiae gemäß lauter und clar

*1570 to 1588:* [that I shall preach] purely and clearly the holy gospel and word of God of the
Old and New Testaments, agreeably to the writings of the apostles and prophets, the three chief
creeds, the Augsburg Confession and the Apology thereof, the Smalcald Articles, and the formula
of concord agreed upon in the seventieth year last past between the theologians and preachers of
my aforesaid gracious lord.

The "formula of concord" of 1570 is again a local one, an agreement among the margrave's own
clergy. From 1573 the oath was followed by a list of books "of the *corpus doctrinae* and *norma
judicii*". This is the list that Ansbach shared with Nürnberg. It names the CA in both its last
edition and its first, "as ratified and subscribed at Naumburg in the year 61", and keeps the
whole Melanchthonian canon. From 1577 the Concord was added (Sehling 11, p. 109):

<!-- doc 264 -->
> 1573-1588:+Verzeichnis der bücher corporis doctrinae et normae judicii Die 3 hauptsymbola —
> das apostolisch, nicänisch und Athanasii. Herrn D. M. Lutheri kleiner und großer katechismus.
> Die augsburgische konfession und sonderlich auch neben der letzten die 1. edition - lateinisch
> und deutsch -, so zu Naumburg anno 61 von kur- und fürsten ratifiziert und unterschrieben
> worden. Die apologia der augsburgischen konfession. Die schmalkaldischen artikel. Die
> repetitio der augsburgischen konfession, so zu dem concilio zu Trient vom kurfürsten von
> Sachsen übergeben worden. Die loci communes Philippi Melanchthonis. Das examen theologicum
> Philippi Melanchthonis. Die definitio appellationum Philippi Melanchthonis. Die responsiones
> Philippi Melanchthonis ad impios articulos bavaricos. Die responsiones Philippi Melanchthonis
> de controversia Stancari. Die brandenburgische kirchenordnung. […] 1577-1588: + und sonderlich
> das concordiabuch

*1573–1588, added:* Register of the books of the *corpus doctrinae* and *norma judicii*. The
three chief creeds: the Apostolic, the Nicene and Athanasius's. Master Dr M. Luther's Small and
Large Catechism. The Augsburg Confession, and especially, beside the last [edition], also the
first edition, Latin and German, which was ratified and subscribed by electors and princes at
Naumburg in the year 61. The Apology of the Augsburg Confession. The Smalcald Articles. The
Repetition of the Augsburg Confession, which was delivered by the Elector of Saxony to the
council at Trent. The *Loci communes* of Philip Melanchthon. The *Examen theologicum* of Philip
Melanchthon. The *Definitio appellationum* of Philip Melanchthon. The *Responsiones* of Philip
Melanchthon to the impious Bavarian articles. The *Responsiones* of Philip Melanchthon on the
controversy of Stancarus. The Brandenburg church order. […] *1577–1588, added:* and especially
the Book of Concord.

From 1588 the oath itself names the creeds, the CA, the Apology, the SA, both catechisms and the
FC of 1580, "wherein the books just recounted are comprehended" (Sehling 11, p. 107):

<!-- doc 264 -->
> Seit 1588: das heilige evangelium und wort Gottes alten und neuen testaments, der propheten
> und apostel schriften, den drei hauptsymbolis, der augspurgischen confession und derselben
> apologia, den schmalkaldischen articuln, kleinen und großen catechismo Lutheri, der anno 1580
> zwischen den evangelischen chur- und fürsten augsburgischer konfession verglichenen und in
> druck verfertigten formula concordiae, darinnen jetzt erzählte bücher samt gründlicher
> erklärung der zwischen etlichen theologen augsburgischer confession vorgefallenenen streitigen
> artikel verfaßt und begriffen, auch ihrer fürstlichen gnaden vor der zeit publizierten
> kirchenordnung gemäß lauter und klar.

*Since 1588:* [that I shall preach] purely and clearly the holy gospel and word of God of the
Old and New Testaments, agreeably to the writings of the prophets and apostles, the three chief
creeds, the Augsburg Confession and the Apology thereof, the Smalcald Articles, Luther's Small
and Large Catechism, the *formula concordiae* agreed upon in the year 1580 between the
evangelical electors and princes of the Augsburg Confession and made ready in print, wherein the
books just recounted are comprehended, together with a thorough explanation of the articles in
controversy that arose between certain theologians of the Augsburg Confession, and also the
church order published aforetime by his princely grace.

The Ansbach consistory order of 1594 made the BoC the subject of the candidate's first
examination question. A man who had not read the whole book was given it to read, and examined
only afterwards. **Brandenburg-Ansbach-Kulmbach, *Konsistorialordnung*, 1594**
(Sehling 11, p. 388):

<!-- doc 291 -->
> sollen die verordneten unsers consistorii ihne zuvorderst befragen, ob er das buech der
> christlichen concordi, zwischen den christlichen churfürsten und stenden augspurgischer
> confession wie auch derselben theologen im 1580. jar publiciert, darinnen unserer kirchen
> einhellig bekanntnus vermög Gottes wort als der ainigen regul, norma und richtschnur, nach
> welcher alle lehrer und lehren gerichtet werden sollen, verfaßt, gelesen, nemblich die
> augspurgische confession, anno 30 Carolo V.dem römischen keyser ubergeben, sambt derselben
> apologia, den dreien symbolis, schmalkaldischen articuln und catechismis Lutheri mit
> angehengter christlicher und gründlicher erklerung etlicher strittigen articuln und unser
> kirchenordnung als unser corpus doctrinae und ob er mit allen diesen erzehlten schriften, so
> wir fur die göttliche ungezweifelte warheit halten, in allen und jeden articuln durchaus ainig
> und solche fur christlich und Gottes wort in allweg gemees erkenne und halte. Im fall er aber
> solche schrieften zuvor nicht gelesen hette, soll ime das ganze buech alsobald zu lesen
> zugestellt und, do solches von ime beschehen, alsdann erst zum examen zugelassen werden.

the appointed [members] of our consistory shall first of all ask him whether he hath read the
Book of Christian Concord, published in the year 1580 between the Christian electors and estates
of the Augsburg Confession and their theologians, wherein the unanimous confession of our
churches is set down according to God's word as the only rule, norm and line by which all
teachers and doctrines are to be judged, namely the Augsburg Confession delivered in the year 30
to Charles V, the Roman emperor, together with the Apology thereof, the three creeds, the
Smalcald Articles and Luther's catechisms, with the Christian and thorough explanation of
certain articles in controversy annexed; and our church order, as our *corpus doctrinae*; and
whether he is wholly at one with all these writings recounted, which we hold for the divine
undoubted truth, in all and every article, and acknowledgeth and holdeth them for Christian and
in every way agreeable to God's word. But in case he hath not read such writings before, the
whole book shall be given him straightway to read, and when he hath done so, then first shall he
be admitted to the examination.

The same order binds the consistorial assessors by oath to the FC (Sehling 11, p. 384).

### 14.2 Nürnberg and its neighbours: the 1573 *norma doctrinae* and the refusal of the FC

The Ansbach-Nürnberg *norma doctrinae* of 1573 has been given above. By Sehling's introduction,
it had to be signed by all the clergy in both territories in 1573 (Sehling 11, p. 486). By the
editors' note it was the confessional standard of Brandenburg-Ansbach and Nürnberg from 1573 to
1588, and of Nürnberg from 1573 "into the nineteenth century" (Sehling 11, p. 109). By Sehling's
introductions:

- **Refused the FC:** Nürnberg (Sehling 11, p. 486), Weißenburg (p. 656) and Windsheim (p. 673).
- **Split:** at Wertheim the clergy refused, but the count signed for himself (p. 706).
- **Signed:** Rothenburg (p. 565). So did the Swabian cities of Kaufbeuren, Kempten, Lindau and
  Memmingen, and the clergy of Nördlingen and Oettingen (Sehling 12).

### 14.3 Regensburg: the CA "in its right sense" (1567), the BoC signed before the consistory (1572/1588)

The imperial city's church order of 1567 receives the CA "in its right, true sense, which the
letter giveth it". It condemns by name the Anabaptists, Zwinglians, Schwenckfelders, Antinomians
and Osiandrians. Every minister is to be asked at his admission whether he holds the CA in this
sense. **Regensburg, *Kirchenordnung* (of 1567?)** (Sehling 13, p. 453):

<!-- doc 450 -->
> Und wie wir also annemen dieselbig confession in ihrem rechten, waren verstand, welchen ihr
> gibt der buchstabe, und apologia desselben 1530. jhars und hernach anno 1537 die artikel, zu
> Smalkalden gestelt und unterschrieben, auch allgemeiner christlicher consens unserer kirchen
> der augspurgischen confession und erklerung Lutheri, also verwerfen wir dagegen als irrig
> alles, was demselben verstand der augspurgischen confession zuwider ist, beide altes und
> neues, gegenwertiges und künftiges, als izt auch sind der widertaufer, zwingler,
> schwenckfelder, antinomer und osianderer etliche leren, bis solang wir eines andern aus
> heiliger schrift göttlichs worts überzeugt werden. Damit auch solche lere raine in unser
> kirchen bleibe und auf unsere nachkommen mit göttlicher verleihung möge gepflanzt werden, so
> ist versehen, das erstlich die kirchendiener alle darauf angenommen und austrücklichen gefragt
> werden, ob sie der reinen augspurgischen confession in ermeltem verstand und mit keiner andern
> widerwertigen lere behaft sind.

And as we thus receive the same confession in its right, true sense, which the letter giveth it,
and the apology thereof of the year 1530, and afterward, in the year 1537, the articles set
forth and subscribed at Smalcald, and also the general Christian consensus of our churches of
the Augsburg Confession, and Luther's explanation; so on the other hand we reject as erroneous
all that is contrary to that same sense of the Augsburg Confession, both old and new, present
and to come, such as are now also certain doctrines of the Anabaptists, Zwinglians,
Schwenckfelders, Antinomians and Osiandrians, until we be convinced otherwise out of the holy
Scripture of God's word. And that such doctrine may remain pure in our church, and by divine
grant be planted upon our descendants, it is provided that first of all the ministers of the
church be all received thereupon, and be expressly asked whether they [hold] the pure Augsburg
Confession in the said sense and are tainted with no other contrary doctrine.

The church government order of 1572, in its form of 1588, first records the confession of the
chamberlain and council themselves (§17.5). It then requires every minister and schoolmaster, at
the start of his appointment, to read the BoC, sign it "with his own hand" before the
consistory, and declare himself on each disputed article before the council and the whole
congregation. **Regensburg, *Kirchenregimentsordnung*, 1572/1588** (Sehling 13, p. 495):

<!-- doc 452 -->
> soll ein jeder kirchen- und schuldiener gleich anfangs seiner bestallung ermelt buch der
> concordien, nachdem er solches mit fleiß gelesen, vor den consistorialen mit 'eigener hand
> underschreiben und allda in specie aller secten, rotten und strittigen articuln halben inhalts
> ermelts buchs der Concordien unterschidlich und lauter erklären, vor einem erbaren rat und
> ganzen christlichen gemain, auch dessen ein offentliche bekentnus und zeugnus von sich geben.

every minister of church and school shall, at the very beginning of his appointment, after he
hath read the said Book of Concord with diligence, subscribe it with his own hand before the
consistorials, and there declare himself severally and plainly in particular concerning all
sects, factions and articles in controversy according to the content of the said Book of
Concord; and before an honourable council and the whole Christian congregation he shall also
give a public confession and testimony thereof.

The order adds a note particular to a mixed city: "since we must, alas, dwell in this city
within one ring wall with the papists", polemic is to be kept within bounds.

## 15. Strasbourg and Alsace

Strasbourg, which had signed the *Tetrapolitana* in 1530 as a mediating city, had subscribed the
CA by 1532. Its path then ran in four steps:

1. **1549:** the CA in the examination of ministers, an order issued under the Interim.
2. **1562:** a public confession at admission according to the CA and the Apology, promised
   "*stipulata manu*".
3. **1563:** the Strasbourg Concord.
4. **1577:** the FC signed "*corde et manu*".

In 1598 the FC was put into the ordination vow. Colmar, by contrast, swore its preachers to the
CA and Apology in 1575 and, now mediating, refused to add the FC in 1578.

### 15.1 The CA in the examination (1549)

The eighth question of Strasbourg's examination order asks whether the candidate acknowledges
the church of Strasbourg as a true church and the CA as "the sum of right Christian doctrine".
**Strasbourg, *Examensordnung*, 1549** (under the Interim; Sehling 20/1, p. 365):

<!-- doc 1318 -->
> Der achte: Erkennet ir auch disse jetzige unsere kirche zu Straßburg für ain ware kirch
> Christi, unsers lieben herrn, und den inhalt unser Augspurgischen Confession für die summa
> recht Christlicher lehre?

*The eighth:* Do ye also acknowledge this present church of ours at Strasbourg for a true church
of Christ our dear Lord, and the content of our Augsburg Confession for the sum of right
Christian doctrine?

### 15.2 "*Stipulata manu*" (1562) and "*corde et manu*" (1577); the FC in the ordination vow (1598)

The order of service of 1577 tells the history in brief. In 1562 the council ordered that no one
be admitted to the ministry who had not first made a public confession according to the CA and
Apology, as newly approved at Naumburg. In 1577 the ministers agreed to admit no one who would
not sign the FC "with heart and hand". **Strasbourg, *Gottesdienstordnung*, [after August
1577]** (Sehling 20/1, p. 512):

<!-- doc 1336 -->
> Die formulam doctrinae belangend ist anno 1562 durch ettliche abgeordnete herren vom rath
> einem ehrwurdigen kirchen convent, pfarrern und helffern befohlen worden, hinfurter keinen in
> den kirchen convent auffzunemen oder in das Strasburgische ministerium einzulassen, der nit
> zuvor sein bekentnuß von allen artickeln christlicher religion, wie die in der Augspurgischen
> confession und der selbigen Apologia (welche dazumal von den chur- und fursten und andern
> stenden Augspurgischer confession zur Naumburg auffs newe war approbirt und underschriben
> worden), offentlich gethan hette, welchem decreto senatus dazumal alle kirchendiener
> nachzukomen stipulata manu zusagten und versprachen. Demnach, als anno 1577 die Formula
> concordiae einem ehrsamen rath zu Straßburgk durch des […] hertzogs von Wirtenbergk legaten
> ist zugestelt und einhendig gemacht worden und die kirchen diener von andern orten derselben
> abschrifft bekomen und sie mit vleiß durchlesen und erwogen, haben sie sich alle einmütiglich
> verglichen, nit allein derselben gleichformig sich zu verhalten, sondern auch hinfort keinen
> in den convent auffzunemen, er underschreybe dan auch beyde corde et manu solch formula
> concordiae,

As touching the *formula doctrinae*: in the year 1562 it was commanded by certain lords deputed
from the council to a reverend church convent, pastors and helpers, henceforth to receive no one
into the church convent nor admit him into the ministry of Strasbourg who had not first made
public his confession of all the articles of the Christian religion, as they are in the Augsburg
Confession and the Apology thereof (which had at that time been approved anew and subscribed at
Naumburg by the electors and princes and other estates of the Augsburg Confession); which decree
of the senate all the ministers of the church then promised and undertook *stipulata manu* [with
hand given in pledge] to obey. Afterwards, when in the year 1577 the *Formula concordiae* was
delivered and handed to an honourable council at Strasbourg by the legate of the […] duke of
Württemberg, and the ministers of the church had received a copy of it from other places and had
read and weighed it with diligence, they all agreed with one accord not only to behave
themselves conformably thereto, but also henceforth to receive no one into the convent unless he
also subscribe the said *formula concordiae* both *corde et manu* [with heart and hand].

The footnote records that the council was more reserved than its ministers. In May 1578 it told
the duke that it would not be drawn into new controversies, and it appealed to the Strasbourg
Concord of 1563. In 1565 the Strasbourg clergy had also used the Pfalz-Zweibrücken Latin
subscription (§12.4).

The church order of 1598 put the FC into the ordination questions. **Strasbourg,
*Kirchenordnung*, 9 April 1598** (Sehling 20/1, pp. 640–641):

<!-- doc 1344 -->
> II. Glaubt ihr auch steiff alles, das uns die heilige Göttliche Schrifft fürgibt Und was in
> den Symbolis oder Summen unsers Christlichen Glaubens, als im gemeinen Symbolo Apostolorum,
> den vier großen haubt Concilien, Auch in der Confession und Apologia, von Chur- und Fürsten
> auf dem Reichstag zu Augspurg anno 1530 ubergeben, und derselben einhelliger in anno 1580
> publicierten Erklärung, welche die Formula Concordiae genennet wirdt, begriffen und widerholet
> ist? Begehret ihr auch, diesen Glauben zubekennen und zulehren mit besten Trewen biß an ewer
> Ende? […] Antwort: Ich glaub und halt diß alles Und bitte Gott, das Er mich darinn ohn alles
> wancken biß in sein himmlischs Reich erhalten wolle.

II. Do ye also believe steadfastly all that the holy divine Scripture setteth before us, and
that is comprehended and repeated in the creeds or sums of our Christian faith, namely in the
common Creed of the Apostles, the four great chief councils, and also in the Confession and
Apology delivered by electors and princes at the diet at Augsburg in the year 1530, and the
unanimous explanation thereof published in the year 1580, which is called the *Formula
Concordiae*? Desire ye also to confess and teach this faith with your best faithfulness unto
your end? […] *Answer:* I believe and hold all this, and pray God that he will preserve me
therein without any wavering unto his heavenly kingdom.

### 15.3 Colmar: an oath on the CA, and the refusal of the FC (1575, 1578)

Colmar introduced the Reformation in 1575. Its preachers swore, besides their citizen's oath, to
preach "agreeably to the Biblical Scriptures, together with the Fathers who agree with them, the
Augsburg Confession and its Apology". They also swore not to condemn publicly those "who are
still at variance in certain points in the reformed church". **Colmar, *Eid der Prediger*, 22
October 1575** (Sehling 20/2, p. 501):

<!-- doc 1408 -->
> Es sollen die predicannten neben irem burger aydt schweren, das sy allein die byblische,
> hailige Altes unnd Newes Testamennts schrifften sampt den Patribus, so deren durchaus
> ubereinstymmen, der Augspurgischen confession und deren Apologi gemeß one schmahen, schennden
> unnd lestern Iheren [sic], predigen unnd die heiligen sacrament außteylen wellen. Item, das sy
> auch niemannden, der sey was religion der wolle, furnemblich die, so noch inn der reformierten
> kirchen ettlicher puncten streyttig, offennlich verdammen,

The preachers shall swear, beside their citizen's oath, that they will teach, preach and
administer the holy sacraments only agreeably to the Biblical, holy Scriptures of the Old and
New Testaments, together with the Fathers who agree with them throughout, [and] to the Augsburg
Confession and the Apology thereof, without reviling, defaming and blaspheming. Item, that they
will also openly condemn no one, be he of what religion he will, and especially those who are
still at variance in certain points in the reformed church.

The words "and the Apology thereof" are a later addition in the margin. When the FC was urged on
the city in 1578, the council, by now mediating, declined. It had already tendered its preachers
an oath, and it would not press the FC on them against that oath. **Colmar, *Ablehnung des
Beitritts zur Konkordienformel*, 10 May 1578** (mediating; Sehling 20/2, p. 509):

<!-- doc 1408 -->
> jedoch so will unnß auß vorangeregten unnd erzelten ursachen, binn unnserm ann e[wer]
> f[urstlich] g[naden] außgangenen schreiben vermeldet, hochbedencklich sein, Formulam concordie
> wider von unnß unsern predigern einmal deferierdt und prestierdt juramentum (dessen inhalt
> e[wer] f[urstlich] g[naden] wir hievor zugeschickt) zu tringen. Bitten e[wer] f[urstlich]
> g[naden] darauff underthenigs vleiß, e[wer] f[urstlich] g[naden] wollenn unnß dißfalß fur
> entschuldiget halten unnd mit der subscribtion gnediglichen

yet for the causes aforesaid and recounted, set forth in our letter to your princely grace, it
will seem to us a very grave matter to press the *Formula concordiae* [upon our preachers],
against the oath once tendered by us to our preachers and performed by them (the content whereof
we have sent to your princely grace before). We therefore beseech your princely grace with
humble diligence that your princely grace will hold us excused herein, and graciously [spare us]
the subscription.

The sworn oath is here the reason for refusing the further subscription.

## 16. Transylvania

The Saxon church of Transylvania, outside the Empire, bound its ministers by oath to the CA. The
Burzenland chapter resolved in 1573 that newly received ministers should be taught the CA so
thoroughly that they could "swear to it solemnly with a good conscience" at the next convention.
**Burzenland, *Beschlüsse des Burzenländer Kapitels zum Eid auf die Confessio Augustana*, 1573**
(Sehling 24, p. 371):

<!-- doc 1694 -->
> 5. Pastores, qui recens ad ministerium receperunt ministros, lectionem et cognitionem
> [Augustanae] [Confessionis] ita inculcent eis, ut in proximo conventu exemplo pastorum bona
> conscientia solenniter in eam iurare possint.

5. Let the pastors who have lately received ministers into the ministry so inculcate upon them
   the reading and knowledge of the [Augsburg] [Confession], that at the next convention, after
   the example of the pastors, they may with a good conscience solemnly swear to it.

In 1601 the newly elected superintendent swore before the altar to keep and defend the religion
"contained in the CA … the Smalcald Articles and the ecumenical creeds". **Transylvanian Saxons,
*Ordnung der Superintendentenwahl*, 1601** (Sehling 24, p. 480):

<!-- doc 1722 -->
> Electus superintendens coram altari aperte, praeeunte voce sua domino Laurentio Kosch pastori
> Requiniensi iuramentum praestitit, se hactenus usitatam in ecclesiis Saxonicis Transsylvaniae
> religionem, in scriptis propheticis et apostolicis comprehensam, in confessione Augustana
> Carolo V. exhibitam et repetitam, articulis denique Schmalcaldicis et symbolis oecumenicis
> contentam constanter conservaturum ac defensurum.

The superintendent elect, before the altar, openly, Master Laurentius Kosch, pastor
*Requiniensis*, going before with his voice, performed the oath that he would constantly keep
and defend the religion hitherto used in the Saxon churches of Transylvania, comprehended in the
prophetic and apostolic writings, exhibited and repeated in the Augsburg Confession to Charles
V, and contained finally in the Smalcald Articles and the ecumenical creeds.

The FC is not named. The norm is the CA, the SA and the creeds, sworn to as the received
religion of a church that lived alongside Catholics, Reformed and Unitarians.

## 17. The laity: confirmation, communion, godparents, citizenship and office

No order in the corpus requires ordinary lay people to subscribe the CA, the FC or any *corpus
doctrinae*. Where an order speaks of "teachers and hearers" being bound to the corpus, as at
Thorn (1575, §7.3) and in Hohenlohe (1578, §13.3), it means the corpus is the standard of what
the hearers are taught. It does not mean they are to sign it. The laity were bound in five other
ways:

1. by **confirmation**, where a confirmation rite existed: a public profession of the catechism,
   a vow to remain in it, and, in the Hessian (mediating) and Reformed forms, a submission to
   church discipline;
2. by the **examination before communion**, and in Reformed churches by an explicit confession
   of the Reformed doctrine;
3. by the **catechism test before marriage**, and in one case before admission as a burgher;
4. by rules for **godparents**;
5. for **magistrates and officials**, by oaths and, in some cities, by subscription.

### 17.1 Confirmation: profession, vow and discipline

The Hessian church orders of 1539 (mediating), written with Bucer's help, created the evangelical
confirmation. The children are questioned on the chief articles before the congregation and
"give themselves publicly to Christ the Lord and his church". The pastor lays hands on them and
"confirms them to Christian fellowship". **Hesse, *Ziegenhainer Zuchtordnung*, 1539**
(mediating; Sehling 8, p. 104):

<!-- doc 2252 -->
> Es sollen auch die eltesten und prediger versehen, daß die kinder, so nun durch die
> catechismos im christlichen verstande so weit bracht sein, daß man sie billich solle zum tisch
> des Herren zulassen, uf ein fürnemes fest, als Ostern, Pfingsten und Weihnachten, für aller
> gemein dem pfarherr an darzu verordnetem ort von ihren eltern und pettern dargestellet werden,
> umb den die eltesten und alle ander diener des worts stehen sollen. Da solle der pfarherr
> dieselbigen kinder die fürnemsten stücke des christlichen glaubens befragen, und nachdem die
> kinder darauf geantwort, sich auch da offentlich Christo dem Herren und seiner kirchen ergeben
> haben, soll der pfarherr die gemein vermanen, den Herrn diesen kindern umb bestendigkeit und
> merung des h. Geistes zu bitten und solches gebet mit einer collect beschließen. Dem allem
> nach soll dann der pfarherr denselbigen kindern die hende auflegen und sie also im namen des
> Herrn confirmieren und zu christlicher gemeinschaft bestetigen, auch darauf zum tisch des
> Herren gehen heißen,

The elders and preachers shall also provide that the children who have now been brought so far
in Christian understanding by the catechisms that they may rightly be admitted to the Lord's
table, be presented on some chief feast, as Easter, Whitsuntide and Christmas, before the whole
congregation, by their parents and godparents, to the pastor at a place appointed for it, about
whom the elders and all other ministers of the word shall stand. There the pastor shall question
the same children on the chief parts of the Christian faith; and after the children have
answered thereto, and have also there given themselves publicly to Christ the Lord and his
church, the pastor shall exhort the congregation to pray the Lord for these children for
steadfastness and the increase of the Holy Ghost, and shall close such prayer with a collect.
After all this the pastor shall lay hands on the same children, and so confirm them in the name
of the Lord and establish them in Christian fellowship, and thereupon bid them go to the Lord's
table,

The Kassel church order of the same year (mediating) gives the questions. The child professes
that he is in the church by baptism and vows to remain in its fellowship. He also promises to
obey God's word and to accept rebuke from the elders. The running head of the page is misprinted
"1589". **Hesse, *Kasseler Kirchenordnung*, 1539** (mediating; Sehling 8, p. 125):

<!-- doc 2253 -->
> Frag: Bistu auch in der kirch und gemein Christi ? Antwort: Ja - Frag: Wie bistu darein
> kommen? Antwort: Durch den heiligen tauf. — Frag: Was ist der? Antwort: Das bad der
> widergeburt, dadurch ich von der angebornen sund gewaschen, Christo meinem Herren eingeleibet
> und mit ihm bekleidet worden bin. — Frag: Wiltu in der gemeinschaft erharren ? Antwort: Ja,
> durch die hülf des Herren in ewigkeit. — Frag: Was vermag diese gemeinschaft der kirchen
> Christi ? Antwort: Daß ich mich in aller gehorsam halt göttlichs worts, dasselbig von den
> verordeneten dienern der kirchen zun gesetzten zeiten und vornemlich uf die sontag mit recht
> gleubigem herzen höre, auch so mich die eltesten der kirchen oder auch andere christen, welche
> ja die seien, der sunde straffen, daß ich das in aller demut zur besserung aufnehm,

*Question:* Art thou also in the church and congregation of Christ? *Answer:* Yea. *Question:*
How camest thou thereinto? *Answer:* Through holy baptism. *Question:* What is that? *Answer:*
The washing of regeneration, whereby I have been washed from inborn sin, incorporated into
Christ my Lord, and clothed with him. *Question:* Wilt thou continue in the fellowship?
*Answer:* Yea, by the help of the Lord, for ever. *Question:* What requireth this fellowship of
the church of Christ? *Answer:* That I keep myself in all obedience to God's word, and hear the
same with a rightly believing heart from the appointed ministers of the church at the set times,
and chiefly on the Sundays; and also, if the elders of the church or other Christians, whosoever
they be, rebuke me for sin, that I receive it in all humility for amendment,

This is a vow of membership, not of confession. The child binds himself to the church's
fellowship and discipline, and no book is named. Strasbourg's church order of 1553 introduced
the same rite, "after confession made and free surrender to the discipline and obedience of the
church". **Strasbourg, *Kirchenordnung von Johannes Marbach*, 1553** (Sehling 20/1, p. 396):

<!-- doc 1321 -->
> Solche christliche und gantz notwendige ceremoni in der kirchen wider anzurichten, die
> getaufften kinder, nach dem sie jetz den catechismum erlernett, offentlich der kirchen
> furzustellen und die nach gethoner bekantnuß und freywilligem ergeben in die zucht und
> gehorsame der kirchen mit hennd aufflegung unnd dem gleubigen gebett dem herren, unserm Gott,
> und seiner lieben gemeind zubefelhenn,

To set up again in the church this Christian and altogether needful ceremony: to present
publicly to the church the baptized children, after they have now learned the catechism, and,
after confession made and free surrender into the discipline and obedience of the church, to
commend them to the Lord our God and to his dear congregation with the laying on of hands and
believing prayer,

The Reformed refugee church order of Marten Micron (Reformed), used in East Frisia, has three
questions. They ask for answers on the chief parts of the catechism, for a vow to remain in
"this confession of faith" and renounce the world and Satan, and for a vow to submit to
Christian discipline. **East Frisia (Reformed), *Microns Ordinancien* (1554), 1565**
(Sehling 7/1, p. 607):

<!-- doc 2116 -->
> Zum ersten, daß sie auf die sonderlichste haupstücken der christlichen religion (von welchen
> sie nach der ordnung des kleinen catechismi gefraget werden), ein jeder besonder, aufs kürzest
> antwort geben. Zum andern, ob sie auch durch die gnade Gottes in diser bekentnuß des glaubens
> bestendig bleiben, darnach ihr leben richten und die welt und dem satan mit allem ihrem pracht
> verleugnen wollen ? Antwort: Ja, wir. Zum dritten, ob sie nicht auch sich der christlichen
> straffe (nach dem wort Gottes) williglich unterwerfen wollen ? Antwort: Ja, wir.

First, that they give answer, each one severally and most briefly, upon the most special chief
parts of the Christian religion (whereupon they are asked after the order of the small
catechism). Secondly, whether they will also by the grace of God abide steadfast in this
confession of faith, order their life thereafter, and renounce the world and Satan with all
their pomp? *Answer:* Yea, we [will]. Thirdly, whether they will not also willingly submit
themselves to Christian correction (according to the word of God)? *Answer:* Yea, we [will].

Electoral Saxony's order of 1580 has no laying on of hands. It teaches that the catechism
examination is itself the "right Christian confirmation": the confirming of the faith that the
godparents confessed at baptism in the child's stead. **Electoral Saxony, *Kirchenordnung* of
Elector August, 1580** (Sehling 1, p. 425):

<!-- doc 44 -->
> Und sollen die pfarrer und kirchendiener das volk fleissig unterweisen, und. mit gutem grund
> berichten, das dis sei die rechte christliche confirmation oder firmung, das ist die
> bestetigung des glaubens, so die paten an stadt des neugetauften kindleins bekant, darauf auch
> das kind getauft worden, wann sie nemlich solches in diesem examine erinnert, und demselben in
> ihrem ganzen leben nachzukommen, fleissig ermanet werden.

And the pastors and ministers of the church shall diligently instruct the people, and inform
them with good ground, that this is the right Christian confirmation or firming, that is, the
confirming of the faith which the godparents confessed in the stead of the newly baptized child,
and whereupon also the child was baptized: namely when they are reminded of the same in this
examination, and are diligently exhorted to follow it all their life long.

### 17.2 Admission to communion

The Lutheran orders required every communicant to be "explored", that is, examined and absolved,
beforehand. Schweinfurt grounds this rule in the CA and the Apology themselves. **Schweinfurt,
*Kirchenordnung*, 1543** (Sehling 11, p. 640):

<!-- doc 304 -->
> soll niemand zum hochwirdigen sacrament gelassen werden, er habe sich denn des abents zuvor
> oder des morgens unter oder nach der mettin dem caplan angezeigt in aller mas, wie davon die
> unseren in der augspurgischen confession mit klaren, hellen, dürren worten eraus sagen und
> bekennen, nemlich in dem artikel von der messe: Nulli admittuntur nisi antea explorati, und in
> der apologia: Porrigitur sacramentum, his qui uti volunt, postquam sunt explorati atque
> absoluti.

no one shall be admitted to the most worthy sacrament unless he have announced himself to the
chaplain the evening before, or in the morning during or after matins, in every manner as our
[divines] say out and confess concerning it in the Augsburg Confession with clear, bright, plain
words, namely in the article of the Mass: *None are admitted unless they have first been
examined*; and in the Apology: *The sacrament is given to those who will use it, after they have
been examined and absolved.*

Pfalz-Neuburg's general articles of 1576 apply the same rule to the young. They add a catechism
test before marriage. **Pfalz-Neuburg, *Generalartikel*, 1576** (Sehling 13, p. 176):

<!-- doc 404 -->
> 2. Es sollen auch die pfarrer und kirchendiener under dem jungen volk niemand zum hochwürdigen
> sacrament zulassen, der nicht sein catechismum gelernet und aus demselben seines glaubens
> notturftige rechenschaft geben kan. 3. Da si auch in den ehestand sich begeben, dieselbige
> beede, so lang nicht verkundiget oder eingesegnet werden sollen, bis si die sex haubtstuk
> christlicher lehr wol gelernet haben.

2. The pastors and ministers of the church shall also admit no one among the young people to the
   most worthy sacrament who hath not learned his catechism and cannot give a sufficient account
   of his faith out of the same. 3. When they also enter into the state of marriage, the two
   shall not be proclaimed nor blessed until they have well learned the six chief parts of
   Christian doctrine.

The Württemberg visitation order of 1557 deals with those who stay away from the Supper "on
account of Zwinglian, Schwenckfeldian, Anabaptist or other sects". They are not refused a
confession they never offered. They are visited privately, twice, and then a third time before
two or three of the discipline-men. **Württemberg, *Visitationsordnung*, 1557**
(Sehling 16, p. 331):

<!-- doc 682 -->
> oder da ainer sich der Zwinglischen, Schwenckhfeldischer, widerteufferischenn oder annderer
> secten halber des nachtmals Christi enteussern wöltte, so soll ain jeder Khirchendiener
> schuldig sein, dieselbige Person zum ersten unnd andern malen allain für sich zubeschikkhen
> unnd allso sie privatim mit allen gutten, christenlichen Leeren und ermanungen beschaidenlich
> berichtenn.

or if anyone would withdraw himself from the Supper of Christ on account of Zwinglian,
Schwenckfeldian, Anabaptist or other sects, every minister of the church shall be bound to send
for the same person alone, the first and the second time, and so instruct him privately and
discreetly with all good Christian teachings and exhortations.

The Reformed orders went further. Nassau-Dillenburg (1582), once it had turned Reformed, admitted to
the Supper only those who had made a confession of "the Reformed doctrine". This applied to
strangers as well. **Nassau-Dillenburg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1582** (Reformed; Sehling 10, p. 163):

<!-- doc 184 -->
> 43. Nemo ad coenam Domini admittatur, nisi qui confessionem doctrinae reformatae pro more
> ecclesiae ediderit et habeat testimonium honestae vitae, sine quibus non admittendi sunt etiam
> exteri.

43. Let no one be admitted to the Lord's Supper unless he have made a confession of the Reformed
    doctrine after the custom of the church, and have the testimony of an honest life; without
    which not even strangers are to be admitted.

This is the nearest thing in the corpus to a lay confessional test, and it is Reformed.

### 17.3 Marriage and citizenship

A test of catechism knowledge could be a condition of civil standing. In the Reformed Kurpfalz
in 1599 couples who could not recite the chief parts were not to be married. No such "ignorant"
was to be received as a burgher or member of the commune without a written certificate from his
pastor. **Kurpfalz, *Mandat zum Katechismusunterricht*, 1 February 1599**
(Reformed; Sehling 19/2, p. 890):

<!-- doc 1175 -->
> keine neue eheleute, welche die capita nicht erzehlen können, proclamiren oder einsegnen, wie
> dan zue diesem ende auch unser gnedigster herr den hiervorigen befelch an die amptleute
> renoviren lassen, das solcher ignoranten keiner zum bürger oder gemeinßman angenommen werden
> solle, er habe dan von dem pfarrer des orts (alda er sich gedencke niderzulassen) ein
> schriftlich urkund und zeugnuß ufzulegen, das er dieselbe zum wenigsten erzelen könne.

[ye shall] proclaim or bless no new married couple that cannot recite the chief parts; as to
this end also our most gracious lord hath caused the former command to the officials to be
renewed, that none of such ignorant persons shall be received as a burgher or member of the
commune, unless he have a written certificate and testimony to show from the pastor of the place
(where he purposeth to settle) that he can at the least recite them.

Earlier, the Margraviate of Baden's mandate against the Anabaptists (1543) had made a negative
test a condition of settlement. One version, C in the apparatus, adds a clause: no one is to be
received as burgher or *Hintersasse* unless it was first certain that he did not belong to the
sect, with a sealed certificate of his former conduct. **Baden, *Wiedertäufermandat*, 1543,
version C** (Sehling 16, p. 515):

<!-- doc 704 -->
> Es sollen auch hinfür die ambtleut kheinen zu [bu]rger [Text zerstört] oder hindersäßen
> aufnhemen oder gedülden, man seie denn zuvor vergwist, das er diser verfürischen Sect nit
> anhengig, sich auch zuvorn an verdechtlichten orten nit gehalten, und er beneben seinem
> Manrecht [war]haffte unnd versigelte urkhund seines hievorigen verhaltens und wesens khonne
> auflegen.

The officials also shall henceforth receive or suffer no one as burgher [text destroyed] or as
*Hintersasse*, unless it be first made certain that he is not an adherent of this seducing sect,
nor hath kept himself before in suspected places, and that he can show, beside his certificate
of free birth, a true and sealed certificate of his former conduct and life.

### 17.4 Godparents

The godparent was the one lay office in the Church's rites, and the orders differ on whether he
must share the parish's confession.

**Excluded.** The Limpurg visitors (1611) were to ask whether the pastor "admits ignorant
children and heterodox persons to be godparents". **Limpurg, *Visitationsordnung*, 1611**
(Sehling 16, p. 629):

<!-- doc 730 -->
> 23. Ob er auch unverständige kinder undt Heterodoxos zue Gevatterschafften admittire.

23. Whether he also admit children without understanding and heterodox persons to be godparents.

**Examined first.** The synod of Wied (1564, Reformed) barred "men of another religion, who
detest our evangelical doctrine", unless they had first been examined by the ministers and given
an account of their faith. **Wied, *Synodalbeschlüsse*, 5 June 1564** (Reformed; Sehling 19/1, p. 466):

<!-- doc 1094 -->
> 6. Ne pueri pro susceptoribus infantium in S. baptismatis administratione admittantur neve
> etiam alienae religionis homines, qui nostram doctrinam Evangelicam detestantur, nisi prius a
> ministris Ecclesiae examinati suae fidei rationem reddiderint.

6. That boys be not admitted as sponsors of infants in the administration of holy baptism, nor
   yet men of another religion who detest our evangelical doctrine, unless they have first been
   examined by the ministers of the church and have given an account of their faith.

**Admitted with discretion.** Nassau-Weilburg (1609) told its pastors that people who did not
hold the unaltered CA in every point were not to be driven from the font, unless they were open
blasphemers. The reason given is that the institution of godparents is "not an express command
of God". **Nassau-Weilburg, *Erläuterungspunkte zur Kirchenordnung*, 1609**
(Sehling 10, p. 329):

<!-- doc 198 -->
> Nachdem es sich bißweilen begibet, das personen, so noch zur zeit der papisten aberglauben in
> vielen stucken zugethan oder sonsten sich nicht in allen puncten der reinen evangelischen lehr
> vermog der ungeenderten, in anno 30 ubergebenen Au[g]spurgischen Confession richtig finden
> konnen, zu gevattern gebetten werden, sollen die kirchendiender, weyl ohnedas die verordnung
> der gevattern nicht eben ein ausgetruckter befelch Gottes, sondern uß guten, erheblichen
> ursachen von menschen verordnet, in solchem fall vernunfftig und fursichtig handeln und nicht
> balt jemant, der nicht ein offentlicher lesterer Gottes, seines h. worts und der hochwurdigen
> sacramenten, von der h. tauf allerdings abtreiben und abhalten,

Forasmuch as it sometimes happeneth that persons who are still addicted in many things to the
superstition of the papists, or otherwise cannot find themselves right in all points of the pure
evangelical doctrine according to the unaltered Augsburg Confession delivered in the year 30,
are asked to be godparents: the ministers of the church shall in such case deal reasonably and
prudently (since the ordinance of godparents is not an express command of God, but was ordained
by men for good and weighty reasons), and not hastily drive away and keep altogether from holy
baptism anyone who is not an open blasphemer of God, of his holy word and of the most worthy
sacraments.

**Both religions admitted.** In biconfessional Soest the council confirmed in 1583 that, for the
sake of peace, godparents of both religions, Roman and Augsburg, had been freely admitted, and
it left the practice unchanged. **Soest, *Anweisung zu den Taufpaten*, 8 June 1583**
(Sehling 22, p. 478):

<!-- doc 1534 -->
> Nachdem biß anhero zu erhaltungh guttenn freidtstandtz unnd einigkeitt zwischenn beider
> religion, so woll der romischenn kirchenn alß dero Augßpurgischer unverfelscheter Confession,
> anno drißigh kay[serlicher] ma[jes]t[ä]tn ubergebenn, verwandtenn stenden das sacrament der
> hilger tauff einer mitt dem anderen zu haltenn, zubestehenn unnd zubekleidenn ohn underscheidt
> frey unnd zugelaßenn wordenn,

Forasmuch as hitherto, for the preserving of good peace and unity between the estates related to
both religions, as well the Roman church as the unfalsified Augsburg Confession delivered in the
year thirty to the imperial majesty, it hath been free and permitted without distinction for the
one to hold, to stand at and to attend the sacrament of holy baptism with the other,

### 17.5 Magistrates and officials

Lay office-holders were bound more strictly than the common people:

- **Councils.** The Heilbronn council swore in 1530, soon after the Diet of Augsburg, to stand
  by the council's majority "whether in the matter of the gospel or otherwise". The accompanying
  council ordinance says that the whole citizenry had been asked its mind and found willing.
- **The council as confessor.** The council of Regensburg made its own confession to the creeds,
  the CA, the Apology, the SA and Luther's catechism (1572).
- **Assessors.** Consistorial assessors swore to the church's confession in Ernestine Saxony
  (1569, §6.1), Prussia (1584, §7.2) and Ansbach (1594, §14.1).
- **City officials.** At Hildesheim in 1593 the burgomasters, syndics, physicians and
  secretaries signed the FC and its Apology "with their own hand" (§10.1).
- **Elders.** The Reformed Kurpfalz had its elders vow to the presbytery order (about 1601).

**Heilbronn, *Eid des Heilbronner Rates auf den neuen Glauben*, 18 November 1530**
(Sehling 17/1, p. 290):

<!-- doc 771 -->
> Es solle ein yeder globen und schwern, was gemaine stat und ein ersamen radt, es sey von des
> ewangeliums oder in ander weg, angee und anlange, bey einem ersamen radt und desselben mers
> sten und bleiben, leib und gut dar strecken wolle und nach seinem besten vermogen zu nutz
> gemainer stat handeln.

Every one shall vow and swear that, in whatsoever concerneth and toucheth the common city and an
honourable council, be it of the gospel or in other ways, he will stand and abide by an
honourable council and the majority thereof, and will stake body and goods [thereon], and will
act to the profit of the common city according to his best ability.

**Regensburg, *Kirchenregimentsordnung*, 1572/1588** (Sehling 13, p. 494):

<!-- doc 452 -->
> Nachmals bekennen sich auch ein erbar cammerer und rate zu den dreien symbolis oder
> glaubensbekantnussen: nemlich der aposteln, nicenischen und Athanasii, item zu der rainen,
> unverfelschten augspurgischen confession, wie die keiser Caroln dem fünften auf gehaltenem
> reichstag zu Augspurg, der wenigern zahl im dreißigsten, überraicht, zu der darüber gestellten
> apologia, den schmalkaldischen articuln und catechismo des teuren manns Gottes Martini
> Lutheri, als solchen schriften und bekentnussen, die aus grund Gottes wort gezogen, mit den
> schriften altes und neues testaments gleichförmig und überainstimmen, inmaßen alle christliche
> churfürsten und stände augspurgischer confession in rechtem, aigentlichen verstand derselben
> zugetan, sich sambt ihren kirchen- und schuldienern einhellig und christlich mit herzen, hand
> und mund bekent und dieselbige underschrieben haben.

Afterwards an honourable chamberlain and council also confess themselves to the three creeds or
confessions of faith, namely the Apostles', the Nicene and Athanasius's; item to the pure,
unfalsified Augsburg Confession, as it was delivered to the emperor Charles the Fifth at the
diet held at Augsburg in the lesser number [of years] in the thirtieth; to the Apology set forth
thereupon; to the Smalcald Articles and the catechism of the dear man of God, Martin Luther; as
such writings and confessions as are drawn from the ground of God's word, and agree and accord
with the writings of the Old and New Testament; even as all Christian electors and estates of
the Augsburg Confession, attached to the same in its right and proper sense, together with their
ministers of church and school, have confessed it with one accord and Christianly with heart,
hand and mouth, and have subscribed the same.

**Kurpfalz, *Presbyterordnung*, about 1601** (Reformed; Sehling 14, p. 598). The inspectors are
to have the newly chosen elders vow to the published order, which they must first have read or
heard read, and so confirm them:

<!-- doc 518 -->
> auch uf diese unsere publicirte ordnung (die sie zuevor entweder gelesen oder lesen gehört
> sollen haben) angeloben laßen und sie also confirmirn und mit pflichten annehmen sollt.

and ye shall cause them to vow upon this our published ordinance (which they shall before have
either read or heard read), and so confirm them and receive them under duty.
