# Ecclesiastical Duties in the Sehling Church Orders

This guide sets out what the evangelical church orders of the sixteenth century, printed in Emil
Sehling's *Die evangelischen Kirchenordnungen des XVI. Jahrhunderts*, required of each estate in
the church. It asks:

- **Clergy.** What were pastors and deacons, superintendents, members and presidents of
  consistories, and professors of theology expected or required to do?
- **Training.** How were ministers trained: school, university and stipend, practice preaching,
  examination, a probationary period, and continuing study after ordination?
- **Laity.** What did the orders require of ordinary church members: attendance, prayer at home,
  confession and communion? Were sextons, schoolmasters and other church workers held to a
  higher standard?
- **Schoolmasters.** What were their church duties?
- **The magistrate.** What were the prince's and the council's duties towards the church?
- **Catechesis.** How often was the catechism taught, and for how long? What did it require? Was
  first communion separate from confirmation, and did each require instruction? Did any order
  give communion to infants? Was there catechesis or counselling before marriage?

**How the guide is laid out.**
- §1 answers each question in brief, and §2 sets out the scope, the sources and the negative
  findings.
- §§3–6 cover the clergy:
  - §3, pastors and deacons;
  - §4, superintendents and deans;
  - §5, consistories, their presidents and members;
  - §6, professors of theology.
- §7 covers the training of ministers.
- §8 covers the laity and the church workers.
- §9 covers schoolmasters, and §10 the magistrate.
- §11 covers catechesis, where all the estates meet: its frequency and length, first communion,
  confirmation, instruction before marriage, and infant communion.
- §12 is a table by order, and §13 a concordance of every order quoted.

**Conventions**

- **Quotations.** Every quotation is Sehling's text as it stands in the `eko.db` database built
  from the digitized edition.
  - Footnote numbers, sigla, folio markers and interleaved apparatus are left out.
  - Sehling's own supplied words in square brackets are kept, as are "[!]" and his other signs.
  - Spelling and punctuation are as printed, including plain OCR errors.
  - "[…]" marks an omission.
  - Every quotation has been checked against the database text, allowing for OCR noise: at least
    90 per cent of its words must be found there in order. Its page has been checked against the
    edition's page markers.
  - Each quotation is preceded by an HTML comment, invisible when rendered, that names the
    `eko.db` document it was checked against.
- **Translations.** The English is formal-equivalence, in the idiom of the Authorized Version:
  - Word order and clause structure are kept where English allows.
  - Stock terms are rendered the same way throughout. *Pfarrer* and *Pfarrherr* are "pastor";
    *Kirchendiener* is "minister of the church"; *Superattendent* and *Superintendent* are
    "superintendent"; *Kirchner*, *Küster*, *Custos* and *Opfermann* are "sexton"; *Hausvater*
    is "householder"; *Gesinde* is "servants"; *Verhör* and *examen* are "examination";
    *aufbieten* and *proclamieren* are "proclaim" (the banns); *copulieren* and *einsegnen* are
    "marry" and "bless".
  - Words the translation supplies are in square brackets.
- **Citations.** Citations are in the form "Sehling volume, page".
  - The half-volumes of the edition are written 6/1, 6/2, 7/1, 7/2.1, 7/2.2, 17/1, 17/2, 19/1,
    19/2, 20/1 and 20/2, following the digitized volumes.
  - Each order is named by place, title and date as they appear in Sehling's running heads,
    which sometimes differ from the database's record headings. Where a passage is Sehling's own
    introduction or note, this is said.
- **Traditions.** Most of the orders cited are Lutheran, and they are not marked. Every order of
  another tradition is marked where it is cited, by its name in brackets after the order, as
  "Kurpfalz 1563 (Reformed)". The traditions follow the inventory in
  [`CHURCH_ORDERS_GUIDE.md`](CHURCH_ORDERS_GUIDE.md), §3:
  - **Reformed**: the Swiss, Calvinist and Heidelberg churches, among them the German
    territories that turned Reformed after 1560 and the French and Dutch stranger churches;
  - **mediating**: orders that stood between the Lutheran and the Reformed: the Upper German
    cities of Bucer's circle before the Interim, Philip of Hesse's church, Hermann von Wied's
    Cologne order, and the Philippist churches of Bremen and Colmar.
  - The mark follows the order cited, not the territory, since a territory could change its
    tradition: Nassau-Dillenburg is Lutheran to 1570, mediating in 1575 and Reformed from 1581.
  - The Transylvanian Saxons' own orders and synods are Lutheran; only the diet decrees of the
    principality are multi-confessional.

## 1. Summary of findings

**1. Pastors: preach, administer, catechize, visit, study, and live as an example** (§3). The
visitation questions sum up the office: preaching, the sacraments, the catechism, weddings, and
visiting the sick and sorrowful (Strasbourg 1598). The pastor's other duties:
- **study** a set list of books: the Bible, Luther's postil, Melanchthon's *Loci* and the
  Augsburg Confession, with copies bought for every parish (Saxony 1557; Hoya 1581);
- **marry**, keep out of taverns and trade, and bring up wife, children and servants as a model
  household, which the visitors inspected;
- **reside**, never leaving the parish overnight without leave and a substitute (Hoya 1581);
- **keep registers** of baptisms, marriages and burials from mid-century (Saxony 1580).

The deacons had the catechism and the sick as their particular charges, "readier to serve the
poor herein than the rich" (Saxony 1555).

**2. Superintendents: visit, hold synod, examine, report** (§4). The Saxon pattern of 1555–1557
gives the superintendent four tasks:
- to watch over the doctrine, life and diligence of the pastors;
- to visit unannounced, hearing sermons and examining the youth;
- to hold a synod every year between Easter and Whitsun;
- to examine and install new pastors, and send to the consistory what he cannot mend.

By 1600 the superintendent was sworn to the confessional corpus and acted as the consistory's
agent (Grubenhagen). In Transylvania the chapters elected their own deans (Lasseln 1572).

**3. Consistories: theologians and jurists; the president disputed** (§5). The consistory judged
marriage cases, church discipline "so far as the degrees of admonition" go, the affairs of
ministers, and church property (Saxony 1580). The orders differed over who should preside:
- the prince in person (Weimar 1561);
- the superintendent (Jena 1569);
- a nobleman (Dresden 1580);
- a lay councillor, so that the clergy should not seem to "lay hold on the worldly sword … like
  the Pope" (Regensburg 1588).

The consistories met weekly and were sworn to secrecy.

**4. Professors: no seminaries** (§6). No order founds a seminary. Professors of theology appear
in the orders as members of consistories, as examiners and ordainers (Wittenberg, Leipzig,
Marburg, Helmstedt), as overseers of the stipendiaries, and as public lecturers.

**5. Training: stipend, practice sermon, examination, probation, synod** (§7). There was no
vicarage year, but training had parts that did similar work:
- stipends for poor students, bound to serve the territory;
- the Tübingen *Stipendium* (1559), where masters preached in the house and were corrected "*in
  inventione, dispositione oder pronunciatione*" the same day;
- practice sermons in the parish with the pastor's leave (Saxony 1555);
- examination and a trial sermon before ordination, and if needed some weeks of instruction at
  Wittenberg or Marburg;
- a probation as deacon before a parish, with a re-examination (Palatinate 1564, Reformed), and
  the new deacons' sermon outlines sent in for criticism after a year (Palatinate 1607,
  Reformed).

After ordination the synods set reading "that the priests may be moved the more diligently to
read and the sooner to buy books" (Nassau 1552). The Reformed monthly convents criticized each
member's sermon and disputed theological questions (Palatinate 1571, Reformed).

**6. Laity: attend, pray at home, commune, be examined** (§8). Every householder had to bring
children and servants to sermon and catechism. Absentees were counted house by house and fined
(Nassau-Weilburg 1576). The legal minimum for communion was once or twice a year (Hohenlohe
1588), with Luther's "once or four times" as the ideal. Parents were to teach the catechism at
home, and the youth to pray "morning and evening, also before and after meals and at their work"
(Dillenburg 1582).

**7. Church workers: a higher standard** (§8.4). Sextons, schoolmasters and organists were held
to the conduct required of ministers: "like other ministers of the church, of honest and godly
conduct, no drunkards" (Hesse 1566, mediating). They were barred from taverns (Hoya 1581) and
questioned at visitation about their own communion. The minister's wife, children and servants
were inspected with him.

**8. Schoolmasters: the catechism as primer** (§9). The schoolmaster taught the catechism, used
it as the reading book, led the boys to sermon and catechism, led the singing, and was visited
four times a year by the pastor (Esslingen 1534, mediating; Saxony 1555, 1580). In the towns
schoolmistresses brought the girls (Palatinate 1595, Reformed).

**9. The magistrate: keeper of both tables** (§10). The Lutheran magistrate was to "uphold both
tables of the Ten Commandments … in maintaining outward discipline" (Mecklenburg 1552). He was
the "defender and nourisher" of churches and schools (Transylvania 1572). In practice he:
- ordered visitation;
- appointed and paid ministers;
- enforced attendance;
- punished what the church could only admonish;
- in the Palatinate (1599, 1608, Reformed), refused citizenship and marriage to those who did
  not know the catechism.

The Reformed limited his office to "the body and temporal goods" (Hanau 1609, Reformed).

**10. Catechesis: how often, how long, and what it required** (§11).
- **Rhythm:** a weekly Sunday catechism of about half an hour, quarterly fortnight-long series
  (Saxon visitations 1529), a yearly examination in Lent, and house-by-house examinations.
- **Requirement:** the text of the five or six chief parts by heart, and enough understanding to
  "pray and give a fair account of the chief articles" (Saxony 1555).
- **Before first communion:** at Hohenlohe (1578), children of about twelve were taught "every
  day for an hour" from Invocavit to Palm Sunday.

**11. First communion and confirmation: usually one act** (§11.3). Where the orders have
confirmation, the children are "confirmed and admitted to communion for the first time" together
(Hesse 1566, mediating). One course of instruction served both. Mansfeld (1580) examined them
publicly the day before, monthly. Lauenburg (1585) confirmed twice a year, and Hessen-Kassel
(1610, Reformed) heard them again after six months. Where there was no rite, as in Electoral
Saxony, examination alone admitted to communion.

**12. Infant communion: acknowledged as ancient, set aside** (§11.6). No order gives the Supper
to infants or argues for it. Four orders name the practice:
- Hesse (1566, mediating) and Hohenlohe (1578) grant that infants communed in the time of
  Cyprian and Augustine, but defer the Supper because of 1 Corinthians 11:28 and "the common
  judgment of the first church". Hesse leaves the old custom "in its worth".
- Oldenburg (1573) says that infants cannot examine themselves or discern the Lord's body.
- The Reformed preacher at Markirch (1561) calls it an error of the ancients, who
  thought that the uncommunicated were lost.

Children who died before first communion were buried like adults (Regensburg 1572/1588;
Henneberg 1582). A dying child who knew the catechism might commune before confirmation
(Nassau-Dillenburg 1575, mediating).

**13. Before marriage: examination, not counselling** (§§11.4–11.5). From the 1560s many orders
refused to proclaim or marry young people who did not know the catechism (Saxony 1580; Hesse
1566, mediating; Zweibrücken 1574), "since they are to keep house and get children and
servants". Some married them on a pledge, backed if need be by sureties, to attend the catechism
afterwards (Wied 1616, Reformed). There was no counselling course. What the orders required was:
- an interview some weeks before, on freedom to marry, parents' consent and the catechism
  (Limpurg 1610: three weeks);
- confession and communion before the wedding (Schwäbisch Hall 1615; Freudenthal 1592);
- the public exhortation and wedding sermon on the duties of marriage.

## 2. Scope, sources and method

### 2.1 Sources and searches

The guide draws on the full text of the Sehling edition in `eko.db`: the church orders,
consistorial and visitation orders, synodal decrees, school and stipend ordinances, and
visitation protocols printed with them. The corpus was searched for the vocabulary of each
question, in High and Low German and Latin spellings:
- **Offices:** *Pfarrer*, *Diaconus*, *Kaplan*, *Superintendent*, *Superattendent*, *Inspector*,
  *Decanus*, *Consistorium*, *Präsident*, *Director*, *Professor*, *facultas theologica*.
- **Training:** *Stipendium*, *Stipendiat*, *Alumnus*, *Probpredigt*, *examen*, *ordinandus*,
  *Synodus*, *Convent*, *censura*, *studieren*, *Bücher*.
- **Laity:** *Hausvater*, *Gesinde*, *daheim*, *Kirchgang*, *Strafe*, *Küster*, *Opfermann*,
  *Schulmeister*, *Schulfrau*.
- **Magistrate:** *Obrigkeit*, *custos utriusque tabulae*, *beide Tafeln*.
- **Catechesis:** *Katechismus*, *Kinderlehre*, *Verhör*, *Examen*, *zum ersten Mal* with
  *Sakrament* or *Nachtmal*, *confirmieren*, and *Katechismus* within a short distance of
  *Braut*, *Eheleute*, *copulieren*, *aufbieten* or *Hochzeit*.
- **Infant communion:** *kinder*, *kindlein*, *unmündig*, *infantes*, *parvuli* and *pueri*
  within a short distance of *Nachtmal*, *Abendmahl*, *Sakrament*, *coena* or *communio*; *prüfe
  sich selbst* and *probet se ipsum* near *Kinder*; *Cyprian*, *Augustin* and *alte Kirche* near
  *Kinder*; *Böhmen*, *Picarden* and *Hussiten*.

Each hit was read in its context. Pages were checked against Sehling's running heads, which
often correct the database's record headings. Among the corrections:
- the Mansfeld agenda quoted is of 1580, though its record is dated 1562;
- the Schwäbisch Hall passages on marriage belong to the revision of 1615, not the order of
  1543;
- the consistory order of 1608 is Ysenburg-Birstein's (Reformed), and the deacons' rule of 1550
  is Breslau's;
- the Palatine passages (Reformed) on probation and convents are from the *Kirchenratsordnung*
  of 1564 and Wenzel Zuleger's report of 1571, though their record is dated 1585.

### 2.2 What was not found

- **No seminary.** No order founds an institution for training ministers apart from the
  universities, the stipend houses and the Latin and cloister schools. The word *seminarium*
  occurs only in the old sense of a "nursery" of church and state.
- **No vicarage year.** No order prescribes a fixed period of practical training under a pastor
  before ordination. The nearest equivalents are practice preaching (§7.2), the probation as
  deacon (§7.4), and Wittenberg's "some weeks" of instruction (§7.3).
- **No continuing-education requirement in hours or courses.** Continuing study was enforced
  through synods, convents, assigned reading and visitation questions (§7.5).
- **No premarital counselling course.** Instruction before marriage meant an examination in the
  catechism and in the freedom to marry, and often confession and communion. The teaching on
  marriage itself was given publicly in the wedding exhortation and sermon (§11.5).
- **No paedocommunion.** No order gives the Supper to infants or defends the practice, and none
  mentions the Bohemian communion of infants. The orders that name it treat it as an ancient
  custom now set aside (§11.6).
- **No separate confirmation course.** Where confirmation existed it coincided with first
  communion, and one instruction served both (§11.3).
- **No prayer rule for lay church workers.** The lay offices had duties of conduct and
  attendance, not of prayer. The daily office bound schools and convents, not sextons (§8.4).

## 3. Pastors and deacons

### 3.1 The office in a list

**The visitation questions.** The orders rarely define the pastor's office in the abstract. They
list what the visitors are to ask about, and those lists are the best summary of what was
expected. Strasbourg's order of 1598 puts the whole office in one sentence and then turns to the
minister's life and household. **Strasbourg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1598** (Sehling 20/1, p. 689):

<!-- doc 1344 -->
> Soll derwegen bei der Kirchen Visitation gehandelt werden: 1. Von der Kirchendiener (es seie
> deren an jedem Orth einer oder mehr) offentlichem Ampt und Befelch, Ob sie denselben mit
> gebürendem Fleiß und Trew verrichten, Als mit Predigen, Sacramenta reichen, Catechismum
> halten, Hochzeiten einsegnen und Krancke und Betrübte besuchen und trösten und anderm
> dergleichen, 2. Von ihrem, der Kirchendiener, Leben und Wandel, Auch von ihrer Haußhaltung,
> Weib, Kinder und Gesinde,

Therefore at the visitation of the church there shall be dealt with: 1. The public office and
charge of the ministers of the church (whether there be one or more of them in each place),
whether they perform the same with due diligence and faithfulness, as with preaching, giving the
sacraments, holding the catechism, blessing weddings, and visiting and comforting the sick and
the sorrowful, and other such things; 2. Their life and conduct, the ministers', and also their
housekeeping, wife, children and servants.

The same headings recur everywhere: preaching ([`SERMONS_GUIDE.md`](SERMONS_GUIDE.md)), the
sacraments and the occasional rites
([`EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md`](EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md)), the catechism (§11 below), the
sick, and the minister's own life. The daily office laid no breviary obligation on the parish
pastor ([`OFFICES_GUIDE.md`](OFFICES_GUIDE.md), §6.5).

### 3.2 Study and the pastor's books

**A reading list.** The Saxon General Articles of 1557 name the books every pastor must read:
the Bible, Luther's postil, Melanchthon's *Loci*, and the Augsburg Confession with its Saxon
*Repetition* of 1551. The elector had a copy of the confessions printed for every parish, to
stay in the church's inventory. **Electoral Saxony, *General-Artikel und gemeiner Bericht*,
1557** (Sehling 1, p. 322):

<!-- doc 37 -->
> Sie sollen ihr weib und kind zur zucht und erbarkeit ziehen, aller unehrlichen hantierungen,
> auch wein - oder bierschenkens, kaufmanschaft und dergleichen hendel sich enthalten, auf der
> canzel die leute ex affectu nicht schmehen, ires studii und ambts warten, und sonderlich neben
> der biblia die postillen Lutheri, locos theologicos Philippi lateinisch oder deutsch, die
> augspurgische confession sampt derselben obbemelten repetition sollen sie oft und fleissig
> lesen, und alle ire predigten und lehr auf dieselben confessionen und declarationen gründen
> und richten, damit einerlei form und eintrechtigkeit in der lehr in s. churf. g. landen
> erhalten, und auf die nachkommenden gepflanzt werde

They shall bring up their wife and children in discipline and honesty, abstain from all
dishonest dealings, also from selling wine or beer, from merchandise and such trades, not revile
the people in the pulpit *ex affectu*, wait upon their study and office; and especially, besides
the Bible, they shall read often and diligently Luther's postils, Philip's *Loci theologici* in
Latin or German, [and] the Augsburg Confession together with its aforesaid Repetition, and
ground and direct all their sermons and teaching upon these same confessions and declarations,
that one form and concord in doctrine may be kept in his Electoral Grace's lands and planted for
those that come after.

**Books bought by the parish.** Where a pastor was too poor to buy them, the parish bought them.
Hoya in 1581 orders the churchwardens to provide "a good Wittenberg Bible, Doctor Luther's
postils and catechisms", the parish making up any shortfall, "that such books may be bought for
the church and remain there" (Sehling 6/2, pp. 1140–1141). The pastor's private study was an
item of visitation: the Limpurg visitation order of 1611 asks "what writings and books he
readeth, and what his private studies be", and the visitor is to exhort him to study if he is
found idle (Sehling 16, p. 627). Synods set reading for the year ahead (§7.5).

### 3.3 Life, household and conduct

**An example to the flock.** The same Saxon articles require ministers and schoolmasters to be
"right and pure in doctrine" and blameless in life, to marry, and to keep out of taverns.
**Electoral Saxony, *General-Artikel und gemeiner Bericht*, 1557** (Sehling 1, p. 322):

<!-- doc 37 -->
> so wollen s. churf. g. das beide kirchen - und schulen - diener vornemblich in der lehre
> richtig und reine sein, auch sonsten in irem leben und wandel sich beide in worten, werken und
> kleidunge erbarlich gegen menniglich, freundlich, züchtig und demütig, und in summa
> allenthalben und in allem christlich und also vorhalten, das sie menniglich, und sonderlich
> ihren schul- und kirchen-kindern nicht anstössig, sondern dermassen mit gutem exempel
> Vorgehen, das die pfar-kinder und sonsten menniglich denselbigen mit lust und frucht
> seliglichen und ane ergernis folgen mügen, hiezu dann vornemblich dienen würde, da sie zu
> vormeidunge menschlicher uppigkeit und heraus folgenden Verdachts sich in den heiligen
> ehestand begeben, […] ires ampts und studirens fleissig abwarteten, saufens, spaciren - gehens
> und anderer ergerlichen leichtfertigkeit, auch aller taberneu und schenk - heuser enthielten

his Electoral Grace will that both ministers of churches and of schools be above all right and
pure in doctrine, and otherwise in their life and conduct, both in words, works and clothing,
behave themselves honestly towards every man, friendly, chastely and humbly, and in sum
everywhere and in all things christianly, and so that they be no stumbling block to any man, and
especially to their school- and church-children, but go before with so good an example that the
parishioners and every man else may follow them blessedly with delight and fruit and without
offence. Whereto it would chiefly serve that they, to avoid human wantonness and the suspicion
that followeth from it, enter into holy matrimony, […] wait diligently upon their office and
study, [and] abstain from drinking, from walking abroad and other offensive frivolity, and from
all taverns and ale-houses.

The articles allow a pastor to sell by the cask the wine or beer of his own glebe or tithe,
"only that they hang out no tavern sign nor set guests to drink in the house"
(Sehling 1, p. 322). Their dress is treated in
[`CHURCH_DECORUM_GUIDE.md`](CHURCH_DECORUM_GUIDE.md), §8.

**The household inspected.** The minister's wife, children and servants were visited with him.
The Württemberg visitation order of 1559 tells the special superintendent to find out "whether
the ministers instruct their children in prayer and the catechism, and keep their boys at school
or teach them themselves at home", and to ask each pastor about the "doctrine, life and
housekeeping" of his colleagues and neighbours "and of their wives and children"
(Sehling 16, p. 387). A Württemberg visitation order of 1547 gives the reason from 1 Timothy 3:
"he that ruleth not his house well, how shall he rule the house of the Lord?"
(Sehling 16, p. 151). The Oldenburg visitation articles of 1609 ask the parish "how the pastor's
wife and children behave themselves towards divine service and live" (Sehling 7/2.1, p. 1192).

### 3.4 Residence and records

**Residence.** A pastor was not to leave his parish overnight without leave and a substitute.
**Hoya, *Kirchenordnung*, 1581** (Sehling 6/2, p. 1140):

<!-- doc 2065 -->
> Derhalben befehlen wir allen und jeden unsern pfarherrn, schulmeistern und küstern, das sich
> niemand lange von seinem kaspel und ampt, es sey eine oder mehr nacht, ohne nötige ursach
> begebe und habe von unsern verordneten inspectorn ein jeder in seinem eingesetzten ampt
> verlaub genommen und eine tüchtige und wolbekante person aus seiner nachbarschaft in unser
> graffschaft in seinem abwesen gefördert und bestellet.

Therefore we command all and every one of our pastors, schoolmasters and sextons that none
betake himself long from his parish and office, be it one night or more, without needful cause,
and [unless] each have taken leave of our appointed inspectors in his appointed office, and have
procured and appointed a fit and well-known person out of his neighbourhood in our county [to
serve] in his absence.

Marienhafe in East Frisia copied the rule in 1593 (Sehling 7/1, p. 728). In the towns the
deacons needed the pastor's leave, as at Breslau in 1550 and Regensburg in 1556
(Sehling 3, p. 418; 13, p. 443).

**The parish register.** From mid-century the pastor kept registers of baptisms, marriages and
burials, and the visitors inspected them. **Saxony, *Kirchenordnung*, 1580**
(Sehling 1, p. 397):

<!-- doc 44 -->
> Damit man auch gewiss sein möge, das ein jeder pfarrer in seiner kirchen vermög unser ordnung
> die neugetauften kinder, sampt ihrer eltern und paten namen, auf welchen tag sie getauft,
> desgleichen auch der neuen eheleut namen, auf welchen tag sie getrauet und hochzeit gehalten,
> auch welchen tag ein jeder verstorben, verzeichnis und register halte, sol der visitator, so
> oft er visitieret, ihme solches buch, darein es alles ordentlich verzeichnet, vorbringen
> lassen

And that it may be certain that every pastor in his church, according to our order, keepeth a
list and register of the newly baptized children together with their parents' and godparents'
names, on what day they were baptized, likewise of the names of the new married folk, on what
day they were wedded and held their wedding, and also on what day each one died, the visitor, as
often as he visiteth, shall have that same book, wherein all this is orderly written, brought
before him.

The register was to stay with the church and not leave with the pastor or his widow.
Nassau-Weilburg (1576) adds confirmations (Sehling 10, p. 307), and some orders add the
communicants (Kapellendorf 1569, Sehling 1, p. 583). In Pirna (1578) the sexton kept the book,
and at Regensburg (1572) he kept the register of the dead (Sehling 1, p. 645; 13, p. 509).

### 3.5 Deacons and chaplains

**The assistant ministers.** The *Diaconus* or *Kaplan* was an ordained assistant
([`MINOR_ORDERS_DEACONS_ELDERS_GUIDE.md`](MINOR_ORDERS_DEACONS_ELDERS_GUIDE.md), §5.1). His
particular charges were the catechism and the sick. **Electoral Saxony, *Generalia, alle
superintendentes belangende*, 1555** (Sehling 1, p. 315):

<!-- doc 36 -->
> Den catechismum sollen sie der jugent vleissig furtragen, und im leren derselben nit ihre
> kunst und geschicklichkeit beweisen wollen, sundern die kindern lehr dem unverstendigen jungen
> volk ufs aller einfeltigst und immer uf einerlei form und weiss fursagen, und also wider von
> ihnen fordern und examinieren. In besuchung der kranken sollen sie willig und unverdrossen
> sein, und mehr bereit, den armen hierin zu dienen, denn den reichen

They shall diligently set forth the catechism to the youth, and in teaching it not seek to show
their art and skill, but say the children's doctrine before the unlearned young folk in the
simplest way and always in one form and manner, and so require it again of them and examine
[them]. In visiting the sick they shall be willing and unwearied, and readier to serve the poor
herein than the rich.

They were to honour and obey their pastor and superintendent and "not belittle them before the
congregation, nor practise against them, nor stir up factions" (Sehling 1, p. 315). The deacon
was chosen by the pastor together with the council (Sehling 1, p. 323).

## 4. Superintendents and deans

### 4.1 The name and the office

**One overseer, many names.** The evangelical orders replaced the bishop's oversight with a
superintendent (*Superattendens*, *Inspector*, *Dechant*, *Decanus*, *Propst*). Augsburg in 1537
(mediating) justifies the office from the apostolic church and says outright that the titles are
one. **Augsburg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1537** (mediating; Sehling 12, p. 55):

<!-- doc 338 -->
> derhalb bei den aposteln und hernacher in den kirchen allweg ain oder gar wenig furnemer
> ufseher, furgänger, superattendeten oder bischof, welche namen all ainerlai heischen, in jeder
> gemain verordnet worden send, damit dann nicht alhie durch vil hirten dester ubler gehuetet
> werde […] In suma: sie sollen allerdingen die höchste sorg und fursehung der gemainen Christi
> uf in haben, versehen und versorgen.

therefore among the apostles, and afterwards in the churches, there have always been appointed
in every congregation one or very few chief overseers, leaders, superattendents or bishops,
which names all mean one thing, that here the flock be not the worse kept through many shepherds
[…] In sum: they shall in all things have upon them, oversee and provide the highest care and
provision of the congregation of Christ.

At Augsburg (1537, mediating) the office rotated quarterly between the council's president and
the senior provost. In the territories it was a standing office. Its installation is in
[`EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md`](EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md), §11.4.

### 4.2 Electoral Saxony, 1555–1557: visitation, synod, report

**The Saxon pattern.** The Saxon General Articles of 1557 set out the superintendent's duties
that most later Lutheran orders follow:
- watch over "the doctrine, life and diligence" of all pastors and church servants in his
  district;
- summon the village pastors once a year, and visit unannounced, to hear their sermons, ask the
  parishioners about their pastor's conduct, examine the youth in the catechism and inspect the
  schools;
- hold a synod every year;
- admonish privately, and report what he cannot mend to the consistory.

**Electoral Saxony, *General-Artikel und gemeiner Bericht*, 1557** (Sehling 1, pp. 320–321):

<!-- doc 37 -->
> Darüber sol ein jeder superattendens nach seiner und der benachbarten pfarherrn gelegenheit
> alle jar zwischen ostern und pfingsten einen synodum halten, und darzu berufen aus den
> stedten, flecken und dörfern alle pastores, so in seine superattendents gehören, und sich
> dorinnen irer lahr und sitten, auch anderer vorfallenden gebrechen erkunden, dieselben in
> besserung richten, insonderheit auch ire relation hören, wie sie ire pfarkinder in examine
> befunden, und was sie sonst vor irrige sachen anzuzeigen haben, und do etwas fürfiele, das er
> nicht vorrichten könte, sol er das an das consistorium, dohin die person und sachen gehörig,
> weisen und gelangen lassen. Do auch der superattendens etwas ungebürlichs oder streflichs von
> einigem pfarherr in seine superattendents gehörig selbst erfaren, oder aber vom lehenherrn,
> oder den eingepfarten erkundigung bekommen hette, sol er denselben in geheim darumb
> ansprechen, und nach gelegenheit der vorbrechunge strafen, mit vorwarnung, do keine besserung
> folgen, und dergleichen klage mehr für ihn kommen würde, das er solches an das consistorium
> gelangen lassen müste

Moreover every superattendent shall, as it suiteth him and the neighbouring pastors, hold a
synod every year between Easter and Whitsun, and call thereto out of the towns, market towns and
villages all the pastors that belong to his superintendency, and therein inquire into their
doctrine and manners and other defects that fall out, and bring the same to amendment; and
especially hear their report, how they have found their parishioners in the examination, and
what other erroneous matters they have to declare. And if anything fell out that he could not
settle, he shall refer it and bring it to the consistory to which the person and matter belong.
And if the superattendent himself have learned of anything unseemly or blameworthy in any pastor
belonging to his superintendency, or have received information of it from the patron or the
parishioners, he shall speak to him about it in secret and rebuke him according to the
circumstances of the offence, with warning that, if no amendment followed and more complaints of
the like came before him, he must bring it before the consistory.

**Other charges.** The superintendent also:
- examined candidates for a parish before sending them to Wittenberg or Leipzig for ordination
  (§7.3);
- installed new pastors before the altar and charged both pastor and people (Sehling 1, p. 321);
- saw that no pastor was dismissed without his knowledge (Sehling 1, p. 312);
- settled the widow's year and the parish inventory when a pastor died (Sehling 1, p. 312).

The 1555 *Generalia* add a yearly meeting of all the superintendents of the Electoral circle at
Wittenberg on the Wednesday after Whitsun, where they reported to the pastor of Wittenberg "as
the upper superintendent" (Sehling 1, p. 312). The visitor's travelling costs were refunded on a
weekly account sent to the elector "into his own hands" (Sehling 1, p. 311).

### 4.3 The superintendent's charge: Grubenhagen, 1600

**An instruction under oath.** The Brunswick consistory's instruction for the superintendent of
Grubenhagen, on which he was sworn in 1600, shows the office at the end of the century: guardian
of doctrine, visitor, and agent of the consistory. **Grubenhagen, *Officium superintendentis
Grubenhagici*, 1600** (Sehling 6/2, p. 1109):

<!-- doc 2058 -->
> 1. Das er sich, dem corpori doctrinae Julio und formulae ooncordiae im gesunden verstande
> durchaus gleichmeißig in thesi et antithesi zu lehren und, das seine untersetzten pfarherrn
> dergleichen thun mugen, vleisiche aufsicht zu haben verpflichte. […] 4. Das er kein dorf oder
> hauß von seiner superintendenz eximiret achte, noch jemands mit seiner befohlnen visitation in
> seinem untergegebenen gezirk praeterire […] 5. Es soll der superintendens keine offentliche
> sunde oder ärgerliche handel den pastoribus und kirchendienern ubersehen

1. That he bind himself to teach in sound understanding, wholly in accord with the *Corpus
   doctrinae Julium* and the Formula of Concord, *in thesi et antithesi*, and to keep diligent
   oversight that the pastors under him may do the like. […] 4. That he hold no village or house
   exempt from his superintendency, nor pass over anyone in his appointed visitation within the
   district put under him […] 5. The superintendent shall overlook no public sin or offensive
   dealing in the pastors and ministers of the church.

He was to change no ceremony without the consistory's ruling, to act always *pro matrimonio* in
marriage cases, and to settle small matters with the district governor rather than "trouble the
consistory lightly" (Sehling 6/2, p. 1109).

### 4.4 Deans of the Transylvanian chapters

**Elected deans.** Among the Transylvanian Saxons the pastors of each chapter elected their own
dean (*decanus*). **Transylvania, Statutes of the Chapter of Lasseln, 1572**
(Sehling 24, p. 361):

<!-- doc 1692 -->
> Officium decani est ecclesiarum et scholarum sua dioecesis agere inspectorem ac pro virili sua
> parte cavere, ne haereses, schismata morumque vitia pullulent. Deinde publicis invigilare
> commodis atque conventus indicere fratribus non nisi sufficiente aliqua ingenti causa. Ad haec
> defendere bonos ac insontes contra quasvis iniurias intra et extra capitulum ipsis illatas.
> Econtra vero severissime animadvertere in rebelles, ignavos, fucos aut quoquomodo infamiam et
> sibi privatim et toti fraternitati palam creantes.

The office of the dean is to act as inspector of the churches and schools of his diocese, and to
take care to the utmost of his power that heresies, schisms and vices of manners spring not up.
Next, to watch over the common good, and to call the brethren to assembly only for some
sufficient and weighty cause. Besides this, to defend the good and innocent against all wrongs
done them within and without the chapter. But on the other hand to punish most severely the
rebellious, the slothful, the drones, and those who in any way bring disgrace both privately on
themselves and openly on the whole brotherhood.

For his pains each brother paid him one florin a year. The chapter also judged marriage cases,
"which pious magistrates of old, not without weighty counsel, referred to the ecclesiastical
court" (Sehling 24, pp. 361–362).

## 5. Consistories, their presidents and members

### 5.1 Composition: theologians and jurists

**A mixed court.** The consistory was a standing court of theologians and lawyers under the
prince, for marriage cases, church discipline and the affairs of ministers and church property.
Wittenberg's of 1539 had two theologians and two doctors of law, mostly professors of the
university (Sehling 7/2.2, p. 306, n. 2). Jena's in 1569 was presided over by the superintendent
of Jena, who was to be a doctor of theology. **Saxe-Weimar, *Ordnung und reformation
ecclesiastici consistorii zu Jena*, 1569** (Sehling 1, p. 234):

<!-- doc 24 -->
> Wir ordnen und wollen auch, das in diesem consistorio der superintendens zu Jen, so ein doctor
> theologiae sein soll, praeses sein, und im zween andere theologi, aus den professorn, oder
> kirchendienern, und dann zween geschickte gottsfürchtige juristen, und unser amptman daselbst,
> oder ein ander person vom adel, als assessores zugeordnet werden

We ordain and will also that in this consistory the superintendent of Jena, who shall be a
doctor of theology, be president, and that two other theologians, out of the professors or the
ministers of the church, and then two able, God-fearing jurists, and our district officer there,
or another person of the nobility, be joined to him as assessors.

### 5.2 The president: prince, nobleman or councillor

**Who presided.** The orders disagree about who should preside:
- **The prince himself.** At Weimar in 1561 Duke Johann Friedrich made himself "the supreme
  president" and meant to attend "in our own princely person", with his brothers deputizing
  (Sehling 1, pp. 230–231).
- **A theologian.** At Jena in 1569 the superintendent presided (above).
- **A nobleman.** Electoral Saxony in 1580 set over its upper consistory at Dresden "an
  understanding, well-qualified [man] of the nobility as president and director of all the
  consistories' affairs", with two jurists and two theologians, the court superintendent and the
  preacher of Dresden (Sehling 1, p. 405).
- **A lay councillor, to keep the clergy from the sword.** Regensburg in 1588 gave the
  presidency to one of the political members. **Regensburg, *Ordnung des consistorii*, 1588**
  (Sehling 13, pp. 513–514):

<!-- doc 453 -->
> damit auch disfalls des consistorii autorität gegen allen personen, so für dasselbig erfordert
> und beschieden, künftiglich, wie sich gebührt und die notturft erfordert, erhalten und die
> kirchendiener aus dem verdacht gesezt, als solten sie widerumb nach dem weltlichen schwert
> greifen und wie der pabst in der kirchen ihres gefallens regieren wollen, soll aus den dreien
> zum consistorio verordneten, politischen, gelehrten männern einem, so propter authoritatem,
> eruditionem et prudentiam vor andern darzu dichtig erkennt, das präsidentenambt und
> directorium des consistorii befohlen werden

and that in this respect also the consistory's authority towards all persons summoned and cited
before it may henceforth be upheld, as is fitting and need requireth, and the ministers of the
church set free from the suspicion that they would again lay hold on the worldly sword and rule
in the church at their pleasure like the Pope, the office of president and the direction of the
consistory shall be committed to one of the three learned political men appointed to the
consistory, who is judged fitter for it than the others *propter authoritatem, eruditionem et
prudentiam*.

Pfalz-Neuburg (1576) likewise set "a director among the three political councillors"
(Sehling 13, p. 237).

**What the president did.** The director:
- put the business to the members and took the vote, "the *umbfrage*", alternating clerical and
  lay members at his discretion (Hanau 1612, Reformed, Sehling 10, p. 539);
- alone made the proposals, "to avoid confusion and ill-will"
  (Ysenburg-Birstein 1608, Reformed, Sehling 10, p. 686).

The Saxon president of 1580 also gave each newly examined pastor a formal charge on his office
before he was sent to his parish (Sehling 1, p. 380).

### 5.3 The business of the consistory

**Its competence.** Electoral Saxony in 1580 sets out the matters that belonged to the
consistory, so that "worldly and church courts be not mixed together". **Saxony,
*Kirchenordnung*, 1580** (Sehling 1, p. 403):

<!-- doc 44 -->
> Erstlich alle ehesachen, wie sie namen haben, welche durch die superintendenten und jedes orts
> oberkeit auf die ihnen zugestellete ordnung nicht können vorrichtet und verglichen werden.
> Alle ergerliche sünde und laster an den lerern und zuhörern wider die erste und andere tafel
> der gebot gottes, allein soviel die gradus admonitionum, und nicht die weltliche strafe
> belanget, wie solche in den synodis verordnet worden. Alle sachen, die pfarrer, kirchen und
> schuldiener, vocation, ampt, dienst, leben, wandel, translation, dimission, suspension,
> handlung und verbrechung belangend, auf mass, wie bei den synodis verzeichnet. Alle sachen, so
> der kirchen, schulen, hospitaln, und gemeiner kasten güter, lehen, einkommen, nutzung, gebeud,
> und besserung, darzu der kirchendiener besoldung betreffen.

First, all marriage cases, whatever their names, which cannot be settled and composed by the
superintendents and the authorities of each place according to the order given them. All
offensive sins and vices in teachers and hearers against the first and second table of God's
commandments, so far only as concerneth the degrees of admonition, and not the worldly
punishment, as these have been ordained in the synods. All matters concerning pastors, ministers
of churches and schools: their calling, office, service, life, conduct, translation, dismissal,
suspension, dealings and offences, in the measure set down for the synods. All matters
concerning the goods, fiefs, income, use, buildings and repair of churches, schools, hospitals
and common chests, and also the salaries of the ministers of the church.

The Saxon upper consistory also had a "general inspection and oversight" of the universities of
Leipzig and Wittenberg, the princely schools and the two lower consistories. Books were not to
be printed without the consistory's censorship (Sehling 1, pp. 402, 407). Brandenburg-Ansbach
(1594) forbade booksellers to sell "new, impure, suspect books" without the consistory's
knowledge, and set the president and political councillors over the endowments of churches and
schools (Sehling 11, p. 382).

**Sessions and secrecy.** The consistories met weekly:
- Hanau (1612, Reformed): every Tuesday afternoon from one o'clock until evening, with extraordinary
  sessions at the director's discretion (Sehling 10, p. 538);
- Ysenburg-Birstein (1608, Reformed): every Wednesday afternoon from one until four or five, members
  arriving late being fined (Sehling 10, p. 685).

Members, secretary and copyists swore to keep the business "in the highest secrecy"
(Brandenburg- Ansbach 1594, Sehling 11, p. 385; Pfalz-Neuburg 1576, Sehling 13, p. 240).

## 6. Professors of theology

**No seminaries.** No order founds a seminary in the later sense. Ministers were trained at the
universities, often on a territorial stipend (§7), and the church orders treat the professors of
theology only where their work touched the church's government.

**What the orders ask of professors.**
- **Sitting in the consistory.** The Wittenberg and Jena consistories drew on the theology
  faculty (§5.1), and the Prussian consistorial order of 1584 names "the *primarius professor
  theologiae* at the university" among its three theologians (Sehling 4, p. 123). Because the
  Saxon consistorials of Leipzig and Wittenberg "each also hath his profession at the high
  school", their sessions were to be timed "that they be in no way hindered in their lectures,
  and the youth not neglected in their studies" (Sehling 1, p. 402).
- **Examining and ordaining.** Candidates were examined and ordained at Wittenberg and Leipzig
  (Saxony 1557, Sehling 1, p. 321), at Marburg before "the teachers of God's word and the
  professors of holy Scripture" (Hesse 1566, mediating, Sehling 8, p. 201), and by the
  theological faculty of Helmstedt, which was not to keep a candidate more than two days nor
  take more than two thalers (Gandersheim recess 1601, Sehling 6/2, p. 892).
- **Overseeing the stipendiaries.** The prefect of the Marburg stipendiaries examined them twice
  a year "in the presence of all the professors of theology, that the examination may have the
  more weight and the wits be stirred to greater diligence". **Hesse, *Stipendiatenordnung*,
  1546** (mediating; Sehling 8, p. 159):

<!-- doc 2255 -->
> Zum vierten soll er sie alle, des jars zweimal in beisein aller professoren theologiae
> examiniren, damit das examen soviel mehr ansehens habe und die ingenia zu großerm vleiß
> excitirt werden.

Fourthly, he shall examine them all twice a year in the presence of all the professors of
theology, that the examination may have so much the more authority, and the wits be stirred up
to greater diligence.

- **Lecturing the clergy.** Braunschweig-Wolfenbüttel (1543) appointed at the cathedral "a
  *lector theologiae*, who is charged with nothing else than to read some lessons publicly in
  holy Scripture" (Sehling 6/1, p. 47). The monks and canons in the monasteries were to hear
  such lectures every weekday morning and evening, "that afterwards, learned in the word of God,
  they may be set over churches and schools" (Sehling 6/1, p. 81).
- **The academy and the church.** At Strasbourg the church convent and the academy quarrelled
  over seats and over "the provision and ordering of the theological lectures and disputations";
  an agreement of 1575 settled it (Sehling 20/1, p. 507).

## 7. Training of ministers

### 7.1 School, university and stipend

**No seminary, no vicarage.** The orders know nothing of a seminary or of a vicarage year in the
modern sense. The path to the ministry ran through the Latin school and the university, and the
churches paid for it with stipends:
- **Territorial stipends.** Electoral Saxony raised "the number of our stipendiaries … who shall
  all be drawn to the service of churches and schools" (1580, Sehling 1, p. 376).
- **Stipends on vacant benefices.** The Saxon visitors of 1540 meant to turn church fiefs into
  stipends as they fell vacant (Sehling 1, p. 288).
- **Stipends from the old monastic goods.** Nassau in 1546 created stipends of 24 florins a year
  from surplus church income, for boys of at least seventeen (Sehling 10, p. 88).
- **Boarding colleges.** Württemberg's boys passed from the *Pädagogium* at Stuttgart and the
  cloister schools to the *Stipendium* at Tübingen (1559, Sehling 16, p. 359). The Reformed
  Palatinate admitted to its stipend only those found fit in the half-yearly examinations of its
  four *paedagogia* (1593, Sehling 14, p. 553).

Stipendiaries were bound by a written obligation to serve the territory that paid for them
(Württemberg 1559, Sehling 16, p. 364; Hohenlohe 1556, Sehling 15, p. 108).

### 7.2 Practice preaching in the stipend: Tübingen, 1559

**Preaching under correction.** The Tübingen *Stipendium* is the nearest thing in the orders to
a training house for preachers. After the master's degree each stipendiary heard the theology
lectures, studied Greek and Hebrew, and preached in the house until he was called.
**Württemberg, *Ordnung des Tübinger Stipendiums*, 1559** (Sehling 17/1, p. 561):

<!-- doc 813 -->
> Es soll ein jeder nach erlangtem gradu Magisterii im Studio Theologiae diser Ordination nach
> one verzug die Lectiones Theologicas fleissig neben dem studio graecae et hebraicae linguae
> zuhoren unnd mit predigen im Stipendio sich zu üben verbunden sein, so lang und biß dero einer
> oder mehr zum Ministerio erfordert würdt.

Every one, after attaining the degree of Master, shall be bound in the study of theology,
according to this ordinance, without delay to hear the theological lectures diligently, besides
the study of the Greek and Hebrew tongues, and to exercise himself in preaching in the
Stipendium, so long and until one or more of them be called to the ministry.

The texts were assigned to them, not chosen. They were to handle them by "an understanding,
right and orderly method, according to the precepts of dialectic and rhetoric". The
superintendents of the house were to tell a preacher "privately, the same day, after the sermon
is held", any faults they found "*in inventione, dispositione oder pronunciatione*"
(Sehling 17/1, p. 561). The stipendiaries also kept "their disputations, declamations and
exercises of style" in the arts faculty (Sehling 17/1, p. 577). The Coburg synod of 1580 asked
for a similar house with "*exercitia disputationum, repetitionum, concionum*" led by *magistri
repetentes* (Sehling 1, p. 253).

**Preaching in the parish.** A young man could also practise in a parish pulpit, with the
pastor's leave and on showing his sermon first. **Electoral Saxony, *Generalia, alle
superintendentes belangende*, 1555** (Sehling 1, p. 314):

<!-- doc 36 -->
> Do aber ein junger gesell, der sich ' begert zuversuchen und zu üben, umb erlaubniss
> zupredigen bitten wurde, soll ihm der pfarrer solchs zu lassen, doch also, das ime der junge
> gesell zuvur jemands schriftlichs zeugnis und seiner furgenomenen predigt concept schriftlich
> oder mundlich furlege.

But if a young fellow, who desireth to try and exercise himself, should ask leave to preach, the
pastor shall allow it him, yet so that the young fellow first lay before him someone's written
testimony and the draft of the sermon he purposeth, in writing or by mouth.

Schwäbisch Hall's synod ruled in 1606 that no student of theology "who will exercise himself in
preaching" should do so without the dean's consent (Sehling 17/1, p. 191).

### 7.3 Examination, trial sermon and instruction before ordination

**The examination.** Before ordination the candidate was examined in doctrine and had to preach.
**Electoral Saxony, *General-Artikel und gemeiner Bericht*, 1557** (Sehling 1, p. 322):

<!-- doc 37 -->
> Und es sol unter andern der ordinandus vor der ordination gefragt, wo und was er studirt, auch
> wo und womit er sich zuvor enthalten und genehret habe, endlich auch fleissig examinirt werden
> in der christlichen lahr, auch einmal oder öfter offentlich predigen, und do befunden, das
> derselbe an lahr und wandel ungeschickt were, sollen die, so inen gesandt und zu der pfarr
> berufen haben oder wöllen, schriftlich von dieses ungeschickligkeit berichtet und vorwarnet
> werden, das sie auf einen andern bedacht sein wollen, der zu solchem pfarrambt tüglich sei.

And among other things the ordinand shall be asked before ordination where and what he hath
studied, also where and wherewith he hath before maintained and supported himself; finally also
be diligently examined in Christian doctrine, and preach publicly once or oftener. And if it be
found that he is unfit in doctrine and life, those that sent him and have called, or would call,
him to the parish shall be informed in writing of his unfitness and warned to think upon another
who is fit for such a pastoral office.

The trial sermon (*Probpredigt*) became standard. In the Reformed Palatinate it was to show "how
he can teach the common people out of Scripture, exhort them to amendment and comfort them",
with attention to "his pronunciation" (Kirchenratsordnung 1564, Sehling 14, p. 412). Henneberg
sent the candidate to preach before the parish, which reported back "how the man sent pleased
them with his person, delivery, bearing [and] repute" (1555, Sehling 2, p. 294).

**Weeks of instruction at Wittenberg.** A candidate who was teachable but not yet fit was not
simply rejected. The superintendent was to send him to Wittenberg "with warning that he be
provided beforehand by those that called him to the pastorate with needful expenses, that he may
be instructed at Wittenberg some weeks, if need be, and await ordination without burdening
anyone" (Saxony 1555, Sehling 1, p. 312). Hesse in 1566 (mediating) told examiners to keep
promising candidates at Marburg for a time. **Hesse, *Kirchenordnung*, 1566**
(mediating; Sehling 8, p. 201):

<!-- doc 2257 -->
> So aber die examinatores etlich aus denen, so zum examen gesant und geschickt befunden werden,
> sollen sie dieselbige vermanen, daß sie ein zeitlang bleiben, die lectiones fleißig visitiren
> und insonderheit in der heiligen schrift sich wol uben, domit sie mögen hernach zum examen
> geschickter kommen.

But if the examiners find some of those that are sent to the examination [not yet] fit, they
shall exhort them to stay a while, to attend the lectures diligently, and especially to exercise
themselves well in holy Scripture, that afterwards they may come to the examination better
prepared.

### 7.4 Probation: deacon before pastor

**The diaconate as apprenticeship.** The closest thing to a vicarage is the rule that a new man
should first serve as deacon under a pastor. **Palatinate, *Kirchenratsordnung*, 1564**
(Reformed; Sehling 14, p. 413):

<!-- doc 504 -->
> Und soll keinem neuling, der zuvor im ministerio nit gewesen, leichtlich eine pfarr bevolhen
> werden, es were dann sach, das einer so geschickt, alt und erfarn, das auß den umbstenden und
> seinem wesen erschiene, das er ein pfarr versehen kundte, so mag man ime wol eine pfarr
> vertrauen, sonst aber nit leichtlich, sonder soll zuvor zu einem diacono gebraucht und nach
> etlicher zeit, wann man ime eine pfarr bevelhen wolte, wider examinirt werden, auf das man
> darauß judiciren möge, ob er in der lere zugenommen

And no novice that hath not been in the ministry before shall lightly be entrusted with a
parish, unless one be so able, old and experienced that it appeared from the circumstances and
his bearing that he could have charge of a parish; then one may well entrust a parish to him,
but otherwise not lightly. Rather he shall first be used as a deacon, and after some time, when
one would commit a parish to him, be examined again, that one may judge thereby whether he hath
increased in doctrine.

The Palatine *Kirchenratsordnung* of about 1593 (Reformed) repeats the rule
(Sehling 14, p. 548). In 1607 the Elector's church council found that "the young *sapientisten*
and other new ministers sent out mostly do not increase but decrease". **Palatinate, *Mandat zu
den Konventen, der Predigtaufsicht und den Diakonen*, 1607** (Reformed; Sehling 19/2, p. 957):

<!-- doc 1195 -->
> Bevelhen demnach, das ihr allen solchen neuen diaconis, wen sie 1 jar in euer inspection
> geweßen, ihre dispositiones concionum abfordern solt, dieselbige nach obgedachtem ecclesiaste
> Buccani censurirent, solche eure censuras oben daruf schreiben und sie uns zuschicken, damit
> auch wir dieselbige, ehe sie alhero citirt werden, leßen, die mängel ihnen daruf undersagen
> und alßdan solche ihre dispositiones ihnen wieder zustellen können.

We command you therefore that from all such new deacons, when they have been a year in your
inspection, you require their outlines of sermons, censure the same according to the
aforementioned *Ecclesiastes* of Bucanus, write your censures above them, and send them to us,
that we also may read them before they be cited hither, show them their faults therein, and then
give them their outlines back.

Elsewhere the new man stood under the eye of his superintendent. Pfalz-Neuburg's consistory kept
"a book … wherein every pastor's or minister's name and surname, age, where he studied, how he
stood in the examination, [how he] held [himself] in the trial sermon" were written
(Pfalz-Neuburg 1576, Sehling 13, p. 172; Hohenlohe 1580, Sehling 15, p. 491).

### 7.5 Continuing study: synods, convents and assigned reading

**Reading set from synod to synod.** The yearly synod was also a school for the pastors. The
Saxon superintendent was to give his pastors "a useful little book, or a piece of the Bible, to
read this coming year", on which they would hear one another at the next synod (1555, Sehling 1,
p. 312). Nassau-Dillenburg in 1552 made the same rule into an examination. **Nassau-Dillenburg,
*Synodalabschied*, 1552** (Sehling 10, p. 93):

<!-- doc 172 -->
> daß in einem jeden synodo den priestern ein oder mher locos proponiert werden soll, uff den
> andern nachvolgenden synodum dieselbigen zu repetiren unnd durchaus zu examiniren und alßdan
> einen andern oder mher zuproponiren, uff den volgenden synodum zu examiniren unnd also
> forthan, damit die priester desto vleissiger zulesen und desto ehe bucher zukauffen verursacht
> werden.

that in every synod one or more *loci* shall be set before the priests, to be repeated and
thoroughly examined at the next synod following, and then another or more set, to be examined at
the following synod, and so forth, that the priests may be moved the more diligently to read and
the sooner to buy books.

**Monthly convents with sermon criticism.** The Reformed Palatinate made the convent of the
pastors a monthly exercise. The president of the church council reported to the Elector in 1571.
**Palatinate, Wenzel Zuleger's report on the classical convents, [1571]**
(Reformed; Sehling 14, p. 446):

<!-- doc 504 -->
> Erstlich ist den kirchen- und schuldienern bevolhen, das sie alle monat einmal uf iren kosten
> sollen an bestimpten orten zusamenkhommen. Doselbst soll einer auß inen ein locum scripture
> teutsch in conventu predigtweiß erkleren. Wan solchs geschehen, soll er abtreten, di andere
> kirchendiener urtheilen, ob er di meinung deß texts recht verstendtlich und erbeulich
> dargeben, auch di lehr, vermanung und straf daraus recht und zu erbauung angezogen. Wo er
> gefelt, soll im das freundtlich undersagt und zur beßerung ermanet werden. Hernach soll durch
> den praesidem […] ein par quaestiones theologicas proponiren, sie latine under sich oder
> theutsch, wo es erbeulich, tractiren

First, the ministers of churches and schools are commanded to come together once every month, at
their own cost, at appointed places. There one of them shall expound a passage of Scripture in
German in the convent, after the manner of a sermon. When this is done he shall withdraw, and
the other ministers judge whether he hath set forth the meaning of the text rightly,
understandably and edifyingly, and drawn from it doctrine, exhortation and rebuke rightly and to
edification. Where he faileth, he shall be told so in a friendly way and exhorted to amend.
After that the president […] shall propose a couple of theological questions, [which] they
[shall] handle among themselves in Latin, or in German where it is edifying.

The later Palatine and Hanau convent orders (1607, 1609, both Reformed) turned this into a full
procedure, with the pastors bound by oath to "submit willingly to the censure of my brethren"
(Sehling 14, p. 609; 10, p. 542).

**Trial sermons for serving pastors.** In Brandenburg the village pastors took turns preaching
trial sermons at the weekday service in the town church, besides the yearly convents, "that they
may be better informed in the articles of faith and the controversies of religion … and have
occasion for more diligence in the study of doctrine" (visitation recess of 1600, Sehling 3, p.
212). At Neuruppin in 1558 the rural pastors were, in Sehling's summary, to be summoned,
examined and made to preach once a quarter (Sehling 3, p. 264).

## 8. The laity

### 8.1 Attending church

**Sermon and catechism.** Every order requires attendance at the Sunday sermon, and most the
afternoon catechism. The duty fell on the head of the household for children and servants.
**Hohenlohe, *Polizei- und Rügordnung*, 1588** (Sehling 15, p. 594):

<!-- doc 613 -->
> Eß söll jederman an son- und feyertagen die predig besuchen, auch ihre kinder und ehehalten
> dartzue schicken und anhalten. Eß soll ein jeder, so seinen verstand erreicht, im jar zum
> wenigsten einmall oder zway das nachtmall empfahen.

Every man shall attend the sermon on Sundays and holy days, and send and hold his children and
servants thereto. Every one that hath reached his understanding shall receive the Supper at the
least once or twice in the year.

**Counted and fined.** In Nassau-Weilburg church censors went round during the service to note
those in the streets, churchyard, taverns, workshops or barns. In the villages the people were
counted house by house. **Nassau-Weilburg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1576** (Sehling 10, p. 309):

<!-- doc 198 -->
> daß insonderheit in Flecken und Dorffschafften, bevorab Sontags und uff die höhere Fest-,
> Feyer- und Bettage, vor der Kirchen von Hauß zu Hausse umbgezehlet unnd diejenige, so ohne
> erhebliche Ursach und vorher gebettener Erlaubnus abwesendt befunden, ohnnachlässig mit einer
> gewissen Mulcta oder Kirchenbuß nach Inhalt dieser Censur Ordnung gestrafft werden.

that especially in market towns and villages, above all on Sundays and on the higher feasts,
holy days and prayer days, a count be made from house to house before the church, and those
found absent without weighty cause and without leave asked beforehand be punished without fail
with a fixed fine or church penance, according to this order of censure.

No one was exempt: not the lords' servants, nor those in privileged houses, nor millers, bakers,
innkeepers, journeymen and carters (Sehling 10, p. 309). Belzig in 1529 already bound "all
householders" to send children and servants to the quarterly catechism sermons on pain of
punishment by the district officer and the council (Sehling 1, p. 527). Where a war or plague
service was called by the bell, Hohenlohe in 1588 required "at the least one person out of every
house" to come, and those at home to pray with them (Sehling 15, p. 542).

### 8.2 Prayer and teaching at home

**The household as a church.** The orders assume that the head of the household teaches the
catechism at home and leads prayer, as Luther's Small Catechism directs ("how a householder
should teach his household"). Brandenburg-Nürnberg (1533) has the pastors exhort parents.
**Brandenburg-Nürnberg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1533** (Sehling 11, p. 188):

<!-- doc 270 -->
> Sie sollen auch die eltern und hausväter fleißig vermanen, das sie ire kinder und hausgesind
> zur predig und gemainen gebet ziehen und sunderlich darauf acht haben, das sie nicht one
> redliche ursach sich allzulang vom heiligen sacrament entziehen.

They shall also diligently exhort the parents and householders to draw their children and
household to the sermon and to common prayer, and especially to take heed that they withdraw not
themselves too long from the holy sacrament without honest cause.

Saxony in 1580 tells parents to teach their children and servants "at home in the chief parts of
the catechism" before sending them to confession, "for some are found so unskilled that they
know not even what the holy sacrament is" (Sehling 1, p. 427). The Reformed synod of Dillenburg
in 1582 sent pastors and elders into the houses. **Nassau-Dillenburg, *Abschied der Dillenburger
Generalsynode*, 1582** (Reformed; Sehling 10, p. 173):

<!-- doc 186 -->
> 1. Die pfarherren oder seniores und presbyteri halten visitationem domesticam, dabey sie mitt
> aller freundlicheit die leuthe ermahnen und erbauwen sollen, und sonderlich darane seyen, daß
> die jugendt daheim morgents und abents, auch vor und nach eßens und an der arbeit, zum gebeth
> und psalmen zu singen angehalten werden.

1. The pastors, or the seniors and presbyters, shall hold domestic visitation, wherein they
   shall exhort and edify the people with all friendliness, and especially see to it that the
   youth be held at home, morning and evening, also before and after meals and at their work, to
   prayer and to the singing of psalms.

The bells called the absent to prayer as well: when the Litany began, Hohenlohe in 1581 had the
great bell rung "so that every man in the field or in the houses be reminded of the common
prayer", to join it "in spirit" though absent in body
(Sehling 15, p. 452; [`ORGAN_INSTRUMENTS_BELLS_GUIDE.md`](ORGAN_INSTRUMENTS_BELLS_GUIDE.md)).

### 8.3 Confession and communion

**How often.** The orders cite Luther's preface to the Small Catechism: whoever does not seek
the sacrament "at the least once or four times a year" is to be feared a despiser of it
(Palatinate 1556, Sehling 14, p. 135; Danzig 1557, Sehling 4, p. 230). The legal minimum was
usually once or twice a year (Hohenlohe 1588, above). The Schulenburg order of 1572 had pastors
admonish and then report to the lords those who did not go "at the least once or twice in the
year" to confession and the sacrament, "and such a one shall not be suffered in the parish at
all" (Sehling 3, p. 149). Before communion every communicant went to confession or to the
examination (*Verhör*) ([`MASS_PRELIMINARIES_GUIDE.md`](MASS_PRELIMINARIES_GUIDE.md)).

### 8.4 Church workers: a higher standard

**Lay officers held to the minister's standard.** Sextons, organists, schoolmasters and church
wardens were not ordained, but the orders held them to the conduct required of ministers. Hesse
in 1566 (mediating) says so of the sextons ("*Opfermänner*"). **Hesse, *Kirchenordnung*, 1566**
(mediating; Sehling 8, p. 211):

<!-- doc 2257 -->
> Es sollen auch dieselbige gleich wie andere diener der kirchen eines erbaren und gottseligen
> wandels sein, kein volseufer, nit unehrlich hantierung treiben. Es sollen aber gemelte
> opfermenner durch die pfarherr und eltesten darzu mit wissen und willen eines superintendenten
> angenommen werden.

These also, like other ministers of the church, shall be of honest and godly conduct, no
drunkards, nor ply dishonest trades. And the said sextons shall be taken on thereto by the
pastors and elders with the knowledge and will of a superintendent.

Oldenburg in 1573 complained that "godless knaves who go about with the black art, soothsaying,
charming, conjuring devils, treasure-digging, also with blasphemy, drunkenness and other
offensive wicked pieces" had been taken on as sextons (Sehling 7/2.1, p. 1121). Hoya (1581)
forbade "our pastors, schoolmasters and sextons" to be found "in offensive company in common
drinking-bouts and public taverns" (Sehling 6/2, p. 1141). Visitors asked about the sexton's
communion as about the pastor's. In the Oldenburg visitation protocols of 1609, quoted in
Sehling's notes, one sexton was found "not yet [to have gone] *ad coenam*", and another had kept
from the sacrament nine years over a quarrel (Sehling 7/2.1, pp. 1185, 1189).

**The minister's household.** The higher standard reached the minister's wife, children and
servants (§3.3). The elders of the Reformed churches were to be "blameless" by the rule of 1
Timothy 3; their duties are in
[`MINOR_ORDERS_DEACONS_ELDERS_GUIDE.md`](MINOR_ORDERS_DEACONS_ELDERS_GUIDE.md), §6. No order
found lays a special prayer rule on the minor offices; their duty was attendance, good conduct,
and the service of the choir or the church door.

## 9. Schoolmasters

### 9.1 A church office

**Teacher, singer and catechist.** The schoolmaster was counted among the "ministers of churches
and schools" and was bound to the same life (§3.3). His church duties were:
- to teach the catechism and prayers;
- to lead the boys in church and lead the singing;
- to bring them to the sermon and watch them there;
- to obey the pastor and the visitors.

His installation and oath are in [`EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md`](EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md),
§12.3, and the choir service in [`OFFICES_GUIDE.md`](OFFICES_GUIDE.md), §6.1. Esslingen's ten
articles for the German schoolmaster (1534, mediating) are the shortest complete statement.
**Esslingen, *Artikel für den deutschen Schulmeister*, [1534]** (mediating; Sehling 17/2, p. 381):

<!-- doc 881 -->
> 1. Er sol sich beflyssen, die kinder zu underwysen und uffzuziehen in gottesforcht, zucht und
> erberkeit lut des heilgen evangelionß und der teglichen predigen. […] 3. Der schulmeister sol
> sich mit leer und gantzem leben allergstalt halten, domit die kinder kein böß exempel von im
> nemmen, wie sich gezympt eynem christlichen, getruwen lermeister. 4. Die fragstuck, klein und
> groß kinderbericht, sullen in der schul in steter übung gehalten werden und den kindern
> furgegeben zu buchstaben und lesen an stat der nammen buchlin, ehe denn sy andere brieff oder
> bucher angryffen. 5. Er sol die kinder mit flyß zu predigen füren, namlich am suntag und uff
> mitwoch zum kinderbericht. […] 7. Er sol die kinder wol uben in zehen gebotten, glauben,
> vatter unser, sacramenten, in psalmen singen und derglychen, wie die buchlin der fragstuck
> ußwysen. 8. Das groß fragstuck sol mann lesen, das klayn sol man ußwendig lernen.

1. He shall endeavour to instruct the children and bring them up in the fear of God, discipline
   and honesty, according to the holy gospel and the daily sermons. […] 3. The schoolmaster
   shall so behave himself in teaching and in his whole life that the children take no evil
   example from him, as becometh a Christian, faithful teacher. 4. The questions, the small and
   great children's instruction, shall be kept in constant use in the school and given to the
   children for spelling and reading instead of the name-book, before they take up other letters
   or books. 5. He shall diligently lead the children to the sermons, namely on Sunday, and on
   Wednesday to the children's instruction. […] 7. He shall exercise the children well in the
   Ten Commandments, the Creed, the Our Father, the sacraments, in singing psalms and the like,
   as the booklets of questions direct. 8. The great questions they shall read; the small they
   shall learn by heart.

The catechism thus served as the reading primer.

### 9.2 In church with the boys

**Leading them to the sermon.** In the towns the German schoolmasters brought their pupils to
the weekday sermon and sang before and after it, so that the Latin school need not lose its
hours. **Saxony, *Kirchenordnung*, 1580** (Sehling 1, p. 370):

<!-- doc 44 -->
> ist für rathsam angesehen, da in einer stadt deutsche schulen seind, das dieselben
> schulmeister mit ihren knaben zu rechter zeit, wenn zu der predigt geleutet wird, in die
> kirchen kommen, und mit ihnen einen deutschen gesang oder zweene singen, darauf die predigt
> folgen sol. Und wenn dieselbige volendet, sol abermals ein deutscher gesang von ihnen sampt
> dem volk gesungen werden.

it is deemed advisable, where there are German schools in a town, that the same schoolmasters
come with their boys into the church at the right time, when the bell is rung for the sermon,
and sing with them a German hymn or two, whereupon the sermon shall follow; and when it is
ended, a German hymn shall again be sung by them together with the people.

At Wittenberg (1533) a school servant sat with the boys who could not get into the pew, "to see
that they hear the sermon and play no knavery" (Sehling 1, p. 703). In Norderdithmarschen (1597)
the boys gathered at their teacher's house before the service on Sundays and feasts and went
with him "in orderly, modest array into the church, to hear the sermon and help with the
singing" (Sehling 23, p. 502). The Oschatz visitors of 1555 wanted the boys "held to diligent
attention and to noting down the sayings from God's word, and always diligently asked what they
have learned from the sermon" (Sehling 1, p. 631).

### 9.3 Girls' schools and schoolmistresses

**Schoolmistresses.** Girls had their own schools in the larger towns, with schoolmistresses or
a schoolmaster for the girls:
- Brück (1530) has the schoolmaster teach "the young maidens … under eleven years with reading
  and writing, also in the catechism and good sayings of Scripture" (Sehling 1, p. 535);
- Wittenberg's maidens came on weekday mornings while the sermon bell rang
  (1533, Sehling 1, p. 706);
- in the Reformed Palatinate in 1595 "every schoolmaster" was to lead his boys, and "every
  schoolwoman her schoolgirls", to the catechism sermon and present them to the ministers
  (Sehling 19/2, p. 856).

### 9.4 Oversight

**Visited by the pastor.** The pastor visited the school. Saxony in 1555 required the pastors,
"at the least four times every year", with a burgomaster or councillor, to "view the schools,
examine the children", and see that the school servants "live without offence … and abstain from
frivolous clothing, also from ale-houses, gluttony and such vices" (Sehling 1, p. 313). The
Württemberg special superintendent asked "whether the schoolmaster with his colleagues keep the
school in order and at its time … and furnish the church with Christian hymns, and what conduct
he and his colleagues lead" (1559, Sehling 16, p. 388).

## 10. The magistrate

### 10.1 Keeper of both tables

**Lutheran doctrine.** The Lutheran orders follow Melanchthon in making the Christian magistrate
"keeper of both tables" of the Law: responsible not only for the second table (civil order) but
for the first (true worship), so far as outward discipline goes. Mecklenburg in 1552 states it
plainly. **Mecklenburg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1552** (Sehling 5, p. 189):

<!-- doc 1922 -->
> Sondern die weltliche oberkeit ist gott diesen dienst schuldig im ganzen göttlichen, ewigen
> gesetz, das ist erstlich alle eusserliche, erkante abgötterei, zeuberei, eidbruch,
> gotteslesterung, öffentliche ketzerei sollen sie abthun und strafen, und dagegen rechte lere
> von gott pflanzen lassen und helfen erhalten […] Und in summa, die oberkeit sol beide tafeln
> der zehen gebot, gott zu ehren, in erhaltung eusserlicher zucht handhaben mit ernstlicher
> execution.

But the worldly authority oweth God this service in the whole divine, eternal law: that is,
first, they shall put away and punish all outward, known idolatry, sorcery, perjury, blasphemy,
open heresy, and on the other hand let true doctrine of God be planted and help to maintain it
[…] And in sum, the authority shall uphold both tables of the Ten Commandments, to the honour of
God, in maintaining outward discipline with earnest execution.

The Palatinate repeated the passage almost word for word in 1556 (Sehling 14, p. 219).

**Nourisher of churches and schools.** The Transylvanian Saxons add the image of the magistrate
as foster-father of the church, from Isaiah 49:23. **Transylvania, *Bekenntnisformel der
Mediascher Synode*, 1572** (Sehling 24, p. 349):

<!-- doc 1691 -->
> ad cuius conservationem opus est ecclesiis et scholis earumque doctoribus, quorum pii
> magistratus debent esse defensores et nutritores, Esaiae 49 cap. dicitur. Debet quoque
> magistratus Christianus tollere scandala manifesta ex ecclesia, ut idolatria et impia dogmata
> pugnantia cum verbo Dei horrendasque blasphemias in Deum

for whose preservation there is need of churches and schools and their teachers, of which pious
magistrates ought to be defenders and nourishers, as it is said in the 49th chapter of Isaiah.
The Christian magistrate ought also to remove manifest offences out of the church, such as
idolatry and impious doctrines contrary to the word of God, and horrible blasphemies against
God.

### 10.2 What the magistrate did

**Duties in practice.** The magistrate's duties were these:
- **To order visitation.** The prince sent the visitors and gave the reason. **Lüneburg,
  *Instruktion zu den Visitationen*, 1565** (Sehling 6/1, p. 576):

<!-- doc 2006 -->
> Weil uns der allmechtig in dis hohe ampt der oberkeit gesetzt, das wir uns schuldig erkennen,
> vor allen dingen Gotts ehr und unser bevolen underthanen selen und seligkeit zu furdern und
> fleiss zu haben, das sein wort lauter und reine geleret, sacramenta recht administriret, alle
> ergernuß abgeschaffet, gute disciplina und christliche zucht erhalten werde

Because the Almighty hath set us in this high office of authority, so that we acknowledge
ourselves bound above all things to further God's honour and the souls and salvation of the
subjects committed to us, and to take care that his word be taught clear and pure, the
sacraments rightly administered, all offences removed, good discipline and Christian order
maintained.

- **To appoint, pay and protect ministers.** Patrons and councils called ministers, but subject
  to the superintendent's examination (§§4, 7). The Saxon articles forbade patrons to charge
  "fief money" or to dismiss a pastor without cause shown before the consistory
  (1557, Sehling 1, p. 321). Councils paid ministers and schoolmasters from the common chest.
- **To enforce attendance and the Sabbath.** The district officer and council punished those who
  kept children and servants from the catechism (Belzig 1529, Sehling 1, p. 527). Absentees were
  fined (Nassau-Weilburg 1576, §8.1), and at Dillenburg in 1618 (Reformed) the fines went into
  the alms chest (Sehling 10, p. 203).
- **To punish what the church could only admonish.** The consistory handled "the degrees of
  admonition, and not the worldly punishment" (Saxony 1580, §5.3). What the church could not
  mend went to the magistrate: the Schulenburg lords were to punish or expel those who would not
  come to the sacrament (1572, Sehling 3, p. 149).
- **To refuse citizenship and marriage to the ignorant.** The Reformed Elector Palatine in 1599
  and 1608 forbade his officials to admit anyone as a citizen, or let anyone marry, who did not
  know the catechism (§11.4).
- **To be preached to.** At Schwäbisch Hall a sermon "on the office of the magistrate" was
  preached each year before the council election (1615, Sehling 17/1, p. 165). The visitor began
  each rural visitation with an exhortation "on the office of Christian magistrates … namely,
  among other things, to take the church diligently in hand after the example of the pious King
  of Judah, Jehoshaphat" (Sehling 17/1, p. 186).

### 10.3 The Reformed limit

**Bodies, not souls.** The Reformed orders drew the line more sharply. **Hanau-Münzenberg,
*Presbyteriumsordnung*, [1609]** (Reformed; Sehling 10, p. 512):

<!-- doc 211 -->
> Dan erstlich erstreckt sich das ampt der obrigkeit allein uber den leib und zeitliche guter
> der underthanen, uber die seelen aber der menschen und ihre ewige wolfahrt haben sie gar nicht
> zue herschen. Hingegen das ampt der eltesten gehet auf die gewißen der menschen, welche sie
> durch ihre vermahnung aus den sunden aufmuntern, durch den hamer des gottlichen gesetzes
> zerknirschen und zue Gott führen und durch die gnadenverheisung des evangelii widerumb
> aufrichten und trösten.

For first, the office of the magistrate extendeth only over the body and temporal goods of the
subjects, but over the souls of men and their eternal welfare they have no rule at all. On the
other hand, the office of the elders goeth to the consciences of men, which they rouse out of
sin by their admonition, crush with the hammer of the divine law and lead to God, and raise up
and comfort again by the promise of grace of the gospel.

Even so, a Hanau sermon plan of 1609 (Reformed) still called the magistrate *custos utriusque
tabulae quoad disciplinam externam*, keeper of both tables as far as outward discipline goes
(Sehling 10, p. 469). The ceremonies by which magistrates were installed and prayed for are in
[`EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md`](EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md), §13.

## 11. Catechesis: where the estates meet

Catechesis was the one duty shared by every estate:
- the pastor and deacon preached and examined;
- the schoolmaster drilled;
- the householder repeated it at home;
- the magistrate compelled attendance;
- the superintendent tested the results at visitation and synod.

The confirmation rites themselves are in
[`EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md`](EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md), §6, and the catechism sermon in
[`SERMONS_GUIDE.md`](SERMONS_GUIDE.md), §4.3. The catechism service itself, its hour, length and
order, is in [`OFFICES_GUIDE.md`](OFFICES_GUIDE.md), §10.

### 11.1 How often and how long

**Three rhythms.** The orders combine three rhythms of catechism teaching:
- **Weekly.** A catechism sermon or children's instruction on Sunday afternoon, often with a
  weekday repetition: Wednesday and Friday at Reuss (1528), "always half an hour"
  (Sehling 2, p. 151). The sermon was to last no more than half an hour (Nassau-Weilburg 1576,
  Sehling 10, p. 235). In a filial village the Sunday sermon was split, "half an hour" for the
  gospel and the other half for the catechism, said "word for word without exposition" (Eisfeld
  1554, Sehling 1, p. 562).
- **Quarterly series.** The Saxon visitors of 1529 ordered the catechism preached four times a
  year, a fortnight each time, with penalties for householders who did not send their children
  and servants. **Belzig, *Ordnung der Stadt Belzig*, 1529** (Sehling 1, p. 527):

<!-- doc 63 -->
> Desgleichen das der katechismus zu vier maln im jar alweg 14 tag ufs wenigst nacheinander
> gepredigt werden, dohin wir alle beerbeten ire kind und gesind bei vormeidung des ambts und
> rats straf zuschicken vorbinden thuen

Likewise that the catechism be preached four times in the year, each time at the least fourteen
days one after another, whither we bind all householders to send their children and servants on
pain of punishment by the district office and the council.

At Brück (1530) the pastor preached "the long catechism every day" for a fortnight before each
of the high feasts (Sehling 1, p. 535).
- **A yearly examination in Lent.** Saxony (1580) prescribed a "yearly examination of the
  catechism, which shall be held in Lent with the young servants" (Sehling 1, p. 424). Gotha
  (1555) taught the catechism daily in Lent from Invocavit to Palm Sunday (Sehling 1, p. 569).
  Pfalz-Neuburg (1576) held the yearly examination "most fitly in Lent, before the ordinary
  visitation", and told the pastors to use "all gentleness" and be content with "a fair answer"
  (Sehling 13, p. 177).

**House to house.** In small places the Saxon pastor examined his people one household at a
time. **Electoral Saxony, *Generalia, alle superintendentes belangende*, 1555**
(Sehling 1, p. 313):

<!-- doc 36 -->
> Auch in den flecken, do der inwoner nit all zuvil, soll der pfarrer ein zeit im jar da zu
> nehmen, und ein haus nach dem andern fur sich bescheiden, und die, so nit lesen konnen oder
> den pfarrern unbekant und vordechtig sein, sonderlich aber kinder und dienstboten in
> sunderheit im catechismo examinieren. So kan er nachmals dieselben, die in solcher verhor
> geschickt befunden sind, mit besserem gewissen zu dem abentmal des herrn kummen lassen […]
> Denn solchs durchaus soll gehalten werden, das niemand zu dem hochwirdigen sacrament
> zugelassen werde, der nit beten konne, und von den furnemsten artikeln der christlichen lehr,
> nit zimlichen bescheid an zeigen kann.

Also in the market towns where the inhabitants are not too many, the pastor shall take a time in
the year for it and summon one house after another before him, and examine in the catechism
those that cannot read or are unknown or suspect to the pastors, but especially children and
servants, each by himself. So can he afterwards let those that are found fit in such examination
come to the Lord's Supper with the better conscience […] For this shall be kept throughout, that
none be admitted to the holy sacrament who cannot pray and cannot give a fair account of the
chief articles of Christian doctrine.

**What had to be known.** The minimum was the text of the chief parts: five in some orders (Ten
Commandments, Creed, Our Father, Baptism, the Supper), six where the Office of the Keys was
added. The Small Catechism was learned by heart, the Large one read or preached (§9.1).

### 11.2 Before first communion

**An examination, and often a set course.** Every order requires an examination before a child's
first communion. Saxony (1580) puts it briefly. **Saxony, *Kirchenordnung*, 1580**
(Sehling 1, p. 424):

<!-- doc 44 -->
> Zum fünften, sollen die pfarrer sonderlich die das erste mal zu dem hochwirdigen sacrament des
> leibs und bluts Christi gehen, im catechismo mit fleis examinieren, ob sie denselben gelernet
> haben, auch ob sie zu der communion sonsten zuzulassen, eigentlich erkundigen.

Fifthly, the pastors shall especially examine with diligence in the catechism those that go for
the first time to the holy sacrament of the body and blood of Christ, whether they have learned
it, and also inquire carefully whether they are otherwise to be admitted to the communion.

Hohenlohe (1578) gives the clearest answer to "how long". The children of about twelve were
taught for an hour a day through Lent. **Hohenlohe, *Kirchenordnung*, 1578**
(Sehling 15, p. 277):

<!-- doc 574 -->
> Weiter sollen auch alle und jede pfarrer in stetten und dörfern alle jar, von Invocavit an biß
> auf Palmarum, diejenigen kinder, die umb das 12. jar sind und nun alters halben das heihg
> abendmal zum ersten mal empfahen sollen, im heiligen catechismo und den artikeln christlicher
> lehr alle tag ein stund, von 12 biß auf 1 uhr, unterrichten und dahin sehen, das keins zum
> abendmal gelassen werde, das mcht zuvor die sechs stück des catechismi könne und ein
> notwendigen verstand der christlichen lehr habe.

Further also all and every pastor in towns and villages shall every year, from Invocavit until
Palm Sunday, instruct those children that are about twelve years old and by reason of age are
now to receive the holy Supper for the first time, in the holy catechism and the articles of
Christian doctrine, every day for an hour, from twelve until one o'clock; and see to it that
none be admitted to the Supper who cannot first [say] the six parts of the catechism and have a
needful understanding of Christian doctrine.

That is some five or six weeks of daily instruction, on top of years of Sunday catechism and
school.

**Age.** The orders that name an age put first communion at about twelve:
- the Ansbach theologians in 1556 speak of children "of twelve years and above"
  (Sehling 11, p. 335);
- the Limpurg visitors in 1611 asked whether the pastor "communicates children under twelve
  years" (Sehling 16, p. 629);
- at Regensburg (1572/1588) children of "eight, nine, ten years" came to confession, and if they
  lacked understanding the parents were to be told "not to hurry with them"
  (Sehling 13, p. 510; §11.6);
- the Reformed church at Emden admitted children "about fourteen years", after a public
  profession before the congregation eight days before the Supper (Micron's *Ordinancien*,
  German edition of 1565, Sehling 7/1, p. 606).

### 11.3 First communion and confirmation

**Usually one act.** Was first communion separate from confirmation? In most orders that have a
confirmation it is the same occasion: the children are examined, confirmed, and admitted to
their first communion together. The Hessian form of 1566 (mediating) is headed "Questions and
answers for the children who are to be confirmed and admitted to communion for the first time"
(Sehling 8, p. 300). The heading recurs in the Hessian *Agende* of 1574 (mediating),
Nassau-Weilburg 1576 and Hanau 1609 (Reformed) (Sehling 8, p. 433; 10, pp. 254, 498). Mansfeld
held the examination publicly in church the day before. **Mansfeld, *Kirchen-agenda*, 1580**
(Sehling 2, p. 233):

<!-- doc 1241 -->
> Nach dem auch in unsern kirchen bis daher diese christliche gewonheit erhalten ist, das man
> die kinder, so zum ersten mal zum hochwirdigen sacrament gehen sollen, den nehesten tag zuvor,
> ehe denn es geschiehet, in der kirchen offentlich verhöret, und hierzu alle monat ein gewisser
> tag in jeder kirchen geordnet und benennet wird, das sie auch, wenn sie im catechismo verhöret
> und bestanden sind, alda nach altem und löblichen gebrauch der kirchen confirmiret, und uber
> sie gott der herr angerufen und gebetet wird, so ermanen wir alle christliche, gottfürchtige
> eltern und hausherren, auch schulmeister und schulmeisterin, das sie solche gewonheit erhalten
> helfen, und auf benente zeit ire kinder, gesind, Schulkinder, den seelsorgern zuverhören
> selbst fürstellen

Whereas also in our churches this Christian custom hath been kept hitherto, that the children
who are to go for the first time to the holy sacrament are publicly examined in the church on
the next day before it happeneth, and for this a fixed day is ordained and named every month in
every church, and that when they have been examined in the catechism and have passed, they are
there confirmed according to the old and laudable use of the church, and the Lord God is called
upon and prayed to over them: so we exhort all Christian, God-fearing parents and householders,
also schoolmasters and schoolmistresses, that they help to maintain this custom, and at the
named time themselves present their children, servants and schoolchildren to the pastors to be
examined.

**Twice a year at Lauenburg.** Saxe-Lauenburg (1585) confirmed twice a year, on the Sunday
before Michaelmas and on Quasimodogeniti. The parents first examined the children at home and
had their schoolmasters hear them, "that they may give good account … distinctly, with clear
voice, without shyness", and then presented them before the altar with the godparents
(Sehling 5, pp. 458–459).

**A second hearing.** Hessen-Kassel (1610, Reformed) required the newly confirmed to repeat
their confession half a year later before the second communion. **Hessen-Kassel,
*Konsistoriumsordnung*, 1610** (Reformed; Sehling 9, p. 118):

<!-- doc 2287 -->
> so sollen die kinder, beits, knaben und megtlein, nicht allein, wann sie zum ersten mahl zum
> h. nachtmal gelaßen und confirmirt werden, zuvor ihr bekantnis nach allen fünff hautpstucken
> christlicher lehr dem pfarherrn thun, sondern sie sollen auch dieselbe jahr ihre bekantnis zu
> mehrer und fernerer behaltnis alzeit ein halb jar nach der confirmation fur dem pfarherrn
> wiederholen und darauff zum andern mahl communiciren

the children, both boys and girls, shall not only, when they are admitted for the first time to
the holy Supper and confirmed, first make their confession before the pastor according to all
five chief parts of Christian doctrine, but they shall also in the same year, for the better and
longer keeping of it, always repeat their confession before the pastor half a year after the
confirmation, and thereupon communicate the second time.

**Examination without confirmation.** Where there was no confirmation rite, as in Electoral
Saxony, the examination alone admitted the child to communion (above;
[`EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md`](EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md), §6.7). No order requires a
separate course for confirmation apart from the course for first communion. The one instruction
served both.

### 11.4 Before marriage

**No catechism, no banns.** Many orders refused to proclaim or marry the young who did not know
the catechism. **Saxony, *Kirchenordnung*, 1580** (Sehling 1, p. 436):

<!-- doc 44 -->
> und da es junge leute, ob sie auch iren catechismum gelernet, ohne dessen erkentnis sie nicht
> aufgeboten werden sollen.

and if they be young folk, [the pastor shall inquire] whether they have also learned their
catechism, without knowledge of which they shall not be proclaimed.

The pastor was also to ask "whether they have received the holy sacrament publicly in the church
with the congregation of God" (Sehling 1, p. 436). Pfalz-Neuburg (1576) set out the whole
interview: kinship, parents' consent, freedom from other promises, and the catechism.
**Pfalz-Neuburg, *Generalartikel*, 1576** (Sehling 13, p. 195):

<!-- doc 404 -->
> erstlich, ob und wie si einander mit sipp- oder magschaft verwandt, zum andern, ob es mit
> wissen und willen der eltern und nit heimblicher weise beschehen, zum dritten, ob si beede
> ledig und sonst niemands anderm versprochen seien, solchem nach si beede aus dem catechismo,
> wie si desselben einen underricht haben, verhören. Da nun in solchem allen kain mangl
> befunden, sollen die neue eheleut ohne alle hindernus vermög der kirchenordnung eingesegnet
> werden.

first, whether and how they be related to one another by blood or by marriage; secondly, whether
it hath been done with the knowledge and will of the parents and not secretly; thirdly, whether
they be both single and not promised to any other; and after this, examine them both out of the
catechism, how far they have instruction in it. If now no defect be found in all this, the new
married folk shall be blessed without any hindrance according to the church order.

**Why the household needed it.** Zweibrücken (1574) gives the reason: the couple would have to
teach their own children and servants. **Pfalz-Zweibrücken, *Kirchenordnung*, 1574**
(Sehling 18, p. 529):

<!-- doc 1011 -->
> Es sollen auch die Pfarrer die newen eheleuth zu sich vor dem kürchgang fordern und von ihnen
> Rechenschafft ires glaubens und der lehr des Catechismi begehren. Dann weil sie haußhalten
> sollen, khinder und gesünde bekhommen, ist jhe nutz, ja es erforderts die hohe nott unnd der
> ernnste bevelch Gottes, das sie sich und die selbigen wissen in bekantnus Christlicher lehr
> und wahrer Religion Recht zu underrichten und auffzuziehen

The pastors shall also call the new married folk to them before the church-going and require of
them an account of their faith and of the doctrine of the catechism. For since they are to keep
house and get children and servants, it is profitable indeed, yea, the high necessity and the
earnest command of God require it, that they know how to instruct and bring up themselves and
them rightly in the confession of Christian doctrine and true religion.

Hesse (1566, mediating) has the same rule (Sehling 8, p. 322). The rule was repeated across the
later century:
- Mansfeld, by resolution of its ministers, 1580 (Sehling 2, p. 233);
- the Transylvanian synod of Hermannstadt, 1565: if they cannot answer, "one shall delay the
  copulation until they learn" (Sehling 24, p. 296);
- Prussia, 1568 (Sehling 4, p. 93);
- the Hessian *Reformationsordnung* of 1572 (mediating): the ignorant were not to be married,
  nor admitted to communion or to stand godparent, and were to be reported to the authorities
  (Sehling 8, p. 398);
- Nassau-Diez 1585 (Reformed), Ysenburg-Birstein 1588, Marienhafe 1593 and the Solms discipline
  order of 1599 (Reformed) (Sehling 10, p. 177; 10, p. 632; 7/1, p. 717; 9, p. 339).

The Reformed Elector Palatine in 1599 and 1608 forbade his officials to accept anyone as a
citizen, or let anyone marry, without a pastor's written certificate that they could recite the
chief parts (Sehling 19/2, pp. 963–964).

**Married on a promise.** Where a wedding could not be put off, Wied (1616, Reformed) married
the couple on a pledge, backed if need be by sureties. **Wied, *Kirchen- und Polizeiordnung*,
1616** (Reformed; Sehling 19/1, p. 500):

<!-- doc 1100 -->
> Und da auß erheblichen ursachen etwa die copulation angehender, junger eheleut länger nicht
> uffgeschoben werden könte, so sollen die angehende eheleut handtgegebener trew zusagen, auch,
> da nötigh, deßwegen burgen setzen, das sie bei der catechismi predigten sich fleißigh
> einstellen und finden laßen wollen, biß sie die lehr desselben wolgelernet und gefaßet haben.

And if for weighty reasons the copulation of young folk about to marry could be put off no
longer, then the couple shall promise by giving their hand, and also, if need be, set sureties
for it, that they will diligently attend and be found at the catechism sermons until they have
well learned and grasped its doctrine.

### 11.5 Premarital instruction and counsel

**No counselling course.** No order prescribes premarital counselling in the modern sense: a
series of meetings on the duties of marriage. What the orders require is three things.

**1. An interview some weeks before.** The couple, or their parents, came to the pastor in good
time. At that meeting the pastor established their freedom to marry and examined their faith.
**Limpurg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1610** (Sehling 16, p. 621):

<!-- doc 729 -->
> Erstlichs sollen die Newe Eheleuth oder an ihrer statt die Eltern, Vormundt oder Freundt
> beederseits drey wochen vor ihrem Kirchgang bey dem Pfarherr sich einstellen oder, do ein oder
> ander theil abwesent, von ihnen schrifftlichen beglaubten schein bringen, der dann fleissig
> auffsehen und forschen soll, ob es mit wissen und willen geschehe deren, in welcher gewaldt
> sie sein, sonderlich der Eltern, ob kein verhinderung wegen naher verwandtschafft oder anderer
> wichtigen ursachen

First, the new married folk, or in their stead the parents, guardians or kinsfolk on both sides,
shall present themselves before the pastor three weeks before their church-going, or, if one or
the other party be absent, bring a written attested certificate from them. And he shall
diligently look and inquire whether it be done with the knowledge and will of those in whose
power they are, especially the parents, [and] whether there be no hindrance by reason of near
kinship or other weighty causes.

In the villages of Brieg (1592) "the bride and bridegroom shall first recite the parts of the
catechism to their pastor on a set day" (Sehling 3, p. 447). The Brieg visitation instruction of
1542 had already ordered people to come when the pastor sent for them, "especially when people
would enter into the estate of marriage" (Sehling 3, p. 441).

**2. Confession and communion before the wedding.** Schwäbisch Hall's revised order of 1615
joins the examination to confession. **Schwäbisch Hall, *Kirchenordnung*, 1543, revised text of
1615** (Sehling 17/1, p. 155):

<!-- doc 762 -->
> Sonderlich aber sollen Newe Eheleuth den H. Ehestandt in Gottesforcht anfahen, mittlen und
> volenden, den Segen von Gott zuerlangen, auch vor der Hochzeit sich zur Beicht schicken und
> darauff die Absolution und das H. Abendtmal empfahen zu erweckung und stärckung eines guoten
> Fürsatzs, Gott in disem Standt nach seinem Wort zudienen und also Christum zur Hochzeit laden,
> der kan im Ehestandt auß Wasser Wein machen, das ist, Leyd inn Frewd verwandlen. In der Beicht
> sollen billich die Jungen, Newen Eheleuth auff deß Pastoris examen von der Lehr deß H.
> Catechismi Rechenschafft geben

But especially shall new married folk begin, continue and end holy matrimony in the fear of God,
to obtain the blessing from God, and also before the wedding betake themselves to confession and
thereupon receive absolution and the holy Supper, for the awakening and strengthening of a good
purpose to serve God in this estate according to his word, and so to invite Christ to the
wedding, who can turn water into wine in matrimony, that is, sorrow into joy. In confession the
young new married folk ought rightly to give account, upon the pastor's examination, of the
doctrine of the holy catechism.

Freudenthal (1592) required bride and groom to come "together to confession and to God's table
before the wedding" (Sehling 3, p. 480).

**3. The exhortation and the wedding sermon.** The teaching on marriage itself was given
publicly, in the exhortation read at the wedding and in the wedding sermon
([`SERMONS_GUIDE.md`](SERMONS_GUIDE.md), §4.7). Pfalz-Neuburg allowed the exhortation, "which is
in itself a fine short sermon on marriage", to be read "for the instruction and reminding of the
new married folk" when there was no time for a sermon (1576, Sehling 13, p. 197). Schwäbisch
Hall's pastors were pointed for marriage cases to the handbooks of Hemmingsen, Heshusius,
Bidembach, Beust, Porta's *Pastorale Lutheri* and Sarcerius (1615, Sehling 17/1, p. 155).

### 11.6 Infant communion

**No order gives the Supper to infants.** Did any order give communion to baptized infants, or
argue for it? None does. Every order that speaks of first communion requires instruction and
examination first (§11.2). Four orders name the communion of infants outright, and all four set
it aside. Their reasons are three:
- the Apostle's command to examine oneself and discern the Lord's body (1 Corinthians 11:28–29),
  which infants cannot do;
- the judgment of the early church;
- the children of Christians are saved by baptism and faith without the Supper, so to wait does
  them no harm.

The orders of Hesse (mediating) and Hohenlohe (Lutheran) grant that infant communion was the
custom in the time of Cyprian and Augustine. Hesse leaves the custom "in its worth" and does not
condemn it. The Reformed preacher at Markirch (1561) calls it an error of the ancients.

**Hesse: the ancient custom left unjudged.** The Hessian order of 1566 (mediating) prefaces its
rite of confirmation with the history. **Hesse, *Kirchenordnung*, 1566**
(mediating; Sehling 8, pp. 286–287):

<!-- doc 2257 -->
> Vor zeiten ward den kindern gleich wie den alten, so bald sie getauft, auch das sacrament des
> leibs und bluts Christi zu essen und zu trinken gereicht, wie das bezeugt Cyprianus in der
> predig de lapsis und Augustinus, wie zuvor vermeldt. Solchs lassen wir in seinem wert beruhen.
> Wir aber, zum teil bewegt durch den spruch des apostels: Der mensch prüffe sich selbst und
> also esse er von diesem brot und trinke von diesem kelche, etc., zum teil folgende dem
> gemeinen urteil der ersten kirchen, verziehen, das heilig abendmal den kindern zu reichen uf
> ein ander zeit, und achten notwendiger sein, daß, welche in der kindheit, da sie noch nicht
> vor sich selbst dem teufel absagen noch den glauben Christi bekennen mögen, durch die hei.
> taufe gereiniget und dem Herrn Christo und seiner kirchen eingeleibt seind, so bald die zeit
> kompt ihres verstands, daß sie mögen in den heuptartickeln christlicher lehr und religion
> underwiesen werden und itzt etlichermaßen underrichtet sein, daß sie alsdenn ihren glauben vor
> der kirchen bekennen und bericht geben von den vornemsten heuptpunkten christlicher religion
> […]

In times past the sacrament of the body and blood of Christ was given to the children to eat and
to drink, even as to the old, as soon as they were baptized, as Cyprian testifieth in the sermon
*de lapsis*, and Augustine, as was said before. That we leave in its worth. But we, moved partly
by the saying of the apostle, Let a man examine himself, and so let him eat of this bread and
drink of this cup, etc., and partly following the common judgment of the first church, defer the
giving of the holy Supper to the children unto another time. And we hold it more needful that
those who in their infancy, when they could not yet of themselves renounce the devil nor confess
the faith of Christ, were cleansed by holy baptism and incorporated into the Lord Christ and his
church, should, so soon as the time of their understanding cometh, that they may be instructed
in the chief articles of Christian doctrine and religion and are now somewhat taught, then
confess their faith before the church and give account of the foremost chief points of the
Christian religion […]

Earlier, the same order explains the ancient custom by the early church's joining of baptism and
the Supper. The newly baptized "all went straightway to the holy Supper, and so also in the
times of holy Augustine it was given to the children, which we yet leave in its worth"
(Sehling 8, p. 273).

**Oldenburg: infants cannot examine themselves.** The Oldenburg order of 1573 argues for infant
baptism and, in passing, against infant communion. **Oldenburg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1573**
(Sehling 7/2.1, p. 1047):

<!-- doc 2148 -->
> Nun kan man durchs blosse wort alleine mit den kinderlein nicht handeln, denn man kan sie noch
> nicht lehren, derhalben mus es durch die sacrament geschehen, wie im Alten Testament durch die
> beschneidung. Vom abentmal aber des Herrn spricht Paulus: Der mensch prüfe sich selbs, item,
> er sol unterscheiden den leib des Herrn, 1.Cor. 11, welches die jungen kinderlein noch nicht
> tun können.

Now one cannot deal with the little children by the bare word alone, for one cannot yet teach
them; therefore it must be done through the sacrament, as in the Old Testament through
circumcision. But of the Supper of the Lord Paul saith, Let a man examine himself; and again, he
shall discern the Lord's body, 1 Cor. 11; which the young little children cannot yet do.

**Hohenlohe: children saved without the Supper.** Hohenlohe in 1578 answered those who held the
Supper needless to the baptized believer. **Hohenlohe, *Kirchenordnung*, 1578**
(Sehling 15, p. 334):

<!-- doc 574 -->
> Darnach, so ist es war, die kinder der Christen werden durch den glauben und tauf selig, on
> den gebrauch des nachtmals, wie Christus sagt: Wer glaubt und getauft wird, der wird selig.
> Wiewol es zu der zeit Augustini der brauch ist gewesen, das man die jungen kinder mit dem
> kelch des Herrn getrenkt hat, jedoch so volget hierauß nicht, das die erwachsenen nicht
> bedörfen des heiligen nachtmals und sey inen genug die empfangenen tauf und glauben.

Moreover, it is true that the children of Christians are saved through faith and baptism,
without the use of the Supper, as Christ saith, He that believeth and is baptized shall be
saved. Although in the time of Augustine it was the custom that the young children were given to
drink of the cup of the Lord, yet it followeth not hereof that the grown need not the holy
Supper, and that the baptism and faith they have received are enough for them.

**Markirch: an error of the ancients.** Arnaud Banc, preacher of the Reformed French
congregation in the Leber valley at Markirch, in the lordship of Rappoltstein, explained in 1561
why his church would not carry the Supper to the sick. Private communion, he wrote, rested on a
false belief. **Rappoltstein, Arnaud Banc's report on the doctrine of the French congregation,
1561** (Reformed; Sehling 20/2, p. 142):

<!-- doc 1371 -->
> Raichet uff die opinion unnd meinung deren, die vermeinen, das alle personen, so das nachtmal
> nit entpfanngen heten, verloren werden; in welchem die alten auch geirt haben, welche
> deßhalben den kleinen kindern das nachtmal geraicht haben.

This pertaineth to the opinion and belief of those who suppose that all persons who had not
received the Supper are lost; wherein the ancients also erred, who for that cause gave the
Supper to the little children.

**Instruction first.** Other orders reach the same practice without naming the ancient custom.
Brenz at Schwäbisch Hall in 1527 drew the line from the catechumenate of the early church.
**Schwäbisch Hall, *Ordnung der Gottesdienste an Werk- und Feiertagen*, 1527**
(Sehling 17/1, p. 66):

<!-- doc 756 -->
> Fuit et is mos in ecclesia recens orta, ut nemo ad baptismum admissus fuerit nisi qui ante sit
> cathecuminus. Nunc cum baptisemur pueri, instituendi sumus in rebus fidei, priusquam ad
> communionem cene dominice admittamur.

This also was the custom in the church newly risen, that none was admitted to baptism but he
that had first been a catechumen. Now, since we are baptized as children, we must be instructed
in the things of faith before we be admitted to the communion of the Lord's Supper.

The Limpurg visitors' question of 1611, whether the pastor "communicates children under twelve
years" (§11.2), presumes the same rule.

**No harm to the child who waits.** An order that deferred the Supper had to say what became of
a child who died before first communion. Regensburg told the confessors to warn parents not to
hurry children of eight, nine or ten years who lacked understanding. **Regensburg,
*Kirchenregimentsordnung*, 1572/1588** (Sehling 13, p. 510):

<!-- doc 452 -->
> weil zu diesem sacrament ein sonderer verstand und prob gehörig, und daß ihnen an ihrer
> seligkeit, da sie sonsten wol erzogen, nicht nachteilig, wie die dann, so sie Gott aus diesem
> leben, ehe dann si zur communion kommen, abfordern solte, sowol als andere erwachsenen auf
> ihrer, der eltern begern sollen mit der gewöhnlichen proceß und ceremonien, laut der
> regimentsordnung zur erden bestättigt werden.

because to this sacrament belongeth a special understanding and trial; and that it is no hurt to
their salvation, if they be otherwise well brought up; as also such children, if God should call
them out of this life before they come to the communion, shall at their parents' desire be laid
in the earth with the accustomed procession and ceremonies, as well as other grown persons,
according to the order of government.

Henneberg abolished a custom of burying such children apart. **Henneberg, *Kirchenordnung*,
1582** (Sehling 2, p. 316):

<!-- doc 1247 -->
> Weil auch an etlichen orten bisanhero der brauch gehalten, das man die kindlein, so das
> abendmal Christi noch nicht empfangen, nicht mit den schülern, wie die alten, zur erden
> bestattet, so sol doch hinfort hierinnen kein unterscheid gemacht, sondern die kinder weniger
> nicht, als die alten, mit vorgesetzten ceremonien zum begrebnus gebracht werden.

And whereas in some places the custom hath hitherto been kept that the little children which had
not yet received the Supper of Christ were not laid in the earth with the scholars, as the old
are, henceforth no difference shall be made herein, but the children shall be brought to burial
with the ceremonies set down above, no less than the old.

**The sick child.** The one exception to the order of instruction, confirmation and communion
was the child in danger of death. Nassau-Dillenburg (1575, mediating) kept the unconfirmed from
the sacrament, but not a dying child who understood the catechism and asked for it.
**Nassau-Dillenburg, *Agende*, 1575** (mediating; Sehling 10, p. 155):

<!-- doc 182 -->
> Welche kinder auff solche weis noch nit verhort und confirmirt seint, die soll man vom
> sacrament abhalten, biß sie durch die confirmation aufgenomen werden. Doch so jemant under den
> kindern kranck und die schwachheit sich etwa geferlich ansehen ließ, so dasselbige
> zimlichermassen verstendigh und mit dem catechismo gefaßet und das abentmahell begeren wurde,
> soll es ime keineswegs abgeschlagen werden, ohnerachtet, das es die confirmation noch nicht
> entpfangen. Es soll aber nicht destoweniger ein solch kindt sich zur confirmation nach
> gelangter gesundheit eintzustellen verpflichten.

Those children that have not yet in such wise been heard and confirmed shall be kept from the
sacrament, until they be received by confirmation. Yet if any among the children be sick, and
the weakness seem somewhat dangerous, and the child be fairly understanding and grounded in the
catechism, and desire the Supper, it shall in no wise be denied it, notwithstanding that it hath
not yet received confirmation. But such a child shall none the less bind itself to present
itself for confirmation when it hath recovered its health.
