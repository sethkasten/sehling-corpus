# Minor Orders, Deacons and Elders in the Sehling Church Orders

This guide covers what became of the medieval grades and liturgical offices below the priesthood
in the evangelical church orders printed in Emil Sehling's *Die evangelischen Kirchenordnungen
des XVI. Jahrhunderts*. It also covers the new offices that took their place. It asks eight
questions:

- Were any of the **minor orders** (porter, lector, exorcist, acolyte) or the **subdiaconate**
  kept as formal orders?
- Do the orders mention or prescribe the **liturgical roles**: lector, thurifer, candle-bearer
  (*accensor*, *ceroferarius*), cross-bearer, torch-bearer, boat-bearer and master of
  ceremonies? This is asked whether or not a formal order stood behind the role.
- Where was the **diaconate** kept? Was it kept as an order below the presbyterate, as a
  liturgical role at the altar, or as a lay office for the poor?
- Where it was kept, was it held to be a separate order **by divine right**, or only **by human
  right**? Were there **lay deacons**?
- Did any order have **lay elders** or **ruling elders**? Were they **ordained**?
- What did each of these offices do, and what were they forbidden to do?
- Which offices were **abolished** or **forbidden**?
- On what **grounds** were the offices kept, created or abolished?

The findings are summarised in §1, and §2 explains the method and the words that most often
mislead. §3 deals with the minor orders and the subdiaconate, and §4 with the liturgical roles.
§5 covers the diaconate in its three forms and the question of divine or human right. §6 covers
elders. §7 gathers how each office was installed, and whether it was ordained. §8 is a table by
order, and §9 a concordance of every order quoted.

**Conventions**

- **Quotations.** Every quotation is Sehling's text as it stands in the `eko.db` database built
  from the digitized edition.
  - Footnote numbers, sigla, folio markers and interleaved apparatus are left out.
  - Sehling's own supplied words in square brackets are kept, as are "[!]", "[sic]" and his
    other signs.
  - Spelling and punctuation are as printed, including plain OCR errors.
  - "[…]" marks an omission.
  - Every quotation has been checked against the database text, allowing for OCR noise: at least
    90 per cent of its words must be found there in order. Its page has been checked against the
    edition's page markers.
  - Each quotation is preceded by an HTML comment, invisible when rendered, that names the
    `eko.db` document it was checked against.
- **Translations.** The English is formal-equivalence, in the idiom of the Authorized Version:
  - Word order and clause structure are kept where English allows.
  - Stock terms are rendered the same way throughout:
    - *Kirchendiener* is "minister of the church", *Diener des Worts* "minister of the word".
    - *Pfarrer*, *Pfarrherr* is "pastor".
    - *Diacon*, *diaken* is "deacon". *Eltesten*, *seniores* is "elders". *Kirchenpfleger* is
      "churchwarden".
    - *ordinieren* is "ordain". *bestätigen*, *bestetigen* is "confirm". *einsetzen* is "set in
      office".
    - *Opfermann*, *Küster* is "sexton".
  - Words the translation supplies are in square brackets.
- **Citations.** Citations are in the form "Sehling volume, page".
  - The half-volumes of the edition are written 6/1, 6/2, 7/1, 7/2.1, 7/2.2, 17/1, 17/2, 19/1,
    19/2, 20/1 and 20/2, following the digitized volumes.
  - Each order is named by place, title and date as they appear in Sehling's running heads.
    Where a passage is Sehling's own introduction or apparatus, not the text of an order, this
    is said.

## 1. Summary of findings

**1. The minor orders were not kept as orders anywhere in the German church orders.** No order
in the corpus ordains a porter, lector, exorcist or acolyte, nor a subdeacon in the medieval
sense. Their disappearance is mostly silent. Where it is argued, the grounds are three:
- They have no warrant in Scripture. Homberg 1526 says "Scripture knows no other ministers than
  bishops, that is presbyters, and deacons of the poor" (§3.2).
- They were later human additions to the apostolic diaconate. The Strasbourg examination order
  of about 1539 gives a short history of the grades (§3.1).
- They belong with the tonsure, the "platten", and other merely human ordinances. Freiberg 1537
  and Brunswick 1528 say so (§3.3).

**The one exception lies outside the Lutheran and Reformed territorial churches.** The *Ordo
ecclesiasticus* of the Bohemian Brethren, printed among the Polish orders, keeps the **acolyte**
as the first ordained grade, under the deacon (§3.4). The young men are received before the
synod with a handshake, a promise and a blessing.

**2. The functions of the minor orders survived without the orders.**
- *Porter:* the porter's keys passed to the **sexton** (*Opfermann*, *Küster*). Hesse 1566 calls
  him the *ianitor* of the Old Testament and the *aedituus* or *custos* of today (§3.5).
- *Lector:* reading passed to the **schoolboys**, who read the epistle at Vespers (Rostock
  1560), and to the **deacon**, the assistant minister.
- *Exorcist:* the office disappeared altogether. Strasbourg says exorcism was "rather a singular
  gift than a common function".

**3. The liturgical roles were rarely prescribed and soon dropped.**
- **Ministrants:** in the 1520s–1540s some Mass-shaped orders still have ministrants
  (*Ministranten*) at the altar. Examples are Nürnberg 1524 and Brandenburg 1540, the latter "in
  deacons' dalmatics" where there are enough clergy. In Pfalz-Neuburg 1543, "where there are
  ministrants", the subdeacon reads the epistle and the deacon the gospel and gives the cup
  (§4.1–4.2).
- **Levites:** Regensburg 1542 kept a full **levitical service**, with a priest celebrant and
  two "levites", a gospeller and an epistoler. Regensburg abolished the levites and their
  vestments in 1554, and again in its church order of 1567. Brandenburg-Ansbach's chapter order
  of 1565/78 forbade "levites after the papistical use" (§4.3).
- **Thurifer:** Luther left incense free in 1523, "we neither forbid it nor require it". Incense
  then disappears from the rubrics (§4.4).
- **Cross:** a **cross** is carried in a rogation procession at Stendal (1541). At burials in
  Schwäbisch Hall (1615) it is allowed "significatively, not effectively" (§4.5).
- **Not found:** the corpus holds no candle-bearer (*accensor*, *ceroferarius*), torch-bearer,
  boat-bearer or master of ceremonies as a prescribed role.

**4. The diaconate survived in three distinct forms, which the same word hides:**
- **The Lutheran *Diaconus*.** This is the second or third minister of a town church: a
  *Helfer*, *Kaplan* or *Capellan*. He is a full minister of the word and sacraments, **ordained
  with the same rite as a pastor**, and usually a step on the way to a pastorate. Electoral
  Saxony 1580 allows a gifted man to become a pastor "without passing through the diaconate"
  (§5.1). Württemberg and Saxony also have a *Subdiaconus*, a third minister. These are grades
  of benefice and seniority, not separate orders.
- **The liturgical deacon and subdeacon at the altar.** These occur in a few early orders and
  are normally priests acting as levites (§4.2).
- **The lay deacon of the poor.** This office was built on Acts 6 and 1 Timothy 3:
  - In **Bugenhagen's** orders the treasury and alms deacons (*diakene*) are honest laymen who
    handle the common chest and visit no one with the word. Brunswick 1528 says they serve as
    Stephen and Lawrence did, "without tonsure and without deacon's coat" (§3.3, §5.2). The same
    office appears in Hamburg 1529, Lübeck 1531, Pomerania, and the Lutheran Dutch congregation
    at Frankfurt (1585).
  - Elsewhere they are called *leviten* (Hadeln 1526), *subdiaconi oder bittherren* (Naumburg
    1537), or *diaconi ecclesiae et pauperum*. In Pomerania 1574 the *diaconi ecclesiae et
    pauperum* are bound by oath; in Pyritz 1539, by express decision, by no oath.
  - In **Hesse 1566** and the **Reformed** churches (Micron 1554/65, Kurpfalz, Nassau, Bentheim)
    the deacons of the poor are one of the four offices. They are elected, examined, and in
    Hesse and Micron **ordained with laying on of hands** (§5.3).

**5. Divine or human right.** No church order sets the deacon as a separate order *by divine
right* above the people and below the presbyter in the Roman sense. The positions are these:
- **One ministry of the word by divine right; grades by human right.**
  - Bugenhagen writes "bishops or preachers (which is all one thing)".
  - Pfalz-Zweibrücken 1539 speaks of "the nearest bishops, whom men also call pastors".
  - Melanchthon's *Wittenberg Reformation* (1545) puts bishops "as a grade above other priests"
    on grounds of "human foresight".
  - The Lutheran diaconate falls under this rule. It is one ministry, graded for order's sake
    (§5.4).
- **The lay deacon of the poor as an apostolic institution.**
  - Homberg 1526 says Scripture knows "deacons of the poor".
  - Strasbourg says the diaconate was instituted "by the Holy Ghost through the Apostles", the
    subdiaconate and the rest only "afterwards".
  - Hesse 1566 cites Acts 6, and so do the Reformed orders. The editors of the Hildesheim volume
    read Bugenhagen's ranking of poor relief among "divine ordinances" as making the deacon's
    office one of divine right (Sehling 7/2.1, p. 811).
  - The Bohemian Brethren keep the diaconate as a grade which "it is not fitting to cast out of
    the church", though "the Antichrist drew it into abuse".

**6. Lay elders existed, chiefly in the Bucerian and Reformed orders, and were often ordained.**
- **Strasbourg (about 1539)** teaches two "orders of presbyters": those who administer word and
  sacraments, and those "employed only for the discipline of the church". Its churchwardens
  (*Kirchenpfleger*) of 1553 are these *seniores* and *presbyteri* (§6.1).
- **Hesse (1539, 1566)** has the elders elected by the ministers and congregation and confirmed
  with prayer. In 1566 they are **"ordained" with laying on of hands** under Acts 14:23. Elders
  also lay hands on new ministers at their ordination (§6.2).
- **The Reformed orders** have elected elders who govern with the ministers, exercise discipline
  (Matthew 18) and oversee the ministers' doctrine and life. The installation forms differ:
  - laying on of hands: Micron 1554/65 and Kurpfalz 1592;
  - prayer and commendation only: Pfalz-Veldenz 1574;
  - handfast vow and public prayer: Kurpfalz about 1601 and Hanau 1609;
  - a sworn oath: Solms (§6.3).
- **The Bohemian Brethren** elect lay "political presbyters" (*presbyteri politici*), and female
  *presbyterae* for the women. These bind themselves by voice and handshake but receive no
  laying on of hands (§6.4).

**7. In the Lutheran orders "elder" is usually not an office of governance.**
- The *Kirchenväter*, *Kirchgeschworene*, *Kastenherren* and *Kirchenpfleger* are lay stewards
  of church goods and alms.
- Memmingen (1532) gives its churchwardens "no temporal jurisdiction", "so that two temporal
  authorities arise not again in our city" (§6.5).
- *Seniores* in Ansbach, Pomerania and Silesia are senior **clergy** of a chapter or synod.

**8. What the offices could not do.**
- Lay deacons and lay elders did not preach or administer the sacraments.
- In the Bohemian order even the ordained deacon might distribute the bread and the cup, but
  only a minister "having the power of the keys" might pronounce absolution and recite the words
  of institution (§5.3).
- Reformed elders had spiritual discipline but no civil punishment. In Kurpfalz the advocates of
  presbyteries pleaded their divine right against Erastus. By the editors' account the dispute
  was settled in 1569 by opinions of Beza, Ursinus and Zanchi, and in 1570 an edict set up
  church discipline (Sehling 14, p. 53; §6.3).

## 2. Scope, sources and the words that mislead

**Sources.** The `eko.db` database was searched for the names of every grade and role in
question, in German, Low German and Latin spellings. The searches covered:
- *ostiarius*, *Türhüter*, *ianitor*;
- *lector*, *Leser*;
- *exorcista*, *Beschwörer*;
- *acoluthus*, *Akolyth*;
- *subdiaconus*, *Unterdiakon*;
- *ministrant*, *Levit*, *Evangelier*, *Epistler*;
- *thuribulum*, *Rauchfass*, *Weihrauch*, *navicula*, *Schiffchen*;
- *ceroferarius*, *Kerzenträger*, *Fackel*, *Leuchter*;
- *crucifer*, *Kreuz vortragen*, *Kreuzträger*;
- *ceremoniarius*, *magister caerimoniarum*;
- *Diaconus*, *Diakon*, *diaken*, *Helfer*, *Kaplan*;
- *Eltesten*, *Senior*, *Presbyter*, *Kirchenpfleger*, *Kirchenälteste*, *Censor*.

The registers of the edition were used to find further passages. The passages were then read in
context.

The corpus is very uneven on these matters:
- The **Lutheran** orders say almost nothing about the minor orders. They simply stopped
  ordaining them, and the Mass rubrics assume a pastor, a deacon (assistant minister), a sexton
  and a school choir.
- The **Bucerian, Hessian and Reformed** orders (Strasbourg, Hesse, East Frisia, Kurpfalz,
  Nassau and the Wetterau counties) say a great deal about elders and deacons. They argue for
  them from Scripture and the early Church.
- The **Bohemian Brethren's** *Ordo ecclesiasticus*, printed in Sehling's Polish volume (vol.
  4), is the only order in the corpus with a graded clergy below the presbyter.

**Words that mislead.** The same word often names quite different offices:

| Word | Usual meaning in the corpus | Also used for |
|---|---|---|
| *Diaconus*, *Diacon*, *Helfer*, *Kaplan* | In Lutheran towns, the second or third **ordained minister** of a parish (§5.1) | The **lay deacon of the poor** (Bugenhagen, Hesse, the Reformed churches; §5.2–5.3); the **ministrant at the altar** in a few early Mass orders (§4.2) |
| *Subdiaconus*, *Unterdiacon* | In Saxony, Württemberg and Halle, the **third minister** of a town church (§3.3) | Naumburg 1537: a **lay alms-collector** (*bittherr*, §5.2); Pfalz-Neuburg 1543: the **epistoler** at Mass (§4.2) |
| *Levit*, *Leviten* | In Regensburg and Nürnberg, the **two assisting clergy** (gospeller and epistoler) at Mass (§4.3) | Hadeln 1526: the **lay deacons of the poor** (§5.2) |
| *Eltesten*, *Seniores*, *Presbyteri* | In Hesse and the Reformed orders, **lay governing elders** (§6.2–6.3) | Also the **ministers of the word**, the "elders who labour in the word" (Hesse 1566, Micron, Kurpfalz 1592); in Ansbach, Pomerania and Silesia, the **senior clergy** of a chapter or synod (§6.5) |
| *Kirchenpfleger*, *Kirchenväter*, *Kastenherren* | In most Lutheran orders, **lay stewards** of church goods (§6.5) | Strasbourg 1553: the **disciplinary elders**, "whom they named *seniores* and *presbyteri*" (§6.1) |
| *Ordinieren* | The ordination of a **minister of the word** | Hesse 1566, Lippe 1538 and Pfalz-Veldenz 1574 also use it of **elders** and **deacons**. In Lippe it means simply to "appoint" deacons |

Two consequences follow:
- A list of the *Diaconi* of a Saxon or Württemberg town is a list of **clergy**. A list of the
  *diakene* of Brunswick or Hamburg is a list of **laymen**.
- "Elder" in a Lutheran church order usually means a churchwarden or a senior pastor, not a
  governing elder in the Reformed sense.

**Not covered.** The following are outside the scope of this guide, except where they explain an
office:
- Schoolmasters, cantors and organists. (Hamburg 1529 lists the sexton and the organists among
  the church servants, and the **midwives** "may well be called church servants" too.)
- Visitors and superintendents.
- Consistories.

## 3. The minor orders and the subdiaconate

No evangelical church order in the corpus ordains porters, lectors, exorcists or acolytes, nor a
subdeacon in the medieval sense of a major order. No order forbids them by name either: the
minor orders are not abolished by statute but simply left out of the new ordination rites, which
know only the one ministry of the word. Three passages give reasons for this. One order, that of
the Bohemian Brethren, keeps the acolyte as a grade of its own.

### 3.1 Strasbourg: a short history of the grades

The Strasbourg examination order for candidates for the ministry asks the candidate to account
for the grades of the clergy. Its answers are the fullest statement in the corpus of how the
reformers viewed them:
- There are two "orders of presbyters": those who preach and administer the sacraments, and the
  lay *seniores* "employed only for the discipline of the church" (on these see §6.1).
- The diaconate alone was instituted "by the Holy Ghost through the Apostles".
- The subdeacons, archdeacons, lectors, acolytes and porters were added "afterwards" as helpers
  to the deacons.
- The exorcist's work was a charism, not an office.

**Strasbourg, *Examensordnung*, Latin version, after 9 September 1539, questions 93–95**
(Sehling 20/1, p. 310):

<!-- doc 1308 -->
> 93. Quot sunt ordines presbyterorum? Responsio: Duo. Unus eorum, qui Evangelium et Sacramenta
> administrant, una cum disciplina, de quibus Paulus apostolus praecipit I Timoth. 3 [1-7] et
> Tit. 1 [7-9], Qui et Episcopi vocantur ab Apostolo. Alter eorum, qui tantum adhibentur ad
> disciplinam ecclesiae, quales seniores, ut olim Synagoga, ita postea ecclesia quoque habuit,
> sine quorum consilio nihil agebatur in ecclesia. […] 94. Quos alios sacros cleri ordines habet
> ecclesia? R.: Prima institutione Spiritus S[ancti] per Apostolos prodita habet ecclesia
> Diaconos. Postea substituti sunt his subdiaconi et praefecti archidiaconi, quorum munus fuit
> communes fidelium oblationes et Eleemosynas procurare et dispensare et in conservanda
> disciplina ecclesiae caeterisque ministeriis ecclesiae, presbyteris inservire ac ministrare,
> quare Diaconi, hoc est ministri, sunt vocati. Accesserunt etiam lectores, qui sacros libros in
> ecclesia recitabant, et Acoluthi, qui presbyteros sectabantur testimonii et ministerii gratia,
> et ostiarii, qui indignos sacris coetibus arcebant. Exorcistarum enim munus magis doni
> singularis erat quam communis functionis, quae sacro ministerio proprie et semper competat.
> 95. Quot sunt necessaria ministeria ecclesiae, quae et hodie habere oporteat? R.: Tria:
> Ministerium muneris pastoralis, procurationis pauperum et Scholae ad instituendam
> adolescentiam ecclesiae.

93. How many are the orders of presbyters? Answer: Two. The one, of them which administer the
    Gospel and the Sacraments, together with discipline, of whom Paul the apostle giveth charge,
    1 Timothy 3 and Titus 1, who also are called Bishops by the Apostle. The other, of them
    which are employed only for the discipline of the church; such elders as of old the
    Synagogue had, so afterward the church also had, without whose counsel nothing was done in
    the church. […] 94. What other holy orders of the clergy hath the church? Answer: By the
    first institution of the Holy Ghost, delivered through the Apostles, the church hath
    Deacons. Afterward were set under these the subdeacons, and archdeacons set over them, whose
    office was to take charge of and dispense the common offerings and alms of the faithful, and
    in the keeping of the discipline of the church and in the other ministries of the church to
    serve and minister unto the presbyters; wherefore they were called Deacons, that is,
    ministers. There were added also lectors, which recited the holy books in the church; and
    Acolytes, which followed the presbyters for the sake of witness and of ministry; and
    porters, which kept the unworthy from the holy assemblies. For the office of the Exorcists
    was rather of a singular gift than of a common function, such as belongeth properly and
    always to the holy ministry. 95. How many are the ministries of the church which are
    necessary, which it behoveth to have even today? Answer: Three: the Ministry of the pastoral
    office, that of the care of the poor, and that of the School for the instructing of the
    youth of the church.

The same order gives the place once held by the deacons and subdeacons to the **schoolmasters**.
They are to be "second from the order of priests". **Strasbourg, *Examensordnung*, Latin
version, 1539** (Sehling 20/1, p. 304):

<!-- doc 1308 -->
> ut sit ordo eorum, qui in scholis ministrant, secundus ab ordine Sacerdotum. Inter ministros
> ecclesiae et eo loco, quo olim Diaconi et subdiaconi erant, qui hanc quoque functionem, cum
> muneribus clerici non funguntur, procuratione eleemosynarum administrabant.

[…] that the order of them which minister in the schools be the second after the order of
Priests: among the ministers of the church, and in that place where of old the Deacons and
subdeacons were, which also administered this function, when they were not performing the duties
of clerics, [together] with the care of alms.

### 3.2 Homberg 1526: "Scripture knoweth no other ministers"

The Homberg church order, which Francis Lambert drafted for the Hessian synod of 1526 and which
was never put into force, is the only order that rejects the higher grades in so many words. It
forbids the dalmatic and tunicle of the deacon and subdeacon because the orders they signify
were "brought in without the testimony of God's words". **Hesse, *Homberger Kirchenordnung*,
1526, chapter [24]** (Sehling 8, p. 46):

<!-- doc 2247 -->
> Dalmaticas, hoc est papisticorum diaconorum vestes aut subdiaconorum nemo de caetero induat.
> Nolumus enim favere ordinibus illis sine Dei eloquiorum testimoniis introductis. Caeterum
> Scriptura alios nescit ministros, praeterquam episcopos, seu presbyteros et pauperum diaconos.
> De missariis autem diaconis nec iota quidem unum in utroque instrumento reperimus, tametsi
> episcoporum adiutores non incongrue diaconi, id est, ministri vocentur. Diaconus enim
> ministrum, et diaconia ministerium significat.

Let no man henceforth put on dalmatics, that is, the vestments of the papistical deacons, or of
the subdeacons. For we will not favour those orders, which were brought in without the
testimonies of the words of God. Moreover, Scripture knoweth no other ministers save bishops, or
presbyters, and deacons of the poor. But of deacons of the mass we find not one jot in either
testament; albeit the helpers of the bishops be not unfitly called deacons, that is, ministers.
For deacon signifieth a minister, and diaconia a ministry.

The passage shows the whole later position in small:
- There is one ministry of the word, in which bishop and presbyter are one.
- There is one apostolic diaconate, for the poor.
- A "deacon" who is the pastor's assistant is allowed, but only in the loose sense of "minister"
  or helper.

### 3.3 Human ordinances, tonsure and vestment

Elsewhere the minor orders go silently, together with the tonsure, the processional cross and
the other "human ordinances" that the visitations swept away. **Freiberg, *Visitationsartikel*
of Dr Jacob Schenk, 1537** (Sehling 1, p. 466):

<!-- doc 48 -->
> Alle menschliche satzungen, so bisher als nötig gehalten, sollen abgethan werden, unangesehen,
> ob sie wol got nicht verpoten hette, als leuten anders dan zu gotlichen emptern, mit dem creuz
> gehen, platten, kappen tragen, fasttage und andere gewisse zeit wehlen.

All human ordinances which have hitherto been held as necessary shall be done away,
notwithstanding that God had not forbidden them: as ringing [of bells] otherwise than for divine
offices, going with the cross, [wearing] tonsures, wearing cowls, fast days, and choosing other
set times.

Bugenhagen makes the same point positively about deacons. The true deacon of Acts 6 needs
neither tonsure nor deacon's vestment: Stephen and Lawrence were deacons without them.
**Brunswick, *Kirchenordnung* of Johannes Bugenhagen, 1528** (Sehling 6/1, p. 449):

<!-- doc 1983 -->
> Sulke diakene, dewile se hebben de heymelicheit des loven in reyner conscientie, konen ock wol
> trösten mit Gades wörde de armen unde elenden, den se mit gelde to hulpe kamen, alse Sanctus
> Stephanus to Hierusalem dede unde Sanctus Laurentius to Rome ane platte unde ane diakenrock.
> By uns overs, wat deme worde Gades by den kranken tokumpt, dohn de predicanten mit der
> heymsökinge, also dat unse diakene alleyne van deme gemeynen gude gelt vorschaffen den
> notroftigen.

Such deacons, since they hold the mystery of the faith in a pure conscience, can also well
comfort with God's word the poor and wretched whom they help with money, as Saint Stephen did at
Jerusalem, and Saint Lawrence at Rome, without tonsure and without deacon's coat. But with us,
whatsoever pertaineth to the word of God with the sick, that do the preachers with their
visiting; so that our deacons only procure money from the common chest for the needy.

Hamburg repeats the point when it allows Lawrence to be remembered in the sermon. **Hamburg,
*Hamburger Kirchenordnung*, 1529** (Sehling 5, p. 514):

<!-- doc 1960 -->
> He droch nene platte edder diakene rock, sunder was ein diaken, alse geschreven steit Actor. 6
> und 1. Tim. 3.

He bare no tonsure nor deacon's coat, but was a deacon, as it is written, Acts 6 and 1 Timothy
3.

**What survived of the name *Subdiaconus*.** In Württemberg, Electoral Saxony and some towns,
*Subdiaconus* survived as the name of a **benefice**: the third preaching post of a town church,
below the pastor, the preacher and the *Diaconus*. It was not a separate order. Württemberg's
order for filling church posts treats "pastors, preachers, deacons, subdeacons" as one list of
ministers of the word, each "called" and examined in the same way. **Württemberg, *Ordnung der
Besetzung von Kirchendienerstellen*, 1559** (Sehling 16, p. 348):

<!-- doc 683 -->
> Demnach soll keiner zu Pfarrer, Prediger, Diacon, Subdiacon, Catechisten oder anderm
> Kirchendienst vociert noch verordnet werden, er bringes dann zuvor seine Glaubwürdige,
> Rechtmessige Testimonia seiner geburt, herkommens, haltens

Accordingly shall none be called or ordained to [be] Pastor, Preacher, Deacon, Subdeacon,
Catechist, or to other service of the church, unless he first bring his credible and lawful
testimonies of his birth, descent, [and] conduct […]

Electoral Saxony's great order of 1580 has a chapter "how all parishes, preacherships,
diaconates and subdiaconates shall be filled". **Electoral Saxony, *Kirchenordnung* of Elector
August, 1580**, table of contents and chapter heading (Sehling 1, pp. 363, 375):

<!-- doc 44 -->
> Vom beruf und annemung der kirchendiener, und wie alle pfarren, predicaturen, diaconat und
> subdiaconat, bestellet werden sollen.

Of the calling and receiving of the ministers of the church, and how all parishes,
preacherships, diaconate and subdiaconate shall be filled.

At Halle the *Unterdiaconi*, the junior ministers, were bound to visit those they had communed
in sickness. They were thus ministers of the sacrament, not servers. **Halle, *Kirchen-Ordnung
der christlichen Gemein zu Hall in Sachsen*, 1573(?)** (Sehling 2, p. 441):

<!-- doc 1257 -->
> Die diaconi aber und sonderlich die unterdiaconi sollen diejenigen, so sie in
> leibesschwacheiten communicirt haben, unerfordert in ihrer krankheit zu besuchen verpflichtet
> sein.

But the deacons, and especially the subdeacons, shall be bound to visit, unasked, in their
sickness those whom they have communicated in bodily infirmities.

### 3.4 The one exception: the acolytes of the Bohemian Brethren

The *Ordo ecclesiasticus in unitate fratrum Bohemorum* (1576), printed in Sehling's Polish
section, keeps a five-step clergy:
- **acolytes**, then **deacons**, then **ministers** (presbyters);
- above them, **con-seniors** and **seniors** (bishops).

The acolytes are boys and young men taken into the pastor's household to be trained for the
ministry. The order justifies the grade from Samuel under Eli, Elisha under Elijah, the sons of
the prophets, Christ's disciples and Paul's companions, and calls it apostolic. **Bohemian
Brethren, *Ordo ecclesiasticus in unitate fratrum Bohemorum*, 1576** (Sehling 4, pp. 275–276):

<!-- doc 1846 -->
> Acoluthi, sunt adolescentuli a pastoribus ecclesiarum in domesticam societatem, eo fine ut in
> futurum ecclesiae usum litteris et moribus diligentius quam apud parentes, vel alibi,
> instruantur, admissi. Sic etenim Samuel apud Eli, 1. Sam. III. 1. Elisaeus apud Eliam, 2. Reg.
> IV. 38. et turba filiorum prophetarum apud seniores suos, 2. Reg. IV. 20. exerceri soliti
> sunt. Sic et Christus discipulos antequam illis ministerium concredidisset, apud se fovit et
> exercitavit. […] Atque hinc est, quod iam inde ab apostolicis temporibus gradus iste in
> ecclesia usurpatus fuerit.

Acolytes are young lads admitted by the pastors of the churches into [their] household
fellowship, to this end, that for the future use of the church they may be instructed in letters
and manners more diligently than with their parents or elsewhere. For so Samuel was wont to be
exercised with Eli, 1 Samuel 3:1; Elisha with Elijah, 2 Kings 4:38; and the company of the sons
of the prophets with their elders, 2 Kings 4. So also Christ cherished and exercised the
disciples with himself before he had entrusted the ministry to them. […] And hence it is that
this grade hath been used in the church even from the apostolic times.

What the acolytes did, according to the same order (p. 276):
- live modestly and in willing service;
- learn the catechism, the Gospels, the shorter Epistles, the Psalms and the hymns by heart;
- read the sacred text at household prayers, and sometimes give a short exhortation on it.

They had no altar duties. The medieval acolyte's candles and cruets have become a seminary
apprenticeship.

The order calls their admission an *ordinatio*, "the first grade of calling". It takes place
before the synod, after an examination, and it has **no laying on of hands**; the Brethren kept
that for deacons and ministers (§5.3, §7). **Bohemian Brethren, *Ordo ecclesiasticus*, 1576,
"Acoluthorum ordinatio"** (Sehling 4, p. 279):

<!-- doc 1846 -->
> Est autem ordinatio I. Acoluthorum. II. Diaconorum. III. Ministrorum. IV. Conseniorum. V.
> Seniorum. […] 2. Provocantur nominatim ex ordine, et se in ecclesiae conspectum sistunt. 3.
> Interrogantur coram tota synodo, num se ecclesiae usibus consecrare velint? num obsequia
> promittant? 4. Praeleguntur ipsis officia; de quibus supra. 5. Horum observationem illi manu
> stipulata promittunt. 6. Priores acoluthi dextrae porrectione in societatem eos cooptant. 7.
> Omnia votis, benedictione, et cantu sacro, concluduntur.

Now the ordination is: I. Of Acolytes. II. Of Deacons. III. Of Ministers. IV. Of Con-seniors. V.
Of Seniors. […] 2. They are called forth by name in order, and present themselves in the sight
of the church. 3. They are asked before the whole synod whether they will consecrate themselves
to the uses of the church, [and] whether they promise obedience. 4. Their duties are read to
them, of which above. 5. The keeping of these they promise with hand given. 6. The former
acolytes take them into [their] fellowship by reaching forth the right hand. 7. All is concluded
with prayers, a blessing, and a sacred song.

### 3.5 Where the functions went

**Porter.** The porter's keys passed to the **sexton** (*Opfermann*, *Küster*, *Kirchner*,
*Mesner*). This was a lay church servant, engaged and paid by the parish. Hesse 1566 draws the
line from the Old Testament gatekeepers to the *aeditui* and *custodes* of its own day. **Hesse,
*Kirchenordnung*, 1566** (Sehling 8, p. 211):

<!-- doc 2257 -->
> Ahn die superintendenten, pfarherr, eltesten und diacon haben auch unser kirchen etlich andere
> diener, welche, dieweil sie die schlüssel zur kirchen, darin die christliche gemein
> zusammenkompt, haben und verwaren, auf- und zuschließen, beinahe denjenigen gleich sein,
> welche man im alten testament janitores genennet und heut, wiegebreuchlich, aeditui und
> custodes heißen. Es sollen auch dieselbige gleich wie andere diener der kirchen eines erbaren
> und gottseligen wandels sein, kein volseufer, nit unehrlich hantierung treiben. Es sollen aber
> gemelte opfermenner durch die pfarherr und eltesten darzu mit wissen und willen eines
> superintendenten angenommen werden.

Besides the superintendents, pastors, elders and deacons, our churches have also certain other
ministers, which, forasmuch as they have and keep the keys of the church wherein the christian
congregation cometh together, [and] open and shut [it], are well nigh like unto them which in
the old testament were called janitores, and today, as is customary, are called aeditui and
custodes. These also, like other ministers of the church, shall be of an honest and godly
conversation, no drunkards, [and] shall ply no dishonest trade. But the said sextons shall be
received by the pastors and elders, with the knowledge and will of a superintendent.

Hamburg puts the sexton under the pastor "with locking, with ringing at the right time". He is
hired and dismissed by the lay deacons who keep the church building (Sehling 5, p. 512).
Nassau-Dillenburg 1582 has the *aeditui* elected by the presbytery with the elders and deacons
(§5.3).

**Lector.** Public reading passed to three groups:
- **Schoolboys.** At Rostock a boy reads the epistle at Vespers in Latin and another reads it in
  German "to the people". **Rostock, *Conformitas ceremoniarum in singulis templis ecclesiae
  Rostochiensis*, 1560–1576** (Sehling 5, p. 289):

<!-- doc 1936 -->
> Darnach soll die epistel von einen iungen gelesen werden lateinisch, und nach der epistel das
> responsorium gesungen werden, drauf sol die epistel deutesch von einem knaben dem volke
> furgelesen werden

Thereafter shall the epistle be read in Latin by a youth, and after the epistle the responsory
be sung; whereupon shall the epistle be read in German by a boy unto the people […]

- **The junior minister.** At Hof the *subdiaconus*, "a minister of the church", reads a collect
  drawn from the Sunday epistle, then the epistle, then its summary. **Hof, *Ordo
  ecclesiasticus*, 1592** (Sehling 11, p. 425):

<!-- doc 294 -->
> Minister ecclesiae, puta subdiaconus, precatiunculam ex epistola dominicali seu festivali
> concinatam legit, post ipsam epistolam et tandem huius summam.

A minister of the church, to wit the subdeacon, readeth a little prayer composed out of the
Sunday or festival epistle; after that the epistle itself; and lastly the sum thereof.

- **The assisting minister (*levite*)** in Nürnberg, who reads a whole chapter "in place of the
  lesson" and expounds it (§4.3).

**Exorcist.** No exorcist appears anywhere. Strasbourg's view that exorcism was a charism, not
an office (§3.1), explains why. Where the exorcism in the baptismal rite was kept, the minister
who baptised spoke it. The Reformed orders dropped even that. Bentheim-Tecklenburg orders the
baptismal "exorcisms, which have no ground in the Word of God", to be abolished
(Sehling 22, p. 269).

**Acolyte.** Outside the Bohemian Brethren (§3.4), the acolyte's tasks at the altar either
lapsed or fell to the ministrants of the early Mass orders (§4.1–4.2). The training role that
the Brethren gave their acolytes was filled elsewhere by the **school**. Strasbourg calls the
school ministry "second after the order of priests" (§3.1).

## 4. The liturgical roles

Most evangelical orders assume that the service is led by one minister at the altar, with the
schoolmaster and boys as the choir and the sexton ringing the bells. The roles asked about are
mostly absent:
- **Common:** the reader (§3.5).
- **Occasional:** the server or ministrant, and the assisting deacon and subdeacon at the altar.
- **Rare:** the cross-bearer.
- **Never prescribed:** the thurifer, the boat-bearer, the candle-bearer and torch-bearer, and
  the master of ceremonies.

The early Mass-shaped orders of Nürnberg, Brandenburg, Pfalz-Neuburg and Regensburg are the
exception. They keep assisting ministers at the altar for a time. Where those assistants are
named, they are usually **ordained ministers** taking turns, not boys or men in minor orders.

### 4.1 Ministrants

The Nürnberg parish churches' order of 1524 keeps a *ministrant* at the altar. The priest asks
him who has announced himself for communion. **Nürnberg, *Gottesdienstordnung der Pfarrkirchen*,
1524** (Sehling 11, p. 52):

<!-- doc 249 -->
> Nach dem alleluia fragt der priester den ministranten, ob sich jemand hab angesagt, das heilig
> sacrament zu empfahen.

After the alleluia the priest asketh the ministrant whether any have announced himself to
receive the holy sacrament.

Elector Joachim II's order for Brandenburg (1540) has the celebrant wear a cope or surplice, not
a chasuble. It allows ministrants "in deacons' coats" (dalmatics) where there are enough clergy,
as in collegiate churches, monasteries and some large towns. **Brandenburg, *Kirchenordnung* of
Joachim II, 1540** (Sehling 3, p. 71):

<!-- doc 1746 -->
> da man es auch an kirchendienern haben kan, als in stiften und klöstern, auch in etlichen
> grossen stedten mag man zu solchem ampt auch ministranten in diaconröcken brauchen.

[…] where there can be had ministers of the church enough for it, as in foundations and
cloisters, and also in certain great towns, ministrants in deacons' coats may also be used for
such an office.

The same order keeps the "customary lights" at the hours and Masses. It abolishes only the
special lights of the confraternities and guilds (Sehling 3, p. 71).

### 4.2 Deacon and subdeacon at the altar

Pfalz-Neuburg 1543 has the celebrant come to the altar "with his ministrants, where and when
they are wont to be used". It then gives the epistle to the subdeacon, the gospel to the deacon,
and the chalice to the deacon. Each of these applies only "where there are ministrants".
**Pfalz-Neuburg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1543** (Sehling 13, pp. 70–71):

<!-- doc 386 -->
> Erstlich soll der priester, so die meß halten will, sampt seinen ministranten, wo und wann man
> dieselben zu gebrauchen pflegt, in iren gewonlichen kirchenornaten nach gewonheit einer jeden
> kirchen zu dem altar gehn und anfenglich darvor kniend das Confiteor oder ein feinen bußpsalm
> sprechen. […] Nachvolgends sol er oder der subdiaconus, wann man ministranten hat, die epistel
> teutsch mit lauter, verstendlicher stimme lesen […] Darnach sol er oder der diaconus, wann man
> ministranten hat, das evangelion auch mit lauter, verstentlicher stimm lesen

First shall the priest which will hold the mass, together with his ministrants, where and when
they are wont to be used, go to the altar in their customary church vestments after the custom
of each church, and at the beginning, kneeling before it, say the Confiteor or a goodly
penitential psalm. […] Afterward shall he, or the subdeacon if there be ministrants, read the
epistle in German with a loud and understandable voice […] Thereafter shall he, or the deacon if
there be ministrants, read the gospel likewise with a loud and understandable voice […]

At the distribution (Sehling 13, p. 75):

<!-- doc 386 -->
> Desgleichen, wo man ministranten hat, sol der diaconus den kelch reichen denen, so den leib
> Christi genossen haben […] Wo man aber nit ministranten hat, da sol der priester den leib des
> Herrn erstlich jederman reichen und darnach erst den kelch

Likewise, where there are ministrants, the deacon shall reach the cup to them which have
partaken of the body of Christ […] But where there are no ministrants, there shall the priest
first reach the body of the Lord to every man, and only after that the cup.

Heilbronn's order of 1530 has the preacher stand at the rood altar "with his two deacons in
surplices". The preacher speaks the words of Christ, and the deacons distribute. Heilbronn's
*Diaconi* were the town's assistant preachers. **Heilbronn, *„Confessio Heilbronnensis“ und
Kirchenordnung*, 1530** (Sehling 17/1, p. 285):

<!-- doc 770 -->
> Nach demselben went sich der prediger ob dem Creutzaltar mit seinen zweien Diaconis in
> korröken gegen dem volkh, verkundigt den thod Christi, was man suchen soll im abenntmall,
> denen, die dann vorhanden sind. Darnach spricht der prediger die wortt Christi uber das brott
> und den wein unnd nach demselben reichen die Diaconi daß sacrament von dem altar denen, so deß
> begeren

After the same the preacher turneth himself at the rood altar, with his two Deacons in
surplices, toward the people, [and] proclaimeth the death of Christ, [and] what one ought to
seek in the supper, to them that are then present. Thereafter the preacher speaketh the words of
Christ over the bread and the wine; and after the same the Deacons reach the sacrament from the
altar to them that desire it […]

In both orders the deacon distributes but does not consecrate. This division of labour runs
through all the orders that have an assisting deacon.

The Regensburg order of 1553, drawn up under Justus Jonas, works with a weekly rota of the
town's **ordained ministers**:
- the *Wochner* (the minister on duty that week) celebrates;
- the second minister, "ministrant or deacon", says the Confiteor with him and gives the cup;
- the third reads a chapter from the pulpit and holds the houseling cloth;
- the fourth, in a surplice, holds the cloth by the cup.

**Regensburg, *Kirchenordnung unter Justus Jonas*, 1553** (Sehling 13, pp. 419–421):

<!-- doc 440 -->
> Erstlich so kombt der priester (das ist der minister, welcher, wie obstehet, wochner ist)
> sambt zwaien ministranten oder diaconen fur den altar […] Der priester selb raicht den leib
> Christi dem volk auf seiner seiten, der ministrant oder diaconus (der secundus ist) raicht das
> bluet Christi dem volk auf der andern seiten des altars. Der diaconus aber, so tercius ist,
> helt das tuechlein auf des priesters seiten, der den leib raicht. Derjenig aber, so quartus
> ist, helt das tuechle bei dem diacono, der das bluet raicht, in einem chorrock. Wo aber kein
> minister vorhanden, so tuet es der prediger oder pfarherr.

First cometh the priest (that is, the minister which, as is above said, is of the week) with two
ministrants or deacons before the altar […] The priest himself reacheth the body of Christ to
the people on his side; the ministrant or deacon (which is second) reacheth the blood of Christ
to the people on the other side of the altar. But the deacon which is third holdeth the little
cloth on the side of the priest which reacheth the body. And he which is fourth holdeth the
cloth beside the deacon which reacheth the blood, in a surplice. But where no minister is at
hand, then the preacher or the pastor doeth it.

### 4.3 Levites: kept, then abolished

In the south German imperial cities, the two assistants at the altar were called **levites**, a
gospeller and an epistoler. Sehling's editor notes that outside cathedrals they were in practice
priests (Sehling 13, p. 391, note 6). Regensburg's first evangelical service, described in the
"True Report" of 1542, has them in full. **Regensburg, *Wahrhaftiger Bericht*, 1542**
(Sehling 13, pp. 391, 393):

<!-- doc 432 -->
> Unter diser letanei geen drei kirchendiener, nemlich der priester, so das ambt singet, sambt
> zwaien leviten, nemlichen einem evangelier und einem epistler, aus dem sagra zum altar, darauf
> das volk gespeiset werden solle, und betet der priester sambt dem evangelier das Confiteor. So
> verordnet dhweil der epistler den kelch, buech, partikl und paten auf den altar. […] Der
> epistler aber helt das tuchle, daruber das volk den leib Christi empfecht und, so jemants aus
> einfalt etwo ein ungeschickligkeit brauchte, den unterweist und bericht er, wie er sich halten
> solle.

During this litany three ministers of the church go out of the sacristy to the altar whereat the
people is to be fed, namely, the priest which singeth the office, together with two levites,
namely a gospeller and an epistoler; and the priest with the gospeller prayeth the Confiteor.
Meanwhile the epistoler setteth in order the chalice, book, particles and paten upon the altar.
[…] But the epistoler holdeth the little cloth over which the people receiveth the body of
Christ; and if any out of simplicity should use some awkwardness, him he instructeth and
informeth how he ought to behave himself.

Nürnberg's report to the council of Goslar (1528) gives the levites the reading and the
exhortation. One reads and expounds a whole chapter of the Epistles "in place of the lesson".
One gives the exhortation to the communicants before the altar. **Nürnberg, *Bericht der Stadt
Nürnberg für den Goslarer Rat*, 30 March 1528** (Sehling 7/2.2, p. 230):

<!-- doc 2186 -->
> Nach solichem tritt der leviten eyner uff den predigestol und liest an stadt der lection ein
> gantz capittel auß Sanct Petrus, Sanct Paulsen oder Sanct Johannesen epistolen […] Darauff
> alßbalde tritt der leviten eyner fur den altar und thut in teutscher sprach ein christliche
> ermanunge

After this one of the levites goeth up into the pulpit, and readeth in place of the lesson a
whole chapter out of the epistles of Saint Peter, Saint Paul or Saint John […] Thereupon
straightway one of the levites goeth before the altar and maketh in the German tongue a
christian exhortation […]

**Abolition.** The levites, and the dalmatics and chasubles that went with them, were the target
of deliberate abolition in the 1550s and 1560s. In April 1554 Nikolaus Gallus and the council of
Regensburg had a declaration read to the congregation. It gives two grounds:
- The levitical garments come from the Old Testament priesthood and the papal Mass, both now
  ended in Christ.
- The pope now requires them as necessary to true worship, and Christian liberty must show "the
  contrary".

**Regensburg, *Warum die leviten- und meßgewand sollen abgetan werden*, 29 April 1554**
(Sehling 13, p. 432):

<!-- doc 443 -->
> Ein ehrbar rat hat sampt uns dienern des worts für gut angesehen, das die leviten- und
> meßgewand bei der communion in unser christlichen kirchen alhie wie bei vielen andern hinfort
> genzlich abgetan sein sollen. Dasselbig aus nachvolgenden ursachen: Erstlich, das beide stück
> (wie zum teil die namen noch anzeigen) vom levitischen priestertum des alten testaments und
> von des bapsts messe in unser kirchen kommen und ihre bedeutung auf den zukünftigen Christum
> gehabt haben. Weil wir denn im neuen testament von gesetzen des levitischen priestertums frei
> und von des bapsts gotteslesterischen messe ledig sind, den cörper Christum selbs und im
> abendmahl nach den worten Christi nit mehr denn ein communion haben, so lassen wir billich den
> jüdischen schatten faren sampt demjenigen, was des bapsts messe anhanget und dienet. Zum
> andern sind die leviten- und meßgewand nit allein vom bapst unrecht in die christliche kirchen
> des neuen testaments eingefüret, sondern werden von ihm noch aufs neu geboten und als nötig
> zum waren gottesdienst mit aberglauben und abgöttischer meinung erfordert. Derhalben, wie wir
> sonst vom bapst, von seiner falschen lere und falschen gottesdienst recht abgesondert sind,
> also sollen wir auch hierin von ihm fort ganz sein gescheiden und zu erzeigung unser
> christlichen freiheit dieses fals das widerspiel beweisen.

An honourable council, together with us ministers of the word, hath thought good that the
levites' and mass vestments shall henceforth be wholly done away at the communion in our
christian church here, as with many others. And that for the following causes. First, that both
pieces (as in part the names yet shew) came into our church from the levitical priesthood of the
old testament and from the pope's mass, and had their signification upon Christ which was to
come. Since then in the new testament we are free from the laws of the levitical priesthood and
quit of the pope's blasphemous mass, [and] have the body, Christ himself, and in the supper,
according to the words of Christ, no more than a communion, we rightly let the Jewish shadow go,
together with that which hangeth upon and serveth the pope's mass. Secondly, the levites' and
mass vestments were not only wrongly brought by the pope into the christian church of the new
testament, but are yet commanded by him anew, and required as necessary to the true service of
God with superstitious and idolatrous opinion. Therefore, as we are otherwise rightly separated
from the pope, from his false doctrine and false service of God, so shall we also herein be
wholly parted from him henceforth, and, for the shewing of our christian liberty, prove the
contrary in this case.

Regensburg's church order of 1567(?) records that the levites, the vestments and the altar
lights were all "let fall" in the end. **Regensburg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1567(?)**
(Sehling 13, p. 480):

<!-- doc 450 -->
> Demnach haben wir auch die kirchenkleidung, als meßgewand, stolen, leviten usw. gleich wie
> auch die liechter, so von anfang der reinen lere gleichwol lange zeit in unser pfarrhe mit
> gehalten worden, entlich sampt den obgemelten, etlichen feiertägen lassen fallen aus ursachen,
> das die ding aus dem alten testament von dem levitischen priestertum, so sein ende hat, mit
> unverstand sind in das neue testament eingefürt und in eitel bapstisch abgötterei gewendet.

Accordingly we have also at last let fall the church vesture, as chasubles, stoles, levites
etc., as also the lights, which from the beginning of the pure doctrine were nevertheless kept a
long time in our parish, together with certain of the holy days above mentioned; for the causes
that these things were brought without understanding out of the old testament, from the
levitical priesthood, which hath its end, into the new testament, and turned into mere
papistical idolatry.

The chapter order of Brandenburg-Ansbach (1565, revised 1578) forbids them outright at the
clergy's chapter meetings. **Brandenburg-Ansbach-Kulmbach, *Kapitelsordnung*, 1565/78**
(Sehling 11, p. 357):

<!-- doc 283 -->
> Es sollen auch keine leviten bapstischem prauch nach dargestellet werden.

Neither shall any levites be set forth after the papistical use.

### 4.4 Thurifer, boat-bearer and candle-bearer

Luther's *Formula missae* leaves both candles and incense at the gospel free. **Luther, *Formula
missae et communionis pro ecclesia Wittembergensi*, 1523** (Sehling 1, p. 5):

<!-- doc 2 -->
> Sexto sequitur evangelii lectio. Ubi nec candelas neque thurificationem prohibemus, sed nec
> exigimus. Esto hoc liberum.

Sixthly followeth the reading of the gospel. Wherein we neither forbid candles nor censing, but
neither do we require them. Let this be free.

Lippe (1538) lists incense among the abuses to be dropped from the Mass, while it leaves
vestments, albs and lights free. **Lippe, *Lippische Kirchenordnung*, 1538**
(Sehling 21, p. 318):

<!-- doc 1459 -->
> mit itlichen korten ceremonien, ahne water in den kelch, wirock, vele creutze tho makende unnd
> andere mißbruke. Averm missewant, alven, lechte, ruchellen sint friggn.

[…] with certain short ceremonies, without water in the chalice, incense, the making of many
crosses, and other abuses. But chasubles, albs, lights [and] rochets are free.

No later order in the corpus takes up Luther's freedom for incense. **No order prescribes a
thurifer or a boat-bearer.** Censing is mentioned only in descriptions of the old Mass, or in
lists of things abolished.

Altar lights were kept in many Lutheran territories, as in Brandenburg 1540 above. But they were
lit and tended by the **sexton**, and no order makes carrying them a liturgical role. **No
candle-bearer (*accensor*, *ceroferarius*) or torch-bearer is prescribed**, not even at the
gospel or the elevation. Regensburg let its lights fall with the levites (§4.3).

### 4.5 Cross-bearer, and the roles not found

The processional cross went with the processions themselves. Freiberg 1537 lists "going with the
cross" among the human ordinances to be done away (§3.3). In Brandenburg it survived in the
Rogation-tide procession, where it is carried before the schoolboys, the priests and the people.
**Stendal, ordinance of the visitors of the Mark Brandenburg, 1541** (Sehling 3, p. 314):

<!-- doc 1785 -->
> und hernach die processionen ordentlich halten, als am ersten ein kreuz vorher tragen, dornach
> die schuler, folgend die priester und hernach die man, junkfrauen und frauen

[…] and afterward hold the processions in order: as, first, to carry a cross before, thereafter
the scholars, following them the priests, and after them the men, maidens and women […]

Schwäbisch Hall's church order of 1543, in its revision of 1615, allows a cross before a coffin.
It must be used "not effectively but significatively", that is, as a sign that the dead died
trusting in Christ's cross, not as a thing that works anything. **Schwäbisch Hall,
*Kirchenordnung* 1543/1615, *Ordnung der Begrebnus*** (Sehling 17/1, p. 173):

<!-- doc 763 -->
> Inmassen auch an ihm selbst nit unrecht ist, ein Creutz vor der Leich hertragen, wann es ohne
> Aberglauben und Abgötterey geschicht, Nemblich also, daß es nit effective, sondern
> significative gebraucht wirt, anzuzeigen, daß der Todte auff das Creutz Jesu Christi
> abgestorben.

Inasmuch as it is also not in itself wrong to carry a cross before the corpse, when it is done
without superstition and idolatry; namely so, that it be used not effectively but
significatively, to shew that the dead man died upon the cross of Jesus Christ.

Neither passage names a **crucifer** as an office. The bearer is not identified.

**Not found.** The corpus contains no prescribed role of **candle-bearer** (*accensor*,
*ceroferarius*), **torch-bearer**, **boat-bearer** or **master of ceremonies**. The nearest
thing to the last is the **cantor** or schoolmaster, who directs the choir and the sung parts of
the service. He is a school officer, not a liturgical minister. The orders of service are
addressed to the pastor himself, and they set out the ceremonies he is to observe.

## 5. The diaconate

The diaconate survived in three forms, and an order often has more than one of them under the
same name:
- the **Lutheran *Diaconus***: an ordained assistant minister of the word and sacraments (§5.1);
- the **liturgical deacon**: an assistant at the altar, normally himself one of the ordained
  ministers (§4.2–4.3);
- the **lay deacon of the poor**: an elected steward of alms and church goods, based on Acts 6
  (§5.2–5.3).

### 5.1 The Lutheran *Diaconus*: an assistant minister, ordained like a pastor

In the Lutheran town churches the *Diaconus* (also *Helfer*, *Kaplan*, *Capellan*) is a second
or third minister, and the *Subdiaconus* (§3.3) a third or fourth. Each preaches, baptises,
hears confession, communes the sick and gives the cup at the altar. Halle provides each of its
three parish churches with "a pastor and two deacons, who are all fit to preach, to reach the
sacrament and to perform other church business". **Halle, *Kirchen-Ordnung der christlichen
Gemein zu Hall in Sachsen*, 1573(?)** (Sehling 2, p. 442):

<!-- doc 1257 -->
> dass itzt bestelte oft ernannte drei pfar kirchen S. Mariae, S. Ulrici und Mauritii, und mit
> drei personen, nämlichen einen pfarher und zweien diaconis versehen und erhalten werde, die
> alle zu predigen, sacrament zu reichen und andere kirchengeschäfte zuverrichten tüchtig sind.

[…] that the three parish churches now established and oft named, St Mary, St Ulrich and [St]
Maurice, be provided and maintained with three persons, namely one pastor and two deacons, which
are all fit to preach, to reach the sacrament, and to perform other business of the church.

**The same ordination.** The *Diaconus* is ordained with the same rite as the pastor. Electoral
Saxony's form for ordaining and investing "a new minister of the church" names "the new pastor
or deacon" throughout. The superintendent lays his right hand on him, as a ceremony "of
christian liberty". He then "ordains, confirms and establishes" him, "by divine command and
ordinance, to be a minister and curate of souls of this congregation". **Electoral Saxony,
*Kirchenordnung* of Elector August, 1580** (Sehling 1, pp. 382–383):

<!-- doc 44 -->
> Unter dem gesang sol der superintendens vor den altar treten, den neuen pfarren oder diacon zu
> sich berufen, und vor ihm zu dem gebet niederknien lassen […] sol sich der superintendens vor
> dem altar gegen dem volk wenden, und wie (nach christlicher freiheit, so von dem herrn Christo
> der kirchen in eusserlichen mittelmessigen ceremonien gegeben) gebreuchlich, sein rechte hand
> dem neuen pfarrer oder diacon auf sein blos heubt legen, und also sagen. Dieweil wir im
> heiligen geist versamlet, gott unsern himlischen vater durch Jesum Christum unsern herrn und
> heiland uber dich angerufen und gebeten, und deshalben nicht zweifeln, er werde uns, laut
> seiner göttlichen zusagung, gnediglich erhört und gewert haben, demnach so ordne, confirmire
> und bestetige ich dich, aus göttlichem befehl und ordnung, zu einem diener und seelsorger
> dieser gemein

During the singing the superintendent shall step before the altar, call the new pastor or deacon
to him, and cause him to kneel before him to prayer […] the superintendent shall turn himself
before the altar toward the people, and, as is customary (according to the christian liberty
given by the Lord Christ to the church in outward indifferent ceremonies), lay his right hand
upon the bare head of the new pastor or deacon, and say thus: Forasmuch as we, gathered in the
Holy Ghost, have called upon and prayed God our heavenly Father through Jesus Christ our Lord
and Saviour over thee, and therefore doubt not that he, according to his divine promise, hath
graciously heard and granted us; therefore I ordain, confirm and establish thee, by divine
command and ordinance, to be a minister and curate of souls of this congregation […]

**A step, not an order.** The diaconate is normally the first post of a young minister, but it
is not a necessary stage. The same order allows a candidate of exceptional gifts to be made a
pastor "without the means of the diaconate". **Electoral Saxony, *Kirchenordnung*, 1580**
(Sehling 1, pp. 381–382):

<!-- doc 44 -->
> Da aber einer im examine dermassen gelert, geschickt und erfaren, und mit gaben zuleren
> ausgerüstet, und im predigen schon geübet, darzu der rituum ecclesiae notdürftiglich berichtet
> erfunden, mag mit demselben dispensiret, und solche person ohne mittel des diaconats, zu einem
> pfarrer verordnet und angenommen werden.

But if one be found in the examination so learned, apt and experienced, and furnished with gifts
to teach, and already practised in preaching, and moreover sufficiently instructed in the rites
of the church, then may there be dispensation with him, and such a person may be appointed and
received to be a pastor without the means of the diaconate.

This Lutheran diaconate is therefore a **rank in one ministry**, distinguished by benefice,
seniority and duties. It is not a sacramental grade. The *Diaconus* could do everything the
pastor did. He was subject to the pastor, as the pastor was to the superintendent, by "human"
order (§5.4).

### 5.2 Lay deacons of the poor in the Lutheran orders

Bugenhagen's orders for Brunswick (1528), Hamburg (1529) and Lübeck (1531) created the most
widespread lay diaconate of the Reformation. These are the **deacons of the common chest**
(*diakene der schatkaste*) and **deacons of the poor** (*diakene der armen*): honest burghers
elected for a term to keep the treasury, collect and distribute alms, and look after the
ministers' maintenance. Bugenhagen grounds the office on Acts 6 and the qualifications in 1
Timothy 3, which he expounds clause by clause. In passing he equates bishops and preachers.
**Brunswick, *Kirchenordnung* of Johannes Bugenhagen, 1528** (Sehling 6/1, p. 449):

<!-- doc 1983 -->
> dat de diakene scholen syn redelick, de eyn gut ruchte hebben, dat se alse ehrlike frame lude
> in allen dingen nicht unrecht handelen. Item dat se nicht twetungich syn, wen wo kan me deme
> sulk gelt unde denst der armen bevehlen, de gerne legen unde achterkosen, vor mynen ogenen wit
> seggen unde anderswör swart. Sulke plegen ock hader unde afval wedder de bischoppe edder
> predicanten (welk alle eyn dink is) anrichten etc. Item: Nicht wynsuchtich edder eyn
> drunkenbolt, wente sulk eyn wert syne collatie söken van der armen gelde

[…] that the deacons shall be honest, such as have a good report, that as honourable, godly
people they deal not wrongly in any thing. Item, that they be not double-tongued; for how can
one commit such money and the service of the poor to him that loveth to lie and to backbite,
[that] saith white before mine eyes and elsewhere black? Such are wont also to stir up strife
and falling away against the bishops or preachers (which is all one thing), etc. Item: not given
to wine, or a drunkard; for such an one will seek his collation out of the money of the poor […]

**What they might not do.** Bugenhagen's deacons do not minister the word. In the early Church
Stephen and Lawrence comforted the poor with God's word, but "with us" the visiting of the sick
belongs to the preachers, and "our deacons only procure money from the common chest for the
needy" (§3.3).

**Collegiate control.** In Hamburg 3 or 4 deacons alone may not lend or make any substantial
grant from the poor's goods. That is done only when "the sixteen deacons" meet on Saturday.
**Hamburg, *Hamburger Kirchenordnung*, 1529** (Sehling 5, p. 535):

<!-- doc 1960 -->
> Vorlenen edder wat stadliken geven van dussem gude der armen schollen sick dre edder veer
> diakene nicht understaen, sunder id schall scheen, wen des sonnavendes de sostein diakene
> edder de meisten van en, wen jo etlike nicht kamen konnen, tosamende sint

To lend, or to give any thing of substance, out of these goods of the poor, three or four
deacons shall not take upon themselves; but it shall be done when on the Saturday the sixteen
deacons, or the most of them if some cannot come, are together […]

Lübeck's order of 1531 follows Hamburg's. Every Saturday the three youngest deacons of each
parish, who hold the keys, empty the poor chest and carry its contents to St Mary's
(Sehling 5, p. 359).

Hamburg's deacons also hire and dismiss the sexton and the organists. They care for the widows
and orphans of deceased preachers. They give yearly gifts to the midwives, who in turn report
poor women in childbed to them (Sehling 5, pp. 512–513).

**Other names for the same office.** The lay deacon of the poor appears under several names:
- ***Leviten*** in the Land Hadeln. They collect at the church door during the general prayer,
  go from house to house once a year for corn, admit the deserving poor to the hospitals "by the
  counsel and with the will of the pastor", and maintain the preachers and the poor from the
  common chest. **Hadeln, *Hadelner Kirchenordnung*, 1526** (Sehling 5, p. 472):

<!-- doc 1955 -->
> dat de leviten, alse men se nöhmet, de predicanten und rechte armen darmit schölen
> underholden. […] Alle sondage und hoge feste schall ein van den leviten mit der bede umme gan
> in der kerken, dewile de predicante up dem stole steit und deit dat gemene gebedt. […] Na rade
> unde mit willen des pastors schölen de leviten in de gasthuse nehmen edder hospitale andere
> hussarmen, dat rechte armen sint.

[…] that the levites, as they are called, shall maintain therewith the preachers and the true
poor. […] Every Sunday and high feast one of the levites shall go about with the bag in the
church while the preacher standeth in the pulpit and maketh the common prayer. […] By the
counsel and with the will of the pastor the levites shall take into the guest-houses or
hospitals other house-poor, which are truly poor.

- ***Subdiaconi oder Bittherren*** at St Wenzel's in Naumburg. There are twelve: three from the
  council and the rest from the community. They stand at the church doors with collecting
  boards. **Naumburg, *Kirchen-Ordnung für die St. Wenzelskirche*, 1537** (Sehling 2, p. 64):

<!-- doc 1219 -->
> Von den bitthern. Darnach sollen zwölf subdiaconi oder bittherrn, drei aus dem rathe, sieben
> aus der gemeine verordnet werden, under welchen alle feiertage sechse für der kirchenthür, so
> balde die predigt aus ist, vor jeder thuer einer mit einer tafel stehen und den armen leuten
> das almosen bitten

Of the beggar-lords. Thereafter shall twelve subdeacons, or beggar-lords, be appointed, three
out of the council, seven out of the community; of whom every holy day six shall stand before
the church doors as soon as the sermon is out, before each door one, with a board, and beg the
alms for the poor people […]

- ***Diaconi*** in Pomerania. At Pyritz (1539) two come from the council and two from the
  guilds. They are expressly **not** put under oath, but they must render account yearly.
  **Pyritz, *Abschied*, 1539** (Sehling 4, p. 519):

<!-- doc 1879 -->
> Visitatores wollen verständigt sein, aus welchem corpore die diaconi erwählt werden. Darauf
> geantwort, dass zwei aus dem rathe und zwei von den werken zu diaconis erwählt, aber nicht mit
> keinem eide beladen werden und müssen alle jahre vor dem rathe zu Pyritz rechnung thun.

The visitors desire to be informed out of what body the deacons are chosen. Whereto answer was
made, that two out of the council and two from the guilds are chosen to be deacons, but are not
laden with any oath, and must every year render account before the council at Pyritz.

The Pomeranian synod of 1574 requires the opposite, an **oath**. **Pomerania, *Statuta
synodica*, 1574** (Sehling 4, p. 491):

<!-- doc 1868 -->
> II. Pastores attendant, ut idonei eligantur diaconi ecclesiae et pauperum, et ut iidem
> jurejurando fidem suam ecclesiae vel pauperibus, juxta ordinationem ecclesiasticam
> obstringant, et quotannis, praesente magistratu et pastore, rationem reddant.

II. Let the pastors take heed that fit deacons of the church and of the poor be chosen, and that
the same bind their faith to the church or to the poor by oath, according to the church order,
and yearly render account in the presence of the magistrate and the pastor.

- ***Diaconi, dat is der armen dieners*** in Lippe (1538). Every parish church is to "ordain"
  them and set up a common chest. The word *ordiniren* here means no more than to appoint.
  **Lippe, *Lippische Kirchenordnung*, 1538** (Sehling 21, p. 335):

<!-- doc 1459 -->
> Hirumb is nodig einer ideren kespelkirchen, diaconos, dat is der armen dieners, tho
> ordinirende und einenn gemeinenn kasten anthorichtende

Therefore it is needful for every parish church to ordain deacons, that is, servants of the
poor, and to set up a common chest […]

The office spread to the Lutheran stranger churches. The **Dutch congregation of the Augsburg
Confession at Frankfurt** founded its poor chest in 1585 and chose as its first *diaconi*
Michael Bode and Lorenz, "the schoolmaster here". **Frankfurt, *Stiftungsbrief des Armenkastens
der Niederländischen Gemeinde A. C.*, 1585** (Sehling 9, p. 604):

<!-- doc 2332 -->
> indem sie zu den ersten diaconis erwählet Michael Bode und Lorenz, allhiesigen schulmeister,
> und zu deputirten die herrn Arnd van Nieden, Peter van Leyden und Renatus Verbeke, absens,
> welche im nahmen des herrn dieses ihr amt angenommen haben.

[…] in that they chose for the first deacons Michael Bode and Lorenz, the schoolmaster here, and
for deputies the lords Arnd van Nieden, Peter van Leyden and Renatus Verbeke (absent), which in
the name of the Lord have taken upon them this their office.

Sehling's editors of the Hildesheim volume point out that Bugenhagen counted the maintenance of
ministers and the poor as part of "divine order". They conclude that the deacon's office must,
by its function, be of divine right (§5.4).

### 5.3 Hesse, the Reformed churches and the Bohemian Brethren: deacons as an ordained lay office

In the Hessian order of 1566 and in the Reformed orders, the deacon of the poor becomes one of
the **standing offices** of the congregation, alongside the ministers of the word and the
elders. He is chosen by the Spirit's own rule (Acts 6:3; 1 Timothy 3:8–12), put on probation (1
Timothy 3:10), and **ordained**.

Hesse 1566 gives the scriptural qualifications. **Hesse, *Kirchenordnung*, 1566**
(Sehling 8, p. 210):

<!-- doc 2257 -->
> hat uns der heilig Geist selbst gezeiget, was vor leut zu diesem ampt erwelet und gebraucht
> werden sollen. Man muß erwelen, sagen die aposteln (Act. 6, 3), menner, die ein gut gerucht
> haben und vol heiliges Geistes und weisheit seint. Und der heilig apostel Paulus, 1. Tim. 3
> (8f. 12), die diacon sollen erbar sein, nit zweizüngig, nicht weinseufer, nicht unehrliche
> handtirung treiben, die das geheimnis des glaubens in reinem gewissen haben, eines weibes mann
> […] und die ihr kinder und ganzes hausgesind wol regiren. (a. R.: Probatio) Welche nun erwelet
> seint, die sollen mit den andern, so in diesem ampt sein, ein zeitlang sich uben und brauchen,
> damit sie sich zu diesem ampt zuvor bereiten und geschickt machen, auf daß auch hiemit ihr
> glaub, fleiß und aufrichtigkeit an ihn probirt und bewert werde.

[…] the Holy Ghost himself hath shewed us what manner of men should be chosen and used for this
office. Ye must choose, say the apostles (Acts 6:3), men which have a good report and are full
of the Holy Ghost and wisdom. And the holy apostle Paul, 1 Timothy 3: the deacons shall be
grave, not double-tongued, not given to wine, not plying a dishonest trade, which hold the
mystery of the faith in a pure conscience, the husband of one wife […] and which rule their
children and whole household well. (In the margin: Probation.) They then which are chosen shall
exercise and employ themselves for a time with the others which are in this office, that they
may prepare and make themselves apt for this office beforehand, to the end that hereby also
their faith, diligence and uprightness may be proved and tried in them.

Hesse holds that the apostles ordained deacons "with common prayer and laying on of hands". It
therefore ordains both elders and deacons in their own church, while noting that the deacon's
office "is not equal to the service of the elders". **Hesse, *Kirchenordnung*, 1566**
(Sehling 8, pp. 207–208):

<!-- doc 2257 -->
> Es haben auch die aposteln mit dem gemeinen gebet und auflegung der hende die diacon ordiniret
> und bestetiget, Act. 6 [1-6], welches ampt doch dem dienst der eltesten beiweilen nicht gleich
> ist. Es sollen aber alweg diese eltesten und auch die diacon in derselbigen kirchen ordiniret
> werden, welcher sie dienen sollen, und dies soll entweder der superintendens, wan er daselbst
> visitiret, oder sonst irgents ein pfarherr sampt einem oder zweien dienern des worts
> verrichten.

The apostles also ordained and confirmed the deacons with common prayer and laying on of hands,
Acts 6; which office nevertheless is by no means equal to the service of the elders. But these
elders, and the deacons also, shall always be ordained in the same church which they are to
serve; and this shall be performed either by the superintendent, when he visiteth there, or else
by some pastor together with one or two ministers of the word.

**Micron's *Ordinancien*.** These are the order of the Dutch stranger church in London (1554),
as printed for Norden in East Frisia in 1565. They set "four deacons or ministers of the poor,
after the apostolic order" beside the elders. **Micron, *Ordinancien* (1554) 1565**
(Sehling 7/1, p. 583):

<!-- doc 2116 -->
> Bald darnach haben wir auch vier diaken oder diener der armen nach der apostolischen ordnung
> mit bewilligung der gemeine angenommen.

Soon after we also received, with the consent of the congregation, four deacons or ministers of
the poor after the apostolic order.

The deacons are confirmed with a form modelled on that for the elders (§6.3). They are presented
to the congregation and questioned:
- whether they feel the inward witness of the Spirit;
- whether they hold the prophetic and apostolic doctrine sufficient;
- whether they will gather and distribute the alms faithfully "without respect of persons";
- whether they will submit to Christian discipline.

The congregation prays, "as thou didst give thy Spirit to Stephen". Then hands are laid on them.
**Micron, *Ordinancien* (1554) 1565, chapter VII, "Form der bestetigung der diaken oder
almosenpfleger"** (Sehling 7/1, pp. 597–598):

<!-- doc 2116 -->
> Wollet ihr nicht die almussen zum nutz der armen fleisig samlen und die versamleten treulich
> und weißlich den armen außteilen, und insonderheit den haußgenossen des glaubens, one einigs
> eusserliches ansehen der person, sonder allein nach eines jeden noturft und armut? Sie
> antworten: Ja, wir, durch die gnade Gottes. […] Wann das gebet volendet ist, werden ihnen die
> hende aufgelegt, und der diener spricht mit heller stimme also: Gott der Herr und unser
> himlischer Vater, der euch zu dem dienst der diakeney beruffen hat, der regiere euch
> gendiglich mit seiner göttlichen kraft, weißheit und gütigkeit

Will ye not diligently gather the alms for the profit of the poor, and faithfully and wisely
distribute that which is gathered unto the poor, and especially unto the household of faith,
without any outward respect of the person, but only according to each one's need and poverty?
They answer: Yea, we will, by the grace of God. […] When the prayer is ended, hands are laid
upon them, and the minister saith with a clear voice thus: God the Lord and our heavenly Father,
which hath called you to the service of the diaconate, govern you graciously with his divine
power, wisdom and goodness […]

Sehling's editor notes that in **London** the elders and the deacons had been installed with
prayer and laying on of hands since 1550. In **Emden** laying on of hands "is not attested", and
the deacons there were apparently not installed after the same pattern as the elders
(Sehling 7/1, pp. 554–555, editor's introduction).

**Without laying on of hands.** Several Reformed orders install deacons with no laying on of
hands:
- **Kurpfalz (1563)**, followed by **Wittgenstein (1581)** and **Bentheim-Tecklenburg**
  (1588/1619). They have "pious and godly men, whom they call *diaconi* or almoners", elected
  per 1 Timothy 3:8. They collect the alms on Sundays and distribute them, and are confirmed
  "with the knowledge and will of the pastors and appointed seniors". No laying on of hands is
  mentioned. **Bentheim-Tecklenburg, *Bentheim-Tecklenburger Kirchenordnung*, 1588/1619**
  (Sehling 22, p. 276):

<!-- doc 1508 -->
> fromme und gottselige Männer, die man Diaconos oder Almusenpfleger nennet, erwehlet und mit
> wissen und willen der Pastorn und verordneten Senioren bestettigt werden, vermug der Lehr und
> gebrauchs der H. Aposteln

[…] pious and godly men, whom men call Deacons or Almoners, be chosen, and confirmed with the
knowledge and will of the Pastors and the appointed Seniors, according to the doctrine and use
of the holy Apostles […]

- **Nassau-Dillenburg (1582).** Here the deacon is the fourth of four offices: ministers,
  doctors, seniors and deacons. The presbytery elects the seniors, the deacons of the poor and
  the sextons (*aeditui*), and the classis confirms them. The minister alone receives
  "confirmation or ordination". **Nassau-Dillenburg, *Kirchenordnung*, 23 January 1582**
  (Sehling 10, p. 160):

<!-- doc 184 -->
> Officia sunt quadruplicia, alia ministrorum, alia doctorum, alia seniorum, alia diaconorum.
> […] 4. Vocatio ministrorum fiat iudicio classis et aliquot seniorum, ad quam pertinent haec
> quatuor: electio, examen, approbatio et confirmatio seu ordinatio. […] 5. Quod ad seniores,
> diaconos pauperum et aedituos eligendos, potestas eligendi sit penes presbyterium, qui postea
> suffragio classici conventus confirmandi ac diligenter de officio admonendi erunt.

The offices are fourfold: some of ministers, some of doctors, some of seniors, some of deacons.
[…] 4. Let the calling of ministers be made by the judgement of the classis and of some seniors;
whereunto pertain these four: election, examination, approbation, and confirmation or
ordination. […] 5. As touching the choosing of seniors, deacons of the poor and sextons, let the
power of choosing be with the presbytery; who afterward shall be confirmed by the vote of the
classical assembly and be diligently admonished of [their] office.

**The Bohemian Brethren.** Their deacon is something else: an ordained **clerical** grade
between acolyte and minister. The deacons are "older acolytes", preparing for the ministry. The
order defends the grade as kept "constantly in the primitive church", which must not be cast out
though Antichrist abused it. **Bohemian Brethren, *Ordo ecclesiasticus*, 1576**
(Sehling 4, p. 276):

<!-- doc 1846 -->
> Diaconi sunt adultiores acoluthi, futuro ministerio iam iam sese praeparantes. Hos fratrum
> ministri pro administris, sive adiutoribus, habent, apostolorum exemplo, Act. VI. v. 2. et
> Philipp. I. 1. Quamuis enim ab apostolis primario ad mensarum ministeria ordinati erant;
> constat tamen et praedicasse ipsos, et baptizasse (ut Stephanus, Act. VI. 10. et Philippus
> Act. VIII. 5. 12. 35. 38.) et ad gradum superiorem, ministerii nempe seu presbyterii, promotos
> fuisse, 1. Timoth. III. 13. Itaque gradus hic diaconatus in primitiva ecclesia constanter fuit
> servatus: ut ex conciliorum canonibus patet. Quem proinde, etiamsi ab antichristo cum ceteris
> in abusum tractus sit, ab ecclesia eliminari non convenit.

Deacons are the older acolytes, now presently preparing themselves for the future ministry.
These the ministers of the brethren have for assistants or helpers, after the example of the
apostles, Acts 6:2 and Philippians 1:1. For although they were ordained by the apostles
primarily to the ministry of tables, yet it is certain that they both preached and baptised (as
Stephen, Acts 6:10, and Philip, Acts 8:5, 12, 35, 38) and were promoted to a higher grade,
namely of the ministry or presbytery, 1 Timothy 3:13. Therefore this grade of the diaconate was
constantly kept in the primitive church, as appeareth from the canons of the councils. Which
therefore, although it hath been drawn into abuse by the antichrist with the rest, it is not
fitting to cast out of the church.

What the Brethren's deacons did (same page):
- study theology;
- teach the young the rudiments;
- practise preaching;
- baptise "as often as the pastor shall command".

At a crowded communion they help distribute both the bread and the cup, but with one limit: only
a minister "having the power of the keys" pronounces the absolution and recites the words of
institution. **Bohemian Brethren, *Ordo ecclesiasticus*, 1576** (Sehling 4, p. 276):

<!-- doc 1846 -->
> Quoties frequentiori coetu sacra coena administratur, nec sufficientes adsunt ministri,
> adhibentur etiam diaconi ad distribuendum, nonsolum calicem, sed et panem; ita tamen, ut non
> nisi minister, clavium potestatem habens, absolutionem populo annuntiet, et verba
> institutionis solemniter praemittat.

As often as the holy supper is administered in a more numerous assembly, and sufficient
ministers are not present, the deacons also are employed to distribute not only the cup but also
the bread; yet so that none but a minister having the power of the keys announce the absolution
to the people and solemnly set forth before the words of institution.

They are ordained before the synod, with an examination by the seniors, a vow, prayer and the
**laying on of hands**, "Acts 6:6". **Bohemian Brethren, *Ordo ecclesiasticus*, 1576,
"Diaconorum ordinatio"** (Sehling 4, p. 279):

<!-- doc 1846 -->
> 3. Illi Christo et ecclesiae voto se obstringunt. 4. Preces pro ipsis fiunt, ordinatorque
> manuum impositione eos confirmat, Act. VI. 6. 5. Offerunt novi diaconi senioribus et ministris
> obedientiae sanctae; priores vero diaconi illis societatis, dextram. 6. Benedictione actus
> clauditur.

3. They bind themselves to Christ and the church by a vow. 4. Prayers are made for them, and the
   ordainer confirmeth them by the laying on of hands, Acts 6:6. 5. The new deacons offer to the
   seniors and ministers the right hand of holy obedience; and the former deacons [offer] to
   them [the right hand] of fellowship. 6. The act is closed with a blessing.

### 5.4 Divine right or human right?

**The question as the church orders put it.** No order asks whether the diaconate is a separate
*order* by divine right in the Roman sense. The orders ask two other questions:
1. Is there one ministry of the word, of which bishop, pastor and deacon are grades?
2. Is the deacon of the poor a lasting institution of the apostles?

**One ministry; grades by human right.** The orders consistently say that bishop, presbyter and
pastor are one office:
- Homberg 1526: "bishops, or presbyters" (§3.2).
- Bugenhagen 1528: "bishops or preachers (which is all one thing)" (§5.2).
- Pfalz-Zweibrücken 1539. It describes the ancient Church as installing a minister "by one or
  two of the nearest bishops, whom men also call pastors". It says that it now has "certain of
  the nearest pastors or elders" lay hands on the new minister. **Pfalz-Zweibrücken,
  *Kirchenordnung „Form und Ordnung“*, 1539** (Sehling 18, p. 63):

<!-- doc 965 -->
> das die gemein einen welet, der ward von einem oder zweien der nechsten bischoffen, die man
> auch pastores oder pfarher nennet, eingesetzt und bestetigt. […] Die nu in lere und leben zu
> diesem ampt thetig erfunden wurden, denen haben wyr furgemelt ettlich der nechsten pfarher
> oder eltesten in der gemein gottes, die hend auff das haupt gelegt und gebetten, das der
> heilig geist die diener des worts in Christlicher lere und leben erleuchten und erhalten
> wölle.

[…] that the congregation chose one, who was set in office and confirmed by one or two of the
nearest bishops, whom men also call pastors or parsons. […] Those then which were found apt for
this office in doctrine and life, upon them have certain of the nearest pastors or elders in the
congregation of God, as aforesaid, laid hands upon the head, and prayed that the Holy Ghost
would enlighten and preserve the ministers of the word in christian doctrine and life.

Where distinctions within the ministry are defended, they are defended as **human order for the
sake of good order**. Melanchthon's *Wittenberg Reformation* (1545) keeps bishops "as a grade
above other priests" so that, "so far as human foresight can consider", there may be a lasting
order. **Ernestine Saxony, *Wittenbergische Reformation*, 1545** (Sehling 1, p. 217):

<!-- doc 20 -->
> unter diesen seelsorgern muss dennoch eine ordnung sein; sie haben nicht alle gleiche gaben,
> können nicht alle richter sein in schweren artikeln der lehre; sie können nicht alle die
> gericht ordnen und halten. […] und die selbigen ort müssen mit personen und unterhaltung also
> versorgt sein, dass es, so viel menschliche fürsichtigkeit bedenken kann, eine beständige
> ordnung sei. Darum müssen bischofe, als ein grad über andre priester, sein

[…] among these curates of souls there must nevertheless be an order; they have not all like
gifts, they cannot all be judges in weighty articles of doctrine; they cannot all order and hold
the courts. […] and the same places must be so provided with persons and maintenance that there
be, so far as human foresight can consider, a lasting order. Therefore must there be bishops, as
a grade above other priests […]

The same text sets "divine and eternal right" apart from "human, transitory ordinance of things
indifferent" (Sehling 1, p. 218). The Lutheran *Diaconus*, the *Subdiaconus* and the
superintendent all stand on the second footing. Electoral Saxony can therefore dispense a
candidate from the diaconate altogether (§5.1). Its ordination rite also calls the laying on of
hands a ceremony of "christian liberty", while it calls the ministry itself a "divine command
and ordinance".

**The deacon of the poor as apostolic.** The lay deacon of the poor is, by contrast, defended as
apostolic in origin:
- **Homberg** (§3.2) knows only "bishops, or presbyters, and deacons of the poor".
- **Strasbourg** (§3.1) says the deacon alone of the lower grades was instituted "by the Holy
  Ghost through the Apostles". Its list of the three ministries that every church needs "even
  today" includes "the care of the poor".
- **Hesse 1566** and **Micron** cite Acts 6 and 1 Timothy 3. They say the Holy Ghost "himself
  hath shewed" what men should be chosen, and they ordain deacons as the apostles did.
- **The Bohemian Brethren** hold that the diaconate "was constantly kept in the primitive
  church" and must not be cast out. In practice, though, they treat it as a probationary step
  towards the presbyterate.

Of Bugenhagen, the editors of the Hildesheim volume write
(Sehling 7/2.1, p. 811, editor's introduction):

<!-- doc 2132 -->
> Bemerkenswert ist, daß Bugenhagen in seiner Schleswig-Holsteinischen KO von 1542 und in seiner
> Wolfenbüttler KO von 1543 die Versorgung der Kirchen- und Schuldiener sowie auch der Armen zur
> göttlichen Ordnung zählt, der er die menschliche Ordnung gegenüberstellt. Von daher, nach
> seiner Funktion, muß demnach das Diakonenamt göttlichen Rechts sein. Es steht in der
> göttlichen Rechtsordnung neben dem Predigtamt!

It is noteworthy that Bugenhagen, in his Schleswig-Holstein church order of 1542 and in his
Wolfenbüttel church order of 1543, reckoneth the maintenance of the ministers of church and
school, and also of the poor, to the divine order, over against which he setteth the human
order. From this, by its function, the office of deacon must accordingly be of divine right. It
standeth in the divine order of right beside the office of preaching!

**Lay deacons.** In this sense **every** Lutheran deacon of the common chest, and every Reformed
deacon, was a **lay deacon**. He was a man who kept his trade and served a term, elected by
council and congregation or by the presbytery, and he had no part in preaching or the
sacraments. The Lutheran *Diaconus*, by contrast, was never a layman. The Bohemian deacon was a
cleric in training.

## 6. Elders

**Two kinds of elders.** Almost every order that speaks of governing elders starts from 1
Timothy 5:17, "the elders that rule well […] especially they who labour in the word and
doctrine". From that verse it distinguishes two kinds of elder (*presbyter*, *senior*,
*Eltester*):
- those who **labour in the word**: the ministers;
- those who **only govern**: the lay elders.

**Lineage.** The lay elder came into the German orders through Bucer, at Strasbourg and in
Hesse. It was then generalised in the Reformed orders of East Frisia, Kurpfalz, Nassau and the
Wetterau counties.

**In the Lutheran orders** "elder" is either a churchwarden without spiritual jurisdiction or a
senior pastor (§6.5).

### 6.1 Strasbourg: the disciplinary *seniores*

The Strasbourg examination order of about 1539 already speaks of a second "order of presbyters",
those "employed only for the discipline of the church" (§3.1). Its authority is twofold:
- the elders of the synagogue;
- Ambrose on 1 Timothy 5. Ambrose complains that such elders had lapsed through "the sloth or
  rather the pride of the teachers, who alone wish to seem something".

Marbach's church order of 1553 identifies the city's *Kirchenpfleger* with those *seniores*.
They are elected to oversee the "whole householding of the church", which the preachers cannot
manage alone. It reads the "church" of Matthew 18 as these elected men, not the whole assembly.
**Strasbourg, *Kirchenordnung* of Johannes Marbach, 1553, "Von den kirchenpflegern und irem
ampt"** (Sehling 20/1, p. 441):

<!-- doc 1321 -->
> Auß der Apostel geschicht ist zu sehen, das in der ersten christlichen kirchen neben den
> aposteln, so das evangelium verkündigt haben, etliche von der gemein dazu erwellet und
> verordnet sind, die da ein sonderlich auffsehen haben solten, damit allentalben recht
> haußgehalten, niemands nicht gottselig nach seinem beruff zu leben versaumpt und also gute
> ordnung in der kirchen als im hauß Gottes gehaltenn würde, die sie seniores und presbyteros
> genennt haben. […] Derenhalben der herr Christus, Matth. 18 [15-18], die unbußfertigen und
> hartneckigen heist der kirchenn anzeigen, das sie von deren gestrafft und zu warer bekerung
> vermanet werden. Die kirche aber versthet der herre nicht die gantze gemein, wie die das wort
> zuhören zusamen kompt, sonder die jenigen, so ordenlicher weiß von solcher versamlung darzu
> erwölet und geben sind, in namen unnd von wegen der kirchen allerley fürgefalne kirchen sachen
> zu verhandlen

Out of the Acts of the Apostles it is to be seen that in the first christian church, beside the
apostles which preached the gospel, certain were chosen and ordained thereto by the
congregation, which should have a special oversight, that everywhere house might be rightly
kept, that none might neglect to live godly according to his calling, and so good order be kept
in the church as in the house of God; whom they named seniores and presbyteri. […] Therefore the
Lord Christ, Matthew 18, biddeth that the impenitent and obstinate be told to the church, that
they may be rebuked by it and admonished to true conversion. But by the church the Lord
understandeth not the whole congregation as it cometh together to hear the word, but those which
in orderly wise are chosen and given thereto by such assembly, to treat in the name and on
behalf of the church all manner of church matters that fall out […]

### 6.2 Hesse: elders elected, "sanctified" and finally ordained

**The Ziegenhain discipline order (1539)**, written under Bucer's influence, introduces elders
into every Hessian congregation. They are chosen by the church "according to the canon of Paul",
as the ministers are. It would be good, the order says, to confirm them publicly "with prayer
and exhortation" and so to "sanctify their office". The civil authority is to uphold their
office as the most necessary "after the office of teaching". **Hesse, *Ziegenhainer
Zuchtordnung*, 1539** (Sehling 8, p. 103):

<!-- doc 2252 -->
> Diese eltesten solle jede kirche, wie auch die diener des wortes mit getreuem ufsehen auf den
> canonem Pauli welen. Und wer auch gut, daß man sie in der kirchen mit offentlichem gebet und
> vermanung zu ihnen den eltesten, ihres ampts fleißig zu warten, zu der gemein, denselbigen
> eltesten im Herren herzlich zu gehorchen, bestetigete und ihr ampt also heiligte. Dero ampt
> und dienst auch die oberkeit getreulich erheben und darob halten soll, als ob dem
> notwendigsten und heilsamsten dienst und ampt, so nach dem ampt der leer in der kirchen sein
> mag.

These elders shall every church choose, as also the ministers of the word, with faithful regard
to the canon of Paul. And it were good also that they should be confirmed in the church with
public prayer and exhortation (to them, the elders, to attend diligently to their office; to the
congregation, to obey the same elders heartily in the Lord), and their office so be sanctified.
Whose office and service the magistracy also shall faithfully exalt and uphold, as the most
necessary and wholesome service and office that may be in the church after the office of
teaching.

**The Hessian church order of 1566** makes the elders a permanent college in every parish. The
superintendent's instructions open with this (Sehling 8, p. 195):

<!-- doc 2257 -->
> Das erste ist, daß in allen kirchen euers zirks seniores, das ist eltesten, erwelet und
> ordinirt werden, welche euch beide an diesem ort und den andern pfarherrn in ihren kirchen mit
> rat und tat und all ihrem vermögen behülflich seien, mit welchen ihr auch von allen sachen, so
> im kirchenregiment fürfallen, ratschlagen und bedechtlich von allen dingen zum furderlichsten
> teilen und handlen mögen. Davon sagt also Lucas (Act. 14,23): Sie ordneten hin und her
> eltesten in der gemein, beteten und fasteten und befolhen sie dem Herrn, an den sie gleubig
> worden waren.

The first is, that in all churches of your district seniors, that is, elders, be chosen and
ordained, which may be helpful both to you in this place and to the other pastors in their
churches with counsel and deed and all their power; with whom ye may also take counsel of all
matters which fall out in the government of the church, and advisedly treat of all things with
all speed. Thereof saith Luke thus (Acts 14:23): They ordained elders here and there in the
congregation, prayed and fasted, and commended them to the Lord on whom they believed.

**Election and qualifications.** The order appeals to Ambrose for the antiquity of the office.
The ministers, together with men of the council and the congregation, elect the elders by vote
(Acts 14:23). The elders must be of the same zeal and life as preachers, "only that they need
not be so excellently learned". **Hesse, *Kirchenordnung*, 1566, "Wie man die eltesten zur
verwaltung der kirchen erwelen und ordiniren sol"** (Sehling 8, p. 207):

<!-- doc 2257 -->
> Darumb sollen in einer jeden kirchen die diener derselbigen gemein sampt etlichen, beid vom
> rat und aus der gemein darzu verordnet, diese eltesten erwelen. Von der form und weis der
> erwelung findet man Act. 14 [23], daß es gemeinlich durch suffragia und stimme geschehen. Zu
> diesem ampt aber muß man eben solche leut, so viel den eifer und das leben betrifft, erwelen
> und haben, wie auch zum predigampt, on allein, daß sie nicht dürfen also vortrefflich gelert
> sein.

Therefore in every church the ministers of the same congregation, together with certain
appointed thereto both from the council and from the congregation, shall choose these elders. Of
the form and manner of the election it is found in Acts 14 that it was done commonly by
suffrages and voices. But to this office one must choose and have even such people, so far as
concerneth zeal and life, as for the office of preaching; save only that they need not be so
excellently learned.

**Their charge.** The ordination address sets the elders "beside the bishops", placed by the
Holy Ghost to rule the church. They are to assist the ministers as councillors assist a
burgomaster. Above all they are to guard against falsification of doctrine, whether by the
public preachers or by "false teachers and wolves". **Hesse, *Kirchenordnung*, 1566**
(Sehling 8, p. 208):

<!-- doc 2257 -->
> Ihr habt gehort, mein geliebten im Herrn, daß neben den bischoffen auch eltesten vom h. Geist
> zur verwaltung der kirchen Gottes gesetzt seind. Darumb will es euch gebüren, daß ihr
> sonderlich große sorge tragt und darauf geflissen seid, wie die kirche wol möge regirt werden,
> und daß ihr den andern dienern, so im wort arbeiten, in allen dingen mit treuem rat und hülf
> forderlich seid, gleich wie sich dan auch dapfere und weise radsherrn gegen dem burgemeister
> halten, wo ein gute ordnung in gemeinem nutz soll angerichtet werden. (1) Vor allen dingen
> aber solt ihr sampt den andern kirchendienern mit höchsten fleiß verhüten, daß nicht etwan ein
> verfelschung der lehr, entweder durch die, so in der kirchen offentlich predigen, oder durch
> falsche lehrer und wölfe, so heimlich neben einschleichen und listiglich betrigen wöllen,
> eingefiiret werden

Ye have heard, my beloved in the Lord, that beside the bishops elders also are set by the Holy
Ghost for the government of the church of God. Therefore it will behove you that ye take
especial great care and be diligent how the church may be well ruled, and that ye be furthersome
in all things to the other ministers which labour in the word with faithful counsel and help,
even as valiant and wise councillors also behave themselves toward the burgomaster where a good
order is to be set up in the common weal. (1) But above all things ye shall, together with the
other ministers of the church, with highest diligence prevent that no falsifying of doctrine be
brought in, whether by those which preach publicly in the church, or by false teachers and
wolves which secretly creep in beside and will craftily deceive […]

**Ordination.** After the prayer the pastor and the other ministers lay hands on the elders, "as
also before said", using the rite of the ministers' ordination (Sehling 8, p. 209):

<!-- doc 2257 -->
> Darnach soll der pfarherr und die andern ministri ihnen die hende auflegen, wie auch zuvor
> gesagt, etc. (9) Nach dem allen soll man die ordinanden in die mit stellen und ihn die kirch
> zu versorgen und zu verwalten mit solchen worten befehlen: So befehlen wir euch diese kirch,
> in welcher ihr zu eltesten seid erwelet worden

Thereafter shall the pastor and the other ministers lay their hands upon them, as is also said
before, etc. (9) After all this, the ordinands shall be set in the midst, and the church
committed to them to care for and to govern with such words: So we commit unto you this church,
in which ye have been chosen to be elders […]

**Elders at the ordination of ministers.** The traffic also runs the other way. At the
ordination of a new minister of the word, the elders lay on hands with the superintendent and
the ministers (Sehling 8, p. 205):

<!-- doc 2257 -->
> Nach dem gebet soll der superintendens sampt den andern dienern des worts und eltesten den
> ordinanden die hend auflegen

After the prayer the superintendent, together with the other ministers of the word and elders,
shall lay hands upon the ordinands […]

The editor of the Hessian volume notes that Johannes a Lasco also knew an ordination of the
governing elders and of the deacons (Sehling 8, p. 208, note 97).

### 6.3 The Reformed orders: four offices, elders who govern with the ministers

**Micron's *Ordinancien*** (London 1554, printed for Norden 1565) gives the classic Reformed
account. There are two "kinds" (*geschlechte*) of elders. Some labour in word and doctrine, and
are called bishops, shepherds and teachers. Others "minister not the public service of the word
and sacraments", but are helpers of the ministers. Paul calls these "governments" (1 Corinthians
12:28). One of the elders presides, and the king's charter calls him the superintendent.
**Micron, *Ordinancien* (1554) 1565** (Sehling 7/1, pp. 587–588):

<!-- doc 2116 -->
> und die andere eltesten, wiewol sie den offentlichen dienst des worts und der sacramenten nit
> bedienen, dennoch sind sie gehülfen der ander diener und stehen ihnen bey mit allem ernst, mit
> rat und hülf, auf daß die gemeine Gottes in aller gottseligkeit, heiligkeit, friede, ordnung
> und erbarkeit nach dem wort Gottes regieret und erhalten werde; darumb werden sie auch von
> Paulo regenten genennet. Unter disen zweien geschlechten der eltisten ist einer auß ihrer zale
> der vornemest, darzu ordentlich erwehlet, auf daß durch seine autoritet ein eintrechtige
> vergleichung unter allen in allen dingen nach dem wort Gottes gehalten werde. Und dieser ist
> von k. m. in ihrem privilegio genennet superintendens, das ist aufseher. Die diaken aber
> tragen sorge für die armen der gemein.

[…] and the other elders, although they minister not the public service of the word and of the
sacraments, yet are they helpers of the other ministers, and stand by them with all earnestness,
with counsel and help, that the congregation of God may be ruled and preserved in all godliness,
holiness, peace, order and honesty according to the word of God; wherefore they are also called
by Paul governments. Among these two kinds of elders one out of their number is the chief,
orderly chosen thereto, that by his authority an accordant agreement may be held among all in
all things according to the word of God. And he is called by the king's majesty in their
privilege superintendent, that is, overseer. But the deacons take care for the poor of the
congregation.

The elders are confirmed after questions and prayer by **laying on of hands**, "by the other
ministers and by the minister of the word" (Sehling 7/1, p. 596). The words are nearly those
later used in Kurpfalz, given below.

**Kurpfalz.** The elders of the Electoral Palatinate were set up by the edict of 1570, after the
dispute with Erastus. Sehling's editors describe that dispute in their introduction
(Sehling 14, p. 53, editors' introduction):

<!-- doc 468 -->
> Umstritten war nicht die Zuchtübung als solche, sondern die Frage, von wem, in welchen Fällen
> und in welcher Weise sie zu handhaben sei. Die Befürworter der calvinischen Kirchenzucht
> standen auf der Linie von Katechismus und Kirchenordnung und unterstrichen das göttliche Recht
> der Presbyterien, die Notwendigkeit der Abmahnung vom Abendmahl bei Irrlehrern und
> besserungsunwilligen Sündern durch dieses und im Prinzip die scharfe Trennung einer weltlichen
> und kirchlichen Strafgewalt.

What was in dispute was not the exercise of discipline as such, but the question by whom, in
what cases and in what manner it was to be handled. The advocates of the Calvinist church
discipline stood on the line of the Catechism and the church order, and underlined the divine
right of the presbyteries, the necessity of exclusion from the supper of false teachers and of
sinners unwilling to amend, by this [presbytery], and in principle the sharp separation of a
temporal and an ecclesiastical power of punishment.

The decision fell in 1569, with opinions by Beza, Ursinus and Zanchi against Erastus. On 13 July
1570 the edict set up church discipline and the classical assemblies. The editors add that
Erastus still won concessions, including the involvement of the magistrate in excommunication
(same page).

Under Frederick IV a new form for installing the elders was issued, dated 9 December 1592; the
editors say the new elders were installed at the end of the New Year's service. Its title is
"Form, how the elders which labour not in the word are confirmed in the congregation". The
elders are questioned:
- whether they feel the witness of the Spirit;
- whether Scripture contains all that is needful to salvation;
- whether they will assist the ministers and accept correction themselves.

The congregation prays, and then hands are laid on them. **Kurpfalz, *Einführung der Senioren*,
9 December 1592** (Sehling 19/2, pp. 789–790):

<!-- doc 1155 -->
> Form, wie man die eltesten, so in dem wort nit arbeyten, in der gemein bestediget etc. […]
> Darnach werden ihnen die händt aufgelegt von den andern dihnern und vom diener des worts,
> welcher spricht mit heller stim also: Gott und unser himlischer vater, der euch mit uns zu dem
> regiment diser seiner gemeindt (nach der lehr seines worts) berufen und ausgesondert hat, der
> erleuchte euch auch durch seinen heiligen geist und stercke euch mit der kraft seiner
> unuberwindtlicher handt, das ihr euern dihnst treulich und bestendig volnfuhren moget zu ehren
> seines namens und erbauung seiner gemeindt, Amen. […] Zum letzten wendet er seine red zu den
> angenommenen eltesten und vermahnet sie, das sie keine acht haben sollen auf die verachtung
> undt haß der welt, keine person ansehen, sondern das sie einen jeden ohne ansehen der person
> vermahnen, bessern und strafen nach dem prauch der christlichen strafe, das sie auch uf die
> lehr, sitten, ja auf das gantze leben aller diner des worts ernstlich acht haben, die wölf vor
> der herdt der gemeyne mit den dihnern des worts wehren

Form, how the elders which labour not in the word are confirmed in the congregation, etc. […]
Thereafter hands are laid upon them by the other ministers and by the minister of the word, who
speaketh with a clear voice thus: God and our heavenly Father, which hath called and separated
you with us to the government of this his congregation (according to the doctrine of his word),
enlighten you also by his Holy Spirit and strengthen you with the power of his invincible hand,
that ye may fulfil your service faithfully and constantly, to the honour of his name and the
edifying of his congregation. Amen. […] Lastly he turneth his speech to the elders received, and
admonisheth them that they regard not the contempt and hatred of the world, respect no person,
but that they admonish, amend and rebuke every one without respect of person according to the
use of christian rebuke; that they also take earnest heed to the doctrine, manners, yea, to the
whole life of all ministers of the word; [and] ward off the wolves from the flock of the
congregation together with the ministers of the word […]

The editors of the volume stress the point: "it is especially noteworthy that they are
**ordained by laying on of hands** by the minister of the church and the individual elders
continuing in office" (Sehling 19/2, p. 695, editors' introduction). The same form was adopted
in Ysenburg-Ronneburg in 1592 (Sehling 10, p. 601).

**Kurpfalz about 1601.** The Palatine *Presbyterordnung* of about 1601 defines the presbytery as
"certain persons chosen yearly" beside the ministers, "as elders and overseers", to deal with
offences in doctrine and life. Their sanctions:
- admonition, once, twice and three times;
- for the obstinate, being "quietly" warned off the sacraments.

The order grounds the office on Christ's own institution in Matthew 18. **Kurpfalz,
*Presbyterordnung*, about 1601** (Sehling 14, p. 595):

<!-- doc 518 -->
> daß durchs presbyterium oder eltistenrhat dieses und anders nicht verstanden werde, dan das
> neben den kirchendienern jedes orts etliche gewiße personen zue eltisten und aufsehern der
> christlichen gemein jerlich erwehlet werden, welche anstatt der gantzen gemein zue gewißen
> zeiten, nachdeme es die not erfordert, zusammenkommen und, was sich in lehr und leben für
> ärgernus in der gemain zuetragen, sich dieselben zue verbeßern und abzueschaffen miteinander
> underreden, auch vermanungen aus dem wort Gottes nach der ordnung des herren Christi zum
> ersten, andern und dritten mahl […] thun und, da einer in offenen sunden und ergernus
> mutwillig verharren wolt, denselben von den hailigen sacramenten in der still mit christlicher
> beschaidenhait abmahnen, biß er beßerung verhaiset und erzeiget. Daß nun diese ordnung in
> Gottes wort gegrundet und Christus selbst solche angeordnet und befolhen habe, weisen
> nachfolgende spruch der heiligen schrift clarlich aus.

[…] that by the presbytery or council of elders nothing else is to be understood than this: that
beside the ministers of the church in every place certain particular persons are chosen yearly
to be elders and overseers of the christian congregation, which in the stead of the whole
congregation come together at certain times, as need requireth, and confer together how the
offences which arise in doctrine and life in the congregation may be amended and done away; and
also make admonitions out of the word of God, according to the order of the Lord Christ, the
first, second and third time […] and, if one would wilfully continue in open sins and offence,
warn him off from the holy sacraments quietly, with christian discretion, until he promise and
shew amendment. Now that this order is grounded in God's word, and that Christ himself hath
ordained and commanded it, the following sayings of holy scripture clearly shew.

These elders take a handfast vow (*angeloben*) to the published order. They are "confirmed" and
announced from the pulpit on New Year's Day, then "confirmed through the common christian
prayer". There is no laying on of hands. **Kurpfalz, *Presbyterordnung*, about 1601**
(Sehling 14, p. 598):

<!-- doc 518 -->
> angeloben laßen und sie also confirmirn und mit pflichten annehmen sollt. Nochmals sollen sie
> der gemeine auf den neuen jarstag von der cantzel verkündet und durch das christliche, gemaine
> gebet bestetiget werden mit fleißiger ermahnung, daß sie jederman für ordentliche eltisten und
> vorsteher der gemein erkennen, in gebürlichen ehren halten und ihnen gehorsam leisten sollen.

[…] thou shalt cause them to vow by handfast, and so confirm them and receive them under
obligation. Afterward they shall be proclaimed to the congregation from the pulpit on New Year's
day, and confirmed through the christian common prayer, with diligent admonition that every man
acknowledge them for orderly elders and governors of the congregation, hold them in due honour,
and render them obedience.

**Hanau-Münzenberg (1609)** copies this procedure. It adds that the minister must teach the
congregation "that the office of elders is of God". This is said partly to comfort the elders,
partly so that the people will not "exempt themselves" from their rebukes. **Hanau-Münzenberg,
*Presbyteriumsordnung*, 1609** (Sehling 10, p. 508):

<!-- doc 211 -->
> in handgelübdnus genommen werden. Darauf fürters folgenden sontag nach einer kurzen predigt
> die eltesten der gemeinde wider sollen verkündiget und also nach beschehener fleißiger
> ermahnung, daß sie jederman fur ordentliche eltesten und vorsteher der gemein erkennen, in
> gebürlichen ehren halten und ihnen gehorsamb leisten sollen, durch das christliche gemeine
> gebet bestettiget werden. Es ist aber bei solcher ankündigung und bestettigung von nöten, daß
> der kirchendiener der gemeinde aus dem wort Gottes deutlich anzeige, 1. daß der eltesten ampt
> von Gott seie aus obgesetzten zeugnußen der heiligen schrift, den eltesten zum besten, damit
> sie solch ampt als einen gottlichen beruf uf sich nehmen […] darnach der gemeinde wegen, daß
> sie solchem ampt sich nicht widersetzen noch davon eximiren

[…] shall be taken under handfast vow. Whereupon on the following Sunday after a short sermon
the elders shall be proclaimed again to the congregation, and so, after diligent admonition made
that every man acknowledge them for orderly elders and governors of the congregation, hold them
in due honour and render them obedience, be confirmed through the christian common prayer. But
at such proclamation and confirmation it is needful that the minister of the church shew the
congregation plainly out of the word of God, 1. that the office of elders is of God, from the
testimonies of holy scripture set down above: for the elders' good, that they may take such
office upon them as a divine calling […]; next, for the congregation's sake, that they set not
themselves against such office nor exempt themselves from it […]

**Pfalz-Veldenz (1574)** calls its elders "censors". They are presented to the assembled
congregation, "commended" from the pulpit with prayer and "set into this high office", "as we
see that the apostles ordained the Elders and commended them to the congregation, Acts 14;
likewise also the Deacons, Acts 6". **Pfalz-Veldenz, *Kirchenordnung*, 1574, "Ordnung der
Eltisten"** (Sehling 18, p. 573):

<!-- doc 1011 -->
> Wölche zu disem hohen ampt erwöhlet sein, die sollen darnach uff einen gewissen tag, wann die
> gemein beyeinander versamlet (wölche auch vermahnet soll werden, fleissig zuerscheinen und
> Gott anzurueffen), berueffen und der gemein fürgestelt, also offentlich mit dem gebett von der
> Cantzel durch den Pfarrer der gantzen Gemein commendirt und inn diß hohe ampt eingesetzt
> werden […] wie wir dann sehen, das die apostel die Eltisten geordinirt und der gemein
> befolhen, Actor. 14. Dessgleichen auch die Diacon, Actor. 6.

Those which are chosen to this high office shall thereafter, upon a set day when the
congregation is assembled together (which also shall be admonished to appear diligently and to
call upon God), be called and set before the congregation, and so publicly with prayer from the
pulpit by the Pastor be commended to the whole Congregation and set into this high office […]
even as we see that the apostles ordained the Elders and commended them to the congregation,
Acts 14; likewise also the Deacons, Acts 6.

**Solms-Laubach (1603; variant of 1594)** binds its elders by an **oath**, "so help us God and
his only Son", to watch over all offence and idolatry. They are to admonish offenders and, if
admonition fails, report them to the proper authority. **Solms-Laubach, *Kirchenzuchtordnung*,
1603, "Eyd der eltesten"** (Sehling 9, p. 338):

<!-- doc 2311 -->
> Wir schweren und geloben, daß nach ausweisung unsers hohen ampts wir fleißig uffsicht haben
> wollen uff alles ergerlich wesen in der kirchen, alle abgötterei, gotteslesterung,
> leichtfertigkeit und anderß, so wieder die ehre Gottes und daß h. evangelium streittet, das
> wir dieselbige, so viel uns möglich, verhindern und die ubertretter, wie immer die gelegenheit
> sein mag, zu einem bessern leben ermahnen wollen, da aber solch ermahnung nicht fruchten
> wolte, daß ohne neidt, haß, freundschafft, feindschafft solch unbußfertig wesen wir an
> geburende ort gelangen und von dannen hulf, rath und beistand erwarten wollen […] so wahr uns
> Gott helf und sein einiger sohn.

We swear and vow that, according to the tenor of our high office, we will have diligent
oversight upon all offensive dealing in the church, all idolatry, blasphemy, levity and other
things which strive against the honour of God and the holy gospel; that we will hinder the same
so far as is possible for us, and admonish the transgressors, howsoever the occasion may be, to
a better life; but if such admonition will not profit, that without envy, hatred, friendship
[or] enmity we will bring such impenitent dealing to the proper place, and from thence await
help, counsel and support […] so help us God and his only Son.

Similar sworn **censors** are found in the **Wild- and Rhinegraviate** (1603). They are chosen
by the pastor and the old censors and confirmed "under oath" by the superintendent and officials
(Sehling 19/2, p. 600).

**Nassau-Dillenburg (1582)** counts the seniors as the third of the four offices. They are
elected by the presbytery and confirmed by the classis (§5.3).

### 6.4 The Bohemian Brethren: lay presbyters and *presbyterae*

The Bohemian Brethren have lay *presbyteri politici*. These are "political presbyters" or
"censors of manners", who oversee the life of the congregation and sit in a place of honour in
church. The men of the congregation elect them by free vote after a visitor's admonition. They
bind themselves "by voice and by hand given" to the bishops, the pastor and the church. The same
is done for the **women elders** (*presbyterae*), with only women present. There is **no laying
on of hands**. **Bohemian Brethren, *Ordo ecclesiasticus*, 1576, "Presbyterorum politicorum
ordinatio"** (Sehling 4, p. 289):

<!-- doc 1846 -->
> Presbyterorum ordinatio, quum opus est, hoc ritu peragitur. Iubentur ante concionem
> vespertinam mature se sistere omnes viri, ibique praemissa a visitatoribus admonitione,
> eligunt liberis votis quos munere illo dignos existimant. Pluribus itaque votis designati,
> vespertina concione finita a visitatore evocantur, illisque officia muneris huius (toto
> auscultante coetu) praeleguntur; illi vero voce et stipulata manu antistitibus unitatis,
> pastori, ecclesiaeque suae, fidem et diligentiam obstringunt. Atque ut in templo quoque
> inspectorum munere fungi possint, peculiari etiam sessionis loco (ad commodiorem populi
> intuitum) cohonestantur. Similiter proceditur cum eliguntur presbyterae; excepto, quod solo
> foemineo sexu praesente res peragitur.

The ordination of presbyters, when there is need, is performed with this rite. All the men are
bidden to present themselves early before the evening sermon; and there, an admonition having
first been given by the visitors, they choose by free votes those whom they judge worthy of that
office. Those therefore which are designated by the most votes are called forth by the visitor
when the evening sermon is ended, and the duties of this office are read to them (the whole
assembly hearkening); and they bind their faith and diligence by voice and by hand given to the
bishops of the unity, to the pastor, and to their church. And that they may also discharge the
office of overseers in the church, they are honoured with a peculiar place of sitting (for the
more convenient view of the people). The like is done when presbyteresses are chosen, except
that the matter is performed with the female sex alone present.

Like the acolytes (§3.4), these presbyters are said to be "ordained", but by a handfast vow
alone. This sets them clearly apart from the deacons and ministers, on whom hands are laid
(§5.3).

### 6.5 The Lutheran orders: churchwardens and clerical seniors

**Churchwardens.** In the Lutheran orders the lay officers of the parish are the churchwardens,
called *Kirchenväter*, *Kirchgeschworene*, *Kastenherren* or *Kirchenpfleger*. They administer
church goods, buildings and alms, often with the council. They are not given spiritual
jurisdiction. Memmingen's discipline order (1532) does give its *Kirchenpfleger* a wide advisory
role over worship, schools, alms and the ban. But it expressly denies them any temporal
jurisdiction, "so that two temporal authorities arise not again in our city". **Memmingen,
*Zuchtordnung*, 1532** (Sehling 12, p. 248):

<!-- doc 365 -->
> Dann wir solchen kirchenpflegern thain zeitlich jurisdiction in unser stat geben noch furter
> zu geben gedenken, auf daz nit abermals zwo zeitlich oberkaiten in unser statt aufkomen

For we give such churchwardens no temporal jurisdiction in our city, neither think to give [any]
hereafter, to the end that two temporal authorities arise not again in our city […]

Dinkelsbühl's twelve *Kirchenpfleger* fill their own vacancies "together with the pastor, who
shall bring the Bible or the church order with him" (*Kirchenpflegeordnung*, 1574; Sehling 12,
p. 131). In Augsburg the *Vergleichsartikel* of 1591 provide six *Kirchenpfleger* of the
Augsburg Confession: three appointed by the council and three chosen by the evangelical
citizens. They take part in the examination of new ministers (Sehling 12, pp. 109–110). The
orders give no rite of installation for any of these wardens.

**Clerical seniors.** Where Lutheran orders speak of *seniores*, they usually mean **senior
clergy**:
- In Brandenburg-Ansbach each dean, with the counsel of the whole chapter, chooses "two seniors,
  the fittest among all his subordinates", as assessors and definitors. They administer the
  superintendency in a vacancy and join him in visitation (*Kapitelsordnung*, 1565/78; Sehling
  11, pp. 347, 350).
- Pomerania and the Silesian duchies (Liegnitz) use *seniores* in the same sense. The Pomeranian
  synod of 1574 bids the younger ministers honour the *seniores*, and has the superintendent
  punish unruly ministers with the counsel of the provosts and seniors (Sehling 4, p. 489).

**"Elders" meaning the pastors.** The ordination rite of the Erbach church order (1560) reads
Titus 1:5 of the pastors themselves. Christ commanded his apostles "to choose and set elders in
all his congregations, which shall preach his gospel pure and unfalsified, dispense the most
worthy sacraments, and diligently provide and perform all cure of souls and shepherd's service".
**Erbach, *Erbacher Kirchenordnung*, 1560, ordination rite** (Sehling 9, p. 446):

<!-- doc 2316 -->
> auch den Seinigen durch seine Apostel befohlen, bey allen seinen Gemeinen Eltesten zu erwehlen
> und zu setzen, die sein Evangelion Rein und unverfälscht predigen, die Hochwürdige Sacramenta
> dispensiren und alle Seelsorg und Hirtendienst fleissig versehen und verrichten sollen.

[…] and also commanded his own through his Apostles to choose and set Elders in all his
congregations, which shall preach his Gospel pure and unfalsified, dispense the most worthy
Sacraments, and diligently provide and perform all cure of souls and shepherd's service.

**No ruling elders in the Lutheran orders.** In short, a Lutheran order that speaks of "elders"
is speaking of the pastors, of the senior pastors, or of the churchwardens. The **ruling elder**
with a share in the power of the keys belongs to the Bucerian, Hessian and Reformed orders.

## 7. Ordained or not: how each office was installed

**Words that do not settle the question.** "Ordain" (*ordiniren*, *ordinatio*) is not decisive
in these texts:
- Lippe uses it of appointing deacons of the poor.
- The Bohemian Brethren use it of a handfast vow.
- Hesse uses it of a full rite with laying on of hands.

- **hands**: laying on of hands, with prayer;

- **vow**: a handfast vow (*Handgelübde*, *manu stipulata*) or an oath;
- **none**: appointment only.

**Summary.** Laying on of hands was used for four groups:
- ministers of the word, everywhere;
- deacons of the poor, in Hesse 1566 and in Micron;
- lay elders, in Hesse 1566, Micron and Kurpfalz 1592;
- the Bohemian Brethren's clerical deacons.

It was not used for:
- Lutheran deacons of the poor or churchwardens;
- the Reformed elders of the Kurpfalz *Presbyterordnung*, Hanau, Veldenz or Solms;
- any minor grade, including the Bohemian acolytes.

**Answer to the question asked.** Were ruling elders ordained? Yes, in Hesse 1566, the
London-Norden order of Micron and the Palatinate form of 1592. Elsewhere they were installed
with prayer, a handfast vow or an oath, and none of these used the language of ordination except
Veldenz, which uses it of the apostles.
