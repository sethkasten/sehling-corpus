# Art, Architecture, Paraments and Vestments in the Sehling Church Orders

This guide covers the visible furnishing of worship in the church orders of the sixteenth
century printed in Emil Sehling's *Die evangelischen Kirchenordnungen des XVI. Jahrhunderts*. It
asks:

- **Art.** Which images did the orders keep, and which did they remove? How were they to be
  removed? What became of the crucifix, the altar panels, carved and painted figures, banners
  and processional crosses? Were new pictures ever ordered?
- **Architecture and furnishings.** How many altars were kept, and where did the minister stand
  at them? Were altars replaced by tables? What became of the lights on the altar, the sacrament
  house, the font, the choir screen? What do the orders say of the pulpit, pews, galleries and
  windows?
- **Paraments.** What cloths covered the altar, pulpit and font? What became of the corporal and
  pall, the cloth held under the communicants, the chalices and the stock of old vestments?
- **Seasons and colours.** Did vestments or paraments change colour with the church year? Were
  there more or fewer candles on feasts or in certain seasons? What became of the Lenten veil,
  the Easter sepulchre and paschal candle, Candlemas candles, and seasonal greenery?
- **Vestments.** Was the Mass vestment (chasuble) kept, and with or without alb? When was the
  cope worn, and when only the surplice? Which orders abolished all three? What were ministers
  to wear outside the service?

**How the guide is laid out.**
- §1 answers each question in brief, and §2 sets out the scope, the search terms and the
  negative findings.
- §3 sets out the principle under which every one of these things was handled: they are
  *adiaphora*, things indifferent, kept or dropped for order, for the weak, or against
  superstition.
- §4 covers images and crucifixes: the Lutheran distinction between idols and histories, orderly
  removal, new pictures, the Reformed clearances, the crucifix, and banners and processional
  crosses.
- §5 covers the building and its furnishings: altars, the minister's position, tables in place
  of altars, lights, the sacrament house, the font, and the pulpit, screens, pews, galleries and
  windows.
- §6 covers paraments, linens and vessels, colours and the seasons, and the paten, host box and
  bread dish.
- §7 covers vestments: the Mass vestment kept, the cope, the vestments abolished, and the
  surplice.
- §8 covers clerical dress outside the service.
- §9 is a table by order, and §10 a concordance of every order quoted.

**Conventions**

- **Quotations.** Every quotation is Sehling's text as it stands in the `eko.db` database built
  from the digitized edition.
  - Footnote numbers, sigla, folio markers and interleaved apparatus are left out.
  - Sehling's own supplied words in square brackets are kept, as are "[!]" and his other signs.
  - Spelling and punctuation are as printed. A few plain OCR errors are corrected.
  - "[…]" marks an omission.
  - Every quotation has been checked against the database text, allowing for OCR noise: at least
    90 per cent of its words must be found there in order. Its page has been checked against the
    edition's page markers.
  - Each quotation is preceded by an HTML comment, invisible when rendered, that names the
    `eko.db` document it was checked against.
- **Translations.** The English is formal-equivalence, in the idiom of the Authorized Version:
  - Word order and clause structure are kept where English allows.
  - Stock terms are rendered the same way throughout. *Bild* and *imago* are "image"; *Gemälde*
    and *pictura* are "painting"; *Tafel* is "panel" (the painted altarpiece). *Chorrock*,
    *Chorhemd* and *superpellicium* are "surplice". *Meßgewand*, *Casel* and *casula* are "Mass
    vestment" or "chasuble"; *Chorkappe*, *Chormantel* and *cappa* are "cope"; *Alba* is "alb";
    *Ornat* and *ornatus* are "ornaments". *Taufstein* is "font"; *Predigtstuhl* and *Kanzel*
    are "pulpit"; *Stuhl* in a church is "pew"; *Borkirche* is "gallery"; *Sakramentshäuslein*
    is "sacrament house"; *Tüchlein* is "little cloth".
  - Words the translation supplies are in square brackets.
- **Citations.** Citations are in the form "Sehling volume, page".
  - The half-volumes of the edition are written 6/1, 6/2, 7/1, 7/2.1, 7/2.2, 17/1, 17/2, 19/1,
    19/2, 20/1 and 20/2, following the digitized volumes.
  - Each order is named by place, title and date as they appear in Sehling's running heads.
    These sometimes differ from the database's record headings. Where a passage comes from the
    editor's introduction or notes rather than from an order, this is said.
- **Traditions.** Most of the orders cited are Lutheran, and they are not marked. Every order of
  another tradition is marked where it is cited, by its name in brackets after the order, as
  "Kurpfalz 1563 (Reformed)". The traditions follow the inventory in
  [`CHURCH_ORDERS_GUIDE.md`](CHURCH_ORDERS_GUIDE.md), §3:
  - **Reformed**: the Swiss, Calvinist and Heidelberg churches, among them the German
    territories that turned Reformed after 1560 and the French and Dutch stranger churches;
  - **Moderate Reformed**: the Bucerian and related orders that stood between the Lutheran and
    the Swiss Reformed: Bucer's Strasbourg and the Upper German cities before the Interim,
    Philip of Hesse's church to 1566, Hermann von Wied's Cologne order, and Colmar after 1578.
  - The mark follows the order cited, not the territory, since a territory could change its
    tradition: Kurpfalz is Lutheran in 1546 and 1556 and Reformed from 1563, Pfalz-Zweibrücken
    Lutheran until 1588, and Pfalz-Simmern Lutheran until it fell to the Electoral Palatinate in
    1598.
  - Orders made under or for the Augsburg Interim of 1548 are marked as Interim orders
    (`CHURCH_ORDERS_GUIDE.md`, §4).
  - Some Lutheran orders took the Reformed line on images, vestments or vessels (Lichtenberg
    1579, Sponheim 1590/91, the Upper Palatine visitation of 1579, Nassau-Weilburg 1617). They
    are Lutheran orders, and the text says where they agree with the Reformed.

## 1. Summary of findings

**1. Everything is "indifferent", and that decides everything (§3).** Vestments, cloths,
vessels, lights, images and altars are *adiaphora*: they "give and take nothing to faith and
conscience" (Brandenburg-Nürnberg 1533). The orders reach three different conclusions from that
one principle:
- *Keep them* for good order, for uniformity with Wittenberg and the neighbouring churches, and
  for the weak: Hannover 1536, Thüngen 1564, Hildesheim 1544, Magdeburg 1583.
- *Drop them* when they foster superstition or blur the difference from the Mass: Regensburg
  1554 and 1567, Transylvania 1557.
- *Forbid them* as contrary to God's word, which is the Reformed position, not an indifferent
  one: the Heidelberg Catechism, Kurpfalz 1565, the Casimirian mandate of 1587 (all Reformed).

**2. Images: idols out, histories in (§4).** The Lutheran orders remove only images that were
prayed to or had lights burned before them, and keep scriptural histories as "the laity's Bible"
(Brunswick 1528, Thüngen 1564, Oldenburg 1573, Regensburg 1567). Removal is to be orderly and by
authority, never by the crowd:
- Strasbourg 1525 (Moderate Reformed) forbids citizens to touch anything.
- Mecklenburg 1552 nails the images to the walls.
- The Upper Palatinate in 1557 removes them by night, takes the carved figures out of mixed
  panels and blacks over the painted ones.

Kurland 1570 is the only order found that commissions new paintings: Old and New Testament
histories, "especially of the resurrection and of the Last Judgement", for the native peasantry.
Ulm 1531 (Moderate Reformed), the Heidelberg Catechism (Q. 98) and Kurpfalz 1565 (Reformed), and
two Lutheran orders that follow them here, Lichtenberg 1579 and Sponheim 1590/91, reject the
"laity's books" argument and clear images and crucifixes alike. The Prussian order of 1568
answers them by name.

**3. The crucifix is the image the Lutheran orders most often name as kept (§4.5).** Hannover
1536, Grubenhagen 1544 (where it "shall stand in the midst of the church") and Anhalt 1562 keep
it. It is carried before funerals at Freiberg 1538 and Wolfstein 1574, stands at the deathbed
(Dietrich 1545) and on the Mansfeld oath table (1586). The Reformed removed it, and the Lutheran
Nassau-Weilburg in 1617 stopped carrying it before corpses. Banners, poles and processional
crosses went with the processions (§4.6).

**4. One altar; the minister before it, facing the people (§5.1–5.3).** Side altars were
removed, leaving three (Wittenberg 1522) or one (Mecklenburg 1552, Upper Palatinate 1557,
Kurland 1570). Colditz 1529 and Gotha 1555 order altars arranged so that the minister faces the
people. The Wittenberg theologians condemned standing *behind* the table as the disorder of
Karlstadt and Müntzer (Radical Reformation) (Wittenbergische Reformation 1545, Allstedt 1533).
Strasbourg 1537 (Moderate Reformed) set up an altar-table facing the people, and the Reformed
orders put tables in place of altars (Siegen 1581, Casimir 1587, Anhalt 1596, Simmern 1598). In
Transylvania the synod of 1565 ordered altars pulled down by "turbulent men" to be restored.

**5. Two candles, no sacrament house (§5.4–5.5).** The usual Lutheran altar has two lights
during the Supper (Allstedt 1533, Calenberg 1544/45, Kurland 1570). Hildesheim 1544 explains
them by the night of the Last Supper. Kurpfalz 1546 (Lutheran) keeps a single lamp, and
Regensburg and Anhalt 1596 (Reformed) drop the lights. No order varies the number of lights by
feast or season. Where the lights vary, they vary with communion: lit when there are
communicants (Marggrabowa and Sensburg 1581), and at Suhl 1562 not lit at all. The endowments
for the eternal light went to the common chest (Württemberg 1536), and the blessing of Candlemas
and Easter candles was abolished (Hesse 1526, Moderate Reformed; Pfalz-Neuburg 1543, Lüneburg
convents 1555). Since the Sacrament was not reserved, sacrament houses and ambries were emptied
(Hesse 1526, Moderate Reformed; Schönburg 1542, Hohenlohe 1553) or removed (Pfalz-Neuburg 1560,
Nassau-Weilburg 1576, Magdeburg 1583, Kurpfalz 1565 (Reformed)). No order uses "tabernacle" for
a furnishing.

**6. The font is moved into sight (§5.6).** The customary font is kept (Prussia 1525), railed
and adorned (Nördlingen 1544), and moved before the choir, raised a step or two (Lüneburg 1564),
or "out of the corners before the face of the church" (Bentheim 1588, Reformed). Bremen 1534
warms the water in winter. The Reformed abolish the font (Kurpfalz 1565, Casimir 1587, Simmern
1598, all Reformed).

**7. Pulpit, pews and galleries (§5.7).** The pulpit appears among the things to be covered and
kept. The choir screen is torn down at Zweibrücken 1558 so that the nuns can be seen at sermon.
Saxony 1580 complains that pews block the view of pulpit and altar and forbids new pews without
leave. Hadersleben 1571 rebuilds its pews to clear the aisle and separate men and women, and
Thorn and Stargard have pew orders. Schoolboys sing from the north gallery at Wittenberg 1533.
Guilds that once kept altars are to keep windows instead (Neumark 1540, Salzwedel 1579).

**8. Paraments are covers, not a system (§6).** No order names an antependium, dossal or pulpit
fall, or prescribes colours. What the orders ask for:
- clean white linen on altar and font (Anhalt 1562, Wolfenbüttel 1569);
- covers made from old vestments for altar, pulpit and font, with a pall (Henneberg 1556), and
  the visitors are to ask whether altar, font, pulpit and desk are "covered as is due"
  (Leiningen 1609);
- a corporal and pall in the sick communion (Brandenburg 1540);
- the silk cloth held under communicants by boys, deacons or honest men (Hohenlohe 1553,
  Regensburg 1553, Speyer 1557, Thorn 1575). The Reformed Amberg mandate of 1567 abolished it,
  and so later did two Lutheran orders, the Upper Palatine visitation of 1579 under the Lutheran
  Elector Ludwig VI and Nassau-Weilburg 1617.

Surplus vestments were sold or cut up for the hospital's bed sheets (Schmiedeberg 1528), or made
into altar cloths (Sponheim 1590/91). The Reformed exchanged chalices for beakers (Siegen 1581,
Casimir 1587, Zweibrücken 1595, all Reformed).

The paten was kept as an ordinary vessel (Hannover 1536) and counted in the inventories (§6.7).
The commonest rubric puts it, with as many hosts as communicants, into the minister's hand at
the Words of Institution (Livonia 1533, the Palatinate 1546, Danzig 1557, Henneberg 1582). At
the sick communion it was held under the communicant's chin (Brandenburg 1540). Havelberg (1558)
forbade elevating it in the chalice's stead. Hohenlohe (1596) and Nassau-Weilburg (1617), both
Lutheran, stopped showing or handling it at the Verba. The Reformed melted or sold patens with
the chalices (Zweibrücken 1595, Wittgenstein 1605, both Reformed) and passed the bread in a
dish.

**9. Vestments: chasuble, cope, surplice (§7).**
- *The Mass vestment* (alb and chasuble) was kept, and in Saxony restored, for the Supper with
  communicants: Leisnig 1529, Allstedt 1533, Grubenhagen 1544, Calenberg 1544/45, Thüngen 1564,
  Wolfenbüttel 1569, Thorn 1575. Riga 1530 and Kurland 1570 made a plain surplice the norm, with
  cope or chasuble on feasts.
- *The cope* replaced the chasuble when there were no communicants, so that the people should
  not think the private Mass had returned (Brandenburg 1540, Pfalz-Neuburg 1543). Brenz allowed
  it to the deacon at Schwäbisch Hall in 1527, but no chasuble, "neither man's nor woman's
  clothing".
- *Abolished:* the dalmatic and tunicle at Homberg 1526 (Moderate Reformed); the "Levite and
  Mass vestments" at Regensburg in 1554, with a reasoned statement read from the pulpit; the
  Mass vestment at Sayn 1575 and Anhalt 1596 (Reformed). Transylvania 1557 wants candles and
  *infulae* laid aside and a white linen garment instead.
- *The surplice* is the common minimum and the vestment most often named: Prussia 1543, Hesse
  1532 (Moderate Reformed), Württemberg 1535 and 1553, the *Cellische Ordnungen* of 1545,
  Regensburg 1554 and 1567, Wittgenstein 1563, Anhalt 1568. Ernestine Saxony and Coburg in 1554
  limit it to the altar. The Palatine Calvinists (Reformed) and the Lutheran Sayn 1582 abolish
  it. Wild- und Rheingrafschaft 1603 introduces a black gown with sleeves, but keeps surplices
  where they are used.

**10. Outside the service (§8).** Ministers are to wear long, honest, priestly coats, not short,
slashed or coloured clothes, so that they may be known as ministers (Grubenhagen 1544, the
*Cellische Ordnungen* of 1545, Regensburg 1567, Wild- und Rheingrafschaft 1603).

**11. Colours and the seasons (§6.6).** No order prescribes liturgical colours or a seasonal
change of vestments or paraments. Coloured vestments nonetheless stayed in use. In 1578 the
Fürstenberg church had a red-and-green velvet chasuble newly given by the Duke of Mecklenburg, a
red velvet chasuble and red damask deacon's coats. At Sorau the boys who held the communion
cloths wore red smocks and green garlands. The Lenten veiling of images is named only to be
ended: Luther rejects it, Mülhausen 1528 (Reformed) drops the unveiled crucifix shown on Good
Friday, and at Regensburg a covered altarpiece was removed all the same. The Lenten veil, the
Easter sepulchre, the paschal candle and the image plays of the feasts were all abolished
(Luther 1526, Pfalz-Neuburg 1543, Hohenlohe 1553). Leiningen-Westerburg 1566 forbids May
branches and decking the church with flowers as heathen customs; only at Hof (1592) did
neighbours strew grass and set up May branches for the dedication procession. There is no Advent
wreath or Christmas tree.

**12. What is absent (§2.3).** There is no evangelical mitre or crosier, and no preaching bands,
cassock, rochet, biretta or episcopal insignia. Liturgical colours are never prescribed. The
orders do not mention altar rails, credence tables, altars of repose, mosaics or stained-glass
programmes, and say nothing of transepts, vaults, buttresses or undercrofts.

## 2. Scope, sources and method

### 2.1 Sources

The guide draws on the full text of the Sehling edition in `eko.db`: the orders themselves, the
visitation articles and decrees printed with them, and, where said, the editor's introductions
and notes. It covers Lutheran, Upper German and Reformed orders alike, since the edition prints
all three, and the Reformed clearances explain much of what the later Lutheran orders defend.

### 2.2 Searches

The corpus was searched with regular expressions for some thirty families of terms, in High and
Low German and Latin spellings. Each hit was read in a window of context. The families, with the
number of document-pages in which each was found, were:

- **Art:** *Bild*, *Bildnis*, *imago*, *statua* (752); *Gemälde*, *Tafel*, *Maler* (810);
  *Crucifix* (69); *Kreuz*, *crux* (1,873, most of them the "cross" of suffering); *Fahne*,
  *Banner*, *vexillum* (80); *Fenster*, *Glasfenster* (93); *Reliquien*, *Heiltum* (227).
- **Building and furnishings:** *Altar* (1,951); *Kanzel*, *Predigtstuhl* (1,266); *Taufstein*,
  *döpe*, *baptisterium* (562); *Lettner*, *Gitter*, *Schranken* (68); *Sakristei*, *Gerbkammer*
  (57); *Gestühl*, *Kirchenstuhl*, *Bänke*, *Empore*, *Borkirche* (174); *Pult*, *Lesepult*
  (71); *Kerze*, *Leuchter*, *Ampel*, *Lampe* (222); *Beichtstuhl* (17); *Sakramentshäuslein*,
  *Monstranz*, *ciborium* (222); *Kirchturm* (118); *Kirchhof*, *Gottesacker* (537).
- **Paraments and vessels:** *Altartuch*, *Corporal*, *Palla*, *Velum*, *Vorhang*, *Zwehle*,
  *Handtuch*, *lavabo*, *purificatorium*, *bursa* (244); *Ornat*, *Ornament*, *Zierat*,
  *Kleinod* (331); *Kelch*, *calix*, *Patene* (1,112).
- **Vestments:** *Chorrock*, *Chorhemd*, *superpellicium*, *Röckel*, *Rochet* (262);
  *Meßgewand*, *Casel*, *casula*, *planeta* (196); *Alba*, *Humerale*, *Amictus*, *Stola*,
  *Manipel*, *Cingulum* (134); *Chorkappe*, *Chormantel*, *cappa*, *pluviale* (188); *Infel*,
  *Mitra*, *Bischofsstab*, *Krummstab*, *baculus* (110); *Dalmatik*, *Levitenrock*, *tunicella*
  (27); *Priesterkleid*, *Kirchenkleid*, *lange Röcke*, *Schaube*, *Talar*, *Barett* (155).

Further searches were made for the English terms of the request that have German equivalents:
mosaic, narthex (*Vorhalle*, *Vorkirche*), transept (*Querschiff*), vault (*Gewölbe*), buttress,
undercroft (*Krypta*, *Gruft*), credence (*Kredenz*), antependium, dossal, altar rail and
kneeling bench (*Altargitter*, *Kniebank*), liturgical colours, preaching bands (*Beffchen*,
*Kragen*), mitre and crosier.

A second pass searched for reservation and the seasons: tabernacle (*Tabernakel*), ambry
(*armarium*, *Almer*, *Wandschrank*, *Sakramentsnische*); colour words (*rot*, *grün*,
*schwarz*, *violett*, *Farbe*) near vestments and cloths; the Lenten veil (*Hungertuch*,
*Fastentuch*), the covering or veiling of images, crosses and altars (*decken*, *verdecken*,
*verhüllen*, *velare*, *tegere*); the Easter sepulchre (*heiliges Grab*) and paschal candle
(*Osterkerze*, *Osterstock*); Candlemas candles (*Lichtmeß*, *Kerzenweihe*); *Tenebrae* and
*Finstermette*; the eternal light (*ewiges Licht*); greenery and flowers (*Maien*, *Kirchen
blümen*, *Gras streuen*, *Kranz*, *Tannenbaum*, *Christbaum*, *Krippe*); and lights named
together with feasts, Sundays and weekdays.

### 2.3 What was not found

The searches came back empty, or with only incidental hits, for most of the architectural and
episcopal vocabulary of the request:

- **Architecture.** No order regulates the nave, transept, vault, buttress, arch, steeple or
  undercroft. *Vorkirche* occurs, but in the sense of a gallery, where the boys are not to be
  suffered during the catechism (Ysenburg-Birstein 1588), or as one item in a list of parts to
  be kept in repair. *Querschiff* occurs once, in an editor's note. Towers, roofs, windows and
  churchyard walls appear only as fabric to be maintained, and bells are treated in
  [`ORGAN_INSTRUMENTS_BELLS_GUIDE.md`](ORGAN_INSTRUMENTS_BELLS_GUIDE.md).
- **Art.** No order mentions mosaics, or regulates what stained glass may show. Statues appear
  only as "carved images" to be removed or left. No order commissions a sculpture. The only new
  pictures ordered are Kurland's (§4.3).
- **Furnishings.** There is no altar rail, no credence table and no altar of repose. *Kredenz*
  occurs only as a letter of credence. Kneeling benches for communicants appear at Hohenlohe
  (§6.3) and, by the editor's report, at Hoya 1581. The rood screen appears only as the *Gitter*
  or *Schranken* to be removed (§5.7).
- **Paraments.** No antependium, frontal, superfrontal, dossal, reredos, runner, pulpit fall or
  lectern fall is named; the orders speak only of covers (*Decken*) for altar, pulpit, font and
  desk (§6.1). The corporal and pall appear once (§6.2). No order regulates the purificator,
  burse, chalice veil or lavabo towel. Font towels appear once (§5.6).
- **Seasons.** No order prescribes liturgical colours or seasonal changes; the colour sequence
  appears only in the editor's notes, and coloured vestments only in an inventory and a later
  report (§6.6). No order varies the number of candles by feast or season. *Tabernakel* never
  names a furnishing. No order prescribes veiling images or crosses in Lent or Passiontide; the
  veiling is mentioned only to be abolished (§6.6). There is no Advent wreath, Christmas tree,
  Easter garden or crib set up in church, and the Tenebrae hearse is never mentioned.
- **Vestments.** No order provides an evangelical mitre or crosier. The *Hirtenstab* occurs only
  as a metaphor for the preaching office, and Transylvanian *infulae* most likely means
  chasubles (§7.3). There are no preaching bands, cassock, cotta, cassock-alb, rochet, biretta,
  zucchetto, galero, humeral veil, pallium, rationale, gloves, tippet or hood. The amice and
  cincture are never named. The stole and maniple appear only to be left free (Hildesheim 1544)
  or dropped (Regensburg 1567), and the dalmatic and tunicle only to be forbidden (Hesse 1526,
  Moderate Reformed; Regensburg 1554). The black gown appears once, in 1603 (§7.4).

### 2.4 Method

Quoted passages were pulled from the database in full and checked as described in the
conventions. Each page was checked against Sehling's running heads, which often correct the
database's record headings. The most important corrections:
- the Bremen order is the *Kirchenordnung* of 1534, not the poor-chest order of 1525;
- the Mansfeld oath form belongs with the consistorial order of 1586;
- the Prussian surplice decree is the *Befehl* of 1 February 1543, and the image passage is from
  the *Kirchenordnung und Ceremonien* of 1568;
- the Sponheim passages are from the Hintere Grafschaft Sponheim (1590/91 and 1607), not
  Zweibrücken;
- the Palatine passage on images, altars and fonts is the Elector's *Befehl an die Amtleute* of
  3 October 1565, printed after the Heidelberg Catechism.

The guide quotes nearly a hundred passages from some eighty orders. It does not claim to list
every mention of an altar or chalice, which number in the thousands, but every order found that
says something distinctive on the questions asked is quoted or cited.

## 3. Things indifferent

### 3.1 The principle

**Brandenburg-Nürnberg 1533.** Every order that deals with these matters begins from the same
place: vestments, cloths, vessels and lights are outward things, neither commanded nor forbidden
by God. The Brandenburg-Nürnberg order of 1533 says it in a sentence. **Brandenburg-Nürnberg,
*Kirchenordnung*, 1533** (Sehling 11, p. 183):

<!-- doc 270 -->
> Die andern zusetz eußerlicher, leiblicher ding als meßgewand, altardeck, silbrein und güldein
> gefeß, lichter etc. sein aller ding frei, geben und nemen dem glauben und gewissen nichts.

The other additions of outward, bodily things, as Mass vestment, altar covering, silver and
golden vessels, lights, etc., are altogether free; they give and take nothing to faith and
conscience.

**Hannover 1536.** Freedom did not mean abolition. The Hannover order lists what it keeps "in
the freedom of the Spirit", for good order and for the weak: the priest's vestment at the altar,
the customary vessels, lights on the altar, the crucifix and honest images, the font and the
altar. **Hannover, *Kirchenordnung*, 1536** (Sehling 6/2, p. 1009):

<!-- doc 2051 -->
> Aus der ursach wöllen wir auch umb guter ordnung willen und allen schwachen zu dienst noch
> etliche gebreuchliche cerimonien behalten, doch in der freiheit des Geistes, als gewönliche
> priesterkleidung bey dem altar, gewönliche gefes, so zu handlung der heiligen sacrament bisher
> sind gebraucht worden, liechter auf dem altar, crucifix und ehrliche bildnis, dadurch kein
> abgötterey getrieben wird, taufstein, altar, christliche gesenge, deudsch und latin, nach
> gelegenheit der zeit

For that cause will we also, for good order's sake and for the service of all the weak, still
keep certain customary ceremonies, yet in the freedom of the Spirit: as the customary priestly
clothing at the altar, the customary vessels which have been used hitherto for the handling of
the holy sacraments, lights upon the altar, crucifix and honest images whereby no idolatry is
practised, font, altar, Christian songs, German and Latin, according to the occasion of the
time.

### 3.2 Keep what each church has

**Magdeburg 1583.** The rule most often given to visitors was to leave each church as it was.
The Magdeburg instruction of 1583 lets Mass vestments, surplices, lights, altar cloths and the
language of singing stand "as each church hath it in use", so that no unrest arise from
changing, abolishing or setting up. Superstitious things are another matter: idolatrous images,
the sacrament house and the monstrance are to go. **Magdeburg, *Instruktion zur Visitation*, 25
May 1583** (Sehling 2, p. 424):

<!-- doc 1256 -->
> messgewand, chorröck, lichter ufs altar, altartucher, singen lateinisch oder teutsch soll man
> pleiben lassen, zu halten oder nicht, wie es ein jede kirche im gebrauch ist, das hiermit
> durch enderung, abthuung oder aufrichtung kein unruhe in kirchen angerichtet werde nach dem
> spruch Christi: Regnum dei non venit cum observatione. Aber ergerliche abergleubige
> ceremonien, ob die wol alt weren, soll man abschaffen, als abgottische bilder, da etwas ein
> cultus were angewandt worden, sacrament heuselein, monstranz

Mass vestment, surplices, lights upon the altar, altar cloths, singing in Latin or in German,
one shall let remain, to keep or not, as each church hath it in use, that hereby through
changing, putting away or setting up no unrest be stirred up in churches, according to the
saying of Christ: *Regnum Dei non venit cum observatione*. But offensive, superstitious
ceremonies, though they were old, shall be abolished, as idolatrous images to which some worship
hath been applied, sacrament houses, monstrance.

### 3.3 Kept for peace and for the weak

**Thüngen 1564.** The Franconian lordship of Thüngen keeps Mass vestment and burning candles
because they were in use "in Dr Martin's lifetime" at Wittenberg, in Meissen, Brandenburg and
the chief imperial cities, and still are. The motive is peace and the weak, not any holiness in
the things. **Thüngen, *Kirchenordnung*, 1564** (Sehling 11, pp. 736–737):

<!-- doc 326 -->
> 4. Vom meßgewand und brennenden lichten oder kerzen. Dieweil das meßgewand, wie mans nennet,
> und die brennenden kerzen bei d. Martini leben gebräuchlich gewest in der wittenbergischen,
> meichsnischen, brandenburgischen und der furnembsten reichsstedten kirchen, auch in solchen
> bis auf diesen tag beides gehalten wird und bei uns des mehrerteils solche beide stücke
> ungeendert gelassen, wollen wir ferner um friedens und einigkeit willen und von wegen der
> schwachen in unsern kirchen also behalten - nicht in meinung, das dadurch dem hochwürdigen
> sacrament sondere wirdigkeit oder heiligkeit widerfahre oder das es notwendig also sein müsse
> oder auch das es den papisten zu gefallen oder sonst jemand zum verdrieß geschehe, sondern
> allein um friedens willen und zu verhütung der schwachen ergernis.

4. Of the Mass vestment and burning lights or candles. Forasmuch as the Mass vestment, as men
   call it, and the burning candles were in use in Dr Martin's lifetime in the churches of
   Wittenberg, of Meissen, of Brandenburg and of the chiefest imperial cities, and both are kept
   in the same unto this day, and with us for the most part both these pieces have been left
   unchanged, we will further keep them so in our churches for peace and unity's sake and
   because of the weak: not in the meaning that thereby any special worthiness or holiness
   befall the most worthy sacrament, or that it must needs be so, or yet that it be done to
   please the papists or to vex any other, but only for peace's sake and to prevent the offence
   of the weak.

**Hildesheim 1544.** The Hildesheim order keeps vestments and lights at the Sacrament on holy
days "not as needful before God, but only as seemly before men", and only until all the
evangelical churches agree to drop them. The stole and maniple it leaves to the preachers'
freedom. **Hildesheim, *Christlike kerckenordeninge*, 1544** (Sehling 7/2.1, p. 852):

<!-- doc 2136 -->
> Dewile nu vele stede unde lande, de dat evangelion angenomen hebben, solcke kleder unde lichte
> allene des hilligen dages by dem sacramente noch beholden, nicht nödtlick vor Godde, sunder
> ailene eerlick vor den minschen, so wille wy se ock beholden, so lange, dat se alle andere, de
> dat evangelion hebben angenomen, eindrechtichlick affdon. Alse denne so wille wy ock nicht
> sünderlick syn, willen unse predicanten nicht gebruken der stolen unde manipelen, dar frage wy
> nichtes na, dat schal öhne fry syn ut dersülven christliken fryheit, alse gesecht.

Now whereas many cities and lands which have received the gospel still keep such clothes and
lights at the sacrament on the holy day only, not as needful before God but only as seemly
before men, so will we also keep them, so long as until all others which have received the
gospel put them away with one accord. Then will we also not be singular. If our preachers will
not use the stoles and maniples, we ask nothing after that; it shall be free to them out of the
same Christian liberty, as is said.

**Hesse 1526** (Moderate Reformed). Even the Homberg synod of 1526, which forbade new purchases
of vestments (§7.3), commended a minimum "that all things be done decently": the minister should
at least wear a surplice, candles should be lit, and the chalice should be seemly. **Hesse,
*Homberger Kirchenordnung*, 1526** (Moderate Reformed; Sehling 8, p. 45):

<!-- doc 2247 -->
> Servetur in ea ritus, quem servus Dei Martinus Lutherus ultimo germanice conscripsit, et ut
> iuxta Paulum cuncta decentius fiant, laudamus si in eius ministerio ad minus induatur
> superpellicium, incendantur cerei, et decens calix habeatur.

Let there be kept therein the rite which the servant of God Martin Luther last wrote in German;
and that, according to Paul, all things be done the more decently, we commend it if in its
ministry at the least a surplice be put on, candles be lighted, and a seemly chalice be had.

### 3.4 The limit

The same principle could cut the other way. Where a thing was used superstitiously, or where
keeping it seemed to make no difference between the evangelical service and the Mass, it ceased
to be indifferent and was to go. The Regensburg reasons of 1554 for dropping the Mass vestment
(§7.3) and the Transylvanian synod of 1557 on candles and vestments (§7.3) both argue in this
way. The Reformed orders went further and treated images, altars and fonts as forbidden in
themselves (§§4.4, 5.3).

## 4. Images and crucifixes

### 4.1 Idols and histories

**Brunswick 1528.** The Lutheran orders do not condemn images as such. They condemn worship
given to them. Bugenhagen's Brunswick order says that good books have been written showing it is
not wrong to have images, especially those in which histories may be seen. The council has
removed only those images before which people prayed and set lights; the rest stay, unless they
too become objects of worship. **Brunswick, *Kirchenordnung*, 1528** (Sehling 6/1, p. 445):

<!-- doc 1983 -->
> Van den bilden synt gude böke gescreven, dat id nicht unrecht edder unchristlick sy, bilden to
> hebben, besundergen dar me inne mach sehn historien unde geschefte. Wy bekennen frilick, dat
> wy in unsen kerken vele lögenbilden unde vele unnutte klotze hebben. Doch dat wy nicht mögen
> bildenstormere syn unde andere lüde, bekande edder frömede, sulks nicht vor ergerlick ansehn,
> hebbe wy alleyne wechgedän mit ordentliker gewalt unde overicheit de bilden, by unde vor
> welken sonderich anbedent unde afgoderye unde sonderge ere mit lichten unde luchteren
> angerichtet wärd. De anderen alle, de nicht hinderlick synt in der kerken, late wy stan

Of images good books have been written, that it is not wrong nor unchristian to have images,
especially those wherein one may see histories and deeds. We confess freely that we have in our
churches many lying images and many unprofitable blocks. Yet, that we may not be image-stormers,
and that other folk, known or strangers, may not look upon such as offensive, we have put away
by ordinary power and authority only those images by and before which special worship and
idolatry and special honour with lights and candlesticks was set up. All the others, which are
no hindrance in the church, we let stand.

**Pomesania 1525.** The bishop of Pomesania's theses put the rule at its simplest: images are
not to be prayed to, and no light is to be lit before them. **Pomesania, *Themata episcopi
Risenburgensis*, 1525** (Sehling 4, p. 29):

<!-- doc 1831 -->
> 16) Die bilder in häusern und kirchen soll man nicht anbeten, denselben auch kein licht
> anstecken.

16) The images in houses and churches one shall not pray to, nor light any light unto them.

**The laity's Bible.** The positive case came from Gregory the Great: pictures are the books of
the unlettered. Thüngen 1564 removes idolatrous images "not agreeable to holy Scripture" and
keeps those taken from both Testaments as "the laity's Bible and books"; pastors and sextons are
to watch that no one, "especially the old women", misuse them for idolatry or sorcery.
**Thüngen, *Kirchenordnung*, 1564** (Sehling 11, p. 739):

<!-- doc 326 -->
> 14. Von bildern und gemälden. Die abgöttischen bilde, so der heiligen schrift ungemeß, soll
> man, wie rechten christen und Gottes kinder eignet und gebüret, gar hinwegtun. Die anderen, so
> aus heiliger schrift alten und neuen testament genommen und den leuten zu erinnerung
> warhaftiger historien, zu christlicher lehr und exempel vieler löblicher und Gott
> wohlgefälliger tugenden dinstlich sind, die lassen wir bleiben und nach dem spruck Gregorii
> der laien bibel und bücher sein. Jedoch sollen die pfarhern und kirchner gute achtung darauf
> geben, das nicht jemand (wie leider zu vil geschieht, insonderheit von den alten weibern)
> solcher leidlicher und in Gottes wort gegründeter bilder entweder zu abgötterei oder zauberei
> mißbrauche.

14. Of images and paintings. The idolatrous images which are not agreeable to holy Scripture
    shall one put away altogether, as becometh and behoveth right Christians and God's children.
    The others, which are taken out of holy Scripture, Old and New Testament, and serve the
    people for remembrance of true histories, for Christian doctrine and for example of many
    laudable virtues well-pleasing to God, those we let remain, and be, according to the saying
    of Gregory, the laity's Bible and books. Yet shall the pastors and sextons take good heed
    thereto, that no one (as alas happeneth too much, especially of the old women) misuse such
    tolerable images, grounded in God's word, either for idolatry or for sorcery.

**Oldenburg 1573.** The Oldenburg order answers those who say "the images in the churches are
mere idolatry" with the same distinction, and quotes Gregory in Latin: *Quod legentibus est
scriptura, hoc idiotis praestat pictura*. **Oldenburg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1573**
(Sehling 7/2.1, p. 1083):

<!-- doc 2149 -->
> Was aber historien sind, die in heiliger schrift geschrieben stehen, von den patriarchen,
> aposteln und von dem Herrn Christo und seinen wunderzeichen, auch leiden, kreuzigung, sterben,
> auferstehen, die in bilder gefasset sind, kan man nicht allein mit guten gewissen, sondern
> auch mit grossem nutz in den kirchen und heusern haben zur erinnerung der grossen woltaten
> Gottes und zu danksagung für dieselbigen.

But what are histories written in holy Scripture, of the patriarchs, of the apostles, and of the
Lord Christ and his miracles, also his suffering, crucifying, dying, rising again, which are set
forth in images, those may one have not only with good conscience but also with great profit in
the churches and houses, for remembrance of the great benefits of God and for thanksgiving for
the same.

**Regensburg 1567.** Regensburg, which had dropped the Mass vestment (§7.3), still took a middle
way with images, as with the organ, figural music and Latin song: neither abolishing them
altogether nor making too much of them. **Regensburg, *Kirchenordnung*, [1567]**
(Sehling 13, p. 481):

<!-- doc 450 -->
> Mit den bildnissen (gleich wie auch mit andern mehr dingen, als orgeln schlagen, figural und
> lateinischen gesang) halten wir (den verstand unser christlichen freiheit gleicherweis darin
> anzuzeigen) dis mittel, das wir sie nit allerding abtun noch zuviel damit machen; machen auch
> die unterscheid deren bildnis und gemelde, so gewisse gute historien der schrift haben und nit
> zu abgötterei oder aberglauben gebraucht werden, auch sonst gute, nüzliche erinnerung geben
> mögen, gegen den andern vielfältigen, bäpstischen, lugenhaften, abgöttischen, unnützen bilden
> und gemelden.

With the images (even as also with more other things, as playing the organ, figural and Latin
song) we hold (to show therein likewise the understanding of our Christian liberty) this mean:
that we neither put them away altogether nor make too much of them; we make also the difference
between those images and paintings which have certain good histories of Scripture and are not
used for idolatry or superstition, and may otherwise give good, profitable remembrance, and the
other manifold, popish, lying, idolatrous, unprofitable images and paintings.

The order goes on to say that the costly stone image of Mary and many wax figures of men and
women, life-size, had already been removed "as Hezekiah did to the brazen serpent".

**Gotha 1555** (Sehling 1, p. 569) likewise forbids "the idolatrous popish images which are not
agreeable to biblical history". **Schweinfurt 1543** (Sehling 11, p. 630) follows Luther: where
preachers lead the people from idolatry to Christ, "the images are already stormed and put away,
so that no one regards them much"; images whose histories are uncertain and lying are to be
removed by an ordinary magistrate. **Luther's *Deutsche Messe*** (Sehling 1, p. 16) wants Holy
Week to be like other weeks, without "the hunger cloth, palm-shooting, covering of images and
whatever more of the juggling-work there is".

### 4.2 Orderly removal

**Strasbourg 1525** (Moderate Reformed). The orders insist that images be removed by authority,
not by the people. The Strasbourg council's mandate of March 1525 forbids every citizen to take
down anything, "small or great, in church or in the street", of images, altars, panels, iron or
stone work, on pain of severe punishment; whoever has a grievance is to bring it to the council.
**Strasbourg, *Mandat über Bilder und Altäre*, 18 March 1525**
(Moderate Reformed; Sehling 20/1, p. 166):

<!-- doc 1282 -->
> Unnser herren rete unnd XXI londt gebietten allen unnd yeden iren burgern, geistlichen unnd
> weltlichen, das sie eygens gewalts unnd von inen selbs sampt oder sunder nichts hinweg thun
> oder abbrechen sollen, es sy clein oder groß, inn kyrchen oder uff der gassen, von byldern,
> altarn, daffeln, ysern oder steynen werck, gar nichts ußgenommen

Our lords the councillors and the Twenty-One command all and each of their burghers, spiritual
and temporal, that they shall not of their own power and of themselves, together or severally,
put away or break down anything, be it small or great, in churches or in the street, of images,
altars, panels, iron or stone work, nothing at all excepted.

**Mecklenburg 1552.** The Mecklenburg visitors were to break down all altars but the high altar,
use the stones for the church, nail the images to the walls, and take away offensive images and
the ciboria. **Mecklenburg, *Visitations-Instruction*, 12 November 1552** (Sehling 5, p. 220):

<!-- doc 1923 -->
> sollen die visitatorn vorordenen und bevel thuen, alle die altare, so uber dem hohen altar
> vorhanden, abzubrechen, die steine zu der kirchen zu gebrauchen und die bilde in den kirchen
> mit nageln an den wenden anzuschlagen, schedliche und ergerliche bilde, und die ciboria hinweg
> zu thuen.

the visitors shall ordain and give command to break down all the altars that are present over
and above the high altar, to use the stones for the church, and to nail the images in the
churches to the walls with nails, [and] to put away harmful and offensive images, and the
ciboria.

**Upper Palatinate 1557.** The Upper Palatinate instruction orders idolatrous images removed by
night, "without special tumult and clatter", by the officials. Where idolatrous figures stand on
one panel with biblical histories, the carved figures are to be taken out and the painted ones
blacked over. **Upper Palatinate, *Visitationsinstruktion*, 1557** (Sehling 13, p. 297):

<!-- doc 413 -->
> Doe auch abgottische pilder neben biblischen historien an einer tafel beisammen, sollen die
> ergerlichen geschnitzten pilder herausgetan und die gemalten mit schwarzer farben vermalt und
> verstrichen werden.

Where also idolatrous images are together with biblical histories on one panel, the offensive
carved images shall be taken out, and the painted ones painted over and smeared with black
colour.

**Grubenhagen 1544.** The Grubenhagen order clears everything except the crucifix, which "shall
stand and remain in the midst of the church", and the Christian panels over the altars. Side
altars, idolatrous panels, candlesticks, banners, poles and screens are to be removed, sold,
destroyed or locked away. **Grubenhagen, *Kirchenordnung*, 1544** (Sehling 6/2, p. 1034):

<!-- doc 2056 -->
> XV. Von den bildern in der kirchen. Alle gotzen und bilder, ausgenomen die biltniß des
> crucifixs unsers Hern Jesu Chr[ist]i, welchs soll mitten in der kirchen sthan und bleiben,
> sampt den christlichen tafeln uber den altarn, die andern sollen von stunden an sampt den
> winkelaltarn, abgottischen taffeln, leuchtern, fhanen, stangen, schranken und andere
> papistenstiftung abgethan und hinweggerumet, verkofft, vernichtiget oder verschlossen werden,
> darmit hinfürder davor keiner seine abgotterey und heucheley dreiben moge.

XV. Of the images in the church. All idols and images, excepting the image of the crucifix of
our Lord Jesus Christ, which shall stand and remain in the midst of the church, together with
the Christian panels over the altars: the others shall forthwith, together with the corner
altars, idolatrous panels, candlesticks, banners, poles, screens and other popish foundations,
be put away and cleared out, sold, destroyed or locked up, so that henceforth no one may
practise his idolatry and hypocrisy before them.

**Hermannstadt 1557.** The Transylvanian Saxon synod of 1557 asks that "fabulous images and
similar pictures" set up for worship be removed and sacred histories kept. **Hermannstadt,
*Synodalbeschlüsse*, 1557** (Sehling 24, p. 262):

<!-- doc 1675 -->
> Cum in templis passim et ubique fabulosae imagines et similes picturae habeantur, quae ad
> cultum sunt praestitutae contra manifestum verbum Dei in veteri et novo testamento
> praescriptum, omnino, ut tollantur, necessum est, historiae vero sacrae et similes, quae
> docent et commonefaciunt de re gravi et seria, retineantur.

Seeing that in the churches here and everywhere fabulous images and like paintings are had,
which are set up for worship against the manifest word of God prescribed in the Old and New
Testament, it is altogether needful that they be taken away; but let sacred histories and the
like, which teach and put in remembrance of a grave and serious matter, be retained.

### 4.3 New pictures

**Kurland 1570.** One order goes beyond tolerating old pictures and orders new ones. In Kurland
the surplus altars and idolatrous images are to be cleared away, and in their place "other
glorious histories out of the Old and New Testament, especially of the resurrection and of the
Last Judgement", are to be painted "for the best of the poor non-Germans", that is, the native
peasantry. **Kurland, *Kirchenordnung*, 1570** (Sehling 5, p. 98):

<!-- doc 1907 -->
> Unsere kirchen sollen nebenst den kirchhöfen reinlich, ehrlich und zierlich zugerüstet sein
> und gebürlicher weise, wie in der reformation angezeigt, gehalten werden, und in den kirchen
> nicht mehr als ein altar, dafür das hochwirdige sacrament verreichet sein mus, die andern, so
> noch vorhanden, sollen weg gereumt und mit allen abgöttischen bilden abgethan, und dafür
> andere herliche historien aus dem alten und neuen testamente, sonderlich von der auferstehung
> und von dem jüngsten gericht, den armen undeutschen zum besten gemalet und christlich
> verordnet werden.

Our churches, together with the churchyards, shall be furnished cleanly, honestly and comely,
and be kept in due manner, as is shown in the reformation; and in the churches [there shall be]
not more than one altar, before which the most worthy sacrament must be ministered; the others,
which are yet present, shall be cleared away and put away with all idolatrous images, and in
their stead other glorious histories out of the Old and New Testament, especially of the
resurrection and of the Last Judgement, shall be painted and ordained in Christian wise, for the
best of the poor non-Germans.

### 4.4 The Reformed clearances

**Ulm 1531** (Moderate Reformed). The Upper German and Reformed orders made no distinction
between idols and histories. Ulm, under Bucer's hand, orders "such worshipped images" removed
from all churches as soon as the people have been taught. **Ulm, *Kirchenordnung*, 1531**
(Moderate Reformed; Sehling 17/2, p. 145):

<!-- doc 837 -->
> So wöllen wir, das sölche verehrte bilder auß allen kirchen und anndern ortten, so bald das
> volck durchs wort etwas der warheyt bericht würdt, durch yedes orts amptleut, denen wir des
> bevelch thon wöllen, hingethon werden

So will we that such worshipped images be put away out of all churches and other places, so soon
as the people is somewhat informed of the truth through the word, by the officials of each
place, to whom we will give command thereof.

**The Heidelberg Catechism.** The Palatine catechism of 1563 (Reformed) refuses Gregory's
argument outright. **Kurpfalz, *Heidelberger Katechismus*, Question 98, *Kirchenordnung*, 1563**
(Reformed; Sehling 14, p. 362):

<!-- doc 504 -->
> [98.] Frag. Mögen aber nicht die bilder als der leyen bücher in den kirchen geduldet werden?
> Antwort. Nein, denn wir nicht sollen weiser sein denn Gott, welcher seine christenheyt nicht
> durch stumme götzen, sonder durch die lebendige predig seines worts wil underwiesen haben.

[98.] Question. But may not images be suffered in the churches as the laity's books? Answer. No;
for we should not be wiser than God, who will not have his Christendom taught by dumb idols, but
by the living preaching of his word.

**Kurpfalz 1565** (Reformed). Two years later Frederick III ordered his officials to clear what
remained: altars, Mount of Olives groups, crucifixes in church, in the street and before the
church, sacrament houses, fonts and holy-water stoups. **Kurpfalz, *Befehl an die Amtleute*, 3
October 1565** (Reformed; Sehling 14, p. 429):

<!-- doc 504 -->
> so befinden wir noch teglich, das noch hien und wieder viel abgötterey uberig plieben, als
> nemlich altarien, ölberg, gotzenwerg, crucifix in der kirchen und uf den strassen, auch vor
> den kirchen, item sacramentheuslin, thaufstein, weichkessel und dergleichen götzenwergk mehr.

so we find still daily that yet here and there much idolatry is remaining, as namely altars,
Mounts of Olives, idol-work, crucifixes in the church and on the streets, also before the
churches, item sacrament houses, fonts, holy-water stoups, and more such idol-work.

The Reformed clearances that followed are quoted in §5.3: Nassau-Dillenburg's Siegen mandate of
1581 (images, panels, organs, altars and chalices), the Casimirian mandate of 1587 and Simmern
1598. Two Lutheran orders went the same way. The Sponheim *Kirchen- und Zensurordnung* of
1590/91 (Sehling 18, p. 653) orders "crucifix and other images which are still present" taken
away, and the Lichtenberg instruction of 1579, in Lutheran Pfalz-Zweibrücken, does the same for
crucifixes. **Lichtenberg, *Dienstanweisung für die Pfarrer*, 1579** (Sehling 18, p. 379):

<!-- doc 992 -->
> 17. ) Sollen auch die abgöttische Bilder, Crucifix, wo deren noch vorhandenn, vermög der
> visitation bevelch allenthalb mit Bescheidenheit hinweg geschafft werdenn.

17.) Shall also the idolatrous images, crucifixes, where such are still present, according to
the command of the visitation, be everywhere put away with discretion.

**The Lutheran reply.** The Prussian order of 1568 answers the "wretched Calvinists and
enthusiasts", who think a man cannot be evangelical unless he storms all paintings and tears
down all images. **Prussia, *Kirchenordnung und Ceremonien*, 1568** (Sehling 4, p. 73):

<!-- doc 1833 -->
> Künden es derhalben auch mit den leidigen Calvinisten und schwermern nicht halten, welche sich
> dünken lassen, man künde nicht evangelisch sein, wenn man nicht alle gemelde stürmet, alle
> bilder darnieder reisst, alle ceremonien abthut, und grob, unbescheiden, ohne zucht und
> ordnung alles lest durcheinander gehen wie das unvernünftige vieh.

We can therefore also not hold with the wretched Calvinists and enthusiasts, which let
themselves think that one cannot be evangelical unless one storm all paintings, tear down all
images, put away all ceremonies, and let all things go rudely, indiscreetly, without discipline
and order, one through another, like the unreasoning beast.

### 4.5 The crucifix

**Kept and honoured.** Hannover 1536 (§3.1) and Grubenhagen 1544 (§4.2) keep the crucifix by
name. The Anhalt visitors of 1562 ordered pastors and churchwardens to keep altar and font
clean, adorned with white linen cloths or tapestries, and to hold the Mass vestment and the
crucifix "as a remembrance of the suffering of Christ" in comely state and honour. **Anhalt,
*Kirchenordnung auf dem Lande*, 22 July 1562** (Sehling 2, p. 562):

<!-- doc 1262 -->
> Zum sechsten, haben wir auch allen pfarherrn und vorstehender bevohlen, das sie iren altar und
> tauf stetlich rein halden mit weissen leinen tuchern ader tapeten zieren, ir gemess gewand und
> was sonst zum gebrauch des heiligen mahls Christi gehort, desgleichen auch das crucifix, als
> ein erinnerung des leidens Christi, vor uns zur seligkeit geschehen, im zierlichen wesen und
> ehren halten sollen.

Sixthly, we have also commanded all pastors and wardens that they keep their altar and font
continually clean, adorn [them] with white linen cloths or tapestries, [and] hold their Mass
vestment and whatever else belongeth to the use of the holy supper of Christ, and likewise also
the crucifix, as a remembrance of the suffering of Christ done for us unto salvation, in comely
state and in honour.

**At funerals.** The Freiberg visitation of 1538 orders a crucifix carried before every funeral,
"especially to the honour of the high and comforting article of the resurrection of the dead".
**Freiberg, *Visitation Verordnung und Artikel*, 1538** (Sehling 1, p. 468):

<!-- doc 49 -->
> 16. Das man auch hinfurt in alleweg zum begrebnus ein crucifix vortrage, sonderlich dem hohen
> und trostlichen artikel der auferstehung der toten zu eren.

16. That one henceforth in every case carry a crucifix before at burial, especially to the
    honour of the high and comforting article of the resurrection of the dead.

Wolfstein 1574 permits it as a witness "on whom the Christians die". **Wolfstein, *Christliche
Instructio des Thomas Stieber*, 1574** (Sehling 13, p. 586):

<!-- doc 463 -->
> Ein crucifix aber vorher zu tragen zum zeugnus, auf wen die christen sterben, nemlich auf den
> gecreuzigten Herrn Jesum Christum, das soll unverboten sein.

But to carry a crucifix before, for a witness on whom the Christians die, namely on the
crucified Lord Jesus Christ, that shall be unforbidden.

The Nassau-Weilburg points of 1617, a Lutheran order, abolish the custom. **Nassau-Weilburg,
*Erläuterungspunkte zur Kirchenordnung*, 1617** (Sehling 10, p. 353):

<!-- doc 199 -->
> 2. Wo daß crucifix bißhero den leychen vorgetragen worden, soll es, wie in andern unsern
> graveunndt herrschafften mehrentheilß lengst gefallen undt verplieben, auch abgestellet
> werden.

2. Where the crucifix hath hitherto been carried before the corpses, it shall also be put down,
   as in our other counties and lordships for the most part it hath long since fallen and
   remained [fallen].

**At the deathbed.** Veit Dietrich's Nuremberg *Agendbüchlein* allows a crucifix at the bedside
of the dying but points beyond it. **Nuremberg, *Agendbüchlein Veit Dietrichs*, 1545**
(Sehling 11, p. 522):

<!-- doc 297 -->
> Das man ein crucifix bei den sterbenden hat, ist an im selb nit bös. Aber wer den trost im
> wort hat, der hat das best crucifix, welchs nit allein die augen ansehen, sonder die ohren
> fassen und das herz in sich bilden kan

That one have a crucifix by the dying is not evil in itself. But whoso hath the comfort in the
word, he hath the best crucifix, which not only the eyes can look upon, but the ears can grasp
and the heart can image within itself.

**On the oath table.** In the Mansfeld consistory, oaths were sworn at a table covered with a
black velvet pall bearing a white cross, with two great brass candlesticks and burning wax
candles, a crucifix and the Bible. **Mansfeld, *Form und weise … mit den öffentlichen
eidesleistungen*, appended to the consistorial order, 1586** (Sehling 2, p. 214):

<!-- doc 1241 -->
> 7. Wird der tisch gedecket mit einen schwarzen sambten leichtuch, welches ein weiss creuze
> hat. 8. Hinten auf den tische gegen dem hofefenster werden gesetzt zwene grosse messingene
> kirchenleuchter mit zweien brennenten wachs kerzen, ferner auf dem tisch ein crucifix und die
> bibel dabei gelegt.

7. The table is covered with a black velvet pall, which hath a white cross. 8. At the back upon
   the table, toward the court window, are set two great brass church candlesticks with two
   burning wax candles; further, upon the table a crucifix, and the Bible laid by it.

The form explains on the next page that the crucifix signifies Christ with the whole treasure of
his merit, and the Bible all the promises of the gospel.

### 4.6 Banners, processional crosses and image plays

Banners (*Fahnen*), poles and processional crosses went with the processions they served. The
Saalfeld visitors of 1533 ordered the council to remove "all screens, candles, banners,
idolatrous images and hindrances, and above all the altars save one" (Sehling 1, p. 654).
Mecklenburg 1552 orders "the banners and crosses which one was wont to carry about in the
papacy" taken out of the churches (Sehling 5, p. 220), and Grubenhagen 1544 lists "banners,
poles" among the things to be removed (§4.2). Brunswick 1528 counts "lights, banners" among the
needless splendour of baptism (Sehling 6/1, p. 356).

The image plays of the great feasts went with them. Hohenlohe 1553 abolishes the carrying about
of the image of the risen Christ at Easter, its drawing up on Ascension Day, the letting down of
the dove at Pentecost and the rocking of the Christ-child at Christmas
(Sehling 15, p. 77; see [`LITURGICAL_CALENDAR_GUIDE.md`](LITURGICAL_CALENDAR_GUIDE.md), §10.10).

## 5. The building and its furnishings

### 5.1 How many altars

**Wittenberg 1522.** The side altars served private Masses, and they went with them. The
Wittenberg order of January 1522 is the earliest: images and altars are to be removed to avoid
idolatry, "for three altars without images are enough". **Wittenberg, *Kirchen-Ordnung*, 1522**
(Sehling 1, p. 697):

<!-- doc 146 -->
> Item die bild und altarien in der kirchen söllen auch abgethon werden, damit abgötterei zu
> vermeiden, dann drei altaria on bild genug seind

Item, the images and altars in the church shall also be put away, that idolatry may be avoided,
for three altars without images are enough.

**One altar.** Later orders keep a single altar for the Supper. Kurland 1570 allows "not more
than one altar" (§4.3), and its sexton prepares only that altar, "since no other or more altars
are to be used, much less to be permitted after popish custom" (Sehling 5, p. 87). Mecklenburg
1552 keeps the high altar alone (§4.2), and the Upper Palatinate instruction of 1557 orders "all
altars save one, whereon one holdeth the Lord's Supper" taken away (Sehling 13, p. 297).
Saalfeld 1533 does the same (Sehling 1, p. 654).

**Württemberg 1536.** Where a church still had several altars, Württemberg let the minister
choose the most convenient. **Württemberg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1536** (Sehling 16, p. 107):

<!-- doc 651 -->
> ein solchen altar zu dem Nachtmal erwölen, der am allergelegnesten sein mag, da man am
> kommelichsten zu und von gehn und alle ding am deutlichsten hören möge, welcher altar auch mit
> brot und wein nach notturfft zugericht sein soll

choose such an altar for the Supper as may be the most convenient of all, where one may go to
and from most commodiously and hear all things most plainly; which altar also shall be furnished
with bread and wine as need requireth.

**Hermannstadt 1565.** In Transylvania the danger ran the other way. The synod of 1565 forbids
the altars to be destroyed, and where "turbulent men" have pulled them down, pastors are to see
them restored without tumult. **Hermannstadt, *Synodalartikel*, 1565** (Sehling 24, p. 293):

<!-- doc 1683 -->
> Altaria nullo modo diruantur, sed, sicubi in nostris ecclesiis per homines turbulentos dirruta
> sunt, dent operam pii pastores, ut sine tumultibus restituantur. Et testimonia extant
> primitivae ecclesiae non in mensis ligneis, sed in altaribus administratam esse coenam Domini.

Let the altars in no wise be pulled down; but if anywhere in our churches they have been pulled
down by turbulent men, let godly pastors take pains that they be restored without tumults. And
testimonies of the primitive church are extant that the Lord's Supper was administered not on
wooden tables but on altars.

### 5.2 Where the minister stands

**Facing the people.** Several Saxon visitations order the altars arranged so that the minister
faces the people. **Colditz, *Verordnung der Visitatoren*, 1529** (Sehling 1, p. 545):

<!-- doc 75 -->
> Zum zehenden das die altaria dermassen zugericht werden, domit der kirchendiner al sein
> kirchenampt gegen dem volk vorrichte

Tenthly, that the altars be so arranged, that the minister may perform all his church office
toward the people.

**Gotha, *Artikel der Visitatoren*, 1555** (Sehling 1, p. 569):

<!-- doc 88 -->
> Zum andern das die abgöttische papistische bilder, so biblischer historien nicht gemess, in
> der kirchen nicht geduldet, auch die altaria dermassen zugericht werden sollen, das die
> priester mit den angesichte kegen dem volk gekart ihr ampt vorrichten mogen

Secondly, that the idolatrous popish images which are not agreeable to biblical histories be not
suffered in the church; also that the altars shall be so arranged, that the priests may perform
their office with the face turned toward the people.

**Not behind the table.** "Facing the people" did not yet mean standing behind a freestanding
table. That posture was associated with Karlstadt and Müntzer (Radical Reformation), and the
Wittenberg theologians condemned it. The *Wittenbergische Reformation* of 1545 hopes for a
uniform Mass "with standing before the altar, that there be not such disorder as in some places,
that the priests stand behind the table" (Sehling 1, p. 214). The Allstedt visitation of 1533,
in Müntzer's former parish, orders every pastor to celebrate "not behind the altar, as some have
done from the time of Thomas Müntzer until now, but to stand before the altar", and not to turn
to the people at the elevation (Sehling 1, p. 510).

**Behind the table.** At the end of the century the Reformed princes ordered exactly that. In
Anhalt in 1596 Johann Georg ordered the ministers to lay aside lights, Mass vestments and
chasubles, to step behind the table "be it of stone or of wood", face the people and break the
bread; the village officers were to remove the panels from the altars and the carved crucifixes
from the churches, and set up a table in place of the altar. **Anhalt, *Verordnung Johann
Georgs*, 3 November 1596** (Reformed; Sehling 2, p. 580):

<!-- doc 1262 -->
> die lichter uf den altaren, mesgewant und caseln ablegen, bei administrirung und auspendunge
> des heiligen nachtmals des herren hinter dem dische (er sei steinern oder holzern) treten, das
> angesichte zu den volke kehren und geheiligete brot bei der austheilunge brechen und dem volke
> nach Christi wort und einsetzunge dasselbe geben solle, und das die schulzen und vorsteher die
> tafeln von dem altarn und die geschnitzte holzerne crucifix aus der kirchen hinweg thun, und
> anstatt des altars einen steinern oder holzern tisch

[that he] shall lay aside the lights upon the altars, Mass vestment and chasubles; at the
administering and dealing out of the holy Supper of the Lord step behind the table (be it of
stone or of wood), turn the face to the people, and break the hallowed bread at the
distribution, and give the same to the people according to Christ's word and institution; and
that the village headmen and wardens put away the panels from the altars and the carved wooden
crucifixes out of the church, and in place of the altar a table of stone or of wood [set up].

**Strasbourg 1537** (Moderate Reformed) had already set up an "altar-table" facing the people so
that everyone might hear every word. **Strasbourg, *Agende*, 1537**
(Moderate Reformed; Sehling 20/1, p. 270):

<!-- doc 1302 -->
> komet der pfarrer und gehet für den altartisch, so sie gegen dem volck, damit jederman alle
> wort vernemmen mög, haben lassen auffrichten, und fahet an das gemein ampt mit nachgenden
> worten

the pastor cometh and goeth before the altar-table, which they have caused to be set up toward
the people, that every man may perceive all the words, and beginneth the common office with the
following words.

### 5.3 Tables in place of altars

The Reformed princes replaced altars with tables outright. **Simmern, *Mandat zur Abschaffung der Bilder und Altäre*, 2 October 1598** (Reformed, issued from Heidelberg after Simmern fell to the Electoral Palatinate; Sehling 19/2, p. 661):

<!-- doc 1126 -->
> das in diesem gantzen ambt Simern allenthalben in den kirchen das gotzenwerck, altaria,
> tauffstein und dergleichen gentzlich abgeschafft unnd an der altarien statt gewonliche disch
> geordent werden sollen

that in this whole district of Simmern, everywhere in the churches, the idol-work, altars, fonts
and the like shall be wholly abolished, and in the altars' stead ordinary tables be ordained.

The Casimirian mandate for the Palatinate in 1587 (Reformed) is a list of such measures.
**Kurpfalz, *Casimirianische Kirchenordnung*, mandate, 1587** (Reformed; Sehling 19/2, p. 757):

<!-- doc 1144 -->
> 13. An stat altarien soll man hultzern tisch in der kirchen machen und setzen lassen. 14. Kein
> tauffstein soll in der kirchen geduldet werden. 15. An stat der kleinen runden hostien soll
> man breite kuchen oder weck in einer schußeln uff den tisch in der kirchen stellen

13. In place of altars one shall cause wooden tables to be made and set in the church. 14. No
    font shall be suffered in the church. 15. In place of the little round hosts one shall set
    broad cakes or loaves in a dish upon the table in the church.

The Siegen mandate of 1581 (Reformed) likewise orders "the actual abolition of the idols,
panels, organs, altars, chalices, and the ordering in their stead in each place of a table and a
beaker" (Sehling 10, p. 158).

**Altar or table.** The Lutheran order of Marienhafe in East Frisia (1593), surrounded by
Reformed churches, uses both words: each church is to have its own table-cloth, chalice, dish
and can, "to use cleanly upon the altar or table of the Lord". **Marienhafe, *Kirchenordnung*,
1593** (Sehling 7/1, p. 710):

<!-- doc 2120 -->
> Zum h. abentmall des Hern soll jede kirch eigen tischtug, kelch, schussel und kandel haben,
> aufen altar oder tisch des Hern fein sauber zu gebrauchen. Und sollens die communicanten
> kniend empfangen ahn einen ende des altars oder tisches das gesegentes [!] brot, ahm andren
> den kelch mit geburlicher reverenz und andacht.

For the holy Supper of the Lord each church shall have its own table-cloth, chalice, dish and
can, to use finely and cleanly upon the altar or table of the Lord. And the communicants shall
receive it kneeling: at one end of the altar or table the blessed bread, at the other the cup,
with due reverence and devotion.

### 5.4 Lights

**Two candles.** The commonest Lutheran arrangement was two candles on the altar during the
Supper. Allstedt 1533 orders "two burning lights during Mass" with alb and chasuble (§7.1), and
the Calenberg synods of 1544/45 "two lights set upon the altar" (§7.1). Kurland 1570 has the
sexton light both wax candles on the altar and a third on the pulpit, and forbids other lights
by day. **Kurland, *Kirchenordnung*, 1570** (Sehling 5, p. 87):

<!-- doc 1907 -->
> alsdenn bereitet der custos das altar, welchs allein zum gottesdienst und dem hochwirdigen
> sacrament verordnet, weil sonst keine andere oder mehr altare zugebrauchen, viel weniger nach
> papistischer gewonheit zuvergönnen, zündet auch an auf dem altar die beiden wachskerzen, wie
> in diesen landen allzeit gebreuchlich, desgleichen auf der canzel zündet er an das dritte
> licht, sonst sollen keine andere lichte bei hellem tage von deutschen oder undeutschen ihrer
> abgöttischen gewonheit nach gebraucht oder gestattet

then the sexton prepareth the altar, which is ordained only for the service of God and the most
worthy sacrament, since no other or more altars are to be used, much less to be permitted after
popish custom; he lighteth also upon the altar the two wax candles, as hath always been the use
in these lands; likewise upon the pulpit he lighteth the third light; else shall no other lights
in broad day be used or permitted, by Germans or non-Germans, after their idolatrous custom.

**Lights at the Supper.** The Hildesheim order of 1544 tells how an evangelical preacher, mocked
for lighting candles on the altar in broad day, answered that Christ held his last supper at
night in a hall full of lights. **Hildesheim, *Christlike kerckenordeninge*, 1544**
(Sehling 7/2.1, p. 852):

<!-- doc 2136 -->
> Ein evangelisch prediger wert spottisch gefraget: Worumme stecke gy lichte an up dem altare ym
> lichten dage, dar gy wol können ane lichte seen? De prediger vant balde ein gudt antwort unde
> sprack: Wy holden dar des Heren aventmal. Christus helt syn leste aventmal unde gaff dat
> sacramente yn der nacht, do he vorraden wart, dar weren vele lichte yn dem groten sale umme
> der düster nacht willen. So steke wy nu ock lichte an, nicht umme der nodt willen ym klaren
> dage, sunder to bedüdende unde to betekende, dat wy dar holden nicht eine middagesmaltidt
> edder etent, sunder datsülve aventetent

An evangelical preacher was asked in mockery: Wherefore light ye lights upon the altar in the
light of day, when ye can well see without lights? The preacher found a good answer straightway
and spake: We hold there the Lord's Supper. Christ held his last supper and gave the sacrament
in the night in which he was betrayed; there were many lights in the great hall because of the
dark night. So we also now light lights, not for need's sake in clear day, but to signify and
betoken that we hold there not a midday meal or eating, but that same evening meal.

**Wolfenbüttel 1569.** The Brunswick-Wolfenbüttel order wants the altar adorned with clean
cloths and lights burning "because this is also kept in the neighbouring reformed churches", the
people being taught that it is a free thing without superstition. **Brunswick-Wolfenbüttel,
*Kirchenordnung*, 1569** (Sehling 6/1, p. 143):

<!-- doc 1974 -->
> Es soll auch der altar mit reinen tüchern und andern ornatu gezieret und bekleidet sein, item
> liechte auf dem altar brennen, weil solches auch in den genachbarten reformierten kirchen
> gehalten, und kan gleichwoll das gemeine volk berichtet werden, das solchs nicht nothwendig,
> als das darin sonderlicher gottesdienst stünde oder die heiligung dieses sacraments daran
> gelegen, sonder wie ein frey mittelding ohne allen aberglauben gehalten werde.

The altar also shall be adorned and clothed with clean cloths and other ornaments; item, lights
shall burn upon the altar, because the same is kept also in the neighbouring reformed churches;
and yet the common people may be informed that such is not necessary, as though any special
service of God stood therein or the hallowing of this sacrament lay thereon, but that it is kept
as a free indifferent thing without all superstition.

Here "reformed" means the neighbouring evangelical churches, not the Calvinists. **Ansbach
1548**, an Interim order, wants the altar candles lit at the beginning of Mass and Vespers and
not put out until the very end (Sehling 11, p. 327). Thüngen 1564 keeps them for peace (§3.3).

**One lamp.** The Palatine provisional order of 1546 (Lutheran; not an Interim order) reduces
the lights to a single lamp and turns the cost of oil and wax to other uses. **Kurpfalz,
*Gemaine maß, die kirchen- und gottesdinst anzurichten*, 1546** (Sehling 14, p. 94):

<!-- doc 472 -->
> 7. Man soll nur ein ampel in der kirchen lassen, den uberigen kosten des oles und wachs
> abstellen, zu andern nötigen diensten der kirchen gebrauchen

7. One shall leave only one lamp in the church, [and] put down the remaining cost of oil and wax
   [and] use [it] for other needful services of the church.

**Abolished.** Regensburg dropped the lights with the Mass vestment (§7.3), and Anhalt in 1596
(Reformed) ordered them laid aside (§5.2).

**Lights for the Supper, not for the feast.** No order found varies the number of altar lights
by the rank of a feast or by the season. Where the orders vary the lights, they vary them by
whether the Supper is held. In the Prussian towns the lights are lit with the Mass vestment when
there are communicants, and on other Sundays the minister wears only the surplice.
**Marggrabowa, *Gottesdienstordnung*, 1581** (Sehling 4, p. 149):

<!-- doc 1837 -->
> In ceremonien soll er nichts neues anfangen und alle zeit, wenn communikanten vorhanden, das
> messgewand gebrauchen, die lichte aufm altar lassen anzünden und da keine communikanten
> vorhanden, den chorrock alle zeit auf die sontage gebrauchen.

In ceremonies he shall begin nothing new, and at all times, when communicants are present, use
the Mass vestment [and] cause the lights upon the altar to be lit; and when no communicants are
present, use the surplice at all times on the Sundays.

The Sensburg order of the same year has the same words (Sehling 4, p. 151). At Suhl in
Henneberg, by contrast, the whole Sunday service with communion, two and a half hours of
ringing, singing, reading, preaching and communion, was held without any lights. **Suhl,
*Ordnung des predigamts und lektionen*, 1562** (Sehling 2, p. 351):

<!-- doc 1250 -->
> Dis alles geschicht ohn liechter, und wehret mit leuten, singen, lesen, predigt und communion
> bei dritthalber stund.

All this is done without lights, and lasteth, with ringing, singing, reading, sermon and
communion, about two hours and a half.

The Gottorf visitors of 1587 still had to ask, parish by parish, whether the candles were lit or
removed. **Gottorf, *Visitationsartikel*, 1587** (Sehling 23, p. 371):

<!-- doc 1621 -->
> 11. An Vestitus Ecclesiasticus tam lineus quam sericus in administratione Coenae sit usitatus
> an abolitus, cerei accensi an remoti.

11. Whether the church vesture, linen as well as silk, be in use at the administration of the
    Supper or abolished; [whether] the candles be lighted or removed.

**Lights for the dark.** Other lights were plainly practical. In the Neumark the guilds, which
had burned candles every Sunday and feast day, were to give wax yearly instead, so that the
church might have light "in the winter when the day is short". **Neumark, *Kasten-Ordnung*, 1
March 1540** (Sehling 3, p. 32):

<!-- doc 1745 -->
> Nachdem auch gross und klein gewercke zuvoren alle sontage und feiertage auf den kerzen lichte
> gebrandt, sollen die rähte gleichfalls mit den gewerken auf einige anzahl wachses jehrlich zu
> geben, handelen, damit des winters wenn der tag kurz ist, lichte zur notturft der kirchen
> möchte gehabt und gebraucht werden.

Whereas also the great and small crafts formerly burned lights on the candles every Sunday and
feast day, the councils shall likewise treat with the crafts to give a certain quantity of wax
yearly, that in winter, when the day is short, lights may be had and used for the need of the
church.

**The eternal light.** The endowments for the perpetual lamp went into the common chest with the
endowments for Masses and vigils. **Württemberg, *Kastenordnung*, 1536** (Sehling 16, p. 96):

<!-- doc 650 -->
> Erstlich, von bestendigem und gewissem einkommen soll zusamen geschlagen unnd in ein Kasten
> eingezogen werden alles, was bißanher auff messen, vigilien, ewigen liecht, wachs und öl
> gewendt ist worden.

First, of steady and certain income there shall be put together and drawn into one chest all
that hath hitherto been spent on Masses, vigils, the eternal light, wax and oil.

The Württemberg order of 1552 repeats the clause (Sehling 16, p. 202). The single lamp of
Kurpfalz 1546, which the editor takes for the eternal light, is the only one found kept.

**Blessed candles.** The blessing of candles at Candlemas was abolished. **Hesse, *Homberger
Kirchenordnung*, 1526** (Moderate Reformed; Sehling 8, p. 49):

<!-- doc 2249 -->
> Interdicimus autem in virtute omnipotentis Dei, ne quis in ipsis festis quicquam agat eorum,
> quae hactenus contra puritatem sermonum Dei introducta fuerunt, ut sunt benedictio candelarum
> in die praesentationis, et superstitiones, quae in diebus aliis praescriptis servabantur.

But we forbid in the power of almighty God that anyone do on these feasts any of those things
which have hitherto been brought in against the purity of the words of God, as are the blessing
of candles on the day of the Presentation, and the superstitions which were observed on the
other days prescribed.

Brandenburg 1540 drops the blessing of "herbs, lights and other things" (Sehling 3, p. 88).
Schweinfurt 1543 has the Candlemas preacher lead the people "from the folly and idolatry which
they formerly had with the lights" to Christ, "the true, eternal light" (Sehling 11, p. 635).
Pfalz-Neuburg 1543 forbids blessing the paschal candle (*osterstock*) with the Easter fire
(§6.6), and the Lüneburg convent reform of 1555 stops the prioress's blessing of palms, Easter
candles, water and fire. **Lüneburg, *Reformatio coenobiorum*, 1555** (Sehling 6/1, p. 615):

<!-- doc 2009 -->
> Weil dann die domina ein zeitlang die palm, osterkerzen, wasser und feuer geweihet hat und
> aber solchs ihr nicht geburet, dann es ohne das Gottes guthe creaturen sein und kein befelich
> in heiliger schrift darvon ist, sonder derselbigen mehr zuwider, so sol solchs furder
> underlassen werden.

Since then the prioress for a time hath hallowed the palms, Easter candles, water and fire, and
yet such belongeth not to her, for they are without that God's good creatures, and there is no
command thereof in holy Scripture, but rather it is contrary thereto, so shall the same
henceforth be left off.

**Tenebrae.** The Holy Week *Tenebrae* survived in the Lutheran orders only as the responsory
*Tenebrae factae sunt*, sung on Fridays or Good Friday, and in places as a Friday bell. No order
keeps the hearse of candles put out one by one. Pfalz-Neuburg 1543 keeps the three Holy Week
Matins "but without any knocking, rumbling and din" (Sehling 13, p. 95), the noise that ended
the office in the dark.

### 5.5 The sacrament house

The evangelical orders gave communion at once and did not reserve the Sacrament, so the
sacrament house lost its purpose. Hohenlohe 1553 forbids the Sacrament to be "kept, locked up or
carried about" (Sehling 15, p. 77). **Schönburg, *Kirchen-Ordnung* of Johann Pfeffinger, 18
October 1542** (Sehling 2, p. 175):

<!-- doc 1235 -->
> 9. Sie sollen kein sacrament noch crysma oder dergl. in dem sacrament häuslein halten, noch
> brennende lampen dafür leiden.

9. They shall keep no sacrament nor chrism or the like in the sacrament house, nor suffer
   burning lamps before it.

**Ambries.** The Homberg synod (Moderate Reformed) forbids reservation in any form, "in ambries
or little boxes". **Hesse, *Homberger Kirchenordnung*, 1526**
(Moderate Reformed; Sehling 8, p. 46):

<!-- doc 2247 -->
> [27] Quia usus sanctae eucharistiae est perceptio eius, et communio fidelium in Christi
> commemorationem, nullibi a modo in armariis seu arcellis reservetur, nullaque ratione
> circumferatur. Haec namque figmenta hominum sunt, ideoque vitanda.

[27] Because the use of the holy eucharist is the receiving of it, and the communion of the
faithful in remembrance of Christ, let it henceforth nowhere be reserved in ambries or little
boxes, and by no means be carried about. For these are figments of men, and therefore to be
shunned.

No order uses "tabernacle" (*Tabernakel*) for a furnishing. The word occurs only for the
tabernacle of Israel and in the psalms.

**Removal.** The Pfalz-Neuburg visitation of 1560 counts sacrament houses and altars not used
for communion among "idolatrous images", to be removed "with Christian discretion", as in the
Upper Palatinate. **Pfalz-Neuburg, *Visitationsordnung*, 1560** (Sehling 13, p. 135):

<!-- doc 399 -->
> So werden wir bericht, das noch abgöttische bilder als sacramentheuslein und altaria, so man
> nit zur communion gebraucht, in etlichen kirchen sein. Da sollen unsere visitatores uf mitl
> und weg denken, ob und wie solche bilder und anders, so zur abgötterei ursach geben kan, mit
> christlicher beschaidenhait abzuschaffen

So are we informed that idolatrous images, as sacrament houses and altars which are not used for
the communion, are still in some churches. There shall our visitors think upon means and ways,
whether and how such images and other things which can give cause for idolatry may be abolished
with Christian discretion.

**Nassau-Weilburg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1576** (Sehling 10, p. 310), joins them with the ossuary:

<!-- doc 198 -->
> 27. Hieher gehören auch die ubergebliebene Todtenbein [=Ossuarium] und genannte Heyligen- wie
> auch Sacramenthäußlin, die man gäntzlich soll abschaffen unnd die Todtenbeyn auff den
> Gottsacker in die Erde verscharren und begraben.

27. Hither belong also the remaining dead men's bones [= ossuary] and the so-called saints' and
    also sacrament houses, which one shall wholly abolish, and bury and inter the dead men's
    bones in the earth in the churchyard.

Magdeburg 1583 (§3.2) and Kurpfalz 1565 (Reformed; §4.4) name the sacrament house too.

### 5.6 The font

**Kept.** The Prussian order of 1525 baptizes in plain water "in the customary font, which may
be renewed as often as is needful". **Prussia, *Artikel der ceremonien und anderer kirchen
ordnung*, 10 December 1525** (Sehling 4, p. 33):

<!-- doc 1832 -->
> Die tauf sol gescheen in der kirchen wie vor, alleine in lauterem wasser, darzu im gewonlichen
> taufstein erhalten, welchs mag verneuet werden, so oft es von nöten ist, und bedarf nicht der
> osterlichen alten ceremonien, auch keines öls oder cresems

Baptism shall be done in the church as before, only in pure water, kept therefor in the
customary font, which may be renewed as oft as it is needful; and it needeth not the old Easter
ceremonies, nor any oil or chrism.

**Moved to sight.** Baptism was to be public, and the font was moved accordingly. Lüneburg 1564
sets the fonts "up before the choir and a step or two high". **Lüneburg, *Kirchenordnung*,
1564** (Sehling 6/1, p. 554):

<!-- doc 2003 -->
> Derwegen ordenen und setzen wir, das in allen pfarren unsers fürstenthumbs die taufsteine, wo
> es nicht allbereit geschehen, oben vor dem chor und ein trit oder zween in die höhe gesetzet
> werden sollen

Therefore we ordain and appoint that in all parishes of our principality the fonts, where it
hath not already been done, shall be set up before the choir, and a step or two on high.

Bentheim-Tecklenburg 1588 (Reformed) moves the fonts "out of the corners before the face of the
church, as hath been done also in many reformed churches of the German nation".
**Bentheim-Tecklenburg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1588** (Reformed; Sehling 22, p. 268):

<!-- doc 1507 -->
> Es sollen auch die Baptisteria oder Tauffsteine auß den Winckelen für das Angesicht der
> Kirchen geruckt werden, Wie dann auch in vielen reformirten Kirchen teutscher Nation beschehen

The baptisteries or fonts also shall be moved out of the corners before the face of the church,
as hath been done also in many reformed churches of the German nation.

**Adorned.** Kaspar Löner at Nördlingen asks the council to rail the font about and adorn it.
**Nördlingen, *Kirchenordnung Kaspar Löners*, 1544** (Sehling 12, p. 314):

<!-- doc 372 -->
> Dazu ist auch mein bit, euere erbare weisheit wöllen den taufstein umbgittern und ziern
> lassen.

Thereto is also my request, that your honourable wisdom will cause the font to be railed about
and adorned.

Kurland 1570 wants "a special, glorious font or font-vessel, with the needful two towels and
cloths" (Sehling 5, p. 99).

**Warm water.** Bremen 1534 tells the sexton to provide warm water in a basin in frosty weather,
so that baptism, given for salvation, does the child's body no harm. **Bremen, *Kirchenordnung*,
1534** (Sehling 7/2.2, p. 439):

<!-- doc 2217 -->
> Im Wynter, wenn ydt gefraren ys, schal men dem köster anseggen, dat he warm water vorschaffe
> (nicht mit synem schaden unde nadeel) yn ein becken, dat de döpe, tho der salicheit gegeven,
> nicht dem kindeken am live schade

In winter, when it is frozen, one shall tell the sexton that he provide warm water (not to his
own harm and hurt) in a basin, that the baptism, given for salvation, harm not the little child
in its body.

**Abolished.** The Reformed orders removed the font altogether: Kurpfalz 1565 (§4.4), the
Casimirian mandate of 1587 and Simmern 1598 (§5.3), all Reformed. The Lutheran Sponheim in 1607
complains that the "old, abolished fonts and corner-Mass altars" ordered removed still lie
broken in some churches, "a great unseemliness" (Sehling 18, p. 669).

### 5.7 Pulpit, screens, pews, galleries and windows

**The pulpit.** The orders say little of the pulpit's form. It appears with altar and font as a
thing to be covered (§6.1) and kept, and in Kurland it bears the third light (§5.4).

**The choir screen.** The Zweibrücken visitation of 1558 orders the screen in the nuns' choir
"torn away", so that the preacher can see that they hear the sermon. **Pfalz-Zweibrücken,
*Visitationsordnung*, 1558** (Sehling 18, p. 270):

<!-- doc 970 -->
> Mittler zeit aber, biß solche verenderung vorgenomen, sollen sy mit ernst angehallten werden,
> die predig Gottes worts zehörn, und darmit mann ihrer gewiß, das dieselbig von ihnen allen
> besuecht, soll ihnen das gitter uff dem Chor hinweg gerissen werden, das sy von einem prediger
> gesehen und sich nicht abziehen köndten.

But in the meantime, until such change be undertaken, they shall be earnestly held to hear the
preaching of God's word; and that one may be sure of them, that the same be attended by all of
them, the grating in the choir shall be torn away from them, that they may be seen by a preacher
and could not withdraw themselves.

Saalfeld 1533 removes "all screens" (*gitter*) with the side altars, and Grubenhagen 1544 lists
*schranken* among the things to be cleared (§4.2).

**Pews.** By the later sixteenth century the nave was filling with pews, and the orders regulate
them. Electoral Saxony in 1580 complains that pews built "here and there" block the view of the
pulpit and of the altar at communion. **Saxony, *Ordnung Herzog Augusts*, 1580**
(Sehling 1, p. 451):

<!-- doc 44 -->
> XXXVI. Von den stülen in der kirchen. Nach dem auch eine gemeine klage einkommen, das hin und
> wider in die kirchen stüle gebauet, dardurch die leute verhindert, das sie weder den prediger
> auf der canzel, noch zu dem altar, wenn das hochwirdige sacrament ausgeteilet wird, sehen
> können, desgleichen auch oftmals solche stüle in den gemeinen gengen aufgerichtet, das die
> leute für denselben nicht wol hin und wieder gehen können. Derwegen sol in den kirchen auf den
> dörfern und stedten hinfüro kein stuel ohne sonderlich vorwissen und erlaubnis des pfarrers
> und der kirchveter gebauet werden.

XXXVI. Of the pews in the church. Whereas also a common complaint hath come in, that here and
there pews are built into the churches, whereby the people are hindered, that they can see
neither the preacher in the pulpit nor [toward] the altar when the most worthy sacrament is
dealt out; likewise also oftentimes such pews are set up in the common gangways, so that the
people cannot well go to and fro before them: therefore in the churches in the villages and
towns henceforth no pew shall be built without the special knowledge and leave of the pastor and
the churchwardens.

No pew is to be hereditary; it is granted only for the holder's lifetime. At Hadersleben in 1571
Duke Johann ordered the old pews in the church torn out and new ones built, because a corpse
could not be carried up the middle of the church and men and women stood mixed on both sides.
**Hadersleben, *Mandat zu den Kirchenstühlen*, 31 May 1571** (Sehling 23, p. 242):

<!-- doc 1598 -->
> Nachdem unser thumbkirch zu Haderschleben mit stulen dermasen bebawet, das man mit keinem
> leich mitten in der kirchen khommen, sunder mit grosem beschwer dasselbe ander wegen umbher zu
> seiner legerstedte bringen mus, auch sunst diese unordenunge zubefinden, das mans und frawen
> personen uff beiden seiten durcheinander stehen

Whereas our cathedral church at Hadersleben is so built up with pews that one cannot come with
any corpse up the middle of the church, but must bring the same with great trouble by other ways
round about to its resting-place; also this disorder is found besides, that men and women
persons stand one among another on both sides.

Thorn (1599) and Stargard (1596) have whole pew orders (Sehling 4, pp. 244, 520–521).

**Galleries.** The Wittenberg order of 1533 sends the schoolboys who cannot fit into the stall
in the middle of the church up the tower stair to "the north gallery", to sing with those below
and keep quiet. **Wittenberg, *Kirchen-Ordnung*, 1533** (Sehling 1, p. 704):

<!-- doc 148 -->
> mit den andern aber, die in den stuel nicht kommen mogen, soll ein schuldiener ordenlich in
> die thurmthur gehen und in aller stille auf die borkirchen gegen mitternacht steigen, aldo mit
> singen, was man darunten singt, auch vleissig aufsehen, das die kinder stil sein, predig horen
> und nicht büberei treiben.

but with the others, which cannot come into the stall, a school servant shall go in order into
the tower door and climb in all quietness up to the gallery toward midnight, there to sing with
what is sung beneath, [and] also look diligently that the children be still, hear the sermon,
and do no knavery.

Saxony 1580 forbids "levity and mischief with laughing, chattering or the like" on the galleries
(Sehling 1, p. 440).

**Windows.** In the Neumark the craft guilds, which had once kept altars with their panels and
candlesticks, were each to keep up one window in the parish church instead. **Neumark,
*Kasten-Ordnung*, 1 March 1540** (Sehling 3, p. 32):

<!-- doc 1745 -->
> Die fenster. So dan von alters die werke in städten gemeiniglich sonderlich stiefft vorleichen
> gehabt dorzu sie einen altar mit tafeln leuchtern und andern altar geräthe haben erhalten
> müssen, sehen wir vor gut an, dos ein raht einer jeden stadt mit ihren werken handele, das sie
> dagegen ein jedes werk ein sonderliches fenster in der pfarrkirchen für und für erhalten

The windows. Whereas of old the crafts in towns commonly had special foundations granted to
them, whereto they had to maintain an altar with panels, candlesticks and other altar-gear, we
think it good that the council of each town treat with its crafts, that in return each craft
maintain a particular window in the parish church for ever.

Salzwedel 1579 and other Brandenburg visitations order the council to make the guilds repair the
windows they were wont to keep (Sehling 3, p. 285). Nothing is said of what the glass showed.

## 6. Paraments, linens and vessels

### 6.1 Covers for altar, pulpit and font

**White linen.** The orders seldom name individual paraments. What they ask for is that altar,
pulpit and font be decently covered and clean. Anhalt 1562 wants altar and font "adorned with
white linen cloths or tapestries" (§4.5), and Wolfenbüttel 1569 the altar "adorned and clothed
with clean cloths and other ornaments" (§5.4).

**Henneberg 1556.** The Henneberg visitors at Römhild ordered fine covers made "from the
ornaments", that is, from the old vestments, for altar, pulpit and font, with a funeral pall and
a fine little cloth for the communicants. **Henneberg, *Abschied der Visitatoren für die
Superintendenz zu Römhild*, 20 December 1556** (Sehling 2, p. 328):

<!-- doc 1247 -->
> Zum eilften in allen kirchen von ornat lassen schone deck machen umb den altar, predigstul,
> taufstein, leichtuch, ein schön tauftuchlin fur die communicantes

Eleventhly, in all churches to cause fine covers to be made from the ornaments, about the altar,
pulpit, font; a pall; a fine little cloth for the communicants.

The Leiningen-Hardenburg visitation articles of 1609 ask the visitors to inquire whether "their
altar, font, pulpit, desk, etc., are covered as is due". **Leiningen-Hardenburg, *Visitations-
und Täuferordnung*, 1609** (Sehling 19/1, p. 315):

<!-- doc 1080 -->
> 42. Ob ihre altar, tauffstein, predigstul, pult etc. der gebühr bedeckt seyen?

42. Whether their altar, font, pulpit, desk, etc., be covered as is due?

The reading desk (*Pult*) appears here among the furnishings to be covered. No order names a
lectern fall, superfrontal, dossal or runner.

**Old vestments made into altar cloths.** The Sponheim order of 1590/91, a Lutheran order, turns
surplices and Mass vestments into altar cloths, and gives what is of little use to the poor.
**Hintere Grafschaft Sponheim, *Kirchen- und Zensurordnung*, 1590/91** (Sehling 18, p. 653):

<!-- doc 1021 -->
> 15. Crucifix unnd anndere bilder, so noch vorhanndenn sein, soll mann hinweg thun unnd
> abschaffenn, Chorröck oder Chorhembder und Meßgewanndt sollenn zu altaar tucher zugericht
> werden, wo aber sie wenig nuz oder nicht zu gebrauchen, hatt mann es armen leuthen zu gebenn.

15. Crucifix and other images which are still present one shall put away and abolish; surplices
    or choir shirts and Mass vestments shall be made up into altar cloths; but where they are of
    little use or not to be used, one is to give it to poor folk.

### 6.2 Corporal and pall

**Brandenburg 1540.** The corporal and pall appear in the rite for the sick. The priest is to
have a special chalice kept for the purpose, a corporal, the hosts in a case, a vessel for wine,
"a special pall wherewith to cover the table further", a surplice and a light. **Brandenburg,
*Kirchen-ordnung*, 1540** (Sehling 3, p. 80):

<!-- doc 1746 -->
> Der priester aber sol ein sonderlichen kilch, darzu verordent, an eim bequemen ort haben,
> sampt einem corporal, und in einem futter die partes, darzu auch ein gefess zum wein, auch ein
> sonderlich palla, damit er den tisch ferner bedecken, item auch ein korrock und licht.

But the priest shall have a special chalice, ordained thereto, in a convenient place, together
with a corporal, and in a case the particles, thereto also a vessel for the wine, also a special
pall wherewith to cover the table further; item also a surplice and light.

No order in the corpus regulates the purificator, burse, chalice veil, lavabo or credence. The
lavabo went with the offertory, which the evangelical orders dropped.

### 6.3 The communion cloth

**Held by boys or men.** Many Lutheran orders kept the cloth held under the communicants' chins,
"to prevent mishap". At Hohenlohe two honest men go to the altar with a silk cloth and little
kneeling benches. **Hohenlohe, *Kirchenordnung*, 1553** (Sehling 15, p. 71):

<!-- doc 539 -->
> Alsdann sollen zwen erbare menner mit einem seidenim duchlein und kleinen benklin, daruf die
> comunicanten niderknien, sich zum altar verfuegen

Then shall two honest men betake themselves to the altar with a silken little cloth and small
benches, whereon the communicants kneel down.

At Regensburg under Justus Jonas the deacons hold it in surplices. **Regensburg, *Kirchenordnung
unter Justus Jonas*, 1553** (Sehling 13, p. 421):

<!-- doc 440 -->
> Der diaconus aber, so tercius ist, helt das tuechlein auf des priesters seiten, der den leib
> raicht. Derjenig aber, so quartus ist, helt das tuechle bei dem diacono, der das bluet raicht,
> in einem chorrock.

But the deacon who is third holdeth the little cloth on the side of the priest who reacheth the
body. But he that is fourth holdeth the little cloth by the deacon who reacheth the blood, in a
surplice.

At Speyer two boys hold the cloths "for the more reverence's sake". **Speyer, *Kirchenordnung*,
1557** (Sehling 19/1, p. 92):

<!-- doc 1057 -->
> dartzu zwen jungen, so die tuchlein umb meher reverentz willen halten, gepraucht werden
> sollen.

whereto two boys, which hold the little cloths for more reverence's sake, shall be used.

At Thorn boys stand on both sides of the altar with silk cloths, while the priests minister in
chasuble and surplice. **Thorn, *Kirchenordnung*, 1575** (Sehling 4, p. 237):

<!-- doc 1844 -->
> Unter diesem stehen wie im ganzen amte die priester mit gewohnlichen kirchenkleidern, kasel
> und chorrock angethan und reichen dem volke das sacrament des leibes und blutes Jesu Christi,
> da auch zu beiden seiten des altars knaben stehen, die seidene tüchlein halten, unrath
> zuverhüten

Under this the priests stand, as in the whole office, clad in the customary church clothes,
chasuble and surplice, and reach to the people the sacrament of the body and blood of Jesus
Christ; where also on both sides of the altar boys stand, which hold silken little cloths, to
prevent mishap.

**Abolished.** The Palatine Calvinists (Reformed) abolished the cloth with the surplice.
**Kurpfalz, *Amberger Mandat*, 20 January 1567** (Reformed; Sehling 13, p. 303):

<!-- doc 415 -->
> Zum andern, das das tuchlein, so man den comunicanten underhelt, abgeschafft werde.

Secondly, that the little cloth which is held under the communicants be abolished.

The Upper Palatinate visitation of 1579, a Lutheran order of Elector Ludwig VI, orders both
things dropped; *verbleiben* here means "be left off", as the editor notes. **Upper Palatinate,
*Visitationsordnung*, 1579** (Sehling 13, p. 315):

<!-- doc 416 -->
> 3. Das der chorrock und die duchlein hinfort allerding verbleiben sollen, wie sich denn
> churf[ürstliche] pfalz uf etlicher entschuldigung des chorrocks halben expresse dahin erclert.

3. That the surplice and the little cloths shall henceforth be left off altogether, as the
   Electoral Palatinate hath expressly declared itself thereto upon the excuse of some touching
   the surplice.

When the parish of Sallern resisted, the elector ordered the abolition carried through, but only
after the superintendent or pastor had preached to the people "de adiaphoris"
(Sehling 13, p. 315). Nassau-Weilburg in 1617 abolishes the cloth "still held before the
communicants in a few places". **Nassau-Weilburg, *Erläuterungspunkte zur Kirchenordnung*,
1617** (Sehling 10, p. 351):

<!-- doc 199 -->
> 4. Wir seindt berichtet, das noch an etlichen wenig orten den communicanten ein tüchlein
> vorgehalten werde indem sie zue des herren disch gehen, daßelbige soll hiermit abgeschafft
> sein

4. We are informed that still in some few places a little cloth is held before the communicants
   as they go to the Lord's table; the same shall herewith be abolished.

### 6.4 The stock of vestments

**Inventories.** The first Saxon visitations dealt with paraments as property. At Schmiedeberg
in 1528 two chalices and the three best Mass vestments are kept for the Mass; the albs are cut
up for bed sheets in the hospital, and the rest sold for the common chest. **Schmiedeberg,
*Verordnung der Visitatoren*, 1528** (Sehling 1, p. 664):

<!-- doc 132 -->
> Zwen kelch und drei messgewant die besten sollen zum gebrauch der messen behalten, was von
> alben ist zurschneiden in das hospital zur notturft der armen zu bettüchern gegeben, und die
> übrigen alben und kasslen verkauft, und das kaufgelt in den gemeinen kasten

Two chalices and three Mass vestments, the best, shall be kept for the use of the Mass; what
there is of albs, cut up [and] given into the hospital for bed sheets for the need of the poor;
and the remaining albs and chasubles sold, and the purchase money [put] into the common chest.

Zahna 1528 has the same provision (Sehling 1, p. 713).

**No new purchases.** The Homberg synod (Moderate Reformed) forbids any further outlay on altar
paraments, chasubles, copes or mantles; what was spent on them is to go to the poor. A minister
who has a chasuble may use it at the Supper or not. **Hesse, *Homberger Kirchenordnung*, 1526**
(Moderate Reformed; Sehling 8, p. 45):

<!-- doc 2247 -->
> [22] Amodo nullae fiant impensae pro altariorum paramentis, casulis, cappis seu chlamidibus et
> similibus, sed magis dispensentur egenis, quae in his frustra consumi consueverunt. Nimirum
> qui se putat in his colere Deum, sine causa id facit, quod sint purae et vanae hominum
> traditiones. Liberum autem sit habenti casulam eadem in coenae dominicae ministerio uti vel
> non, tamen de caetero haud quaquam emantur

[22] From henceforth let no outlays be made for the paraments of altars, chasubles, copes or
mantles and the like; but let that which was wont to be spent in vain on these be dispensed
rather to the needy. Truly, he that thinketh to worship God in these doeth it without cause, for
they are pure and vain traditions of men. But let it be free to him that hath a chasuble to use
the same in the ministry of the Lord's Supper or not; yet for the rest let them by no means be
bought.

### 6.5 Chalices and beakers

**Two chalices.** Kurland 1570 wants two chalices in every parish, "one for the healthy, the
other for the sick" (Sehling 5, p. 99), and Brandenburg 1540 a separate chalice for the sick
(§6.2).

**Beakers.** The Reformed replaced chalices with beakers. The Casimirian mandate of 1587
(Reformed) orders all but one of the chalices sent to the treasury and silver beakers used
instead (Sehling 19/2, p. 757), and Siegen 1581 (Reformed) orders "a table and a beaker" (§5.3).
At Zweibrücken in 1595, now Reformed, all chalices and patens were to be collected after Easter
and handed to the goldsmiths to make beakers "for the more conformity with other reformed
evangelical churches". **Pfalz-Zweibrücken, *Mandat zur Ablieferung der Vasa sacra*, 7 April
1595** (Reformed; Sehling 18, p. 410):

<!-- doc 998 -->
> Nachdem auch ann statt der kelch, so mann bishero in administrirung des heilgen abentmals
> gebraucht, hinfüro umb mehrer gleichförmigkeit willen mit andern reformirten evangelischen
> Kirchen laut hiemit kommender ordnung silberne Becher gebraucht werden sollen, so ist hiemitt
> unser gnediger befelch, das ir den kirchenscheffner euers ahnbevolhenen ambts ufferleget, alle
> Kelch sambt den Patenen, sie seien waß materi, sie wöllen, zusammen zu bringen

Whereas also in stead of the chalices, which hitherto have been used in administering the holy
Supper, henceforth, for the sake of more conformity with other reformed evangelical churches,
according to the order herewith coming, silver beakers shall be used: so is herewith our
gracious command, that ye lay upon the church steward of your committed district to bring
together all chalices together with the patens, be they of what material they will.

### 6.6 Colours and the seasons

**No sequence of colours.** No order in the corpus prescribes liturgical colours, or a change of
vestments or paraments by season or feast. The only accounts of the seasonal colour sequence are
the editor's notes on the chasuble (for example Sehling 6/1, p. 424). Riga 1530 allows a cope or
chasuble on the high feasts over the plain surplice, and Kurland 1570 "other silken vestments,
chasubles, Mass vestment" on the high feasts "for a change" (§7.1). Neither says anything of
colour.

**Coloured vestments in use.** Inventories show that coloured vestments remained in use and were
even given new. The Mecklenburg visitors of 1578 found at Fürstenberg a red-and-green velvet
chasuble that Duke Johann Albrecht had given that very summer, a red velvet chasuble, two red
damask deacon's coats and two brass candlesticks on the altar. **Mecklenburg, *Ordnung der
Visitatoren für Fürstenberg*, 1578** (Sehling 5, p. 265):

<!-- doc 1927 -->
> Inventarium. Drei silberne verguldete kilch. Zwei silberne verguldete patenen. Eine rote und
> grüne verteilte sampte kasell mit kleinen vergülten spengelin, das kreutz auf dem rugge mit
> der andern zugehorunge. Die hat M. G. F. und herr herr Johans Albrecht etc. diesen itzigen
> sommer zu Fürstenburgk in die kirche gegeben. Eine rote sammet kasell samt der zugehörunge.
> Noch zwei andere alte kaseln, die werden nicht gebraucht. Zwei rote dammaschen diakon röcke
> mit vergülten borten vorsetzet, und sunst das leinen wandt, das man notturftiglich zum altar
> gebraucht. Zwei misinsche leuchter auf dem altar.

Inventory. Three silver gilt chalices. Two silver gilt patens. A red and green parted velvet
chasuble with small gilt clasps, the cross on the back, with the other appurtenances; the same
hath my gracious prince and lord, lord Johann Albrecht, etc., given into the church at
Fürstenberg this present summer. A red velvet chasuble together with its appurtenances. Two
other old chasubles besides, which are not used. Two red damask deacon's coats set with gilt
borders; and otherwise the linen cloth which is needfully used for the altar. Two brass
candlesticks on the altar.

At Sorau in Lower Lusatia, the boys who held the communion cloths wore red choir smocks and
green wreaths on their heads. The extract of the Sorau order of 1595 is known only from a
chronicle of 1710, which lists it among customs "named therein and since fallen out of use".
**Sorau, *Kirchenordnung*, extract after J. S. Magnus, 1595** (Sehling 3, p. 372):

<!-- doc 1795 -->
> 1. An hohen festtagen giengen alle personen, die in der kirche waren, männer und weiber, jung
> und alt zum opfer. 2. Nach geschehener consecration des brods und weins beim heil, abendmahl
> war die elevatio gebräuchlich. 3. Die knaben, so auf seiten des altars die tüchlein hielten,
> hatten rote chorkittel und grüne kränzlein auf ihren häuptern.

1. On high feast days all persons who were in the church, men and women, young and old, went to
   the offering. 2. After the consecration of the bread and wine was done at the holy Supper,
   the elevation was in use. 3. The boys who held the little cloths at the sides of the altar
   had red choir smocks and green garlands upon their heads.

The only other colours prescribed are those of mourning and penance: the black velvet pall with
a white cross on the Mansfeld oath table (§4.5), and the black cloths hired out for funerals at
Breslau in 1528 (Sehling 3, p. 399).

**Veiling in Lent and Holy Week.** The Lenten veiling of images is mentioned in only a handful
of places in the corpus, and always in order to end it. Luther's *Deutsche Messe* rejects the
Lenten veil ("hunger cloth") and the covering of images (§4.1). No order keeps either, and none
prescribes veiling crosses or images in Passiontide.

The Mülhausen preachers' opinion on Holy Week of 1528 (Reformed) shows the other end of the
veiling: the crucifix unveiled and shown at the Good Friday office. They ask that this "may well
be left off, with some chants", together with the burial of the Lord in the sepulchre and the
wooden clappers. **Mülhausen, *Gutachten zu den Gottesdiensten in der Karwoche*, [1528]**
(Reformed; Sehling 20/2, p. 207):

<!-- doc 1380 -->
> Des glichen die begrebniß des herrn yn das grab mitt andern brüchen abgestelt werde, ouch, das
> man nit mitt den hulzernen tafeln klöpfet, sunder wie andere mol mitt den gloggen zu den
> ämptern lüte. Man möcht auch wol das crucifix, so dann an dem selbigen tag in dem ampt zeigt
> wirdt, mitt etlichen gsangen underlassen.

Likewise that the burial of the Lord in the sepulchre, with other uses, be put down; also that
one knock not with the wooden boards, but ring to the offices with the bells as at other times.
One might also well leave off the crucifix which is shown on that same day in the office, with
some chants.

The same opinion stops the washing of the altars in Holy Week, unless a man wishes to wash his
own altar (Sehling 20/2, p. 206).

**Veiled, but removed all the same.** At Regensburg a carved Coronation of Mary on the high
altar had been kept covered, apparently as a compromise. It was nonetheless taken away and
replaced by a painted panel of the "sum of the evangelical preaching". **Regensburg,
*Kirchenordnung*, [1567]** (Sehling 13, p. 481):

<!-- doc 450 -->
> Hernach ist gleicherweis die coronatio Mariae zur himmelkönigin auf dem hohen altar, wiewol
> verdeckt, dennoch hinweggereumt, dargegen aber anstat aufgericht ein gemalte tafel auf
> demselben altar, darin die summa der evangelischen predigten altes und neues testaments zu
> sehen.

Afterward the Coronation of Mary as Queen of Heaven on the high altar was likewise, although
covered, nevertheless cleared away, and in its stead a painted panel set up upon the same altar,
wherein the sum of the evangelical preaching of the Old and New Testament is to be seen.

**Other Holy Week furnishings.** The image plays of the church year went with the veils, as did
the Easter sepulchre, the paschal candle and the blessings of the seasons. For the sepulchre and
the Good Friday rite, see [`EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md`](EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md), §20.4.
**Pfalz-Neuburg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1543** (Sehling 13, p. 96):

<!-- doc 386 -->
> Dargegen sollen unterwegen bleiben erstlich alle spectacula und schauspil, so man mit bildern
> getriben hat als am Palmtag mit dem esel, am Karfreitag mit dem crucifix und grab, am Ostertag
> mit umbtragen des bilds der urstend Christi, am Auffartstag mit aufziehung desselben, am
> Pfingstag mit herablassen der tauben, am Christag mit dem kindlein wiegen und alles, was
> dergleichen allenthalben oder an etlichen fordern orten im brauch gewest ist. […] Man sol auch
> nicht mer weihen oder segnen weihwasser und salz, wie alle sonntag beschehen, noch wachs zu
> Lichtmeß [2. Februar] noch aeschen am Aschermitwoch noch palmen am palmtag noch osterstock
> noch tauf noch feur am osterabent

On the other hand there shall be left off, first, all spectacles and plays which have been
practised with images: as on Palm Sunday with the ass, on Good Friday with the crucifix and the
sepulchre, on Easter Day with carrying about the image of the resurrection of Christ, on
Ascension Day with drawing up the same, on Whitsunday with letting down the dove, on Christmas
Day with rocking the little child, and all that hath been in use of the like kind everywhere or
in some places heretofore. […] Neither shall one any more hallow or bless holy water and salt,
as was done every Sunday, nor wax at Candlemas [2 February], nor ashes on Ash Wednesday, nor
palms on Palm Sunday, nor the paschal candle, nor the font, nor the fire on Easter Eve.

Hohenlohe 1553 repeats the list of image plays almost word for word (Sehling 15, p. 77).

**Greenery and flowers.** The orders treat seasonal greenery as folk custom, not as church
furnishing. The Leiningen-Westerburg discipline order of 1566 forbids, among the "heathen"
customs of Shrovetide and the year, setting up May branches, carrying banners and decking the
church with flowers. **Leiningen-Westerburg, *Kirchenzuchtordnung*, 1566**
(Sehling 19/1, p. 227):

<!-- doc 1073 -->
> So dannoch der Haidnisch wandel getrieben und gehalten würt, nemlich mit pratten heischen,
> Butzen gehn, Lehen außruffen, Mayen stecken, Hagel baum brennen, Johans feuwer machen unnd
> drüber springen, auch andere Gottlose narrenwerck, Fahnen tragen, Kirchen blümen unnd gegen
> dem Wetter leutten ubet, So wöllen Wir, daß solch ergerlich unnd unGöttlich wesen unnd werck
> abgeschafft

Whereas nevertheless heathenish conduct is practised and kept, namely with begging for roasts,
going about in masks, crying out the "fiefs", setting up May branches, burning the hail-tree,
making St John's fires and leaping over them, and other godless fool's-work, carrying banners,
decking the church with flowers, and ringing against the weather: so will we that such offensive
and ungodly conduct and work be abolished.

The Palatine mandates of 1562 (Reformed) likewise forbid the young men and maids to set up May
branches (Sehling 14, pp. 272–273). The one approving mention is at Hof. On the anniversary of
the 1572 dedication of St Michael's, the neighbours strewed the lane with grass and set up "fine
May branches" on both sides for the procession, "to the honour of God and his church"
(Sehling 11, p. 467; see [`EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md`](EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md), §14.3).

**Not found.** No order mentions an Advent wreath, a Christmas tree, a crib set up in church
(only the rocking of the Christ-child, abolished), or Easter flowers. The Advent wreath and the
church Christmas tree belong to later centuries.

### 6.7 The paten, the host box and the bread dish

**The searches.** The corpus was searched for the paten (*Patene*, *Paten*, *patena*, *patina*,
*pateen*; 140 passages once the many godparents, also *Paten*, were set aside). It was also
searched for the vessels beside it: the box for the hosts (*Büchse*, *Lade*, *Schachtel*,
*Kapsel*, *pyxis*, *ciborium*) and the Reformed dish or plate (*Schüssel*, *Teller*, *Platte*).

**An ordinary vessel, kept.** The Lutheran orders kept the paten as they kept the chalice, as a
plain instrument of the Supper. Hannover (1536): "We use the customary vessels for the
administration of the holy sacraments: chalice, paten, etc. For what Scripture doth not forbid,
that we will not let be forbidden us" (Sehling 6/2, p. 1005). The paten was set out on the altar
with the other things:
- **Regensburg (1542):** the epistoler "setteth the chalice, book, particles and paten on the
  altar" (Sehling 13, p. 391).
- **Regensburg (1567):** the priest and the *secundus* carry chalice and paten out of the
  sacristy, and the *secundus* unwraps them and makes them ready on the altar
  (Sehling 13, p. 462).
- **Ritzebüttel (1556):** the priest stands at the altar in the customary Mass vestment, with
  "paten, chalice, bread and wine and burning wax lights" set on it (Sehling 5, p. 558).
- **Gottleuba (1567):** "in the office they use lighted candles on the altar, chalice, paten,
  Mass vestment and all other ornaments" (Sehling 1, p. 567).
- **Danzig (1557):** the sexton is to watch "so long as the book, the can, the chalice and the
  paten stand on the altar", that no harm or theft befall them (Sehling 4, p. 214).
- **Strasbourg (1598):** a box with the particles, a can of wine, the chalice and the paten are
  set on the altar at the beginning of the service (Sehling 20/1, p. 611).

**In the hand at the Words of Institution.** The commonest rubric puts the paten, with as many
hosts as there are communicants, into the minister's hand while he says or sings the words over
the bread. **Kurpfalz, *Gemaine maß*, 1546** (Sehling 14, p. 97):

<!-- doc 472 -->
> Und wann er uf diese wort khompt: name er das brot, so nymbt er die paten, daruf die hostia
> alle liegen, sovil der communicanten, die sich dann zuvor angezeigt, absolvirt und zugelassen
> sein, in die hende und, wann die verba consecrationis vollendet sein, legt ers wider von sich.
> Deßgleichen thut er auch nachvolgents mit dem kelch

And when he cometh to these words, "he took the bread", he taketh the paten, on which lie all
the hosts, as many as there are communicants who have announced themselves beforehand, been
absolved and admitted, into his hands; and when the words of consecration are finished, he
layeth it away from him again. Likewise doth he afterwards also with the chalice.

The same rubric appears in many orders:
- **Livonia (1533):** the priest takes "the bread upon the paten into his hand", turns about
  over the altar toward the people, and speaks the words aloud (Sehling 5, p. 16).
- **Mecklenburg (1540/1545):** after the Lord's Prayer he "taketh the paten with the bread into
  his hand" and sings the Verba, turned toward the altar (Sehling 5, p. 154).
- **The *Cellische Ordnungen* (1545):** the priest lays "as many particles as belong to the
  communicants" in the paten, turns with it toward the people, and says the Verba in German
  (Sehling 1, p. 301).
- **Danzig (1557):** he takes "the paten with the appointed host into his hand without all
  papistical crossing and other gestures" (Sehling 4, p. 168).
- **Neuenrade (1564):** "he taketh the paten with the bread and turneth to the people"
  (Sehling 22, p. 519).
- **Schwarzburg (1574):** "he taketh the paten of the bread, wherein are as many particles as
  there are communicants" (Sehling 2, p. 133).
- **Henneberg (1582):** the minister shall "always have the paten with the particles in his
  hand" when he says "This is my body" (Sehling 2, p. 309).

The number of hosts on the paten was counted to the number of communicants. The Palatinate
(Reformed) wants nothing left over to be reserved (above). Württemberg in 1562 found the
minister in some places repeating the Verba "when there is no more bread and wine on the paten
and in the chalice", and in others not (Sehling 16, p. 436).

**Held under the chin.** At the sick communion of Brandenburg (1540) "the priest shall also
specially hold the paten cleanly under" as he gives the host (Sehling 3, p. 80). Pfalz-Neuburg
(1543) adds "that nothing fall" (Sehling 13, p. 88). The Palatine sick order of 1556 dropped the
instruction (editors' note, Sehling 14, p. 111). In the Mass the same office passed to the
communion cloth held by two men or boys (§6.3).

**Shown, and then not shown.** The paten could be shown to the people with the chalice.
Havelberg (1558) insists that the elevation of the sacrament remain, "much less shall the paten
be elevated in the stead of the chalice" (Sehling 3, p. 230). Hohenlohe abolished the showing in
1596. **Hohenlohe, *Verbesserungen der Kirchenzeremonien*, 1596** (Sehling 15, p. 659):

<!-- doc 628 -->
> Also auch die monstration belangend patinae et calicis under der recitation verborum coenae
> achten wir auch, dieweyl brott und wein in der handlung des abendmalls ohne das auf dem altar
> offentlich aufgestelt sind, das derowegen solche monstratio panis et vmi (weyl es unsere
> gnedige herrschaft je also haben will) soll eingestelt werden

So also concerning the showing of the paten and the chalice during the recitation of the words
of the Supper, we also judge, since bread and wine in the action of the Supper are in any case
set out openly on the altar, that therefore such showing of bread and wine (since our gracious
lordship will have it so) shall be given up.

Nassau-Weilburg's explanations of 1617 likewise call it "unnecessary that the minister, while he
speaketh the words of institution of the holy Supper, layeth hold of the paten and the chalice
severally", and order it stopped (Sehling 10, p. 351). At Hof, before Pangratius's time, the
deacon of the week broke his large host, "left a particle lying in the paten after the old
catholic custom", and after communion dropped it into the chalice (Sehling 11, p. 453).

**In the inventories.** Patens were counted with the chalices:
- Potsdam (1541): "2 patens" beside five chalices; at the chapel of St Gertrude "the patens and
  vestments are burnt" (Sehling 3, pp. 259–260);
- Oschatz (1555): "8 chalices with their patens" (Sehling 1, p. 628);
- Fürstenberg in Mecklenburg (1578): "two silver gilt patens" (Sehling 5, p. 265; §6.6).

The Merseburg visitors of 1544 asked what the church had "in store in patens, monstrances,
chalices and other silver" (Sehling 2, p. 12). Other orders charge the church officers with the
patens and other treasures:
- the *Altermann* of Ermsleben (1564) hands over "the treasures of chalices, patens, albs,
  chasubles" to his successors (Sehling 2, p. 488);
- Pfalz-Neuburg (1576) orders the church doors and windows barred "with strong grilles and iron
  bolts" for the sake of the "chalices, patens and other" treasures (Sehling 13, p. 207);
- the Sponheim visitation order of 1590/91 lists, among the things each church must have, "a
  chalice and a paten for the communion; a capsule or box for the wafers or hosts; a pewter can,
  or where needful two, for the wine" (Sehling 18, p. 655).

**The host box.** The particles were kept in a box (*Lade*, *Schachtel*, *Kapsel*): Sponheim and
Strasbourg (above; both Lutheran). The *ciborium* for reservation went with the monstrance:
Pomerania 1535 needs no ciborium (Sehling 4, p. 342), and Teschen 1584 names "the shutting of
the sacrament into the ciborium" among idolatrous ceremonies (Sehling 3, p. 461). Reservation is
treated in §5.5.

**The Reformed dish.** The Reformed gave up the paten with the chalice:
- **Pfalz-Zweibrücken (1595, Reformed):** all chalices and patens were to be collected and made
  into beakers (§6.5).
- **Ysenburg-Birstein (1598, Reformed):** the ministers are not to use "consecrated chalices,
  patens and altars", but ordinary bread, broken and given into the hands, and "a clean drinking
  vessel" for the wine (Sehling 10, p. 645).
- **Wittgenstein (1605, Reformed):** the Mass vestments, patens and chalices are to be sold to
  advantage and a beaker bought for each place (Sehling 22, p. 149).
- **The dish.** In its place the bread was carried in a dish (*Schüssel*). Micron's London order
  (Reformed), printed with the Emden orders, passes the dishes with the bread among those
  sitting at the table (editors' comparison of the Supper forms, Sehling 7/1, p. 576). The
  Lutheran Marienhafe order of 1593 also names "table cloth, chalice, dish and can" for the
  Supper (Sehling 7/1, p. 710).

**Not found.** No order regulates the paten's material or size, or the veil and burse that go
with it. None mentions the paten's use at the offertory, or the minister's wiping of it.

## 7. Vestments

### 7.1 The Mass vestment kept

**Allstedt 1533.** In Electoral Saxony the Lutheran visitors restored the Mass vestment where
radicals had dropped it. At Allstedt, where Müntzer (Radical Reformation) had celebrated in a
surplice or in ordinary clothes, pastors are to wear alb and chasuble "as is now done at
Allstedt and also at Wittenberg", with two burning lights. These ceremonies serve good
discipline and do no harm. **Allstedt, *Ordnung der Visitatoren*, 1533** (Sehling 1, p. 510):

<!-- doc 53 -->
> So auch etzliche bisanher ohne alben und casulen in einem korock, auch etzliche in schlechten
> kleidern mes gehalten, sollen sie furthin alben und casulen, wie itz zu Alstedt auch zu
> Wittenberg und anderswo gehalten wird, auch gebrauchen, darzu zwei brennende licht unter
> messen auch haben, dann diese ceremonien, welche zu ordnung guter zucht, dienen und
> unschedlich sein

Since also some until now have held Mass without albs and chasubles in a surplice, and some also
in plain clothes, they shall henceforth also use albs and chasubles, as is now kept at Allstedt
and also at Wittenberg and elsewhere, [and] have also two burning lights during Mass besides;
for these ceremonies, which serve for the order of good discipline and are harmless …

**Leisnig 1529.** At Leisnig, where for six years the pastors had consecrated "without surplice
and Mass vestment, simply in the coat", every pastor in the whole district is to use the Mass
vestment whenever he consecrates, conforming to Torgau and Wittenberg. **Leisnig, *Verordnung
der Visitatoren*, 1529** (Sehling 1, p. 605):

<!-- doc 103 -->
> die pfarrer und jenigen, so dem volk das sacrament geraicht, ane korrock und messgewand siecht
> im rock consecrirt, ist ernstlich bevolen, das forthin alle pfarrer nicht allein zu Leisnik
> sunder im ganzen kreis messgewand und andere unschedliche cerimonien, so oft sie consecriren,
> brauchen, und allenthalben nach dem buchlen der visitator, auch dem angefangnem brauch der
> kirchen zu Torgau und Wittemberg sich gleichformig machen sollen.

[whereas] the pastors and those who have reached the sacrament to the people have consecrated
without surplice and Mass vestment, simply in the coat, it is earnestly commanded that
henceforth all pastors, not only at Leisnig but in the whole district, use the Mass vestment and
other harmless ceremonies as oft as they consecrate, and everywhere conform themselves to the
booklet of the visitors and to the begun use of the churches at Torgau and Wittenberg.

**Calenberg 1544/45.** The Calenberg synods order the chasuble and two lights and abolish the
elevation, for uniformity and "for the simple and weak's sake". **Calenberg-Göttingen,
*Synodalkonstitutionen*, 1544/1545** (Sehling 6/2, p. 868):

<!-- doc 2038 -->
> setzen demnach und ordenen, das die celebratio coenae dominicae nicht anders, dann in der
> ordenunge vermeldet, gehalten, die elevatio gar abgeschafft, die mesgewender oder casel
> angezogen und 2 liechte auf den altar gesetzt werden sollen, nicht, das an solchen ceremonien
> etwas sonderlichs gelegen sey, sonder das wir gerne eine gleicheit und ehrbarkeit umb der
> einfeltigen und schwachen willen

[we] appoint therefore and ordain that the celebration of the Lord's Supper be held no otherwise
than is set forth in the order; the elevation wholly abolished; the Mass vestments or chasuble
put on; and two lights set upon the altar: not that anything special lieth on such ceremonies,
but that we would gladly [have] a uniformity and seemliness for the simple and weak's sake.

**Wolfenbüttel 1569.** The Brunswick-Wolfenbüttel order requires the ministers, when there are
communicants, to come to the altar "not only in their customary clothes" but in their church
ornaments. **Brunswick-Wolfenbüttel, *Kirchenordnung*, 1569** (Sehling 6/1, p. 143):

<!-- doc 1974 -->
> pastores und kirchendiener, so messe halten wöllen, wenn communicanten vorhanden sein, nicht
> bloß mit ihren gewönlichen kleidern, sondern in ihrem ornatu ecclesiastico, als alben, caseln
> und meßgewand fein ehrlich und mit grosser andacht und anruffung des Sons Gottes vor den altar
> tretten und das officium missae anfahen, halten und verrichten.

pastors and ministers who will hold Mass, when communicants are present, [shall] step before the
altar not merely in their customary clothes but in their church ornaments, as albs, chasubles
and Mass vestment, finely and honestly and with great devotion and calling upon the Son of God,
and begin, hold and perform the office of the Mass.

**Grubenhagen 1544.** Grubenhagen orders alb and chasuble and lights at the Sacrament, "to the
honour of the sacrament", and the surplice for all other offices in church. **Grubenhagen,
*Kirchenordnung*, 1544** (Sehling 6/2, p. 1032):

<!-- doc 2056 -->
> III. Vom kleyde und lichtern, die man zum sacrament gebrauchen sol. Und in solcher
> administrirung der heiligen sacramenta solen die pfarhern dem sacrament zu ehren alven und
> kassel anziehen und licht auf dem altar brennen, auch in deutsche sprache die episteln und
> evangelia mit einem gewonlichen accentu singen, aber ausserhalbe dem ampte in der kirchen
> sollen der pfarher und opperman ihre korrock anhaben und gebrauchen.

III. Of the clothing and lights which one shall use for the sacrament. And in such administering
of the holy sacraments the pastors shall, to the honour of the sacrament, put on alb and
chasuble and [have] lights burn upon the altar, and also sing the epistles and gospels in the
German language with a customary accent; but outside the office in the church the pastor and the
sexton shall have on and use their surplices.

Thorn 1575 has the priests minister "in the customary church clothes, chasuble and surplice"
(§6.3), and Thüngen 1564 keeps the Mass vestment for peace (§3.3). Hildesheim 1544 keeps
vestments and lights but leaves stole and maniple free (§3.3).

**Riga 1530.** The Riga order makes the variation itself a lesson in freedom: a plain surplice
on Sundays, a cope or chasuble over it on high feasts, since there are still plenty of them,
"that one may see that neither this nor that is needful". **Riga, *Kirchenordnung*, 1530**
(Sehling 5, p. 17):

<!-- doc 1897 -->
> Auf die fest aber, mag er ein chorkappe oder casel (die weil ihr noch genug verhanden ist, und
> doch sonst, wiewol sie viel gelt gekost haben, da verterben) schlecht über das röckel ziehen,
> auf das durch solche stete verwandlung und enderung die freiheit solcher eusserlichen ding,
> destebas vermerckt werd, auf das man sehe, das wider dis noch jens nötig sei, und das eben so
> wenig an schwarze oder blaue röckel, als wenig als an chorröckel oder chormentel oder casel,
> die freiheit des evangeliums gebunden

But on the feasts he may simply draw a cope or chasuble over the little surplice (since there
are still enough of them, and otherwise, though they have cost much money, they spoil there),
that by such constant change and alteration the freedom of such outward things may be the better
perceived; that one may see that neither this nor that is needful, and that the freedom of the
gospel is bound as little to black or blue coats as to surplices or copes or chasubles.

**Kurland 1570.** Kurland follows Riga: a white linen garment or surplice at the altar, any
festal vestment allowed on high feasts, none in the pulpit. **Kurland, *Kirchenordnung*, 1570**
(Sehling 5, p. 99):

<!-- doc 1907 -->
> demnach sollen die priester nach altem, löblichen, gebliebenem gebrauch dieser kirchen für dem
> altar, wenn sie des sontags oder zu andern zeiten die communion halten, in einem weissen,
> leinen kleide oder chor röcklein verkleidet, dem altar dienen […] Wenn man aber auf die
> obnangezeigten hohen festen zur verendrung andere seitgewant, caseln, mesgewand, priesterliche
> kleidung und dergleichen wil für dem altar gebrauchen, ist auch niemands verboten. Auf der
> canzel aber sol man keine verkleidung, darum das es in diesen landen nach der Rijgischen
> kirchenordnung ungewöntlich, gebrauchen.

therefore the priests, according to the old, laudable, remaining use of this church, shall serve
the altar before the altar, when they hold the communion on Sunday or at other times, clad in a
white linen garment or little surplice […] But if on the aforesaid high feasts one will use
before the altar, for a change, other silken vestments, chasubles, Mass vestment, priestly
clothing and the like, it is also forbidden to no one. But in the pulpit one shall use no
vesting, because it is unaccustomed in these lands according to the Riga church order.

### 7.2 The cope when there is no communion

Where there were no communicants, several orders forbade the chasuble, so that the people should
not think the private Mass had returned. The minister wore a cope or a surplice instead.
**Pfalz-Neuburg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1543** (Sehling 13, p. 77):

<!-- doc 386 -->
> so sol der priester nit in gewonlichem meßgewand, sonder in einem chormantel oder nur in
> seinem chorrock für den altar treten und es aller ding halten, wie oben gesetzt ist

then the priest shall step before the altar not in the customary Mass vestment, but in a cope or
only in his surplice, and keep all things as is set above.

**Brandenburg, *Kirchen-ordnung*, 1540** (Sehling 3, p. 71):

<!-- doc 1746 -->
> Es sollen auch, wenn solche empter gehalten werden, die priester kein casula, sondern allein
> ein korkappen oder auf den dörfern, do die korkappen nicht weren, ein schlechten korrock
> anhaben, damit das einfeltig volk nicht auf voriger meinung bleib oder geleitet werde, als
> wolt man die mess nach voriger weiss mit der communion on communicanten widerum anheben und
> halten

The priests also, when such offices are held, shall have on no chasuble, but only a cope, or in
the villages, where there were no copes, a plain surplice; that the simple people abide not in,
nor be led into, the former opinion, as though one would begin and hold again the Mass after the
former manner, with the communion without communicants.

**Schwäbisch Hall 1527.** Brenz allows the deacon a cope at the common prayer, so that church
vesture be not wholly rejected, but not a Mass vestment, "for it is neither a man's nor a
woman's clothing". **Schwäbisch Hall, *Kirchenordnung*, 1527** (Sehling 17/1, p. 50):

<!-- doc 755 -->
> Und das die kirchencleydung nit gar verworffen werde, mocht der diaconus, so sich bezimen
> wolt, des gebets ernst und herlichait zuerzaigen, ain Cormantel anlegen, wie bißhieher zu
> vesper und metten gewont war, aber nit ein meßgewandt, dan es kein mans noch weybs claidung
> ist, sonder zu gar nerrisch anzusehen vor der gemeinen versamlung.

And that church vesture be not wholly rejected, the deacon, if it would beseem him to show forth
the earnestness and majesty of the prayer, might put on a cope, as hitherto was the custom at
Vespers and Matins; but not a Mass vestment, for it is neither a man's nor a woman's clothing,
but altogether too foolish to look upon before the common assembly.

### 7.3 Vestments abolished

**Dalmatic and tunicle.** The Homberg synod of 1526 (Moderate Reformed) forbids the vestments of
deacons and subdeacons outright, since it will not favour orders without warrant in God's word.
**Hesse, *Homberger Kirchenordnung*, 1526** (Moderate Reformed; Sehling 8, p. 46):

<!-- doc 2247 -->
> [24] Dalmaticas, hoc est papisticorum diaconorum vestes aut subdiaconorum nemo de caetero
> induat. Nolumus enim favere ordinibus illis sine Dei eloquiorum testimoniis

[24] Dalmatics, that is, the vestments of the papistical deacons or subdeacons, let no one put
on henceforth. For we will not favour those orders without the testimonies of the oracles of
God.

**Regensburg 1554.** The fullest statement of reasons in the corpus is Regensburg's, written by
Nikolaus Gallus to be read from the pulpit, on why the "Levite and Mass vestments" are to go.
First, both come from the Levitical priesthood and the papal Mass. **Regensburg, *Warum die
leviten- und meßgewand sollen abgetan werden*, 29 April 1554** (Sehling 13, p. 432):

<!-- doc 443 -->
> Ein ehrbar rat hat sampt uns dienern des worts für gut angesehen, das die leviten- und
> meßgewand bei der communion in unser christlichen kirchen alhie wie bei vielen andern hinfort
> genzlich abgetan sein sollen. Dasselbig aus nachvolgenden ursachen: Erstlich, das beide stück
> (wie zum teil die namen noch anzeigen) vom levitischen priestertum des alten testaments und
> von des bapsts messe in unser kirchen kommen und ihre bedeutung auf den zukünftigen Christum
> gehabt haben.

An honourable council together with us ministers of the word hath thought good that the Levite
and Mass vestments at the communion in our Christian church here, as with many others, shall
henceforth be wholly put away. The same for the following causes: First, that both pieces (as
the names in part still show) have come into our church from the Levitical priesthood of the Old
Testament and from the pope's Mass, and have had their signification upon the Christ to come.

Second, the pope still requires them as necessary. Third, they hinder the simple and make it
look as if there were little difference between the papal and the evangelical service. Anyone
still offended after so many years of teaching "is hardly any more to be counted among the
weak". The surplice stays, but only as a mark of the minister. **Regensburg, *Warum die leviten-
und meßgewand sollen abgetan werden*, 29 April 1554** (Sehling 13, p. 433):

<!-- doc 444 -->
> Doch lassen wir leviten- und meßgewand also fallen, das wir noch zur zeit den chorrock lassen
> bleiben, wie wir ihn bisher haben behalten aus freiem willen weder umb des bapstes oder
> interims willen noch, das er ichts zum gottesdienst nötig sein sölte, sondern allein zu
> unterscheid der diener, dieweil sie da im ampt stehen und zwischen der andern gemeine

Yet we let the Levite and Mass vestments so fall, that we still for the time let the surplice
remain, as we have kept it hitherto of free will, neither for the pope's nor for the Interim's
sake, nor that it should be anything needful to the service of God, but only for a difference of
the ministers, while they stand there in office, and the rest of the congregation.

The Regensburg order of [1567] repeats the reasons and adds the stole and the lights.
**Regensburg, *Kirchenordnung*, [1567]** (Sehling 13, p. 480):

<!-- doc 450 -->
> Demnach haben wir auch die kirchenkleidung, als meßgewand, stolen, leviten usw. gleich wie
> auch die liechter, so von anfang der reinen lere gleichwol lange zeit in unser pfarrhe mit
> gehalten worden, entlich sampt den obgemelten, etlichen feiertägen lassen fallen aus ursachen,
> das die ding aus dem alten testament von dem levitischen priestertum, so sein ende hat, mit
> unverstand sind in das neue testament eingefürt und in eitel bapstisch abgötterei gewendet.

Accordingly we have also at last let fall the church vesture, as Mass vestment, stoles, Levite
[vestments], etc., even as also the lights, which nevertheless from the beginning of the pure
doctrine were kept a long time in our parish, together with some of the aforesaid feast days,
for the causes that these things were brought without understanding out of the Old Testament,
from the Levitical priesthood, which hath its end, into the New Testament, and turned into mere
popish idolatry.

**Transylvania 1557.** The Hermannstadt synod holds that burning candles, *infulae* and
ornaments, though indifferent in themselves, have ceased to be indifferent because so many
churches have cast them out as popish dregs. They are to be laid aside in time, once the people
are taught. A seemly white linen garment without images may serve instead. **Hermannstadt,
*Synodalbeschlüsse*, 1557** (Sehling 24, p. 262):

<!-- doc 1675 -->
> Incensio candelarum, infulae et ornatus, etiam si res mediae sint et per se neque prosint
> neque obsint, tamen cum plurimae ecclesiae eas ut feces Papisticas abiecerint et nunc sine
> scandalo retineri non possint ac in numero rerum mediarum propter hoc accidens esse desierint,
> successu temporis, ubi populus edoctus fuerit, verum usum sacramenti non in ista specie
> externa et fulgente consistere […] abiici debent. Cum autem sine aliquo decoro speciali haec
> actio sacra et gravissima peragi non possit, linea veste alba decente, carente idolis id fieri
> potest, ut ex primitivae ecclesiae testimonio manifestum est.

The burning of candles, *infulae* and ornaments, even if they be indifferent things and of
themselves neither profit nor hurt, yet since very many churches have cast them away as popish
dregs, and they cannot now be retained without offence, and by this accident have ceased to be
in the number of indifferent things, ought to be cast away in course of time, when the people
shall have been taught that the true use of the sacrament consisteth not in that outward and
shining show […]. But since this sacred and most weighty action cannot be performed without some
special seemliness, it may be done in a seemly white linen garment, void of images, as is
manifest from the testimony of the primitive church.

*Infula* here most likely means the chasuble, as in other Transylvanian and Hungarian usage, not
the mitre.

**Others.** The Sayn synod of 1575 drops the Mass vestment and keeps the surplice
(Sehling 19/1, p. 356). Anhalt 1596 (Reformed) orders "Mass vestment and chasubles" laid aside
(§5.2), and the Lutheran Sponheim 1590/91 turns them into altar cloths (§6.1).

### 7.4 The surplice

**The minimum.** Where nothing else survived, the surplice (*Chorrock*) usually did. It is the
vestment most often named in the corpus, and the reasons given for it are always the same: to
distinguish the minister in office from the congregation, and to keep the service seemly. Duke
Albrecht's Prussian ordinance of 1543 forbids any pastor to preach, baptize or give the
sacraments without at least a white surplice. **Prussia, *Befehl*, 1 February 1543**
(Sehling 4, p. 60):

<!-- doc 1833 -->
> kein predig thun, taufen oder sacramenta reichen, es sei dann, sie haben zum wenigsten einen
> weissen chorrock an, domit dannoch hierinnen auch eusserlich ein weltlicher unterscheid
> zwischen dem diener des worts, wann er in seinem ambt ist, und einem andern, der nicht darzu
> berufen, zu sehen.

[shall] do no preaching, baptizing or reaching of sacraments, unless they have on at the least a
white surplice, that so herein also outwardly a worldly difference may be seen between the
minister of the word, when he is in his office, and another who is not called thereto.

**Hesse 1532** (Moderate Reformed). The Hessian order uses the surplice "for the people's sake",
to revive a reverence "almost wholly extinguished". **Hesse, *Ordenung der Christlichen
kirchen*, 1532** (Moderate Reformed; Sehling 8, p. 76):

<!-- doc 2252 -->
> und, wen es versamlet ist, ists umb des volks willen fur geschickt angesehen (ut vel sic
> quodam modo plebis reverentiam pene prorsus extinctam alamus), einen chorock zu brauchen

and, when it is assembled, it is thought fit for the people's sake (*that even so we may in some
manner nourish the reverence of the common people, almost wholly extinguished*) to use a
surplice.

**Württemberg 1535.** The preface to the Württemberg order of 1535 grants that clothing makes no
man godly or ungodly, but rules the surplice in for all church offices. It is "not the least
outward ornament of the church" that the minister be known by an honest, distinct garment. Many
weak believers had been put off the gospel by seeing ministers stand "in a stumpy little coat".
**Württemberg, *Vorrede zur Kirchenordnung*, 1535** (Sehling 16, p. 133):

<!-- doc 652 -->
> Von der klaidung im kirchenampt. Die kleidung macht an ir selbs weder frum noch unfrum unnd
> wurdt under die freyen stuck gezelet. […] so soll fürohin alwegen in den kirchen emptern der
> Chorrock gebraucht werden. Dan dieweyll alles in der kirchen zierlich und ordenlich zu ghen
> soll, so ist es nicht die geringst eusserlich kirchen zier, das der jenig, so im brauch des
> kirchenampts vor der gemeinen kirchen versamblung stheet, bey einem erbarn underschidlichen
> claid erkennet werde. Darzu ists offenbar, das vill schwach glaubige on andere ursach, auch
> von wegen diser ursach, das sie den kirchendienern on biß anher gewonliche kirchen cleidern
> allein in einem stumpfen rocklin in den emptern sehen sthen, ein mercklich abscheuhen von der
> predig des hailgen evangelium, auch von brauch der sacramenten uber komen.

Of the clothing in the church office. Clothing of itself maketh neither godly nor ungodly, and
is reckoned among the free things. […] so shall henceforth the surplice be used always in the
church offices. For since all things in the church should go comely and in order, it is not the
least outward ornament of the church that he who standeth before the common assembly of the
church in the exercise of the church office be known by an honest, distinct garment. Moreover it
is manifest that many weak in faith, for no other cause but this cause also, that they see the
ministers standing in the offices without the hitherto customary church clothes, only in a
stumpy little coat, have gotten a notable abhorrence of the preaching of the holy gospel and
also of the use of the sacraments.

The great Württemberg order of 1553 confirms it "until our further decision". **Württemberg,
*Kirchenordnung*, 1553** (Sehling 16, p. 266):

<!-- doc 671 -->
> So mögen wir leiden, das die kirchendiener in allen ämptern, so sie in der kirchen verrichten
> sollen, den gwonlichen Chorrock biß auff ferner unsern bescheid gebrauchen und sonst auch in
> allweg sich einer ehrlichen, gebürlichen kleidung fleissigen, damit nicht allein ir wort und
> predig, sonder auch ir kleidung, weiß und geberde ein leer der tugendt seien.

So may we suffer that the ministers in all offices which they are to perform in the church use
the customary surplice until our further decision, and otherwise also in every way study an
honest, seemly clothing, that not only their word and preaching but also their clothing, manner
and bearing may be a teaching of virtue.

**The Cellische Ordnungen 1545.** The Albertine Saxon ordinances of 1545 make it a rule of
clerical discipline. **Albertine Saxony, *Die Cellischen Ordnungen*, 1545** (Sehling 1, p. 302):

<!-- doc 33 -->
> 2. In ubunge gotlicher ampter in der kirchen söllen die priester allewege ein chorrock
> anhaben, dan es sol in der kirchen decenter venuste et honeste zugehen.

2. In the exercise of divine offices in the church the priests shall always have on a surplice,
   for in the church things shall go decently, comely and honestly.

**Wittgenstein 1563.** Wittgenstein leaves the old vestments free but keeps the surplice "with
one accord", since a minister should be somewhat distinguished from others in his clothing.
**Wittgenstein, *Kirchenordnung*, 1563** (Sehling 22, p. 97):

<!-- doc 1493 -->
> Dieweil es aber gleichwoll billich und ehrlich ist, das ein kirchendiener sein ampt züchtig
> vor der gemeine verrichte und in der kleidung von andern etwas underschieden werde, soll man
> den chorrock darzu eintrechtiglich erhalten und brauchen. Und sollen die pastores sampt den
> kirchenmeistern versehung thun, das die gebreuchliche kirchen ornamenta bestellet und sauber
> gehalten werden.

But since it is nevertheless meet and honest that a minister perform his office modestly before
the congregation and be somewhat distinguished from others in his clothing, one shall keep and
use the surplice thereto with one accord. And the pastors together with the churchwardens shall
take order that the customary church ornaments be provided and kept clean.

**Anhalt 1568.** In Anhalt the surplice is worn at all three Sunday sermons and may be left off
on weekdays. **Anhalt, *Abschied* of Prince Bernhard, 1568** (Sehling 2, p. 570):

<!-- doc 1262 -->
> desgleichen soll des sontags zu allen dreien predigten der chorrock gebraucht, die wochen uber
> auch nachgelassen werden.

likewise the surplice shall be used on Sunday at all three sermons, and over the week also be
let go.

**Limited.** In Ernestine Saxony after 1554 the surplice was dropped in the pulpit but kept at
the altar. **Ernestine Saxony, *Instruction* of the dukes, 1554** (Sehling 1, p. 224):

<!-- doc 21 -->
> idoch solle der chorock auf der canzel zugebrauchen in alwege und an allen orten durchaus
> abgeschaft, aber von dem altar unter der sacrament raichung nochglassen werden

yet the use of the surplice in the pulpit shall be abolished in every way and in all places
throughout, but at the altar during the reaching of the sacrament be permitted.

Coburg in the same year limits it to the communion, worn only by the one who celebrates.
**Coburg, *Vorschaffung*, 1554** (Sehling 1, p. 544):

<!-- doc 74 -->
> Zum sechsten der chorock sol wider zu den begrebnussen noch auf der canzel noch sonsten in
> andern kirchenampten, sondern allain zur communion von der ainigen person welche das ampt
> heldet, gebraucht werden.

Sixthly, the surplice shall be used neither at burials nor in the pulpit nor otherwise in other
church offices, but only at the communion, by the one person who holdeth the office.

**Abolished.** The Palatine Calvinists abolished the surplice with the communion cloth at Amberg
in 1567 (Reformed), and the Lutheran Upper Palatine visitation of 1579 dropped both again under
Ludwig VI (§6.3). The Sayn consistory did the same in 1582, in the same protocol that orders the
remaining images removed (Sehling 19/1, p. 377).

**The black gown.** The Wild- und Rheingrafschaft order of 1603 is the only order found that
prescribes a black gown. Each minister is to have a black church gown with sleeves made "at the
first opportunity" and wear it in his office. White surplices are to be kept where they are in
use, without superstition, and not abolished without the count's knowledge. **Wild- und
Rheingrafschaft, *Kirchenordnung*, 1603** (Sehling 19/2, p. 630):

<!-- doc 1121 -->
> Beßonders aber wollen wir, daß jeder unßerer kirchendiener erster gelegenheit nach ihme ein
> schwartzen kirchenrock mit ermeln zeuge und in verrichtunge seines ampts anziehe. Waß aber die
> weiße chorröck anlangett, wo dieselbige in unßern kirchen in usurpantz und gebräuchlich sein,
> da soll man solche ohn superstition oder aberglauben behalten und ohn unßer vorwißen nit
> abschaffen.

But especially we will that each of our ministers at the first opportunity get himself a black
church gown with sleeves, and put it on in the performing of his office. But as for the white
surplices, where the same are in practice and use in our churches, there shall one keep them
without superstition or superstitious belief, and not abolish them without our knowledge.

### 7.5 What the orders do not mention

The orders never mention the amice, cincture, maniple (except to leave it free at Hildesheim),
tunicle (except under "Levite vestments"), humeral veil, rochet, biretta, zucchetto, galero,
pallium, rationale, episcopal gloves, tippet or hood, nor any preaching bands. No evangelical
order provides a mitre or crosier, and none prescribes liturgical colours (§6.6). See §2.3.

## 8. Clerical dress outside the service

**Long priestly coats.** The orders want the minister to be recognisable in the street as well
as in church. Grubenhagen 1544 adds to its rule on vestments (§7.1) that pastors are otherwise
to go in long priestly coats. **Grubenhagen, *Kirchenordnung*, 1544** (Sehling 6/2, p. 1032):

<!-- doc 2056 -->
> Sonsten sollen sie in zimlichen, ehrlichen, langen pristerlichen rocken oder kleydungen ghan
> und nicht hertretten wie landsknechte und andere wilde junge leute.

Otherwise they shall go in seemly, honest, long priestly coats or clothing, and not stride along
like soldiers and other wild young people.

The Albertine ordinances of 1545 give the same rule more fully. **Albertine Saxony, *Die
Cellischen Ordnungen*, 1545** (Sehling 1, p. 302):

<!-- doc 33 -->
> 1. Die priester söllen eherliche lange cleider tragen, nicht also verkürzt und verstummelt,
> wie die leichtfertigen leute einher gehen, auch nicht zur schnitten ader bunte kleider tragen
> und in götlichen ampten keine ausgeschnittene schue tragen, die do ergerlich seind.

1. The priests shall wear honest long clothes, not so shortened and docked as the light-minded
   folk go about, nor wear slashed or coloured clothes, and in divine offices wear no cut-out
   shoes, which are offensive.

**To be known.** Regensburg [1567], which kept the surplice so that ministers in office might be
known, adds that "outside the office also they should have their honest, priestly clothing for
distinction's sake". **Regensburg, *Kirchenordnung*, [1567]** (Sehling 13, p. 481):

<!-- doc 450 -->
> auch darumb, das die kirchendiener, wenn sie in ihrem ampt sind, in und für der andern gemein
> dester mehr erkent werden, wie sie sonst auch außer dem ampt unterscheids halben ihre
> ehrliche, priesterliche kleidung haben söllen.

also for this cause, that the ministers, when they are in their office, may be the more known in
and before the rest of the congregation, as they should also otherwise, outside the office, have
their honest, priestly clothing for distinction's sake.

The Wild- und Rheingrafschaft order of 1603, which introduced the black gown (§7.4), says the
same. **Wild- und Rheingrafschaft, *Kirchenordnung*, 1603** (Sehling 19/2, p. 630):

<!-- doc 1121 -->
> Dann obwohl die kleidung in der kirchen, auch außerhalb derselben, keinen menschen weder
> heiliger noch unheiliger machen, jedoch weil alles ehrlich unndt zierlich zugehen soll,
> demnach wollen wir, daß unßere kirchendiener in undt außer ihrem ampt sich also mit kleidung
> tragen, daß dem ansehen deß ministerii und kirchendieners, so viel möglich, nichts benohmen
> werde.

For although clothing in the church, and also outside it, maketh no man either holier or
unholier, yet because all things should go honestly and comely, therefore we will that our
ministers, in and outside their office, so bear themselves in clothing that nothing be taken
from the dignity of the ministry and of the minister, so far as is possible.

Württemberg 1553 asks the same "honest, seemly clothing", so that the ministers' dress, manner
and bearing may be "a teaching of virtue" (§7.4). Riga 1530 reminds them that the gospel is not
bound to "black or blue coats" any more than to surplices (§7.1).

**Related.** Freiberg 1537 abolishes "wearing tonsures [and] cowls" among the merely human
ordinances, and Brunswick 1528 wants no tonsure and no special coat for deacons (see
[`MINOR_ORDERS_DEACONS_ELDERS_GUIDE.md`](MINOR_ORDERS_DEACONS_ELDERS_GUIDE.md), §3.3).
