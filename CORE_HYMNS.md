# Core Hymns of Liturgical Life

The hymns, chants and canticles at the centre of liturgical life in the church orders, as listed
so far, with the English title of each in the Hymn Index. The title is the **HYMN TITLE**,
column A of the LCMS Worship Lutheran Hymn Index (`LCMS-Worship-Lutheran-Hymn-Index.xlsx`, once
in this repository's `Hymn Index/` folder), given exactly as the spreadsheet has it. For most
hymns that is the title in LSB, TLH or ELH. For a hymn that none of the later hymnals prints, it
is the first line in an older book: Walther's Hymnal (WH, Matthew Carver's English translation
of KELG, numbered like it), ELHB (1912) or The Lutheran Hymnary (TLHy, 1913).

**Hymnals** gives the hymn's numbers in LSB, TLH and KELG where it has them, and otherwise in
other hymnals, so that its row can be found; the abbreviations are listed at the
[end](#abbreviations). A dash means that the index has no row for the text; its literal English
is given instead. The notes after the tables explain the entries marked with a number. The book
treats these hymns in [Chapter 8](LITURGICAL_AND_ECCLESIASTICAL_LIFE.md#ch8).

## Notes

<a id="note-a"></a>**1. Latin original and German version.** The index keeps some Latin hymns and their German
versions on separate rows, each with its own title in column A.
- *Kyrie fons bonitatis* is "Lord God, our Father, Thou our chiefest stay" (TLHy 43). Its German
  version, "Kyrie, Gott Vater in Ewigkeit", is "Kyrie! God, Father in
  Heaven Above" (LSB 942, where it is titled "Kyrie! God, Father"; TLH 6).
- "Christe, der du bist Tag und Licht" is the German version of *Christe, qui lux es et dies*.
  Its row takes its title from WH and ELHB: "Christ, everlasting Source of light" (KELG 313, ELHB
  34). The Latin hymn has the better-known "O Christ, Who Art the Light and Day" (LSB 882, TLH
  559).
- *Iam maesta quiesce querela* (Prudentius) is "Despair not, O heart in thy sorrow" (TLHy 595).
  Its German version, "Hört auf mit Trauern und Klagen", is "Now Hush Your Cries and Shed No
  Tear" (KELG 408, ELH 480).
- *Veni Creator Spiritus* is "Come, Holy Ghost, Creator Blest". Luther's German version, "Komm,
  Gott Schöpfer, Heiliger Geist", is "Come, God, Creator, Holy Ghost" (KELG 132, ELHB 249).

The index records TLHy 43 and TLHy 595 as probable, not confirmed, translations of their Latin
originals.

<a id="note-b"></a>**2. Luther's creed hymn.** Two hymns open "We All Believe in One True God": Luther's (LSB 954,
TLH 251) and Clausnitzer's (LSB 953, TLH 252). Column A tells them apart by a word added to the
title, "Maker" for Luther's and "Father" for Clausnitzer's. The hymnals print both as "We All
Believe in One True God". The church orders mean Luther's.

<a id="note-c"></a>**3. *Veni Sancte Spiritus*.** The name covers two texts. At Pentecost it is the sequence *Veni,
Sancte Spiritus, et emitte caelitus*, with a stanza of "Nun bitten wir" after every two verses
([§8.8.3](LITURGICAL_AND_ECCLESIASTICAL_LIFE.md#s8-8-3)); TLH 227, "Come Holy Ghost in Love",
is that sequence. At the opening of the service and at ordination
([§13.10.2](LITURGICAL_AND_ECCLESIASTICAL_LIFE.md#s13-10-2)) it is the antiphon *Veni, Sancte
Spiritus, reple tuorum corda fidelium*, sung with a versicle and a collect. The index has no
row for the Latin antiphon. Luther's German hymn on it, "Komm, Heiliger Geist, Herre Gott", is
"Come, Holy Ghost, God and Lord" (LSB 497, TLH 224).

<a id="note-h"></a>**4. Titles in `hymns.db` for hymns the index lacks.** None of the hymnals in the index has "Erbarm
dich mein, o Herre Gott", "Danksagen wir alle Gott" or "Wo Gott zum Haus nicht gibt sein
Gunst". The book's own hymn database, `hymns.db`, gives them English titles: "Have Mercy on Me,
O Lord God", "We All Give Thanks to God" and "Unless the Lord Builds Up the House". No hymnal
here has those titles, so the table gives the literal English instead.

<a id="note-g"></a>**5. Two hymns on Psalm 124.** Luther's "Wär Gott nicht mit uns diese Zeit" is "If God Had Not
Been on Our Side" (TLH 267). Justus Jonas's "Wo Gott der Herr nicht bei uns hält" is "If God
were not upon our side" (KELG 438, ELHB 284). The titles are close, but the hymns are
different.

<a id="note-e"></a>**6. Canticles.** The index lists a canticle wherever a hymnal prints it, so some canticles have
more than one row. The table gives the following rows:
- *Te Deum*: "Te Deum laudamus", the row of LSB's Matins *Te Deum* (p. 223) and of Luther's
  German *Te Deum*, "Herr Gott, dich loben wir" (KELG 337).
- *Nunc dimittis*: "Lord, Now You Let Your Servant", the canticle of TLH's communion service (p.
  29) and of ELH. Luther's hymn on it, "Mit Fried und Freud ich fahr dahin", is "In Peace and
  Joy I Now Depart" (LSB 938, TLH 137).
- *Magnificat*: "My Soul Now Magnifies the Lord" (LSB 934, TLH 275). The index also has "My soul
  doth magnify the Lord" (ELHB 572) and "Magnificat" (HS'98 803).
- *Benedicite*: "All You Works of God, Bless the Lord" (LSB 930). The index also has "All You
  Works of the Lord" (LSB 931, LW 9).
- *Venite* (Psalm 95): only ELHB 575, "O come let us sing".
- *Benedictus*: "Blessed be the Lord God" (ELHB 570). The index also has "Blessed Be the God of
  Israel" (CW'21 954) and "Blest Be the God of Israel" (KELG 152, CW'93 275).

<a id="note-d"></a>**7. Latin chants known through a German version.** The index has no row for these Latin texts,
but it has the German versions of three of them:
- *Da pacem, Domine*: Luther's "Verleih uns Frieden gnädiglich", "Grant Peace, We Pray, in
  Mercy, Lord" (LSB 777, 778).
- *Media vita in morte sumus*: Luther's "Mitten wir im Leben sind", "In the Very Midst of Life"
  (LSB 755, TLH 590).
- *Dies est laetitiae*: "Der Tag, der ist so freudenreich", "Now Hail the Day So Rich in Cheer"
  (TLH 78, KELG 17).

The German version of *Grates nunc omnes reddamus*, "Danksagen wir alle Gott", is not in the
index either.

<a id="note-f"></a>**8. "Sei Lob und Ehr".** The church orders' closing hymn "Sei Lob und Ehr" is "Sei Lob und Ehr mit
hohem Preis", the last stanza of Speratus's "Es ist das Heil uns kommen her"
([§8.15.3](LITURGICAL_AND_ECCLESIASTICAL_LIFE.md#s8-15-3)), so the table gives that hymn's title,
"Salvation Unto Us Has Come". The English "Praise and honour be to the highest good" belongs to
a later hymn, Johann Jakob Schütz's "Sei Lob und Ehr dem höchsten Gut" (1675), which is "Sing
Praise to God, the Highest Good" (LSB 819, TLH 19).

<a id="note-i"></a>**9. The Quempas.** The index files the three parts of the Quempas Celebration in WS'69 (718a–c) on
one row, "He Whom Shepherds Once Came Praising" (LW 54). *Nunc angelorum gloria* is the second
part, 718b, whose own first line is "The glorious angels came today". *Resonet in laudibus* is
in the index only as the name of the tune of the third part, 718c ("God's own Son is born a
child"). The Latin text has no row, and neither has "Joseph, lieber Joseph mein", which is sung
to the same melody.

<a id="note-j"></a>**10. "Ich glaub an Gott".** In the church orders this is the Apostles' Creed sung word for word in
German prose, "Ich glaub an Gott Vater" ([§8.9](LITURGICAL_AND_ECCLESIASTICAL_LIFE.md#s8-9)). It
is not a hymn, and no hymnal prints it as one. `hymns.db` calls it "I Believe in God (Credo)".

<a id="note-k"></a>**11. "So wahr ich leb".** The hymn of the keys is Nikolaus Herman's "So wahr ich leb, spricht Gott
der Herr" (1560), which is *"As Surely as I Live," God Said* (LSB 614, TLH 331). Johann
Heermann's "So wahr ich lebe, spricht dein Gott" (KELG 229) is a later and different hymn.
