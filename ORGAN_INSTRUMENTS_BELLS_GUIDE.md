# Organ, Instruments and Bells in the Sehling Church Orders

This guide covers instrumental music and sounded signals in the church orders of the sixteenth
century printed in Emil Sehling's *Die evangelischen Kirchenordnungen des XVI. Jahrhunderts*. It
asks:

- **The organ.** Was it kept? Did the organist play a prelude before the service or before a
  hymn, interludes between the verses or the parts of the service, or a postlude after the
  blessing? When was it silent? What might the organist not play?
- **Other instruments.** Were instruments other than the organ played in church? If so, which,
  when, and by whom?
- **Bells.** Which bells were rung: tower bells, a choir bell, a little bell or handbell at the
  altar, a Sanctus bell? When were they rung: before church, at the canonical hours, at the
  consecration and elevation, at the Sanctus, during the Lord's Prayer, at the *Gloria in
  excelsis* on Easter Day, during certain hymns, for the dead, at baptisms and weddings, against
  storms?

**How the guide is laid out.**
- §1 answers each question in brief, and §2 sets out the scope and sources.
- §3 covers the organ: its defence and its limits, the prelude, the alternation of organ and
  singers, the postlude, the seasons and rites in which it was silent, and the organist.
- §4 covers the other instruments: trombones, town pipers and fiddlers, the carillon and the
  tower-blower, and the minstrels of weddings.
- §5 covers the bells: their purpose, the summons to church, the daily and prayer bells, bells
  within the Mass, bells at hymns and the litany, the dead, baptisms and weddings, storms, and
  the lesser signals of sermon and alms.
- §6 is a table by order, and §7 a concordance of every order quoted.

**Conventions**

- **Quotations.** Every quotation is Sehling's text as it stands in the `eko.db` database built
  from the digitized edition.
  - Footnote numbers, sigla, folio markers and interleaved apparatus are left out.
  - Sehling's own supplied words in square brackets are kept, as are "[!]" and his other signs.
  - Spelling and punctuation are as printed. A few plain OCR errors are corrected.
  - "[…]" marks an omission.
  - Every quotation has been checked against the database text, allowing for OCR noise: at least
    90 per cent of its words must be found there in order. Its page has been checked against the
    edition's page markers.
  - Each quotation is preceded by an HTML comment, invisible when rendered, that names the
    `eko.db` document it was checked against.
- **Translations.** The English is formal-equivalence, in the idiom of the Authorized Version:
  - Word order and clause structure are kept where English allows.
  - Stock terms are rendered the same way throughout. *Orgel schlagen*, *orgeln* and *up der
    orgel spelen* are "play [on] the organ". *Leuten* is "ring"; *Zeichen* is "sign"; *Puls* is
    "pulse" (a single spell of ringing). *Beiern* is "chime" (striking the hanging bell with its
    clapper, not swinging it). *Glöcklein*, *Schelle* and *campanula* are "little bell" or
    "handbell". *Küster*, *Köster*, *Mesner* and *custos* are "sexton".
  - Words the translation supplies are in square brackets.
- **Citations.** Citations are in the form "Sehling volume, page".
  - The half-volumes of the edition are written 6/1, 6/2, 7/1, 7/2.1, 7/2.2, 17/1, 17/2, 19/1,
    19/2, 20/1 and 20/2, following the digitized volumes.
  - Each order is named by place, title and date as they appear in Sehling's running heads.
    These sometimes differ from the database's record headings. Where a passage comes from the
    editor's introduction or notes rather than from an order, this is said.
- **Traditions.** Most of the orders cited are Lutheran, and they are not marked. Every order of
  another tradition is marked where it is cited, by its name in brackets after the order, as
  "Kurpfalz 1563 (Reformed)". The traditions follow the inventory in
  [`CHURCH_ORDERS_GUIDE.md`](CHURCH_ORDERS_GUIDE.md), §3:
  - **Reformed**: the Swiss, Calvinist and Heidelberg churches, among them the German
    territories that turned Reformed after 1560 and the French and Dutch stranger churches;
  - **Moderate Reformed**: the Bucerian and related orders that stood between the Lutheran and
    the Swiss Reformed: Bucer's Strasbourg and the Upper German cities before the Interim,
    Philip of Hesse's church to 1566, Hermann von Wied's Cologne order, and Colmar after 1578.
  - The mark follows the order cited, not the territory, since a territory could change its
    tradition: Kurpfalz is Lutheran under Ottheinrich (1556) and Reformed from 1563.
  - Orders made under or for the Augsburg Interim of 1548 are marked as Interim orders
    (`CHURCH_ORDERS_GUIDE.md`, §4).

## 1. Summary of findings

**The organ**

1. **Kept by the Lutherans, kept out of the service by the Reformed.** Luther would have "all
   the organs piped" if it served the young (1526). No Lutheran order abolishes the organ.
   Wittenberg's castle church in 1525 limits it to the Sunday *Te Deum* and German songs.
   Strasbourg in 1598 defends "singing and organ-playing" against the Reformed, who cast them
   out as "papistical leaven". Reformed Emden (1594) keeps its organ but uses it only before the
   service, while the people gather, and after it, as they leave (§3.1).
2. **Preludes: yes, of two kinds** (§3.2).
   - **A voluntary while the people gather.** At Strasbourg 1598 the organist begins "at the end
     of the last ringing"; at Emden 1594 (Reformed), "before nine o'clock, when the people gather".
   - **Announcing each piece.** The organist begins a chant or plays the first stanza of a hymn,
     and the choir or people take it up (Gelnhausen [1568], Naumburg 1537/38, Nördlingen 1555,
     Schweinfurt 1576, Hof 1592).
   - Strasbourg requires the prelude to be "even that which the congregation shall afterward
     sing", not "strange pieces and motets".
   - The practice of the organist "preambulating" before every item belongs to the seventeenth
     century (Bischofswerda 1676).
3. **Interludes: yes, chiefly as alternation** (§3.3).
   - **The usual form.** The organ plays a verse or stanza in turn with choir and people, and
     the verse it plays is usually not sung (Regensburg 1553, Naumburg, Nördlingen, Prussia
     1568, Pomerania 1569, Hof). At Regensburg's Vespers, though, "all the verses [are]
     nevertheless sung wholly throughout, for the better understanding".
   - **Limits on the organ's share.** Strasbourg allows "once or at the most twice" in a hymn;
     Reutlingen 1574, one stanza after every two sung. At Bergedorf 1544 the people first sing
     the whole psalm. Schleswig-Holstein 1542 has the German hymns sung "without organ".
   - **Pieces between the parts of the service.** These are separate from the alternation
     (§3.4). Schönburg 1542 has them after the epistle, after the Creed, and after the sermon
     "until the priest come to the altar". Schweinfurt 1576 has one in place of the *Et in
     terra*.
4. **Postludes: yes** (§3.5).
   - **After the blessing.** Strasbourg allows "other pieces" or Latin motets for those who wish
     to stay.
   - **Elsewhere.** Gelnhausen: "Let the organist close with a little piece". Schönburg: "a good
     piece" after communion. Prussia 1568: a piece after the *Nunc dimittis*. Hamburg 1556:
     "before and after the sermon". Emden 1594 (Reformed): as the people leave.
5. **Silent seasons and rites** (§3.6).
   - **Seasons.** Lent or Passiontide (Hamburg 1529, Nördlingen 1555); Advent II to Christmas
     Eve (Hof 1592); Rogation week (Pomerania 1569); lower feasts (Henneberg 1582).
   - **Rites.** The litany (Henneberg 1582); communion (Wolfenbüttel 1543, Schönburg 1542 except
     on high feasts, Reutlingen 1574); baptism (Thorn 1560–1570).
   - **Forbidden music.** Worldly songs, love-songs, dances and galliards (Calenberg-Göttingen
     1542, Hamburg 1556, Pomerania 1569, Stettin 1573, Emden 1577 (Reformed)) (§3.7).

**Other instruments**

6. **Rare, and tied to figural music, courts, towns and weddings** (§4).
   - **Trombones.** "Trombones and other such instruments" accompany the psalms at the Ansbach
     court in 1538, and at weddings in church.
   - **Town pipers and fiddlers.** Danzig in 1612 retains the town pipers (three masters, three
     journeymen) and three fiddlers "for the choir", to play whenever there is figural music.
   - **Emden** (Reformed). The organist-town-piper of 1577 serves with "Christian music and other
     instruments" on feast days, tends the carillon and blows from the town-hall tower. The
     editor's notes add trombone-playing "in the organ" (1579), and a tower-watchman's psalms on
     cornett and trombone (1679).
   - **Wedding minstrels.** Pipes, fiddles, drums and trumpets play the bridal party to the
     churchyard (Hamburg 1529). They must not disturb the sermon (Nassau-Dillenburg 1570,
     Herford 1532), and they are withheld from a bride who has lost her honour (Schwäbisch Hall
     1543/1615).
   - **Not found.** No order names a lute, regal, cornett or trumpet in the service itself.

**Bells**

7. **Purpose.** Bells call people to the Word and to prayer, not to "the pomp of the feasts"
   (Wittenberg 1525, Saxe-Lauenburg 1585). Some orders curbed festal chiming (Danzig 1567,
   Bentheim 1601 (Reformed)); most kept more bells for greater days (Altenburg 1533, Danzig
   1612) (§5.1).
8. **Before church.** The usual summons is three signs, the last "together", or three with one
   bell and a little bell last (Gelnhausen [1568], Esslingen 1540 (Moderate Reformed), Bentheim
   1601 (Reformed)). Danzig 1612 names its seven bells and gives the full Sunday and weekday
   ringing (§5.2).
9. **At the canonical hours and through the day** (§5.3).
   - **Morning and evening, and the prayer bell.** The ringing at morning and evening was kept,
     even where no Matins or Vespers was held (Prussia 1544). It was reinterpreted as a bell
     *pro pace*, for peace, government and against the Turk. Many orders also rang at noon or
     eleven (Saxony 1528, Allstedt 1533, Meissen 1540, Wolfenbüttel 1543, Kurpfalz 1546,
     Lüneburg 1564, Hoya 1581, with its prayer). It also served as a time-signal for workers.
   - **The Ave Maria and Salve.** These names were dropped or the ringing recast (Heilbronn
     1532, Coburg 1554/55, Lichtenberg 1579).
   - **The Friday bell for the Passion.** Kept by some (Kurpfalz 1546, Thüngen 1564), abolished
     with the *Tenebrae* ringing by others (Hohenlohe 1582).
10. **At the elevation and consecration** (§5.4).
    - **A sign with the little bell before the consecration.** Prussia 1525, Riga 1530 and
      Kurland 1570.
    - **Ringing at the elevation.** The Danish *Ordinatio* of 1537 allows it, "the bells
      meanwhile sounding according to custom". The Interim restored it at Ansbach (1548). At Hof
      it lasted until about 1560, "with a threefold pulse of the little bell".
11. **At the Sanctus.** Only Hatzkerode (1534?) rings a bell during the German Sanctus, and that
    is to call the communicants into the choir. There is no "Sanctus bell" in the corpus (§5.4).
12. **During the Lord's Prayer.** Not in the Mass. The Our Father is tied to the bell only in
    the daily prayer bells and the Friday bell (§5.4).
13. **At the *Gloria in excelsis* on Easter.** Not found (§5.4).
14. **During hymns and chants** (§5.5).
    - **The *Te Deum*.** Ringing to call the people to the sermon and Mass (Prussia 1544 and
      1568). All the bells at the Christmas *Te Deum* (Hof 1592).
    - **The litany.** The greatest bell so that those in the field may join in (Hohenlohe 1582),
      or singing "under the ringing" (Regensburg 1567).
    - **Not found.** No order rings during a particular stanza.
15. **For the dead** (§5.6).
    - **Kept as a call to the living.** "That the living also may consider the hour of their own
      dying" (Meissen 1540).
    - **Abolished.** The Saturday ringing "for all faithful souls" (Zwickau 1529, Reuss 1552).
    - **Regulated.** Equality (Ulm, Moderate Reformed), fees by the number of bells (Danzig
      1578), silence during sermons (Herford 1532).
    - **Withheld.** From despisers of the Sacrament (Zwickau 1533) and from unbaptized infants
      (Lippe 1571).
16. **At baptisms and weddings** (§5.7).
    - **Baptism.** A new evangelical custom: a sign with a bell, often the little bell, calls
      the congregation to a baptism held outside the regular service (Heilbronn c. 1529,
      Kurpfalz 1556, Ansbach 1556, Limpurg 1610).
    - **Weddings.** At Hof the bell is rung half an hour later than usual.
17. **Against storms.** Kept as a call to prayer by some, abolished by most (§5.8; see
    `EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md`, §15.5).
18. **Small bells.** A little bell in the choir calls the scholars (Prussia 1568); a little bell
    calls communicants (Haldensleben 1564); a little bell ends the sermon when the hour-glass
    runs out (Zerbst 1545); and a hand-bell accompanies the alms collection during sermons
    (Brandis 1529). Little bells at the Sacrament were rejected by the Reformed (Micron) and at
    Transylvanian sick-communions (§§5.4, 5.9).

## 2. Scope, sources and method

**Sources.** The guide draws on the whole of Sehling's edition as held in `eko.db`: volumes
1–24, including the half-volumes, with the editors' introductions and notes. Passages from an
editor's introduction or note are marked as such, and so are witnesses later than about 1615,
which are cited only for comparison: Bischofswerda 1676, and the Emden tower-watchman of 1679
(Reformed).

**Searches.** The corpus was searched for:
- **The organ and organist**: *orgel*, *orgeln*, *organist*, *organicen*, *organum*,
  *präambulieren*, *schlagen* near a chant or hymn.
- **Other instruments**: *posaune*/*basune*, *zinke*, *pfeifer*, *fidler*/*geige*, *laute*,
  *trommete*/*trompete*, *pauke*, *bunge*, *saitenspiel*, *spielleute*, *instrument*,
  *klockenspil*, *cymbala*.
- **Bells**: *glocke*, *glöcklein*, *schelle*, *campana*/*campanula*, *leuten*/*läuten*/*lüden*,
  *beiern*, *puls*, *zeichen*, *pro pace*, *betglocke*, *Ave Maria*, *Salve*, *wetterleuten*,
  *tenebre*, *schiedung*.

Because *leuten* ("ring") is spelled like *Leute* ("people") in many of the orders, ringing
passages were found by searching for a bell word within a few words of a ringing verb, and of
the Mass item in question (Sanctus, elevation, consecration, *Pater noster*, *Gloria*, *Te
Deum*, litany, baptism, burial, wedding, storm).

**Negative findings.** Several things the guide reports as "not found" were searched for
directly:
- a "Sanctus bell" (*Sanctusglocke* or any compound), a *Wandlungsglocke*, a hand-bell
  (*Handglocke*);
- ringing during the Lord's Prayer of the Mass;
- ringing at the Easter *Gloria in excelsis*;
- ringing at a named stanza of a hymn;
- a regal, a lute, a cornett or a trumpet in the service.

A passage phrased in an unexpected way may have escaped these searches. The negatives mean only
that no such passage was found.

**What the guide does not repeat.** The guide does not repeat what is quoted in full elsewhere;
see the related guides listed at the head of this file.

## 3. The organ

### 3.1 Kept, and defended

**Luther.** Luther's preface to the *Deutsche Messe* names the organ in the same breath as the
bells, as means that may serve the teaching of the young. **Luther, *Deutsche Messe und ordnung
gottis diensts*, 1526** (Sehling 1, p. 11):

<!-- doc 3 -->
> umb solcher willen mus man lesen, singen, predigen, schreiben und tichten, und wo es hulflich
> und fodderlich dazu were, wolt ich lassen mit allen glocken dazu leuten und mit allen orgeln
> pfeiffen und alles klingen lassen, was klingen kunde.

for the sake of such must one read, sing, preach, write and make verses; and where it were
helpful and furthering thereto, I would have all the bells rung thereto, and all the organs
piped, and let sound everything that could sound.

**Wittenberg 1525.** The Wittenberg order for the castle church, a year earlier, is far more
sparing. The bells are a signal, not festal pomp, and the organ, since it is already there, may
sound only at the Sunday *Te Deum* and at German songs. **Wittenberg, *Wie es einer zeit mit den
ceremonien der kirchen gehalten wirt*, 1525** (Sehling 1, p. 699):

<!-- doc 147 -->
> Item campanis utantur ad signum quo significetur quando conveniendum sit ad cantum et quando
> ad audiendum verbum dei. Non vero utantur illis ad pompam illam festorum. Organis (quando iam
> illic sunt) possunt, si voluerint, uti solum dominica die ad te deum laudamus et si quando
> germanica carmina cecinerint.

Item, let them use the bells for a sign, whereby it may be signified when they are to come
together to the singing and when to the hearing of the word of God. But let them not use them
for that pomp of the feasts. The organs (since they are already there) they may use, if they
will, only on the Lord's day at the *Te Deum laudamus*, and whenever they sing German songs.

**The Lutheran norm.** No Lutheran order in the corpus abolishes the organ. Where a church had
one, the orders assume an organist and regulate him. Wolfenbüttel 1543 commends him as "honest,
and good also for the service of the art of music", and lets the pastor, council and treasury
pay him "more or less, according to the worth of his art, for organists are very unequal"
(Sehling 6/1, p. 45).

**Strasbourg 1598 against the Reformed.** By the end of the century the organ had to be
defended. The Strasbourg order of 1598 opens its chapter "Von dem Gesang und den Orglen" against
those who would cast singing and organ out of the church as popish leaven. Sehling's editor
notes that the Reformed are meant. **Strasbourg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1598**
(Sehling 20/1, pp. 602–603):

<!-- doc 1344 -->
> Gleich wie wir nit billichen könden noch sollen den vielfältigen Mißbrauch, welcher in dem
> Bapstumb mit dem Gesang und den Orglen getrieben worden, Also könden wir auch der jenigen
> Fürnemmen nit loben noch gut heißen, Welche das Gesang und das Orglen als eitel Bäpstischen
> Saurteig auß der Kirchen allerdings außmustern. […] Was aber die Musicam figuratam und die
> Orgeln anbelanget, Bringet es die Erfahrung selbst mit, das dadurch der Zuhörenden Hertzen und
> Gemüte ermuntert und erfrischet werden, Gott, den Herrn, auch mit ihrem Mund unnd Gesang desto
> frölicher zu loben und zu preisen. Jedoch soll in allweg dahin gesehen werden, Das solche
> figurata Musica und das Orglen weder das gemeine Gesang der gantzen Kirchen noch den ubrigen
> Gottesdienst mit Predigen und Betten verhindere und zulang auffhalte.

Even as we cannot nor ought to approve the manifold abuse which was practised in the papacy with
singing and organs, so likewise can we not praise nor approve the purpose of those who cast
singing and organ-playing wholly out of the church as mere papistical leaven. […] But as
touching figural music and the organs, experience itself showeth that thereby the hearts and
minds of the hearers are stirred up and refreshed to laud and praise God the Lord the more
joyfully with their mouth and singing also. Yet shall it be in every way looked to, that such
figural music and organ-playing neither hinder nor too long hold up the common singing of the
whole church, nor the rest of the service with preaching and praying.

**The Reformed.** The Reformed orders went the other way. Micron's London order (Reformed)
(German edition 1565) rejects "altars, lights, little bells, vestments" (§5.4). At Reformed
Emden the organ survived in the great church, but it was kept out of the service altogether.
**Emden, *Kirchenordnung*, 1594** (Reformed; Sehling 7/1, p. 489):

<!-- doc 2113 -->
> Des orgels is under dem gesang neen gebruck, ock by dem gadesdenst nicht, ahne allein des
> Sondages vor negen uhren, wenn sick dat volk samlet, und darna, wenn idt tor kercken wedder
> utgaet.

Of the organ there is no use during the singing, nor at the service, save only on Sunday before
nine o'clock, when the people gather, and afterward, when it goeth out of the church again.

The editor adds that the Reformed synods of Dordrecht (1574, 1578) and Middelburg (1581) pressed
for the abolition of organ-playing, and that in some Dutch towns the organ passed to the town
council, which had the organist play at set hours to keep people out of the taverns
(Sehling 7/1, p. 476, editor's note). The Emden order thus gives exactly the two places that
Lutheran orders also gave the organ outside the singing: a prelude while the people gather and a
postlude as they leave.

### 3.2 The prelude

The orders know two kinds of prelude: a voluntary while the congregation gathers, and the
organist's announcement of each piece before it is sung.

**While the people gather.** Strasbourg has the organist begin as soon as the last bell has
stopped and "a fair congregation" is there, and play nothing but what the people are about to
sing. **Strasbourg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1598** (Sehling 20/1, p. 603):

<!-- doc 1344 -->
> Darumb, wann man die Musicam figuratam und die Orgel gebrauchen will, Soll es folgender
> gestalt gehalten werden: Erstlich, das der Organista bei zeit anfange zu ende des letsten
> leutens, als bald ein zimliche Gemein versamlet ist, Wie dann deßgleichen die Senger in allen
> andern Predigten auch thun sollen, wann schon nit georglet oder figurirt wird. Zum andern, das
> er vor und unter dem Gesang der Kirchen nit frembde Stucke und Moteta schlage, Sondern eben
> das jenig, was hernach die Gemeine singen soll.

Therefore, when one will use figural music and the organ, it shall be held after the following
manner. First, that the organist begin betimes, at the end of the last ringing, as soon as a
fair congregation is assembled; as likewise the singers shall do at all other sermons, even when
there is no organ-playing or figural music. Secondly, that before and during the singing of the
church he play no strange pieces and motets, but even that which the congregation shall
afterward sing.

The Emden order of 1594 (Reformed; §3.1) has the same voluntary "before nine o'clock, when the
people gather".

**The organ begins each piece.** In the orders that describe the festal service in detail, the
organ begins the hymn or chant and the singers take it up. At Gelnhausen the organist begins the
*Veni Sancte Spiritus*, plays the first stanza of the German psalm, and plays "a little piece"
after it. The editor glosses "schlage das erst gesetz" as "plays the first stanza".
**Gelnhausen, *Kirchenordnung*, [1568]** (Sehling 9, p. 652):

<!-- doc 2341 -->
> Nachdem man beydt, sommer und winter, umb sieben uhr das erst zeichen, das ander zu halben
> achten, und umb achte zusammen geleutet, hebe der organist (weil der furhanden) das veni
> sancte spiritus an, nachmals singe es der chor aus. Darauff fange der organist einen teutschen
> psalmen an, schlage das erst gesetz, nachmals singe in der chor aus, one abwechselung der
> orgel. Nach dem psalmen schlage der organist ein stucklein, darauff folget die collect und
> lection, darnach fange der organist zuweilen das: Nun bitten wir, zuweilen das: Wir glauben
> all an einen Got an, der chor singe es gar aus. […] Darauff folget die collect und
> benediction. Der Organist beschlies mit einem stucklein.

After the first sign hath been rung at seven o'clock, both summer and winter, the second at half
past seven, and [all the bells] together at eight, let the organist (if there be one) begin the
*Veni Sancte Spiritus*; afterward let the choir sing it out. Thereupon let the organist begin a
German psalm, play the first stanza, [and] afterward let the choir sing it out, without
alternation of the organ. After the psalm let the organist play a little piece; thereupon
followeth the collect and lesson; after that let the organist begin sometimes *Nun bitten wir*,
sometimes *Wir glauben all an einen Gott*, [and] let the choir sing it quite out. […] Thereupon
followeth the collect and benediction. Let the organist close with a little piece.

Schweinfurt 1576 likewise has "the organist begin to play the Sunday Kyrie, which the choir
singeth after", and at the Sunday introit the organist "beginneth to play the introit, which the
choir singeth after, as is customary" (Sehling 11, pp. 647–648). At Hof the organist "always
first plays the melody" of each verse of the German *Magnificat*
(Sehling 11, p. 429; see `OFFICES_GUIDE.md`, §9.4).

**"Präambuliret der organist".** The fullest late example is not a sixteenth-century order. It
is a service order sent by the council of Bischofswerda to its cantor in 1676, printed by
Sehling in a note. The organist "preambulates" before the Kyrie, after the epistle, after the
Gospel and catechism, and before the blessing; at the noon service before the hymn, before the
sermon hymn and before the hour of prayer (Sehling 2, p. 106). It shows where the practice of
the sixteenth-century orders led, but it is late evidence and is cited here only for comparison.

### 3.3 Interludes and alternation

**Alternation.** The commonest use of the organ in the orders is *alternatim*: the organ takes a
verse or stanza in turn with the choir or people, and the verse it takes is played, not sung.
Regensburg is explicit. Sehling's editor glosses the first sentence "that is, these pieces are
not sung", and the communion stanzas "as an interlude". **Regensburg, *Kirchenordnung* of Justus
Jonas, 1553** (Sehling 13, p. 421):

<!-- doc 440 -->
> So aber ein organist vorhanden ist, so schlecht er den introit sambt dem Kyrie eleison, Et in
> terra, Patrem, Sanctus und Agnus Dei auf der orgel, auch sub communione in den teutschen
> gesengen ein gesetz umb das ander. […] Und woe der organist verhanden, so schlecht er einen
> vers umb den andern auf der orgel, doch das gleichwol alle vers durchaus ganz gesungen werden
> umb bessers verstands willen.

But if an organist be present, he playeth the introit together with the *Kyrie eleison*, *Et in
terra*, *Patrem*, Sanctus and *Agnus Dei* on the organ, and also under the communion, in the
German songs, one stanza after another. […] And where the organist is present, he playeth [at
Sunday Vespers] one verse after the other on the organ; yet so that all the verses be
nevertheless sung wholly throughout, for the better understanding.

**Three and four choirs.** Naumburg's St Wenzel order sets out the German songs in alternation
of schoolboys and people, each with its own cantor. When the organ plays there are three choirs,
and when the maidens sing, four. **Naumburg, *Kirchen-Ordnung* for St Wenzel, 1537/1538**
(Sehling 2, p. 72):

<!-- doc 1219 -->
> Und solche gesang alle die deuzsch sein, wen das volk versamlet ist, werden ein chor umb den
> andern gesungen, also der schüler chor ein gesetz singet und anhebt, darnach das volk den
> andern chor helt, welches auch seinen eigenen cantorem hat. Wan man aber orgel schlegt, werden
> drei chor gehalten, und hebt die orgel alwegen an, ausgenommen zum et in terra heben die
> knaben an und darnach die orgel, darauf der chor und dan das volk. Wan aber auch die
> jungfrauen zu chor singen, so werden vier chor gehalten, und singen die drei chor alle gesetz
> der deuzschen geseng, einen vers umb den andern, und schlegt der organist ad tonum darein.

And all such songs as are German are sung, when the people is assembled, one choir after the
other: the scholars' choir singeth and beginneth a stanza, and after that the people keepeth the
other choir, which also hath its own cantor. But when the organ is played, three choirs are
kept, and the organ always beginneth, save that at the *Et in terra* the boys begin and after
them the organ, thereupon the choir and then the people. But when the maidens also sing in
choir, four choirs are kept, and the three choirs sing all the stanzas of the German songs, one
verse after the other, and the organist playeth in between in the [same] tone.

The same passage, almost word for word, stands in Caspar Löner's Nördlingen order as revised in
1555 (Sehling 12, p. 320). Hof's three-choir introit with organ, boys, older boys and girls is
quoted in `PROPERS_GUIDE.md`, §4.5.

**How much the organ might take.** The orders that care most for the congregation's singing
limit the organ's share:
- **Strasbourg 1598**: after intoning, the organist may play "not more than once or at the most
  twice" amid the church's singing (quoted in §3.5).
- **Reutlingen 1574**: two stanzas sung, then one played, "about as long as one of the sung
  stanzas", so that the young and the people learn the hymns. **Reutlingen, *Artikel der
  Schulvisitation*, 1574** (Sehling 17/2, p. 58):

<!-- doc 830 -->
> zu anndern son- unnd feyertagen aber allwegen zwen vers oder zwey gesetz gesungen unnd darauff
> eins (das ohngevar so lang alls der gesungnen gesetz eins weren) georglet solle werden, damit
> der jugent unnd gemeinem volckh die christliche kirchen geseng desto bekantter und sie desto
> eintrechtig unnd gevlißner mitzusingen angereitzt mögen werden.

but on other Sundays and holy days always two verses or two stanzas shall be sung, and thereupon
one (which might last about as long as one of the sung stanzas) shall be played on the organ; to
the end that the Christian church songs may be the better known to the youth and the common
people, and they the more stirred up to sing along with one accord and diligently.

- **Bergedorf 1544**: the organ may play on feast days, but only after the people have sung the
  whole psalm, and only what has been sung. **Bergedorf, *Kirchenordnung*, 1544**
  (Sehling 5, p. 389):

<!-- doc 1950 -->
> Wahr ock orgelen in den kercken weren, die magh men woll an den virdagen gebruken, de psalme
> unde den vorgemelthen kercksangh darup spelen, doch also, dat dat volk de ganzen psalme
> thovoren singe, unde up den orgelen allene, wat gesungen is, spele.

Where also there be organs in the churches, one may well use them on the holy days, [and] play
the psalms and the aforesaid church song thereon; yet so that the people sing the whole psalms
first, and that on the organs only that be played which hath been sung.

- **Schleswig-Holstein 1542**: in the cathedral and collegiate churches the Latin Gloria
  alternates choir and organ, but the German Gloria, the German hymn in place of the sequence
  and the German Creed are sung by "the whole church" straight through "without organ".
  **Schleswig-Holstein, *Kirchenordnung*, 1542** (Sehling 23, p. 146):

<!-- doc 1576 -->
> Averst des Sondages edder yn den Festen, Wann de Prester gesungen hefft: Gloria yn Excelsis
> Deo, Darup de Chor unde nicht de örgeln Antwerden: Et yn terra Pax Hominibus Bona voluntas, so
> schal men gar unde gantz uphören, Darmit de gantze Kercke hernamals singen möge: Allene Godt
> yn der höge sy eer etc., Welcker gesanck ane underlath beth thom ende schal gesungen werden
> ane Orgelent. Darna so vart dat Chor unde orgelen vort tho singende: Laudamus te, Benedicimus
> te etc., beth thom ende, Darna Alleluia. Averst yn stede der Sequentien schal wedderumme
> singen de gantze Kercke einen düdeschen sanck ane underlath unde ock ane örgelen.

But on Sunday or on the feasts, when the priest hath sung *Gloria in excelsis Deo*, and the
choir and not the organ hath answered *Et in terra pax hominibus bonae voluntatis*, then shall
they stop altogether, that the whole church may afterward sing *Allein Gott in der Höh sei Ehr*,
etc., which song shall be sung without break to the end, without organ-playing. After that the
choir and organ go on to sing *Laudamus te*, *Benedicimus te*, etc., to the end; after that the
Alleluia. But in the stead of the sequence the whole church shall again sing a German song
without break and also without organ.

**Organ and the people's hymn at communion.** Pomerania's *Agenda* lets the organ play during
the communion hymns, but only three times: once at the beginning, once in the middle and once at
the end. **Pomerania, *Agenda*, 1569** (Sehling 4, p. 439):

<!-- doc 1865 -->
> Wenn dise gesenge under der communion gesungen werden, schölen de organisten eren gesank mit
> der orgel deste körter maken, unde nene weltlike, lichtverdige gesenge slan. De pastor schal
> vorschaffen, dat de orgeln der maten modereret werden, dat men de düdischen psalme under der
> communion mit der gemeine ganz tom ende singe, unde dat de vörgesetteden gesenge
> ummeschichtich gesungen werden, dat dat chor unde dat volk einen vers umme den andern singe,
> up dat se alle to gelick den schölern unde der gemeine gebrücklick werden, derwegen kan de
> organiste ein mal tom anfange, ein mal im middel, na gelegenheit, einmal tom ende, vor der
> collecta, orgelen.

When these songs are sung under the communion, the organists shall make their song on the organ
the shorter, and play no worldly, light songs. The pastor shall provide that the organs be so
moderated that the German psalms under the communion be sung with the congregation wholly to the
end, and that the aforesaid songs be sung in turn, the choir and the people singing one verse
after the other, so that they all be alike in use by the scholars and the congregation.
Therefore the organist may play once at the beginning, once in the middle as occasion serveth,
[and] once at the end, before the collect.

**Hamburg 1556** lets the organist play "fittingly in between" the verses of the German psalm
sung in place of the alleluia and sequence, "as elsewhere is customary, or, as the time allows,
close the song with the organ" (Sehling 5, p. 552). Stettin forbids its organists to "fantasize
on the organ while other good Christians sing the psalms with the congregation" (§3.7).

### 3.4 Organ pieces between the parts of the service

Beside the alternation, some orders give the organ a short piece of its own between the parts of
the service. The fullest list is Schönburg's. **Schönburg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1542**
(Sehling 2, pp. 170–171):

<!-- doc 1235 -->
> Vor dem introitu kyrie eleison, soll der chor ein kyrie, ein christe und wiederum ein kyrie
> singen und der organista dazu schlagen. An oder für das letzte mag er auch ein gut kurz
> stücklein schlagen etc. […] 3. Nach der epistel schlägt der organist, darauf singt der chor
> das alleluja, zuweilen den sequenz, so fern der rein und christlich ist, darauf die ganze
> ecclesia einen teutschen psalmen oder christlich lied, was man will, wie im gesang-büchlein zu
> finden. 4. Nach dem evangelisten, dem patrem etc. lateinisch schlägt der organist, singet die
> kirche: Wir glauben etc. 5. Nach der predigt schlägt der organista etwas, bis der priester
> über den altar kommt, praeparavitque panem et calicem offerendum. […] 7. […] Der organista
> soll sub communione nicht schlagen, es sei denn an hohen festen; aber nach gehaltener
> communion mag er ein gut stücke schlagen.

Before the introit, [at the] *Kyrie eleison*, the choir shall sing one Kyrie, one Christe and
again one Kyrie, and the organist play thereto. At or before the last he may also play a good
short little piece, etc. […] 3. After the epistle the organist playeth; thereupon the choir
singeth the alleluia, sometimes the sequence, so far as it is pure and Christian; thereupon the
whole church [singeth] a German psalm or Christian song, whichever one will, as is found in the
song-book. 4. After the Gospel [and] the *Patrem*, etc., in Latin, the organist playeth; the
church singeth *Wir glauben*, etc. 5. After the sermon the organist playeth something, until the
priest come to the altar and have prepared the bread and the cup to be offered. […] 7. […] The
organist shall not play under the communion, except on high feasts; but after the communion is
held he may play a good piece.

**Schweinfurt 1576** puts an organ piece in the place of the *Et in terra*: "upon the Gloria,
etc., and instead of the *Et in terra*, etc., the organist playeth some music"
(Sehling 11, p. 647). Gelnhausen's "little piece" after the psalm (§3.2) is of the same kind.

### 3.5 The postlude

**After the blessing.** Strasbourg keeps the organist's freer music, and Latin motets, for the
end of the service, when those who want to listen may stay and the rest may go about their
business. **Strasbourg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1598** (Sehling 20/1, p. 603):

<!-- doc 1344 -->
> Damit sich auch das Orgeln und Singen nit zu lang verziehe, Soll er, nach dem er intonirt, nit
> uber ein- oder zum höchsten zweimal unter das Gesang der Kirchen schlagen, Sondern derselben
> ihre zeit lassen, ihr Gesang mit gemeiner Stimme und Andacht zuverrichten. Wenn aber der
> Gottesdienst allerding verrichtet und dem Volck der Segen gegeben ist, Als dann mag der
> Organist auch andere Stucke schlagen oder auch Lateinische Moteta singen lassen, Dabei dann
> jederzeit ohn zweivel die jenige gern bleiben werden, die Lust und Anmut dazu haben. Und wird
> doch das gemeine Volck, welches lieber seinen Geschüften nachgeht, dadurch nit verhindert noch
> aufgehalten. Wann auch die Gesänge, welche das Volck singet, getheilet werden, das entweder
> der Organist dazwischen schlägt oder ein theil des Gesangs biß nach der Predigt gesparet wird,
> Welches letstere doch allein bei den längeren Gesängen geschehen soll, Soll man die Gesänge
> also theilen, das, was dem Verstand und innhalt nach zusammen gehöret, bei einandern bleibe
> und nit von einandern gerissen werde.

And that the organ-playing and singing be not drawn out too long, he shall, after he hath
intoned, play not more than once, or at the most twice, amid the singing of the church, but
leave it its time to perform its singing with common voice and devotion. But when the service is
wholly performed and the blessing given to the people, then may the organist play other pieces
also, or have Latin motets sung; whereat those will without doubt always gladly stay who have
pleasure and liking thereto; and yet the common people, which would rather go after its
business, is not thereby hindered nor held up. When also the songs which the people singeth are
divided, so that either the organist playeth between, or a part of the song is kept until after
the sermon (which latter yet shall be done only with the longer songs), one shall so divide the
songs that what belongeth together in sense and content remain together and be not torn from one
another.

**Other postludes.**
- **Gelnhausen [1568]**: "Let the organist close with a little piece" (§3.2).
- **Schönburg 1542**: "after the communion is held he may play a good piece" (§3.4).
- **Emden 1594** (Reformed): the organ only "when it goeth out of the church again" (§3.1).
- **Prussia 1568**: at Vespers the organist plays alternate verses of the *Magnificat*, and
  "after the *Nunc dimittis*" plays a piece. **Prussia, *Kirchenordnung und Ceremonien*, 1568**
  (Sehling 4, p. 75):

<!-- doc 1833 -->
> Wo man einen organisten hat, schlecht er einen vers um den andern, das nach dem magnificat der
> chor die deutsch antiphonam singet und der organist nach dem nunc dimittis ein stück schleget.
> Darauf schleusst man mit dem versikel, collekten und benedicamus domino.

Where there is an organist, he playeth one verse after the other, so that after the *Magnificat*
the choir singeth the German antiphon, and the organist after the *Nunc dimittis* playeth a
piece. Thereupon they close with the versicle, collects and *Benedicamus Domino*.

- **Hamburg 1556**: the organists are to play "before and after the sermon" on holy days and
  working days, and at Vespers on all holy eves and days. **Hamburg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1556**
  (Sehling 5, p. 547):

<!-- doc 1963 -->
> Dat organisten, erer kunst wol erfaren, unde ehrlike menner angenamen werden, düsse schollen
> beide hillige dage unde werkeldage in den kerken, dar to acht schlägen geprediget wert, vor
> unde nah der predige, ock to der vesper in allen hilligen avenden unde dagen na des chores
> ordinarii meldinge up der orgelen spelen. De organisten schollen sick mit denen im chore des
> gesanges vergeliken, und eindrechtichlick, dat se im chore anfangen, up den orgelen spelen. De
> organisten schollen sick entholden, werltlike, schimplike unde untüchtige lede in den karken
> to spelende.

That organists well skilled in their art and honest men be taken on. These shall play on the
organs, both on holy days and working days, in the churches where there is preaching at eight
o'clock, before and after the sermon, also at Vespers on all holy eves and days, according to
the direction of the choir's *ordinarius*. The organists shall agree with those in the choir
about the singing, and play on the organs in accord with what they begin in the choir. The
organists shall refrain from playing worldly, scurrilous and unseemly songs in the churches.

### 3.6 When the organ was silent

**Seasons.**
- **Lent and Passiontide.** Hamburg 1529 lets the organ "rest for a time, especially the
  fortnight over, when the Passion is preached" (Sehling 5, p. 513). Nördlingen 1555 lets the
  organ "remain out" in Lent until Easter, except on the Annunciation (Sehling 12, p. 320).
- **Advent.** Hof suspends the organ from the second Sunday of Advent to Christmas Eve. **Hof,
  *Ordo ecclesiasticus*, 1592** (Sehling 11, p. 432):

<!-- doc 294 -->
> Nota. Hac dominica suspenduntur organa usque ad vigiliam Nativitatis Christi.

Note. On this Sunday the organs are suspended until the vigil of the Nativity of Christ.

- **Rogation week.** Pomerania 1569 has "no figural song nor organ" in Rogation week, except on
  the Ascension and its vigil
  (Sehling 4, p. 466; quoted in `LITURGICAL_CALENDAR_GUIDE.md`, §10.5).
- **Lower feasts.** Henneberg 1582 keeps the lower feasts "without organ", and Aschersleben 1575
  sings the Vespers of apostles' days "without the organist" (cited in
  `LITURGICAL_CALENDAR_GUIDE.md`, §10.5).

**Rites.**
- **The litany.** On the Henneberg prayer day of 1582 the German litany is sung "without organ
  or figural music" (Sehling 2, p. 311).
- **The communion.** Wolfenbüttel forbids the organ after the sermon in the Mass, so that the
  Supper may be attended to "in remembrance of Christ". **Wolfenbüttel, *Kirchenordnung*, 1543**
  (Sehling 6/1, p. 45):

<!-- doc 1972 -->
> Süst mach de organiste alle Sundage na willen des pastoris spelen. Overst in der misse schal
> men yd nicht lange maken, dat de predike nicht werde vorhindert und dat de leyen dar ock ruhm
> hebben, Christum mit düdeschem gesange to lavende. Doch na der predike in der misse schal men
> nicht up den orgelen spelen, sondern alleine des aventmals Christi dar wahrnemen tho Christi
> gedechtnisse.

Otherwise the organist may play every Sunday at the pastor's will. But in the Mass one shall not
make it long, that the sermon be not hindered, and that the laity also have room there to praise
Christ with German song. Yet after the sermon in the Mass one shall not play on the organs, but
attend only to the Supper of Christ there, in remembrance of Christ.

Schönburg 1542 likewise keeps the organ silent under the communion except on high feasts (§3.4),
and Reutlingen 1574 drops the organ "wholly" on communion days because the service is then
longer (Sehling 17/2, p. 58).
- **Baptism.** At Thorn baptism is to be held when the church is quiet, not under the singing or
  the organ. **Thorn, *Kirchenordnung*, 1560–1570** (Sehling 4, p. 230):

<!-- doc 1844 -->
> Die taufe soll gehalten werden, wen es in der kirchen stille ist, nicht unterm gesang oder
> orgeln, mit aller andacht und ererbietung, und nicht mit unnützen geschwetz der beistehenden

Baptism shall be held when it is still in the church, not under the singing or organ-playing,
with all devotion and reverence, and not with the idle chatter of the bystanders

**At weddings, by private arrangement.** Hamburg 1529 allows the organ and the scholars' *Te
Deum*, or figural music, at a wedding, "as the people have arranged it with the organist and
cantor for their drink-money" (§4.4).

### 3.7 The organist: what he might not play

The orders do not prescribe repertory beyond the hymns and chants. They forbid the secular:
- **Calenberg-Göttingen 1542**: organists are to set the spiritual songs themselves or obtain
  settings, "for love-songs and worldly songs are not at all fitting to be played in the
  congregation". **Calenberg-Göttingen, *Kirchenordnung*, 1542** (Sehling 6/2, p. 785):

<!-- doc 2036 -->
> Desgleichen sollen auch vleis thun die organisten, das sie die geistlichen lieder entweder
> selbs absetzen oder von anderen abgesetzt uberkomen. Denn bulen und weltliche lieder wollen
> sich in der gemeine zu schlagen gar nicht gebüren aus ursachen, das nach der lere Pauli 1.
> Corint. am 14. alles zur besserung daselbs geschehen sol.

Likewise the organists also shall take diligence that they either set the spiritual songs
themselves or obtain them set by others. For love-songs and worldly songs are not at all fitting
to be played in the congregation, for this cause, that after the teaching of Paul, 1 Corinthians
14, all things there should be done unto edifying.

- **Hamburg 1556**: no "worldly, scurrilous and unseemly songs" (§3.5).
- **Pomerania 1569**: "no worldly, light songs" (§3.3).
- **Stettin 1573**: the visitation's articles for the organists. **Stettin,
  *Visitations-Abschied*, 1573** (Sehling 4, p. 533):

<!-- doc 1883 -->
> III. Weil auch unraht und schade daraus erfolget, wen fremde ohne unterscheid auf die orgel
> kommen, sollen sie niemand ohne vorwissen des pastoris darauf lassen […]. V. Zu rechter zeit
> dieselbigen stimmen und einrichten, nicht wen man beicht höret oder albereit hat angefangen zu
> singen. VI. Sie sollen auch nicht weltliche lieder oder tentze auf der orgel schlagen. […] IX.
> Mit den cantoribus sollen sie auch einmütig sein, nicht oftmals inen zu vordruss zu hoch
> stimmen oder sonst mit unterheulen oder wen andere gute christen die psalmen mitsingen in der
> gemeine auf den orgeln fantasiren. X. Die leute nicht ubersetzen in den hochzeiten, wan sie
> gefordert werden, das sie in den kirchen aufwarten, sondern nehmen, was verordnet ist. XI. Sie
> sollen sich nicht ausgeben vor gemeine spielleute, daraus vorachtung des ministerii folgt.
> XII. Das sie auch das ministerium in allen ehren halten und nicht von der orgel alsbald aus
> der kirchen laufen und gottes wort verachten und verseumen.

III. Since also mischief and harm follow when strangers come upon the organ without distinction,
they shall let no one upon it without the pastor's knowledge […]. V. [They shall] tune and set
the same at the right time, not when confession is being heard or the singing hath already
begun. VI. They shall also play no worldly songs or dances on the organ. […] IX. They shall also
be of one mind with the cantors, not often, to their vexation, pitch too high, or otherwise howl
underneath, or fantasize on the organs when other good Christians are singing the psalms with
the congregation. X. [They shall] not overcharge the people at weddings, when they are required
to attend in the churches, but take what is appointed. XI. They shall not pass themselves off as
common minstrels, from which contempt of the ministry followeth. XII. That they also hold the
ministry in all honour, and do not run straight from the organ out of the church and despise and
neglect God's word.

- **Emden 1577** (Reformed): the town's organist, who was also the town piper, is forbidden "all
  galliards and other light dances" (§4.2).

**Cantor, organist and pastor.** The organist is subject to the pastor (Hamburg 1556, Stettin
1573) and is to agree with the choir about what is sung (Hamburg 1556). His appointment by
contract is treated in `EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md`, §12.3 (Hildesheim 1581).

## 4. Other instruments

**In brief.** Apart from the organ, the church orders mention instruments in church only rarely,
and then for figural music on feasts, at a court, or at weddings. The instruments named are
trombones (*Posaunen*, *basunen*), the town pipers' wind instruments (*Pfeifer*), fiddles
(*Fidler*), and, outside the service, the carillon, the cornett and trombone of the
tower-blower, and the drums, trumpets, pipes and strings of the wedding minstrels. No order in
the corpus names a regal, a lute, a cornett or a trumpet in the service itself, and none names a
"Sanctus trumpet" or any instrumental signal at the altar other than a bell.

### 4.1 Trombones at the Ansbach court, 1538

Margrave George's mandate of 1538 in defence of figural and choral singing tells the clergy that
at his residence at Ansbach the psalms and other scriptural songs are not only sung but
accompanied with trombones and other instruments, as is also done at weddings in church.
**Ansbach, *Figural und coral zu ziemlicher zeit in der kirchen zu singen*, 1538**
(Sehling 11, p. 397):

<!-- doc 292 -->
> So lassen wir auch soliche geseng als nemblich psalmen und ander ding, aus heiliger gotlichen
> schrift gezogen, und nit weltliche, zu unzucht raizende liedle, hie zu Onoltzbach als in
> unserm furstlichen anwesen (zu geburlicher zeit) nit allain singen, sonder denselben gesengen
> mit pusaunen und andern dergleichen instrumenten beistand tun - nit zu unserm oder der unsern
> wollust, sonder zuvorderst, ja auch allein zu der ehre Gottes des Almächtigen, dahin auch
> solichs wie sonst alles anders entlich gerichtet und gemeint sein sölle -, wie auch hie zu
> allen hochzeiten in der kirchen nit unzimblich geschicht und gehalten wurdet.

So also do we cause such songs, namely psalms and other things drawn out of the holy divine
Scripture, and not worldly ditties that provoke to lewdness, here at Onolzbach, as at our
princely residence (at fitting time), not only to be sung, but also have these songs assisted
with trombones and other such instruments (not for the pleasure of us or ours, but first of all,
yea, solely to the honour of God Almighty, whereto this, like all else, shall in the end be
directed and meant), as is also not unseemly done and observed here at all weddings in the
church.

### 4.2 Emden (Reformed): the organist as town piper, the carillon and the tower

In 1577 the council of Emden engaged Paul Hanssen, already the town piper, as organist of the
great church for a year. His contract binds him to the organ, to "Christian music and other
instruments" on feast days, to the carillon, and to blowing from the town-hall tower with a
journeyman and a pupil. **Emden, *Organistenvertrag*, 1577** (Reformed; Sehling 7/1, pp. 475–478):

<!-- doc 2113 -->
> 3. Tom drudden soll he idt orgel mit flyte und guider voirsorge bedienen, und up den festdagen
> mit christlicher musica und anderen darin stemmenden instrumenten, sovele eme moeglich,
> bedienen. […] 4. Item idt klockenspil, alse syther beschehen, up gewonlichen tyden versorgen.
> […] 6. Und dewile he nu dit jair beide dienste togelycke werd bedenen, soll he up seinen
> kosten einen guiden gesellen beneffens sick tor stadt dienst holden, die mit eme und einen
> seiner discipulen, dairto bequem, alle weke twiemal van den raithuißtorn […] 8. Item alle
> galiarden und sunst lichtferdige danse soll he in gene wegen spelen, vele weiniger danzscholen
> holden.

3. Thirdly, he shall serve the organ with diligence and good care, and on the feast days serve
   with Christian music and other instruments according therewith, so far as he may. […] 4.
   Item, [he shall] tend the carillon at the customary times, as hath been done hitherto. […] 6.
   And whereas he will now this year serve both offices together, he shall at his own cost keep
   a good journeyman beside him for the town's service, who with him and one of his pupils fit
   thereto [shall] twice every week [blow] from the town-hall tower […] 8. Item, he shall in no
   wise play any galliards or other light dances, much less keep dancing-schools.

The sixth article goes on to have the tower-blowing done also "through the whole eight days" of
the annual fairs. Sehling's editor adds from the church accounts that in 1579 Paul's brother
Janken was paid "for that he adorned the church with trombone-blowing in the organ", that boys
sang at the high feasts "in the organ" in 1584 and 1587, and that a tower-watchman engaged in
1679 had to blow "a psalm with cornett and trombone" at eleven and at sunset, and a verse of a
psalm or hymn every hour of the night (Sehling 7/1, pp. 476–477, editor's notes). The last is a
seventeenth-century witness, and the instruments in the organ were added to a Reformed service
whose order of 1594 kept the organ out of the singing (§3.1).

### 4.3 Danzig: town pipers and fiddlers in the choir, 1612

The Danzig order of 1612 is the one order in the corpus that puts instrumentalists on the
church's payroll for the service. Beside the choir-men and assistants, the town pipers (three
masters and three journeymen) and three fiddlers are retained "for the choir", and must come
"with their instruments" whenever there is figural music, and obey the *Kapellmeister*.
**Danzig, *Kirchenordnung für Danzig*, 1612** (Sehling 4, p. 212):

<!-- doc 1844 -->
> Noch werden nebenst diesen chorgesellen und astanten der stadt pfeifer, nemlich drei meister
> und drei gesellen zu chor gehalten […]. Item, nebenst diesen pfeifern sein auch zu chor
> bestellet drei fidler; den gibt man semptlich alle quartal 9 M, thut das ganze jahr 36 M. Dess
> haben diese beiderlei spielleute auch zu geniessen, das sie auf hochzeiten für andere
> gefördert werden, und müssen ihnen keine frembde spielleute eingriff thun. […] Für diss
> salarium und accidentien seind beide, die pfeifer und fidler schuldig, allezeit, wen man
> figuriren soll, nebenst dem chorgesellen und astanten mit ihren instrumenten sich fruhzeitig
> einzustellen und auch nebenst ihnen den capellmeister in allen, wie es mit der musica
> verordnet, zugehorchen, und wer hierinnen nachlessig gefunden würde, soll nach erkenntnüss der
> kirchenväter gestrafet werden. Imgleichen sein sie auch zu brautmessen zeit aufzuwarten
> schuldig, sintemal sie von den breutigam ihre besoldunge dafür empfangen.

Further, beside these choir-men and assistants, the town pipers, namely three masters and three
journeymen, are kept for the choir […]. Item, beside these pipers there are also engaged for the
choir three fiddlers; to them together is given every quarter 9 marks, which maketh for the
whole year 36 marks. Both these kinds of minstrels have also this benefit, that they are
preferred before others at weddings, and no strange minstrels may encroach upon them. […] For
this salary and these perquisites both the pipers and the fiddlers are bound, whenever there is
to be figural music, to present themselves betimes with their instruments beside the choir-men
and assistants, and with them to obey the *Kapellmeister* in all things, as is ordained for the
music; and whoever is found negligent herein shall be punished at the discretion of the
churchwardens. Likewise they are bound to attend at the time of nuptial Masses, since they
receive their pay for it from the bridegroom.

The lutenists named in Danzig's ordinances on wedding feasts belong to the feast, not to the
church (Sehling 4, p. 197).

### 4.4 Weddings: minstrels to the churchyard gate

The minstrels of a wedding procession played the bridal party to church. The orders let them
play up to the churchyard and keep them out of the service.

**Hamburg 1529.** The minstrels play before the bride and bridegroom as far as the churchyard;
in church there may be organ and singing by private arrangement. **Hamburg, *Kirchenordnung*,
1529** (Sehling 5, p. 516):

<!-- doc 1960 -->
> also dat vormiddage de brudegam mit den mans voran und dar na de brudt mit orer selschup na
> der karcken gaen, und de spellude spelen vor en her bet an den kerckhof. Dar machme denne in
> der karcken up den orgelen spelen und singen mit den scholeren Te deum laudamus, edder wat
> anders van gade, edder ock in figurativis etc., wo de lude idt denne mit dem organisten und
> cantoren vor ohre drankgelt hebben bestellet. Dar truwet me se denne vor dem hogen altar, wo
> wontlick

so that in the forenoon the bridegroom with the men go before, and after them the bride with her
company, to the church, and the minstrels play before them as far as the churchyard. There one
may then in the church play on the organs and sing with the scholars *Te Deum laudamus*, or
something else of God, or also in figural music, etc., as the people have then arranged it with
the organist and cantor for their drink-money. There they are then wedded before the high altar,
as is customary

**Not during the sermon.** Nassau-Dillenburg bids the wedding party come to church in time, so
that the preacher is not disturbed by their music. **Nassau-Dillenburg, *Visitationsabschied*,
1570** (Sehling 10, p. 112):

<!-- doc 180 -->
> Es sollen sich auch breutgam unnd braut sampt iren freundtschafften unnd geladenen gesten zur
> bestimptten predigstunde zeitlich zur kirchen finden, damit der prediger, so er die predig
> schon angefangen hette, durch das pfeiffen, geigen unnd ander hochzeitlich spiel nicht geirret
> und andere unruig gemacht werden

The bridegroom and bride also, together with their kinsfolk and invited guests, shall be found
at church in good time at the appointed hour of preaching, that the preacher, if he had already
begun the sermon, be not put out, nor others made unquiet, by the piping, fiddling and other
wedding music

**Herford 1532** asks the council to keep everything silent near the church while the sermon is
preached: bells, drums, trumpets and pipes. **Herford, *Kirchenordnung*, 1532**
(Sehling 21, p. 171):

<!-- doc 1442 -->
> Id is ock van noden und themlick, dat se sick entholden und stille sin under dem prediken,
> Alle klocken, id sy den doden offt nicht, Item bungen und trometen, pipen und solckes, dat up
> dem kerckhove offt sus na by der kerken de predikers offt thohorers vorstoren mochte

It is also needful and seemly that they refrain and be still during the preaching: all bells, be
it for the dead or not; item drums and trumpets, pipes and such things as might disturb the
preachers or hearers on the churchyard, or otherwise near by the church

**A sign of honour withheld.** At Schwäbisch Hall a couple who had not waited for their wedding
went to church without music. **Schwäbisch Hall, *Kirchenordnung*, 1543/1615**
(Sehling 17/1, p. 155):

<!-- doc 762 -->
> Braut und Bräutigam, die Ihrer Ehren nit erwartet, sondern darwider gehandelt, und diß
> außfindig worden, sollen ohne Saitenspiel und die Braut mit bedecktem Haupt zur Kirchen gehen.

Bride and bridegroom who have not waited for their honour, but have acted against it, and this
hath been found out, shall go to church without string-music, and the bride with covered head.

**Trumpets and kettledrums at the feast.** The trumpets and drums named in the orders belong to
the wedding procession and feast, and the sumptuary orders regulate or forbid them:
- **Bremen 1546**: all estates may use the council's minstrels, "the first two with the trumpets
  and the other two with pipes and drums" (Sehling 7/2.2, p. 496).
- **Danzig 1590/1595**: the wedding ordinances forbid trumpets, the military fanfare
  (*Feldgeschrei*) and kettledrums at weddings, as "no burgher's merriment" (Sehling 4, p. 195).
- **Nordstrand 1605**: trumpets are wholly forbidden at weddings, christening feasts and other
  feasts (Sehling 23, p. 411).
- **Ulm 1531** (Moderate Reformed): according to the editor, the council's decree of 18 June
  1531 on baptisms and weddings asked that weddings be blessed without any display, including
  music with drums, wind and string instruments (Sehling 17/2, p. 68, editor's introduction).

**The organist as minstrel.** Stettin 1573 forbids its organists to overcharge for playing at
weddings, or to "pass themselves off as common minstrels" (§3.7). Ansbach 1538 (§4.1) and Danzig
1612 (§4.3) show instruments at weddings in church; Hof 1592 has figural music "before and
after" the wedding (Sehling 11, p. 430).

### 4.5 Drums on the tower at Zwickau

The visitors at Zwickau stopped the drumming and carousing on the tower of St Mary's, like the
fencing-school, during the Sunday afternoon sermon. **Zwickau, *Verordnung der Visitatoren*,
1533** (Sehling 1, p. 723):

<!-- doc 152 -->
> Das pauken und queserei auf dem kirchenthurm zu unser liben frauen, desgleichen die
> fechtschul, soll under des nachmittags predigt des sontags und anderer feste absein und nicht
> gehalten werden bis noch vorendung derselben predigt.

The drumming and carousing on the church tower of Our Dear Lady, likewise the fencing-school,
shall cease during the afternoon sermon of the Sunday and other feasts, and not be held until
after the end of the same sermon.

## 5. Bells

### 5.1 What the bells are for

**A signal to the Word and to prayer.** The orders agree that bells exist to call people to the
preaching of the Word and to common prayer. Wittenberg 1525 (§3.1) allows them "for a sign …
when they are to come together to the singing and when to the hearing of the word of God", and
not "for that pomp of the feasts". Saxe-Lauenburg sets out the same principle, then forbids
weather-ringing and secular use of the bells. **Saxe-Lauenburg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1585**
(Sehling 5, p. 423):

<!-- doc 1953 -->
> Und weil die glocken umb des gottesdienstes willen fürnemlich vorordnet worden, dass die leute
> damit zum gehör göttliches wortes und gemeinem gebete gefodert werden, und aber im bapstthumb
> greulicher missbrauch und aberglauben mit den glocken getrieben ist worden, als die
> schreckliche donnerwetter damit von einander zu treiben, so sol solches gottloses wesend
> hiemit genzlichen abgeschaffet, und bei peen und harter ungnade vorboten sein, und sollen auch
> die glocken zu keinem weltlichen gebrauch und sachen gerüret, noch gezogen werden, es geschehe
> denn, dass wegen des feindes oder feursnot, oder wenn die fronendienste den leuten
> angekündiget sollen werden, oder sonsten in nothwendigen gemeinen sachen das volk zusammen
> müste geleutet werden. Und sol solch leuten alles von niemand, denn von dem cüster geschehen,
> weil ihm die glocken amptes halben befohlen sein.

And since the bells were ordained chiefly for the sake of divine service, that the people be
summoned by them to the hearing of God's word and to common prayer, and yet in the papacy
horrible abuse and superstition have been practised with the bells, as to drive asunder the
terrible thunderstorms with them, such godless doing shall hereby be wholly abolished, and
forbidden on pain of penalty and heavy displeasure. And the bells shall not be touched nor
pulled for any worldly use or matter, unless it be that the people must be rung together because
of the enemy or of fire, or when the labour-services are to be announced to the people, or
otherwise in necessary common affairs. And all such ringing shall be done by none but the
sexton, since the bells are committed to him by virtue of his office.

**Against pomp.** Some orders cut back the festal ringing and chiming as a relic of the old
feasts:
- **Danzig 1567**: "the ringing and chiming on the feasts and their vigils, which are not
  appointed and have no ground in God's word", is to be left off by the ringers, "to avoid
  papistical suspicion" (Sehling 4, p. 187).
- **Bentheim 1601** (Reformed): the count renews his mandate against "the unnecessary, manifold
  ringing, and especially the superstitious chiming with the bells", on Sundays and feasts as at
  funerals. **Bentheim-Tecklenburg, *Mandat zum Glockenläuten*, 1601** (Reformed; Sehling 22, p.
  302):

<!-- doc 1508 -->
> daß sie sich hinfürter deß beyerenß mit den glocken durchauß enthalten unnd auff den son-,
> auch den festagenn zu den predigten dreimahl vorher nacheinander nur mitt einer glockenn unnd,
> wan daß beschehen, zu ende uffs letzste mit einem [kl]einem glöckschen, sofern ein solchß
> verhandenn, leuten, auch zu begrebnuß der thodtenn bei der maß, so in ihrer g. kirchenordnung
> außtrücklich angedeütet, verbleiben, unnd sich deß anderen ubermessigen täglichen leütenß
> keineßwegß hinfürter zubrauchen eüsseren sollen

that they [the sextons] shall henceforth wholly refrain from chiming with the bells, and on
Sundays and also on feast days ring to the sermons three times beforehand, one after the other,
with one bell only, and, when that is done, at the end, last of all, with a little bell, if
there be one; and at the burial of the dead keep to the measure expressly set out in his Grace's
church order; and shall in no wise henceforth make use of the other excessive daily ringing

**Rank by bells.** Most Lutheran orders, though, kept the festal ringing as a mark of the day.
At the Altenburg castle chapter in 1533, working days are rung in with one great bell, and
Sundays and feasts "with more bells, to distinguish the time"
(Sehling 1, p. 516; quoted in `LITURGICAL_CALENDAR_GUIDE.md`, §10.7). Danzig 1612 chimes the
great feasts with the three larger bells and the small feasts "with the Osanna" (ibid.).

### 5.2 The summons to church

**Three signs.** The usual summons is three spells of ringing, often with different bells, the
last of them "together" (*zusammen*) with all the bells:
- **Gelnhausen [1568]**: the first sign at seven, the second at half past seven, and all
  together at eight (§3.2).
- **Bentheim 1601** (Reformed): three times with one bell, then a little bell last (§5.1).
- **Esslingen 1540** (Moderate Reformed): a forewarning with the bell used on working days for
  singing, then a second, short sign with the noon bell, after which the sermon begins at once.
  **Esslingen, *Läuteordnung für den Predigtgottesdienst*, 1540**
  (Moderate Reformed; Sehling 17/2, p. 394):

<!-- doc 886 -->
> das man hinfüro uff die sonntäg zu den dreyen predigen vor unnd nach mittags allwegen ain
> vorzaichen mit der singglocken, so man an wercktagen pflegt zu prauchen, ain gutte weil
> leutten und folgents, so man die predigen schier anfahen will, das annder zaichen mit der
> mittag glockenn unnd dasselbig ganntz kurtz leutten soll. Allso wann dasselbig ander zaichen
> mit der bemellten mittag glokken außgeleuttet, das alls balld die predigen angefangen werde.

that henceforth on Sundays, to the three sermons before and after noon, one shall always ring a
fore-sign with the singing bell, which is wont to be used on working days, a good while; and
afterward, when the sermons are about to begin, the second sign with the noon bell, and that
quite short. So that when the same second sign with the said noon bell hath been rung out, the
sermons be begun straightway.

**Restored where it had been dropped.** At Allstedt the visitors of 1533 restored the ringing to
the sermon "where Thomas Müntzer abolished it" (Sehling 1, p. 511; quoted in §5.3).

**Kept plain.** Heilbronn's sexton was told around 1529 to ring only "for the early-Mass sermon
and child baptism, but never more for the office of the Mass and Vespers"
(Sehling 17/1, p. 226, editor's introduction; §5.7). Zwickau 1529 keeps the ringing to sermon,
Mass and Vespers, but "to Vespers and for the dead, also early to the lesson", with one bell
only (§5.6).

**The sexton and the clock.** Ringing was the sexton's office. Hamburg 1529 bids the sextons
"take care that all the bells strike evenly and rightly" (Sehling 5, p. 512). Saxe-Lauenburg
1585 asks the pastors to get village churches a striking clock, or at least a sundial, "or that
the sexton at least have a compass, whereby he may know how to order himself with the ringing
and other things at the due time" (Sehling 5, p. 423).

**The Danzig bells, 1612.** The fullest description of a ring of bells and its use is the Danzig
order of 1612 for St Mary's. **Danzig, *Kirchenordnung für Danzig*, 1612**
(Sehling 4, pp. 202–203):

<!-- doc 1844 -->
> Art. 12. Wieviel glocken auf den kirchenthurm sein, und wie sie heissen, item, wie sie
> gebraucht werden. Die erste und grosseste glocke wird Ave Maria genant von alters, darumb das
> es die beteglocke ist. Sie wird auch Sigismundus und itziger zeit gemeiniglich die grosse
> glocke genant, wird von 12 mann gezogen. Die andere wird Osanna genant, auch die sturmglocke,
> und wird von fünf mann gezogen. Die dritte heisst Apostolica, darumb das auf die aposteltage
> damit gebeigert wird, wie hernach folget, und wird von fünf mann gezogen. Die vierte heisset
> Dominicalis, darumb das sie alle sonntage gebrauchet wird, von vier mann gezogen. Die fünfte
> wird Ferialis genandt, darumb das sie alle tage gebrauchet wird, von zwei mann gezogen. Die
> sechste heisst Sybilla oder Campana Cymbala, darumb das man alle zeit, wenn es von nöten damit
> signiert, und wird von einem manne gedeutet. Die siebende henget über diesen glocken allen und
> wird lange glocke genannt, darumb das sie zu gewisser zeit, entweder eine ganze stunde, oder
> zum kürzesten eine halbe stunde lang geleutet wird. Auch nennt man sie landglocke. Darumb das
> man sie umb die stadt herumb weit auf dem lande hören kan, dieselbe wird von einem mann
> gezogen. […] Am sonntage des morgens, wen der seger viere schleget, wird mit der Dominical ein
> puls von einem schock zügen oder ein wenig länger geleutet, und bald darauf die lange glocke,
> bis es fünfe schlaget, balde hernach wen es fünfe geschlagen, mit der Dominical und Ferial
> zusammen von zwei schock zügen, darnach wen es sechs geschlagen, wird mit der Osanna ein puls
> von zwei schock zügen zur morgen predigt, und den auf segers halb zwölf zu mittage mit der
> Osanna zur mittags predigt.

Art. 12. How many bells there are on the church tower, and what they are called; item, how they
are used. The first and greatest bell is called of old Ave Maria, because it is the prayer bell.
It is also called Sigismund, and nowadays commonly the great bell; it is pulled by twelve men.
The second is called Osanna, also the storm bell, and is pulled by five men. The third is called
Apostolica, because it is chimed on the apostles' days, as followeth hereafter, and is pulled by
five men. The fourth is called Dominicalis, because it is used every Sunday; [it is] pulled by
four men. The fifth is called Ferialis, because it is used every day; [it is] pulled by two men.
The sixth is called Sybilla or *Campana Cymbala*, because one signals with it at all times when
it is needful, and it is sounded by one man. The seventh hangeth above all these bells and is
called the long bell, because at a set time it is rung either a whole hour or at the shortest a
half hour long. It is also called the land bell, because one can hear it far out in the country
round about the city; the same is pulled by one man. […] On Sunday in the morning, when the
clock striketh four, a pulse of three-score pulls or a little longer is rung with the Dominical,
and soon after the long bell, until it strike five; soon after, when it hath struck five, with
the Dominical and Ferial together, of two score-and-sixty pulls [twice three-score]; after that,
when it hath struck six, a pulse of twice three-score pulls is rung with the Osanna to the
morning sermon; and then at half past eleven by the clock, at midday, with the Osanna to the
midday sermon.

The article goes on to the afternoon, the working days, funerals (signalled with the Sybilla,
then rung with the bells the family has paid for), the apostles' days, and the high and low
feasts.

### 5.3 The daily bells: morning, noon and evening, and the prayer for peace

**Kept for prayer.** The morning and evening bell of the medieval *Angelus* (the "Ave Maria")
and the evening *Salve* bell were nearly everywhere kept, but given a new meaning: a call to
pray for peace (*pro pace*), for the government and against the Turk, and a time-signal for
workers. The Prussian order of 1544 keeps the daily ringing in villages even where there is no
Matins or Vespers. **Prussia, *Kirchenordnung*, 1544** (Sehling 4, p. 67):

<!-- doc 1833 -->
> Es ist nicht ein böser gebrauch, das man in dörfern und kleinen flecken nach alter gewonheit
> zu morgen und abends teglich leutet, ob man gleich da selbst weder metten noch vesper helt,
> sondern zur erinnerung dem volk, damit ein eusserlichs denkzeichen zum gebet gibt, es sei in
> heusern ader aufem felde, umb gemeinen frieden und allerlei wolfart zu bitten und zur
> danksagung für alle wolthat des herren gottes, darumb ist es für gut angesehen, das es auch
> also hinfurt teglich gehalten werde.

It is not an evil custom that in villages and small market towns one ringeth daily, after old
custom, at morning and evening, although one holdeth there neither Matins nor Vespers, but for a
reminder to the people, whereby an outward token is given to prayer, be it in the houses or in
the field, to pray for the common peace and all manner of welfare, and for thanksgiving for all
the benefits of the Lord God. Therefore it is thought good that it be kept daily so henceforth.

**Saxony 1528.** The Saxon visitors tell pastors not to quarrel with their people over the
*pacem* ringing, but to teach them its right use. **Saxony (Ernestine), *Unterricht der
Visitatoren*, 1528** (Sehling 1, p. 170):

<!-- doc 9 -->
> Viel pfarherr zanken sich auch mit den pfarrleuten umb unnötige und kindische sachen, als vom
> pacem leuten, und der gleichen. An solchen sachen sollen billich die pfarherr, als die
> vernunftigen, umb friedens willen, den leuten weichen, und sie unterrichten, wo solchs leuten
> unrecht gebraucht, das es nu fort wol gebraucht würde

Many pastors also quarrel with their parishioners about needless and childish matters, as about
the ringing of the *pacem* and the like. In such matters the pastors, as the reasonable ones,
should rightly give way to the people for peace's sake, and instruct them, where such ringing is
wrongly used, that it be well used from now on

**Allstedt 1533.** The *pro pace* ringing stays, but "the abuse with the Ave Maria" is gone, and
the people are to pray for peace and give thanks. **Allstedt, *Ordnung der Visitatoren für die
Stadt und das Amt Allstedt*, 1533** (Sehling 1, p. 511):

<!-- doc 53 -->
> Wo es auch in den dorfern gewonheit ist gewesen, das man des morgens oder des abents pro pace
> hat geleutet, do sollen es die pfarrer nochmals bleiben lassen, doch sollen sie das volk
> daneben vormanen, das sie als denn gott umb einen zeitlichen friede mit ernst und vleis bitten
> wollen, auch darneben fur alle gnade und wolthat danksagung thun, nachdem der misbrauch mit
> dem ave Maria abgethan, dann solch leuten nicht vor einen gottes dienst wie etwan gehalten
> wird, sondern das dadurch das volk zu danksagung und wie berurt umb friede zu bieten ermanet
> sei, und daran unterschied des tages zu erkennen. Wo auch Thomas Müntzer das leuten zur
> predigt abgeschaft, do sol man es, wie vor alters geschehen wiederumb anfahen und nicht
> unterlassen

Where also it hath been the custom in the villages to ring *pro pace* in the morning or the
evening, there the pastors shall let it still remain; yet they shall withal admonish the people
that they then pray God earnestly and diligently for temporal peace, and also give thanks for
all grace and benefits, since the abuse with the Ave Maria hath been done away; for such ringing
is not held for a divine service, as it once was, but that thereby the people be admonished to
thanksgiving and, as aforesaid, to pray for peace, and thereby to know the division of the day.
Where also Thomas Müntzer abolished the ringing to the sermon, there one shall begin it again as
was done of old, and not leave it off

**The Palatinate 1546: peace, workers and the Passion.** **Kurpfalz, provisional
*Kirchenordnung*, 1546** (Sehling 14, p. 96):

<!-- doc 472 -->
> Es scheinet auch nutzlich, das morgens und abents die glocken pro pace geleut werde, wie auch
> die alten gethon haben, und das volck darbei erindert wurde, fur gemeinen friden Got zu
> bitten, und auch die tagloner bei solchem zaichen in und aus irer arbeit giengen, item uf den
> mittag zu ailfen oder zum wenigsten uf den freytag die glock geleut würdet, zu erindern das
> volck des leidens Cristi.

It seemeth also profitable that the bell be rung *pro pace* morning and evening, as the ancients
also did, and that the people be thereby reminded to pray God for the common peace, and also
that the day-labourers go to and from their work at such a sign; item, that the bell be rung at
midday at eleven, or at the least on Friday, to remind the people of the sufferings of Christ.

**Noon and the Turk.** Wolfenbüttel moves the *pro pace* to eleven o'clock daily, as a prayer
for peace, good government and against the Turk. **Wolfenbüttel, *Kirchenordnung*, 1543**
(Sehling 6/1, p. 71):

<!-- doc 1972 -->
> Pro pace. Alle middage, wenn de klocke elfen sleyt, schal men in jeweliker parkercken de
> klocke slan edder lüden pro pace, dat is, dat de lüde dardorch vermanet werden, tho biddende
> Got umme frede und gut regiment, darmit wy ock bidden vor de overicheit wedder unse vyende,
> wedder den Türken etc., darane is uns vele gelegen.

*Pro pace.* Every midday, when the clock striketh eleven, one shall strike or ring the bell in
every parish church *pro pace*; that is, that the people be thereby admonished to pray God for
peace and good government, whereby we also pray for the magistrates against our enemies, against
the Turk, etc., wherein much lieth for us.

**The three daily prayer bells.** Lüneburg and Hoya ring morning, noon and evening. **Lüneburg,
*Kirchenordnung*, 1564** (Sehling 6/1, p. 552):

<!-- doc 2003 -->
> Weil auch gebreuchlich, das die betglocken abends, morgens und zu mittage geleutet werden, so
> sol das volk vermanet werden, wenn solchs geschicht, das sie ihr gebet zu Gott dem Herrn für
> friede und alle zeitliche und ewige wolfart thun und ihr gesinde auch darzu halten, sie sein
> im hause, im felde oder wo einer ist, und das sie sich des nicht schemen

Since it is also customary that the prayer bells be rung at evening, morning and midday, the
people shall be admonished, when that is done, to make their prayer to God the Lord for peace
and all temporal and eternal welfare, and to hold their household thereto also, whether they be
in the house, in the field, or wherever one is, and that they be not ashamed of it

Hoya gives the prayer to be said "upon the ringing *pro pace*". **Hoya, *Kirchenordnung*, 1581**
(Sehling 6/2, p. 1143):

<!-- doc 2065 -->
> Beware uns, lieber himlischer Vater, und deine ganze christenheit diesen tag oder nacht und
> allezeit für allem ubel, für des leidigen teufels drauen, für einem schnellen, bösen,
> unversehenen todt, für greuliche plage unsers leibes, für feur und wassersnoth und für allen
> unsern feinden, als Türken, Moscowiter, babst und allen ketzern und tyrannen und für aller
> gefehrligkeit, verleihe gnediglich deinen segen und friede deinen kindern, erhalt auch unsere
> pastorn und obrigkeit sampt allen haushaltern, auf das wir dich recht erkennen, fürchten und
> vertrauen, in ruhe und friede leben und deinen segen bey uns entpfinden mögen. Amen

Keep us, dear heavenly Father, and thy whole Christendom, this day or night and at all times,
from all evil, from the threatenings of the wretched devil, from a sudden, evil, unforeseen
death, from grievous plague of our body, from fire and flood, and from all our enemies, as the
Turks, Muscovites, the pope and all heretics and tyrants, and from all danger. Graciously grant
thy blessing and peace to thy children; preserve also our pastors and magistrates, together with
all householders, that we may rightly know, fear and trust thee, live in rest and peace, and
feel thy blessing among us. Amen

**The Ave and Salve abolished or recast.**
- **Coburg 1554/55**: the three pulses morning and evening "which in the papacy were called the
  Ave Maria and Salve" are to cease, with the weather-ringing, "so that we be not counted
  papistical"; but the magistrate may have one pulse rung for workers or for shutting the gates.
  **Coburg, *Vorschaffung* of the visitation, 1554/55** (Sehling 1, p. 544):

<!-- doc 74 -->
> Zum sibenden, das die drei puls mit der glocken zu morgends und abents, welchs man im babstumb
> das Ave Maria und salve genant, sol umb der frembden und benachbarten leute willen, domit man
> uns nicht vor bebstisch achte, desgleichen das geleut zum wetter vorbleiben. Do aber die
> obrikait zur morgenstunde umb der arbeiter leut willen oder zum thorschlissen einen puls
> wolten thuen lassen, sol inen frei stehen.

Seventhly, that the three pulses with the bell morning and evening, which in the papacy were
called the Ave Maria and Salve, shall cease for the sake of strangers and neighbouring folk, so
that we be not counted papistical; likewise the ringing for the weather. But if the magistrates
would have one pulse rung at the morning hour for the sake of the working people, or for the
shutting of the gates, it shall be free to them.

- **Heilbronn 1532**: the council abolished the evening *Salve* but kept the ringing at the same
  hour, followed by singing and prayer for peace, with a sign rung on the prayer bell.
  **Heilbronn, *Gottesdienstordnung*, 1532** (Sehling 17/1, p. 305):

<!-- doc 779 -->
> Dweyll ain ersamer radt das salve zu abent zeyt abgestelt, hat ir weyßhait an die stat alle
> wercktag zur selben abent zeyt wie bißher zum salve zu leutten, und, so ußgeleut, die
> lateinischs und teutschs schulmaister oder ir vicarii und schuler erscheinen sollen, ain
> gesang umb gmain friden zu halten, darbey auch ain diacon erscheinen und das beyweßend volck,
> umb gmain friden zu bitten, ermanen, mittel sollichs bets mit der betglockhen ain zaichen alß
> bißher geleut werden solle.

Whereas an honourable council hath abolished the *Salve* at eventide, their wisdom hath
[ordered] in its stead that every working day at the same evening hour one ring as hitherto to
the *Salve*, and, when it hath been rung out, the Latin and German schoolmasters or their
deputies and scholars shall appear to hold a song for the common peace; a deacon also shall
appear thereat and admonish the people present to pray for the common peace; [and] in the midst
of such prayer a sign shall be rung with the prayer bell, as hitherto.

- **Lichtenberg 1579**: the Ave bell is no longer to be a threefold pulse but one continuous
  pulse, a plain morning and evening bell. **Lichtenberg, *Dienstanweisung für die Pfarrer des
  Amtes Lichtenberg*, 1579** (Sehling 18, p. 377):

<!-- doc 992 -->
> Zum Sechsten soll das Ave Mariae leutten, wie man es nennt, hinfürtter nicht ein trinus,
> sondern ein Continuus pulsus sein, das es für ein morgen und abent Glock oder leutten gehalten
> werde.

Sixthly, the Ave Maria ringing, as it is called, shall henceforth be not a threefold but a
continuous pulse, that it be held for a morning and evening bell or ringing.

**The Friday bell for the Passion.** The Friday ringing at the hour of the crucifixion was kept
by some orders, as at the Palatinate in 1546 (above). **Thüngen, *Kirchenordnung*, 1564**
(Sehling 11, p. 741), having abolished weather-ringing:

<!-- doc 326 -->
> Aber das leuten, so am freitag zur gedechtnus des leidens und sterbens Christi geschickt, ist
> nicht zu verwerfen und, wes sich alle und jede christen dabei zu erinnern haben, soll
> weitleuftiger zu gelegener zeit durch den pfarrherrn vermeldet werden.

But the ringing which is done on Friday in remembrance of the suffering and death of Christ is
not to be rejected; and what all and every Christian is to call to mind thereby shall be set
forth more at large, at a fitting time, by the pastor.

Hohenlohe 1582, on the other hand, abolishes the Friday *Tenebrae* ringing outright and puts a
ringing to the Friday litany in its place (§5.5).

**Other examples.**
- **Breslau 1585**: in time of plague the council asks those in the street, when the evening
  *pro pace* is rung, to kneel and pray, and orders the ringing lengthened
  (Sehling 3, pp. 405–406).
- **Lippe 1571**: the prayer bell at morning, noon and evening is kept "for many moving
  reasons", but not as though it must be kept; where it has lapsed it need not be restored. The
  preachers are to explain where the *pro pace* ringing came from, and how it fell into abuse
  under the papacy "as if it were a service done to the Virgin Mary" (Sehling 21, p. 442).

### 5.4 Bells within the Mass

**The question.** In the Roman Mass a small bell (*Schelle*, *campanula*) was rung at the
Sanctus and at the elevation of the host and chalice, and the tower bell was often tolled as
well. The evangelical orders dropped the Canon, but several kept the elevation for a time, and
some kept the bell with it.

**A bell sign before the consecration.** The earliest Prussian and Livonian orders keep a sign
with the little bell between the Preface and the consecration:
- **Prussia, *Artikel der Ceremonien*, 1525** (Sehling 4, p. 32):

<!-- doc 1832 -->
> Auf die volendeten prefation und consecration gibt man ein zeichen mit der schellen, darnach
> so bald elevirt der priester beideteil des sacraments ane mittel nacheinander. Unter des und
> so bald die prefation samt der evangelischen benediction aus ist, singet der chor sanctus
> deütsch oder lateinisch nach gelegenheit, mit wenig noten.

Upon the finished Preface and consecration a sign is given with the little bell; thereafter
straightway the priest elevateth both parts of the Sacrament, without interval, one after the
other. Meanwhile, and as soon as the Preface together with the evangelical blessing is ended,
the choir singeth the Sanctus in German or Latin as occasion serveth, with few notes.

- **Riga, *Kirchenordnung*, 1530** (Sehling 5, p. 16):

<!-- doc 1897 -->
> Auf die predigt folget die prefatio, welche der priester deutsch singet bis auf das per
> Christum, inclusive. Da geb man bald ein zeichen mit eim glöcklein. Dar nach so bald sol der
> priester zum ersten das brot auf der pateen in die hand nemen, und sich ob dem altar umkeren
> zum volck, und die wort der benedeiung oder consecration wol laut, deutsch und vernemlich
> sprechen.

Upon the sermon followeth the Preface, which the priest singeth in German as far as the *per
Christum*, inclusive. Then let a sign be given straightway with a little bell. Thereafter
straightway shall the priest first take the bread upon the paten into his hand, and turn himself
at the altar to the people, and speak the words of blessing or consecration well aloud, in
German and distinctly.

- **Kurland, *Kirchenordnung*, 1570** (Sehling 5, p. 90):

<!-- doc 1907 -->
> Auf die praefation folget die consecratio gegen dem volke, wenn das zeichen mit dem glöcklein
> gegeben ist. Bald singt das chor sanctus, Esaiae dem propheten etc.

Upon the Preface followeth the consecration toward the people, when the sign with the little
bell hath been given. Straightway the choir singeth the Sanctus, *Jesaia dem Propheten*, etc.

**Ringing at the elevation.** The Danish *Ordinatio* of 1537, which stands behind the
Schleswig-Holstein order of 1542, leaves the elevation to the minister's liberty, "the bells
meanwhile sounding according to custom". **Denmark, *Ordinatio ecclesiastica*, 1537, from
Sehling's apparatus** (Sehling 23, p. 90):

<!-- doc 1576 -->
> Et subinde a verbis decenti elevatione, si ita visum fuerit ministro elevet sonantibus interim
> cymbalis iuxta consuetum. Nam in his servanda est libertas christiana, ita tarnen ut de hac
> populus ante satis praemoneatur et nihil horum mutetur sine consensu et mandato
> superintendentis.

And forthwith after the words let him elevate with a seemly elevation, if it so seem good to the
minister, the bells meanwhile sounding according to custom. For in these things Christian
liberty is to be kept; yet so that the people be sufficiently forewarned of it beforehand, and
that nothing of these things be changed without the consent and command of the superintendent.

Under the Interim the Ansbach *Auctuarium* ordered both the elevation and the bell.
**Brandenburg-Ansbach, *Auctuarium*, 1548** (Interim order; Sehling 11, p. 327):

<!-- doc 278 -->
> Item es sollen auch die priester nach gewönlichem alten gebrauch die beede gestalt des
> heiligen sacraments aufheben und darzu leuten oder klingen lassen.

Item, the priests also shall, after the customary old usage, lift up both kinds of the holy
Sacrament, and have [the bell] rung or tinkled thereto.

At Hof the elevation with a threefold pulse of the bell survived until about 1560. The order of
1592 records its passing. **Hof, *Ordo ecclesiasticus*, 1592** (Sehling 11, p. 407):

<!-- doc 294 -->
> Elevatio autem benedicti panis et vini cum trino campanulae pulsu, qua maiores nostri ad
> insignem movebantur pietatem, pectora percutientes genuaque flectentes coram Christo
> Salvatore, quem pane et vino quasi velatum spectabant, ante annos ferme triginta, pastore tum
> d. doctore Johanne Streitbergero, abrogata est, quod haec ceremonia in caeteris marchionatus
> ecclesiis locum amplius non haberet.

But the elevation of the blessed bread and wine with a threefold pulse of the little bell,
whereby our forefathers were moved to notable piety, beating their breasts and bending their
knees before Christ the Saviour, whom they beheld as it were veiled in the bread and wine, was
abrogated almost thirty years ago, Dr Johann Streitberger being then pastor, because this
ceremony no longer had place in the other churches of the margraviate.

Anhalt in 1568 still kept the elevation on high feasts, without mention of a bell
(Sehling 2, p. 570; quoted in `LITURGICAL_CALENDAR_GUIDE.md`, §10.6).

**A bell to call the communicants.** Two orders ring a bell in the Mass to call the communicants
forward. At Hatzkerode the sign is given *during the German Sanctus*. This is the only bell in
the corpus rung at the Sanctus. **Hatzkerode, *Kirchenordnunge*, 1534(?)** (Sehling 2, p. 587):

<!-- doc 1263 -->
> Nach geschener predigt sol ein deutsch lied ader das deutsche sanctus Jesaia, dem propheten,
> das geschach nach gefallen des pfarhers, gesungen werden. Indes sol mit der glocke ein zeichen
> geben werden, alsdan sollen die communicanten in den chor gehen und zur rechten die manne, zur
> linken die frauen und junkfrauen treten.

After the sermon is done, a German song or the German Sanctus, *Jesaia dem Propheten*, shall be
sung, as it pleaseth the pastor. Meanwhile a sign shall be given with the bell; then shall the
communicants go into the choir, and the men stand on the right, the women and maidens on the
left.

**Haldensleben, *Ordo caeremoniarum ecclesiae Haldenslebensis*, 1564** (Sehling 2, p. 457):

<!-- doc 1257 -->
> 6. Post concionem vero accedunt ad altare communicaturi per campanulam quandam a custode
> convocati.

6. But after the sermon those who are to communicate come to the altar, called together by the
   custos with a certain little bell.

**A choir bell.** At Königsberg in 1568 "the little bell in the choir" is rung after the early
sermon to call the cantor and scholars (§5.5).

**The Sacrament to the sick.** The medieval viaticum went through the streets with light and
bell. The orders divide:
- **Baden 1533** still has the Sacrament carried to the sick "with light and bells going before"
  (Sehling 16, p. 510).
- **Brandenburg-Ansbach 1556** lets a sign be given with the bell, "yet distinctly", when the
  minister goes to the sick (Sehling 11, p. 336, quoted in §5.7). The editor notes that this is
  neither the passing bell nor the Catholic bell that called the faithful to follow the
  Sacrament.
- **Transylvania 1543**: the minister goes to the sick "without candles, little bells and other
  pomp, which entice the unlearned to idolatry" (Sehling 24, p. 196).
- **Micron 1554/1565** (Reformed) rejects "altars, lights, little bells, vestments" altogether.
  **Micron, *Ordinancien* (1554), East Frisian German edition, 1565** (Reformed; Sehling 7/1, p.
  615):

<!-- doc 2116 -->
> haben darumb keinswegs solche ceremonien angenomen, die eitel und unütz sind oder zu einiger
> abgötterey zihen und Christum Jesum in seinem priestertumb verfinstern, als da sind: altaren,
> lichten, schellen, kleider, denen etwas geheimnuß zugeschriben wird, und was dergleichen ding
> mehr sind.

[we] have therefore in no wise received such ceremonies as are vain and unprofitable, or draw to
any idolatry and darken Christ Jesus in his priesthood: as are altars, lights, little bells,
vestments, to which some mystery is ascribed, and whatever more such things there be.

**Not found.** The corpus has:
- **No Sanctus bell as such.** The word *Sanctusglocke* (or any compound of it) does not occur.
  The Hatzkerode bell during the German Sanctus calls the communicants; it does not mark the
  Sanctus.
- **No ringing during the Lord's Prayer in the Mass.** The Lord's Prayer is tied to bells only
  outside the Mass: the daily prayer bells (§5.3), and the Friday bell at eleven, at which,
  according to a later folk custom cited by the Hohenlohe editor, those in the field said an Our
  Father (Sehling 15, p. 452, editor's note).
- **No ringing at the *Gloria in excelsis*, at Easter or otherwise.** The festal bells of Easter
  belong to the summons and the chiming of the feast (§5.1), not to the Gloria.
- **No altar bell at the words of institution** after the elevation fell away. The bell before
  the consecration survives only in Prussia 1525, Riga 1530 and Kurland 1570.

### 5.5 Bells at the *Te Deum*, the litany and other chants

**Ringing during the *Te Deum*.** In Prussia the bell was rung during the German *Te Deum* at
the end of Matins, to summon the people to the sermon and the Mass that followed. **Prussia,
*Kirchenordnung*, 1544** (Sehling 4, p. 67):

<!-- doc 1833 -->
> darauf thut der diacon ader kirchendiener ein kurze ermanung vom heiligen sacrament für die,
> so communiciren wöllen, an stadt eines capitels; auf die ermanung singt die ganze kirche das
> deudsch Te deum laudamus, darunter wird zur predigt geleut und das lied Jesus Christus unser
> heiland, fur der predigt gesungen.

thereupon the deacon or minister of the church maketh a short exhortation concerning the holy
Sacrament for those who will communicate, in the stead of a chapter; upon the exhortation the
whole church singeth the German *Te Deum laudamus*, under which the bell is rung to the sermon,
and the song *Jesus Christus unser Heiland* is sung before the sermon.

The Prussian order of 1568 repeats it, and adds a choir bell. **Prussia, *Kirchenordnung und
Ceremonien*, 1568** (Sehling 4, p. 79):

<!-- doc 1833 -->
> Sobald diese predig geschlossen, soll man mit dem kleinen glöcklein auf dem chor leuten und
> sich der cantor mit den schulgesellen und knaben in die kirche finden. […] Nach dem symbolo
> lieset ein knabe eine lektion herniden fur dem altar gegen dem volk, wie alle lektiones sollen
> gelesen werden und folget darauf das Benedictus dominus deus Israel cum Antiphona, oder das
> deutsch Te deum laudamus Lutheri: O gott, dich loben wir, o gott, wir danken dir etc.
> Unterdess leutet man wiederum mit der grossen glocke und singet die ganze kirche nach dem
> Benedictus oder Te deum laudamus: Wir glauben etc.

As soon as this sermon is concluded, one shall ring with the little bell in the choir, and the
cantor with the school assistants and boys shall betake themselves into the church. […] After
the Creed a boy readeth a lesson below before the altar toward the people, as all lessons shall
be read, and thereupon followeth the *Benedictus Dominus Deus Israel* with the antiphon, or
Luther's German *Te Deum laudamus*, *O Gott, dich loben wir, o Gott, wir danken dir*, etc.
Meanwhile one ringeth again with the great bell, and the whole church singeth after the
*Benedictus* or *Te Deum laudamus*: *Wir glauben*, etc.

**All the bells at the Christmas *Te Deum*.** At Hof's Christmas Matins the organ plays the
responsory, the German *Te Deum* alternates between organ and choir, and all the bells are rung
until it ends. **Hof, *Ordo ecclesiasticus*, 1592, Christmas Day** (Sehling 11, p. 413):

<!-- doc 294 -->
> II. Lectio ex novo testamento. Hac finita organicen more consueto modulatur responsorium,
> sequente choro. Verbum caro factum est. Mox concio habetur. Hanc excipt canticum sanctorum
> Ambrosii et Augustini germanicum: Herr Got, dich loben wir. Incipienten organicinem sequiitur
> totus chorus et ita per vices finitur cantus. Unter diesem gsang leutet man mit allen glocken
> bis zu end desselben.

II. The lesson from the New Testament. This being finished, the organist playeth the responsory
in the customary manner, the choir following: *Verbum caro factum est*. Straightway the sermon
is held. After it cometh the canticle of Saints Ambrose and Augustine in German, *Herr Gott,
dich loben wir*. The organist beginning, the whole choir followeth, and so the song is finished
in turns. During this song one ringeth with all the bells until the end of the same.

**The litany.** Hohenlohe rings the largest bell when the Friday litany begins, so that those in
the field and at home may join in the prayer, and abolishes the *Tenebrae* ringing. **Hohenlohe,
*Kirchen- und Schulordnung*, 1582** (Sehling 15, p. 452):

<!-- doc 589 -->
> [12.] Von leiten deß Tenebre uf den freytag. Nachdem zum gemeinen gebett uf dem freytag nicht
> jederman kommen kan, sonder durch notwendige gescheft uf dem veld wie auch die bestelte
> taglöner darvon abgehalten werden, so wöllen, ordnen und bevehlen wir, sobald die letaney
> angefangen, das alsdann mit der grösten glocken ein gut zeichen geleitet, dardurch meniglich,
> so uf dem veld oder in den heußern, des gemeinen gebetts erinnert, uf das, ob sie woll bey der
> versambleten gemein in der kirchen mit dem leib nicht gegenwertig, doch nach dem geist und mit
> andechtigem gebett darvor nicht abgesondert seien, sonder dartzu ermanet werden. Dargegen soll
> das tenebreleuten in allen kirchen und an allen orten in unserer grafschaft gentzlichen
> abgeschaft sein und nicht mehr gestattet werden.

[12.] Of the ringing of the *Tenebrae* on Friday. Forasmuch as not every one can come to the
common prayer on Friday, but [some] are kept from it by necessary business in the field, as also
the hired day-labourers, we will, ordain and command that, as soon as the litany is begun, a
good sign be rung with the greatest bell, whereby every one in the field or in the houses may be
reminded of the common prayer; to the end that, although they be not present in body with the
assembled congregation in the church, they may yet not be separated from it in spirit and in
devout prayer, but be admonished thereto. On the other hand the *Tenebrae* ringing shall be
wholly abolished in all churches and in all places in our county, and no longer be permitted.

At Regensburg the litany is sung *under* the ringing, while the people gather for the early
sermon and communion. **Regensburg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1567** (Sehling 13, p. 461):

<!-- doc 450 -->
> Erstlich wird in beiden kirchen, der neuen pfarre und zu S. Oswald, unter dem leuten gesungen
> durch die deudschen schulen und gemein die preußisch letanei oder etwas de tempore deudsches.

First, in both churches, the New Parish and St Oswald's, the Prussian litany, or something
German of the season, is sung by the German schools and the congregation under the ringing.

**Hymn stanzas.** No order prescribes ringing during a particular stanza of a hymn. What the
orders have instead is ringing during a whole chant that ends one part of the service and leads
into the next: the *Te Deum* at Matins (Prussia, Hof), the litany (Hohenlohe, Regensburg). The
Hohenlohe editor records a later folk custom of ringing on Good Friday while "O Lamm Gottes" was
sung (Sehling 15, p. 452, editor's note), but that is nineteenth-century evidence and is not in
the order.

### 5.6 Bells for the dead

**Ringing at a death, as a reminder to the living.** The Saxon visitors kept the bell rung for
the dead, but gave it a new reason. **Meissen, *Ordnung der Visitatoren für die Stadt Meissen*,
1540** (Sehling 2, p. 52):

<!-- doc 1216 -->
> Vom leuten, so jemand gestorben ist. Es sol den toten geleutet werden, auf das die lebendigen
> auch bedenken die stund und zeit ires sterbens und ir leben besseren etc. Dieses anzeigen mit
> dem geleut sol bald nach irem sterben geschehen. Es sol auch forthin frue und des abends, wie
> bisher auch geschehen, pro pace geleutet werden, auf das das volk erinnert werde fur einen
> gemeinen fried der christenheit zu bitten. Leutgeld von toten. Es sol dem kirchner das
> leutgeld, wie fur lange gegeben ist, folgen.

Of ringing when any one hath died. The bell shall be rung for the dead, that the living also may
consider the hour and time of their own dying, and amend their life, etc. This announcing with
the bell shall be done soon after their death. Also henceforth the bell shall be rung *pro pace*
early and at evening, as hath been done hitherto, that the people be reminded to pray for a
common peace of Christendom. Ringing-money for the dead. The ringing-money shall go to the
sexton, as it hath been given of old.

**Saturday ringing for the souls abolished.** The weekly ringing "for all faithful souls" went:
- **Zwickau, *Verordnung der Visitatoren*, 1529** (Sehling 1, p. 721):

<!-- doc 151 -->
> Zur predigt, messe und vesper, auch den todten mag man leuten, doch das es zur vesper und den
> todten auch frue zur lection bei einem leuten mit einer glocken bleibe. Desgleichen mag man
> pro pace leuten, doch das das folk des wol bericht werde. […] Das leuten fur die seelen am
> sonnabent soll abgeen.

To the sermon, Mass and Vespers, also for the dead, one may ring; yet so that to Vespers and for
the dead, also early to the lesson, it remain at one ringing with one bell. Likewise one may
ring *pro pace*, yet so that the people be well instructed therein. […] The ringing for the
souls on Saturday shall cease.

- **Reuss, *Kirchen-Ordnung* of Heinrich IV, 1552** (Sehling 2, p. 156), which in the same
  article abolishes weather-ringing:

<!-- doc 1234 -->
> Wetterleuten. Von diesem artikel ist sonderlich gehandelt, und eintrechtiglich beschlossen,
> weil das leuten an vielen orten unrichtigkeit gebirt, das solchs auch allenthalben sol
> abgethan sein und bleiben, doch sol solches mit vleis uf den canzeln praecavirt, und das volk
> one das zum gebet allezeit treulich vermanet werden. Das leuten am sonnabent allen gleubigen
> seelen soll auch abgeschafft werden, dann es ergerlich, und stinkt nach dem pabstumb.

Weather-ringing. This article was specially dealt with, and it was resolved with one accord
that, since the ringing breedeth disorder in many places, it also shall be and remain done away
everywhere; yet this shall be diligently forewarned of from the pulpits, and the people, without
it, faithfully admonished at all times to prayer. The ringing on Saturday for all faithful souls
shall also be abolished, for it is offensive and smelleth of the papacy.

**Equality and fees.** Ulm's undated visitation articles (Moderate Reformed) ask "that in the
ringing in memory of the dead equality be kept" (Sehling 17/2, p. 193). In practice the number
of bells was a matter of fees. Danzig's burial order of 1578 sets the schoolboys' pay by the
bells rung. **Danzig, *Begräbnisordnung*, 1578** (Sehling 4, p. 191):

<!-- doc 1844 -->
> das auf dem schlag zwei nachmittag die schüler fur der thuren sollen anfangen zu singen bis zu
> halb dreien, und dafur soll man ihnen geben, wan eine glocke gelautet wirdt, zehen groschen
> von zweien glocken zwanzig groschen, und wan alle glocken gelautet werden, 40 groschen.

that at the stroke of two in the afternoon the scholars shall begin to sing before the doors
until half past two, and for that they shall be given, when one bell is rung, ten groschen; for
two bells, twenty groschen; and when all the bells are rung, 40 groschen.

**Not during the sermon.** Herford 1532 silences "all bells, be it for the dead or not" while
the sermon is preached (§4.4).

**Burial without bells.** The bell was withheld as a mark of exclusion or of grief:
- **The despisers of the Sacrament.** **Zwickau, *Verordnung der Visitatoren*, 1533**
  (Sehling 1, p. 723):

<!-- doc 152 -->
> Wurd auch jemands so halstarrig und rohe sein, das es uber und wider gescheene vormanung und
> bedraung nicht zu kirchen gienge und wider gottes wort horet, noch das hochwirdige sacrament
> bei seinem gesund entpfinge, auch in seiner krankheit desselben nicht begeret und also on das
> hochwirdig sacrament sturbe, den soll man unbeleut und unbeleitet mit den schulern in still
> begraben.

If also any one were so stiff-necked and rude that, over and against admonition and threatening
given, he went not to church, nor heard God's word, nor received the most worthy Sacrament in
his health, nor desired it in his sickness, and so died without the most worthy Sacrament, him
shall one bury in silence, without bells and without escort of the scholars.

- **Unbaptized infants.** **Lippe, *Kirchenordnung*, 1571** (Sehling 21, p. 432), which buries
  them in the churchyard "for the comfort of the sorrowing parents", but without bells:

<!-- doc 1462 -->
> Sondern sollen zum Trost der betrübten Eltern auff die Kirchhöfe oder Sepulturn anderer
> Christen und getaufften Kinder begraben werden, Jedoch ohn Glockengeleuth und Gesenge der
> Kirchendiener und Schüler, Nicht der meinung, das wir die kleinen, ungetaufften Kinder,
> insonderheit, so von fromen, Christgleubigen Eltern im Heiligen Ehestande geboren sein,
> verdammen

But they shall be buried, for the comfort of the sorrowing parents, in the churchyards or burial
places of other Christians and baptized children; yet without ringing of bells and singing of
the ministers of the church and scholars: not in the meaning that we condemn the little
unbaptized children, especially those born of godly, Christian-believing parents in holy wedlock

Limpurg 1610, by contrast, rings "the small bell" for children carried to burial "before or
after baptism" in its village of Münster (Sehling 16, p. 623).

### 5.7 Bells at baptisms and weddings

**Baptism.** Where a child was baptized outside the regular service, a bell called the
congregation to witness it:
- **Kurpfalz, *Kirchenordnung*, 1556** (Sehling 14, p. 121):

<!-- doc 478 -->
> Wir halten auch für nützlich, so ausserhalb der gemeinen predig oder kirchenversamlung ein
> kind getauft werden solle, in massen kurtz hievor gemeldet, daß ein zeichen mit einer glocken
> beschehe, damit andere leuth dardurch, zum taufhandel zu kommen, vermahnet werden.

We hold it also profitable, if a child is to be baptized outside the common sermon or assembly
of the church, as was mentioned a little before, that a sign be given with a bell, that other
people may thereby be admonished to come to the act of baptism.

- **Brandenburg-Ansbach, *Vereinbarungen der Theologen auf der Synode*, 1556**
  (Sehling 11, p. 336):

<!-- doc 280 -->
> So man aber in kürchen taufet, soll man mit dem kleinen glocklin darzu leuten und das zeichen
> des creuzes auslassen. […] Bei den kranken kein corrock gebrauchen, tamen sit liberum in suis
> locis, und (doch underschidlich) mit der glocken ein zeichen gegeben werden.

But when one baptizeth in church, one shall ring thereto with the little bell, and leave out the
sign of the cross. […] With the sick no surplice [is] to be used, *yet let it be free in their
places*; and (yet distinctly) a sign [is] to be given with the bell.

- **Limpurg 1610** settles a complaint by ordering the ringing before baptism with the small
  bell in all country churches. **Limpurg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1610** (Sehling 16, p. 623):

<!-- doc 729 -->
> 1. Das Leuten bey der Künder Tauff. Solch Leutten soll hünfüro auff dem Landt bey allen
> Kürchen (außerhalb der Statt Gailendorff), es wer gleich hiebevor in einem oder andern Ortt
> inn gebrauch geweesen oder nicht, vor der Tauff mit dem klainen Glöckhlin beschehen unnd
> verrichtet werden.

1. Of the ringing at the baptism of children. Such ringing shall henceforth be done and
   performed in the country at all churches (except the town of Gaildorf), whether it was in use
   before in one place or another or not, before the baptism, with the small bell.

- **Heilbronn**, around 1529, kept the bell for "child baptism" when it stopped ringing for the
  Mass and Vespers (Sehling 17/1, p. 226, editor's introduction).

The Regensburg editor notes that a bell for baptism was unknown in the Middle Ages
(Sehling 13, p. 436, editor's note), so this is a new evangelical use, not a survival.

**Weddings.** At Hof the bell for a wedding service is rung half an hour later than usual.
**Hof, *Ordo ecclesiasticus*, 1592** (Sehling 11, p. 430):

<!-- doc 294 -->
> Wann eine hochzeit vorhanden, wird ein halbe stund lengsamer geleutet, dann sönsten
> gebreuchlich

When there is a wedding, the bell is rung half an hour later than is otherwise customary

### 5.8 Bells against storms

The abolition of weather-ringing, with the bell baptism behind it, is treated in
`EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md`, §15.5, which quotes Saxony 1528 and 1580 and summarizes
Hohenlohe 1582 and the other territories. The orders fall into three groups:
- **Kept, re-explained as a call to prayer**: Saxony 1528 (Sehling 1, pp. 170–171), the Upper
  Palatinate 1579 (one bell at night), Hohenlohe 1582 (one bell, not all, and not as long as the
  storm lasts), Limpurg 1610 (the great bell only, for a quarter of an hour).
- **Abolished**: Ulm (undated articles, Moderate Reformed, "as it hath begun"), Reuss 1552
  (§5.6), Coburg 1554/55 (§5.3), Thüngen 1564, Saxony 1580, Saxe-Lauenburg 1585 (§5.1), and the
  Oldenburg visitations of 1609–1610, which keep the prayer bell instead
  (Sehling 7/2.1, p. 1212).
- **Asked about at visitation**: Hohenlohe 1558 asks whether there is ringing against weather,
  fog, frost or heat (Sehling 15, p. 122).

### 5.9 Lesser signals: the sermon glass and the alms bell

**The end of the sermon.** At Zerbst the sexton turned an hour-glass when the preacher went up
into the pulpit, and rang a little bell when it had run out. **Zerbst, *Vereinbarungen des
gesammten Ministeriums*, 1545** (Sehling 2, p. 593):

<!-- doc 1263 -->
> Uf das aber solliches gehalten moge werden, soll ein seiger von drei virtel stunden vor dem
> canzel stehen, damit der prediger sehen moge, wann er schliessen soll, und der custer soll
> auch zween zaiger, einen von einer stunden, den andern von dreien virtel stunden bei sich
> behalten, und den saiger von einer stunden zur hochmessen, und den zeiger von dreien virtel
> stunden aber zu allen andern predigen gebrauchen und umkeren, so bald der predicant uf die
> canzel gehet, und wann also der seiger vor einer oder dreien virtel stunden ausgelaufen, so
> soll er mit einer darzu verordenten schellen clingen, damit der predicant schliesse und der
> gemeine urlauben moge.

But that this may be kept, a three-quarter-hour glass shall stand before the pulpit, that the
preacher may see when he should conclude; and the sexton also shall keep by him two glasses, one
of an hour, the other of three quarters of an hour, and use the hour-glass at High Mass and the
three-quarter glass at all other sermons, and turn it as soon as the preacher goeth up into the
pulpit; and when the glass of one hour or of three quarters hath run out, he shall tinkle with a
little bell appointed thereto, that the preacher may conclude and dismiss the congregation.

**The alms bell.** At Brandis the churchwardens, or someone they appoint, go round "under all
sermons, or before and after," with a little bell to collect alms. **Brandis, *Ordnung für die
Stadt Brandis*, 1529** (Sehling 1, p. 533):

<!-- doc 66 -->
> darzu sollen sie under allen predigen, oder aber vor und nach, mit einem schellen glocklein
> umbgehn oder zu geen bestellen, und das almusen wie zu Grimme und anderswo von den leuten
> bitten, und den armen wie berurt widerumb austeilen.

thereto they shall go about, or appoint someone to go, with a little hand-bell during all the
sermons, or else before and after, and ask the alms of the people, as at Grimma and elsewhere,
and distribute it again to the poor, as aforesaid.

The Württemberg common-chest orders give the street collectors for the poor "a bell or little
bell, whereby every one may be reminded to give alms"
(Sehling 16, p. 96; repeated at Sehling 16, p. 202, and Sehling 6/1, p. 263). This was a street
bell, not a church bell.
