# Sermons and Preaching in the Sehling Church Orders

This guide collects what the evangelical church orders printed in Emil Sehling's *Die
evangelischen Kirchenordnungen des XVI. Jahrhunderts* say about the **sermon**. It asks:
- What did the reformers and the orders think the sermon was **for**, and what was it to
  contain?
- **On what occasions** was there preaching: Sundays and feasts, weekdays, the catechism, Lent
  and the Passion, prayer days, funerals and weddings?
- **What texts** were preached: the old Sunday gospels and epistles, books in course, the
  catechism, the heads of doctrine?
- **How** was a sermon to be made, and in what manner of speech?
- **How long** was it to last?
- What did the orders say about **postils**, written sermons and preparation?
- What were the limits on **rebuke and controversy** in the pulpit?
- What was done in the pulpit **before and after** the sermon?
- How were **preachers** tested and corrected?
- What was required of the **hearers**?

**How the guide is laid out.**
- §1 summarizes the findings, and §2 sets out the method and cautions.
- §3 gives Luther's rulings of 1523 and 1526 and the Saxon visitation of 1528.
- §4 covers the occasions and times of preaching, and §5 the texts.
- §6 covers how to preach, and §7 the length of sermons.
- §8 deals with postils and preparation, and §9 with rebuke and polemic.
- §10 covers the pulpit acts around the sermon.
- §11 covers preachers, and §12 hearers.
- §13 is a table by order, and §14 a concordance.

**Conventions**

- **Quotations.** Every quotation is Sehling's text as it stands in the `eko.db` database built
  from the digitized edition.
  - Footnote numbers, sigla, folio markers and interleaved apparatus are left out.
  - Sehling's own supplied words in square brackets are kept, as are "[!]", "[sic]" and his
    other signs.
  - Spelling and punctuation are as printed, including plain OCR errors.
  - "[…]" marks an omission.
  - Every quotation has been checked against the database text, allowing for OCR noise: at least
    90 per cent of its words must be found there in order. Its page has been checked against the
    edition's page markers.
  - Each quotation is preceded by an HTML comment, invisible when rendered, that names the
    `eko.db` document it was checked against.
- **Translations.** The English is formal-equivalence, in the idiom of the Authorized Version:
  - Word order and clause structure are kept where English allows.
  - Liturgical names (Matins, Vespers, *Magnificat*, Our Father) are kept in their customary
    English or Latin forms.
  - Words the translation supplies are in square brackets.
- **Citations.** Citations are in the form "Sehling volume, page".
  - The half-volumes of the edition are written 6/1, 6/2, 7/1, 7/2.1, 7/2.2, 17/2, 19/1, 19/2,
    20/1, 20/2 and so on, following the digitized volumes.
  - Each order is named by place, title and date as they appear in Sehling's running heads.
    Where a passage is Sehling's own introduction or apparatus, this is said.
- **Traditions.** Most of the orders cited are Lutheran, and they are not marked. Every order of
  another tradition is marked where it is cited, by its name in brackets after the order, as
  "Kurpfalz 1563 (Reformed)". The traditions follow the inventory in
  [`CHURCH_ORDERS_GUIDE.md`](CHURCH_ORDERS_GUIDE.md), §3:
  - **Reformed**: the Swiss, Calvinist and Heidelberg churches, among them the German
    territories that turned Reformed after 1560 and the French and Dutch stranger churches;
  - **Moderate Reformed**: the Bucerian and related orders that stood between the Lutheran and
    the Swiss Reformed: Bucer's Strasbourg and the Upper German cities before the Interim,
    Philip of Hesse's church to 1566, Hermann von Wied's Cologne order, and Colmar after 1578;
  - **Philippist**: the churches that held to Melanchthon's later teaching and his *Corpus
    doctrinae* and refused the Formula of Concord: the joint Hessian synods and orders of
    1568–1598, Bremen 1556–1575, Nassau-Dillenburg 1575 and Anhalt 1590–1594.
  - The mark follows the order cited, not the territory, since a territory could change its
    tradition: Kurpfalz is Lutheran under Ottheinrich (1556) and Reformed from 1563.

## 1. Summary of findings

**1. The sermon is the reason for the assembly** (§3).
- Luther's first rule, in 1523: the congregation "shall never come together" unless God's Word
  is preached and prayer made. Where the Word is not preached, "it is better that one neither
  sing nor read nor come together".
- The visitors of 1528 required **repentance** to be preached with forgiveness. Preaching
  forgiveness alone makes people "secure and fearless". Later orders speak of preaching
  "repentance, law and gospel".

**2. The occasions** (§4).
- **Sunday and holy days.** The gospel was preached in the morning at the Mass, and the
  catechism (or the epistle) in the afternoon. Luther's Wittenberg had three Sunday sermons: an
  early one on the epistle for the servants, the gospel at the Mass, and the Old Testament at
  Vespers.
- **Weekdays.** One or two weekday sermons, usually Wednesday and Friday (1528). Great towns had
  more. Early sermons were held for servants and craftsmen before work. Village weekday sermons
  were often suspended at harvest, or held only in winter.
- **The catechism.** The catechism sermon came on Sunday afternoon, and was also preached
  through in series several times a year (Torgau: every quarter; Reutlingen: four times a year).
- **Lent and the Passion.** Luther refused "eight hours" of Passion preaching on Good Friday: an
  hour a day in Holy Week. The later orders divide the Passion harmony into a fixed series of
  sermons (six in Lent at Henneberg; three on Maundy Thursday and Good Friday at Coburg).
- **Prayer days** (*Bettage*), weekly or monthly, with a sermon of repentance.
- **Funeral sermons** spread from the 1550s. They were to be short, without praise of the dead,
  and often limited to half an hour. Some towns abolished them.
- **Wedding sermons**: allowed if "short, chaste, Christian", or abolished.

**3. The texts** (§5).
- The Lutheran orders **kept the Sunday gospels and epistles**. Luther's reason was that few
  preachers could handle a whole book "mightily and profitably".
- **Books in course** were preached at Vespers and on weekdays.
- Some orders also asked for the *loci communes* to be covered through the year. The Reformed
  Palatinate allowed a book in course on Sunday too.

**4. How to preach** (§6). The orders describe one shape:
- prayer;
- the text;
- a short summary;
- one to three doctrines;
- their application to the hearers;
- a brief repetition;
- the general prayer.

The sermon is to teach, reprove and comfort: to terrify the secure and comfort the troubled. It
is to be in plain words, "so that he may be understood", fitted to the hearers, "in good
German".

**5. Length** (§7).
- **The chief Sunday sermon: one hour at most**, often three quarters.
- **Weekday, afternoon, catechism, funeral and wedding sermons: half an hour.**
- The reason given is the hearer. "The common people can in no wise remain attentive longer"
  (Augsburg 1537, Moderate Reformed). The common man is like "a sick man, to whom one must set
  things often, but little at once" (Württemberg 1536). "Long sermons … weaken the memory and
  make the hearers weary" (Regensburg 1572).
- Sermons were to be shorter in winter cold (Castell 1579). Overrunning to an hour and a half
  drew complaints (Hohenlohe 1594).

**6. Postils** (§8). There are two phases.
- **Early.** Luther thought it best to read his postil from the pulpit, for weak preachers and
  against the sects. Saxony in 1545 told poor village pastors to read Corvinus or Luther's
  *Hauspostille* "until" they could preach themselves.
- **Later.** The complaint reverses: young preachers "adorn themselves with strange feathers"
  (Saxony 1580). Pastors are to write their own sermons (Nassau-Dillenburg 1570), to use "pure
  postils" without leaning on them (Wolfstein 1574), and not to go up into the pulpit "without
  foregoing meditation" (Sponheim 1608).

**7. Rebuke and controversy** (§9).
- Preachers were to rebuke vices "in general", and **name no one**. Names were read from the
  pulpit only for excommunication by sentence of the consistory, and in the banns.
- No private grudges in the pulpit.
- The doctrinal quarrels brought mandates against "railing and condemning": "disputations belong
  in the universities, and not in the churches in the pulpit" (Ansbach 1570).

**8. Around the sermon** (§10). The orders kept the medieval group of vernacular pulpit pieces
around the sermon:
- **before**: an exhortation and prayer, often the Our Father or "Nun bitten wir"; in the
  Palatinate (Reformed) the apostolic greeting;
- **after**: the open confession and absolution, the general prayer with particular
  intercessions, and the notices and banns.

**9. Preachers** (§11).
- Candidates passed an examination and a **trial sermon**.
- Pastors already in office preached practice sermons on set texts before the superintendent
  (Saxony 1577).
- In the Palatine class conventions (1587, Reformed) a colleague's sermon was **censured point
  by point**: division of the text, scope, digressions, pace, "good German".

**10. Hearers** (§12).
- Attendance was a duty, enforced by counting and fines (one person per house at weekday
  sermons, Ysenburg 1588).
- Taverns were shut and trade, games and labour service forbidden during the sermon.
- Chattering, sleeping, coming late and leaving early were fined (Ysenburg-Ronneburg 1591,
  Reformed).
- The people were to be exhorted to stay to the end of the service.

## 2. Scope, sources and cautions

**What was searched.** The `eko.db` database was searched for the sermon by its names, in High
and Low German and Latin spellings:
- *predig*, *predigt*, *predigen*, *sermon*, *concio*;
- *Frühpredigt*, *Mittagspredigt*, *Vesperpredigt*, *Wochenpredigt*, *Katechismuspredigt*,
  *Passionspredigt*, *Bettag*, *Leichpredigt*, *Hochzeitpredigt*, *Brautpredigt*, *Probpredigt*.

It was also searched for:
- length (*stund*, *halbe stund*, *drei viertel*);

- rebuke and controversy (*canzel* with *schelten*, *schmehen*, *lestern*, *mit namen*, *privat

The passages were then read in context, and their orders identified from Sehling's running

**Cautions.** Five things limit what the corpus can show:
- **Church orders are not sermons.** Sehling prints rules, visitation articles and reports, not
  sermons or homiletic handbooks. The orders say what was required and what went wrong. How
  sermons actually sounded must be learned from the printed sermons and postils, which lie
  outside the corpus.
- **Silence is not absence.** Many orders say only "the sermon follows". Lengths, days and
  methods are stated where someone thought them in doubt. A missing rule does not mean that
  there was no custom.
- **Complaints are evidence of the rule.** The many complaints (sermons of an hour and a half,
  borrowed postils, railing from the pulpit, empty weekday sermons, taverns open during the
  sermon) show the abuses the rules were made against. They do not show how common the abuses
  were.
- **Orders of other traditions.** Sehling prints Reformed orders for the Palatinate,
  Hesse-Kassel, Nassau, Ysenburg, Hanau, Anhalt, East Frisia and the Lower Rhine, the Moderate
  Reformed orders of the Upper German cities and Philip's Hesse, and the Philippist orders of
  Bremen and of Hesse under Philip's sons. They are cited here where they bear on the same
  questions (the Palatine class conventions, the greeting, books in course, the length of the
  sermon), and each is marked with its tradition. The wider Reformed practice of Sunday
  preaching in course is noted but not pursued.
- **Dates.** Several texts survive in later copies, or are pastors' reports undated except by
  the visitation. Their dates are given as Sehling gives them.

## 3. Luther and the visitors: the sermon first

### 3.1 1523: no gathering without the Word

**The first rule.** Luther's first sketch of a reformed service names three abuses: the Word was
silenced, fables filled the silence, and the service was done as a work. From this he draws the
rule on which every later order rests. The congregation is never to meet unless God's Word is
preached and prayer is made, "though it be most briefly". **Luther, *Von ordenung gottis diensts
in der gemeine*, 1523** (Sehling 1, p. 2):

<!-- doc 2 -->
> Drei grosse missbreuch sind in den gottis dienst gefallen. Der erst, das man gottis wort
> geschwigen hat, und alleine gelesen und gesungen in den kirchen, das ist der ergiste
> missbrauch. […] Nu dise missbreuch abzuthun, ist aufs erst zu wissen, das die christlich
> gemeine nimer soll zu samen komen, es werde denn da selbs gottis wort gepredigt und gebett, es
> sei auch aufs kurzist. […] Darumb wo nicht gotts wort predigt wirt, ists besser, das man wider
> singe noch lese, noch zu samen kome.

Three great abuses are fallen into the service of God. The first, that God's word hath been kept
silent, and [men have] only read and sung in the churches; that is the worst abuse. […] Now to
put away these abuses, it is first to be known that the Christian congregation shall never come
together, except God's word be there preached and prayer made, be it also most briefly. […]
Therefore where God's word is not preached, it is better that one neither sing nor read nor come
together.

The same treatise makes the daily morning and evening gatherings into a lesson with its
exposition (see [`OFFICES_GUIDE.md`](OFFICES_GUIDE.md) §3.1). Preaching is the reason for the
assembly, not an ornament of it.

### 3.2 1526: three sermons on a Sunday

**The Wittenberg Sunday.** In the *Deutsche Messe* Luther sets out the Sunday he had in
Wittenberg. There are three sermons:
- at five or six, after psalms "as at Matins", on the **epistle**, for the household servants
  who cannot come later;
- at the Mass, at eight or nine, on the **gospel**;
- at Vespers in the afternoon, on the **Old Testament** "in order, one after another".

**Luther, *Deutsche Messe und ordnung gottis diensts*, 1526** (Sehling 1, p. 13):

<!-- doc 3 -->
> Des heiligen tags oder sontags lassen wir bleiben die gewonlichen epistel und evangelia und
> haben drei predigt. Frue umb funfe oder sechse singet man etliche psalmen als zur metten.
> Darnach predigt man die epistel des tages, aller meist umb des gesindes willen, das die auch
> versorget werden und gottis wort hören, ob sie ja in andern predigeten nicht sein kunden. […]
> Unter der messe umb acht oder neune predigt man das evangelion, das die zeit gibt durchs jar.
> Nach mittage unter der vesper fur dem Magnificat predigt man das alte testament ordentlich
> nacheinander.

On the holy day or Sunday we let remain the customary epistles and gospels, and have three
sermons. Early, at five or six, certain psalms are sung, as at Matins. Thereafter the epistle of
the day is preached, most of all for the household servants' sake, that they also may be
provided for and hear God's word, if haply they could not be at the other sermons. […] At the
Mass, at eight or nine, the gospel is preached which the season giveth through the year. In the
afternoon, at Vespers, before the *Magnificat*, the Old Testament is preached in order, one
after another.

The same pattern of morning, Mass and Vespers sermons runs through the town orders for the rest
of the century (§4.1). The Vespers sermon of the north German towns is described in
[`OFFICES_GUIDE.md`](OFFICES_GUIDE.md) §7.4.

### 3.3 The visitors of 1528: repentance and faith, Wednesday and Friday

**What to preach.** The *Unterricht der Visitatoren* of 1528 begins its doctrine with a
complaint. Many preach only the forgiveness of sins and leave out repentance, and the people
grow "secure and fearless". **Ernestine Saxony, *Unterricht der visitatoren an die pfarrherrn*,
1528** (Sehling 1, p. 152):

<!-- doc 9 -->
> Denn Christus spricht, Luce am letzten cap., das man predigen sol in seinem namen, busse und
> vergebung der sünden. Aber viel itzund sagen allein von vergebung der sünde, und sagen nichts,
> oder wenig, von busse. So doch on busse kein vergebung der sunden ist. Es kan auch vergebung
> der sünden nicht verstanden werden on busse. Und so man die vergebung der sunden prediget on
> busse, folget, das die leute wenen, sie haben schon vergebung der sunden erlanget, und werden
> dadurch sicher und forchtlos.

For Christ saith, in the last chapter of Luke, that repentance and forgiveness of sins should be
preached in his name. But many now speak only of the forgiveness of sins, and say nothing, or
little, of repentance. Whereas without repentance there is no forgiveness of sins. Neither can
forgiveness of sins be understood without repentance. And when forgiveness of sins is preached
without repentance, it followeth that the people ween they have already obtained forgiveness of
sins, and become thereby secure and fearless.

**When to preach.** The same book fixes the week. Besides the Sunday morning and Vespers
sermons, there is to be preaching on **Wednesday and Friday**, from "useful and not hard books".
**Ernestine Saxony, *Unterricht der visitatoren an die pfarrherrn*, 1528** (Sehling 1, p. 168):

<!-- doc 9 -->
> In der wochen sol man predigen am mitwoch und freitag. Es sol auch ein pfarherr vleis ankeren,
> das man nützliche und nicht schwere bücher fürneme zu predigen. Das auch der glaube also
> geprediget werde, das man der rechtschaffen christlichen busse, gottes gericht, gottesforcht,
> und guter werk […] nicht vergesse, denn man on die busse glauben nicht haben oder verstehen
> mag. Am feiertag, soll man morgens und zur vesper predigen, morgens das evangelion,
> nachmittag, weil das gesinde und junge volk in die kirchen kömpt, halten wir für gut, das man
> sontags nach mittag stetigs für und für, die zehen gebot, die artikel des glaubens, und das
> vater unser predige und auslege.

In the week one shall preach on Wednesday and Friday. A pastor shall also apply diligence that
useful and not hard books be taken in hand to preach. [And] that faith also be so preached, that
true Christian repentance, God's judgement, the fear of God and good works […] be not forgotten;
for without repentance one cannot have or understand faith. On the holy day one shall preach in
the morning and at Vespers: in the morning the gospel; in the afternoon, because the servants
and the young folk come into the church, we hold it good that on Sunday afternoon one
continually, over and over, preach and expound the Ten Commandments, the articles of the faith
and the Our Father.

These two passages set the pattern for the century: gospel on Sunday morning, catechism in the
afternoon, and one or two weekday sermons, with repentance preached together with faith.

## 4. Occasions and times

### 4.1 Sundays and feasts: the gospel in the morning, the catechism after noon

**The standard Sunday.** By mid-century the standard parish Sunday has two sermons:
- in the morning, on the **gospel** of the day, with the Lord's Supper when there are
  communicants;
- in the afternoon, on the **catechism** (in towns often the epistle instead, or both).

Where the young people cannot come on weekdays because of the field work, the catechism is kept
for Sunday and the epistle moves to a weekday. **Albertine Saxony, *General-Artikel und gemeiner
Bericht*, 1557** (Sehling 1, p. 323):

<!-- doc 37 -->
> sollen sie ire predigten also ordenen und disponiren, das allewege auf die sontage und andere
> christliche fest das evangelium früe geprediget, und, do communicanten vorhanden, das ambt
> gehalten, nachmittag aber allezeit der catechismus geprediget und geübet, und sonsten in der
> wochen auch eines tages einmal geprediget werde. Da auch das junge volk in der wochen von
> wegen des ackerbauens, oder anderer arbeit verhindert nicht leichtlich könt in die kirchen
> kommen, sol der catechismus auf den sontag nach mittage, und die epistel auf einen andern tag
> in der wochen ausgelegt werden

they shall so order and dispose their sermons, that always on the Sundays and other Christian
feasts the gospel be preached early, and, where communicants be present, the office [of the
Mass] be held, but in the afternoon the catechism be at all times preached and exercised, and
otherwise in the week also on one day [there] be preaching once. And whereas the young folk in
the week, by reason of the tilling of the fields or other work, could not easily come into the
church, the catechism shall be expounded on Sunday afternoon, and the epistle on another day in
the week

The great towns had more. Wittenberg in 1533 had preaching on Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and
Friday mornings (Sehling 1, p. 701).

### 4.2 Weekday sermons

**One or two a week.** The weekday sermon (*Wochenpredigt*) is required almost everywhere. Its
usual days are Wednesday and Friday, or one of them. In the villages it is often suspended when
the fields need the people. **Schlieben, *Kirchen-Ordnung für die Stadt Schlieben*, 1529**
(Sehling 1, p. 659):

<!-- doc 129 -->
> Am donnerstage in der wochen wirt eine predigt gethan, ausgenommen mitten im sommer von wegen
> des volks notiger arbeit und mitten im winter von wegen des volks, da wirt sie unterlassen.
> Anfenglich singet man einen deutschen gesang, dabei sich das volk samle. 2. Folget die
> predigt, darin entweder der catechismus gehandelt wird, auf das er in einem jahre hinaus
> gepredigt werden kan, oder so aposteltage fürfallen, die man nicht feierlich helt, wird
> dasselbige evangelium an solchem tage in der wochen gepredigt, oder epistola dominicalis. In
> der fasten wirt historia passionis dazu gebraucht.

On Thursday in the week a sermon is made, except in midsummer by reason of the people's needful
work, and in midwinter by reason of the people; then it is left off. At the beginning a German
hymn is sung, whereby the people may gather. 2. The sermon followeth, wherein either the
catechism is handled, that it may be preached through in one year, or, if apostles' days fall
which are not kept solemnly, the same gospel is preached on such a day in the week, or the
Sunday epistle. In Lent the history of the Passion is used for it.

Reutlingen turns the rule round. The village pastors are to preach a weekday sermon in
**winter**, "when there is no special business in the field". **Reutlingen, *Kirchenordnung*, c.
1531** (Sehling 17/2, p. 42):

<!-- doc 827 -->
> Sollichs soll auch den pfarhern in dörffern auffgelegt werden, also das sy auch den
> catechismum zu vier malen im jar predigen auff die feirtag nach dem morgenessen, darzu, das sy
> zu wintters zit, wann man nit sonder geschefft am feldt hat, alle wochen, so kein feyrtag
> darynn ist, am wercktag nach gelegenheit eins yden fleckenn auch ain predig thuen.

This shall also be laid upon the pastors in the villages, that they also preach the catechism
four times in the year, on the holy days after the morning meal; moreover, that in winter time,
when there is no special business in the field, every week wherein is no holy day, they also do
a sermon on a working day, according to the occasion of each place.

**The early sermon for working people.** In the towns an early weekday sermon (*Frühpredigt*)
was held before the day's work, for servants, day labourers and craftsmen, and kept to half an
hour. **Zwickau, *Verordnung der Visitatoren*, 1556** (Sehling 1, p. 726):

<!-- doc 155 -->
> Die zwene caplan in unser frauen pfarkirchen sollen drei tage in der wochen die fruepredigt
> vor das gesinde, tagelöner und handwergsleut halten, als nemlich dinstag, freitag und
> sonabend, weil die andern drei tage ane das geprediget wird. Desgleichen sollen auch die zwene
> zu S. Katharinen in irer pfarkirchen thun, und in peden kirchen nit lenger dan ein halb stunde
> die predigt vorziehen.

The two chaplains in Our Lady's parish church shall hold the early sermon three days in the week
for the servants, day labourers and craftsmen, namely Tuesday, Friday and Saturday, because on
the other three days there is preaching anyhow. Likewise shall the two at St Katharine's also do
in their parish church, and in both churches not draw out the sermon longer than half an hour.

Late in the century the weekday sermon was often badly attended. The counts of Ysenburg and
others required at least one person from each house to be present (§12.1).

### 4.3 Catechism sermons

**Every Sunday afternoon, and in series.** The catechism sermon (*Katechismuspredigt*) is
normally the Sunday afternoon sermon (§4.1). Many orders add a series in which the whole
catechism is preached through in a few weeks. Reutlingen has it "four times in the year" (§4.2).
At Torgau the four deacons preached the Small Catechism every quarter, for a fortnight on end.
**Torgau, *Verordnung der Visitatoren von 1575*** (Sehling 1, p. 683):

<!-- doc 141 -->
> Der pfarher im spital herr Joachim Sella legt des sontags zur früepredigt das evangelium
> dominicale aus, des donnerstags durch das ganze jar erkleret er den catechismum Lutheri. Der
> kleine catechismus würde alle quartal, vierzehen tage nacheinander in der wochen von den vier
> diaconis ordentlichen geprediget und erkleret.

The pastor in the hospital, Herr Joachim Sella, expoundeth the Sunday gospel at the early sermon
on Sunday; on Thursday, through the whole year, he explaineth Luther's catechism. The Small
Catechism is every quarter, fourteen days one after another in the week, preached and explained
in order by the four deacons.

The catechism sermon was often followed by an examination of the children (*Kinderlehre*,
*examen*). Its length was usually set at half an hour (§7.2).

### 4.4 Lent and the Passion

**Luther: not eight hours on Good Friday.** The *Deutsche Messe* keeps Lent and the Passion
gospels, but not the old Holy Week. Holy Week shall be "like other weeks", save that the Passion
is preached an hour a day. **Luther, *Deutsche Messe und ordnung gottis diensts*, 1526**
(Sehling 1, p. 16):

<!-- doc 3 -->
> doch nicht also, das man das hunger tuch, palmen schiessen, bilde decken und was des gauckel
> werks mehr ist, halten oder vier passion singen oder acht stunden am karfreitag an der passion
> zu predigen haben, sonder die marterwoche sol gleich wie ander wochen sein, on das man die
> passion predige des tages eine stunde durch die woche oder wie viel tage es gelustet, und das
> sacrament neme wer do wil.

yet not so that one keep the Lenten veil, the shooting of palms, the covering of images, and
whatever more there is of that juggling work, or sing four Passions, or have to preach eight
hours on the Passion on Good Friday; but Holy Week shall be even as other weeks, save that the
Passion be preached an hour a day through the week, or on as many days as one listeth, and that
whosoever will receive the sacrament.

**The Passion harmony in series.** The later orders divide the Passion history into a fixed
number of sermons. Henneberg 1582 has six in Lent, the first on Quinquagesima as a summary of
what is to come (Sehling 2, p. 313). The Coburg synod of 1580 advises that the whole history be
laid out on Maundy Thursday and Good Friday "in three distinct sermons". **Coburg, *Beschlüsse
des coburgischen Synodus*, 1580** (Sehling 1, p. 255):

<!-- doc 26 -->
> doch wird es vor nützlichen gehalten, das in dörfern und stedten die ganze historia passionis
> uf den donnerstag und karfreitag, wie sie sich begeben, in dreien unterschiedlichen predigten,
> dem volk kürzlich und einfeltig ausgeleget, damit die ganze historia, wie auch den andern
> festen der geburt und himmelfart Christi gebreuchlich, der gestalt das gemeine volk
> unverstendig dieselbige besser fassen, lernen und behalten mögte.

yet it is held profitable that in villages and towns the whole history of the Passion, on the
Thursday and Good Friday, as it came to pass, be expounded to the people briefly and simply in
three distinct sermons, that the whole history (as is customary also at the other feasts of the
Nativity and Ascension of Christ) the unlearned common people might in such wise the better
grasp, learn and retain [it].

### 4.5 Prayer days

**Days of repentance.** From the 1540s, and above all in time of war, plague and the Turkish
threat, the orders appoint **prayer days** (*Bettage*). These were weekly, monthly or
occasional, with a sermon of repentance at their centre. **Hesse, *Kirchenordnung*, 1566**
(Moderate Reformed; Sehling 8, p. 260):

<!-- doc 2257 -->
> Derhalben werden bei uns uf gewisse bestimpte tage bettage angestelt und gehalten, in welchen
> durch die predig die leut in erkantnus ihrer sünde bracht, zur buß und glauben vermanet, zum
> ernsten gebet und zu abbittung der straffe, auch zur besserung ihres lebens treulich
> angehalten werden.

Therefore with us prayer days are appointed and held on certain fixed days, in which by the
sermon the people are brought into knowledge of their sins, exhorted to repentance and faith,
and faithfully held to earnest prayer and to the begging off of the punishment, and also to the
amendment of their life.

The Palatine orders of the 1590s (Reformed) required attendance at the monthly prayer day on
pain of a fine (Sehling 19/2, pp. 777, 780).

The calendar of prayer days is treated in
[`LITURGICAL_CALENDAR_GUIDE.md`](LITURGICAL_CALENDAR_GUIDE.md), §13.5, and the Litany sung on
them in [`EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md`](EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md), §22.4.

### 4.6 Funeral sermons

**Not a eulogy.** The funeral sermon (*Leichpredigt*) spread from the 1550s. At Gardelegen in
the Mark, the first funeral sermon was held in 1554 (Sehling's introduction, Sehling 3, p. 218).
Most orders allow it when the family asks for it. Many keep it short and forbid praise of the
dead. **Kurpfalz, *Kirchenordnung*, 1563** (Reformed; Sehling 14, p. 406):

<!-- doc 504 -->
> Darauf soll er eine kurtze predig oder vermanung thun und sich ubriges lobens der
> abgestorbenen enthalten, damit die leichpredigen nit in mißbrauch gerahten.

Thereupon shall he do a short sermon or exhortation, and refrain from excessive praise of the
departed, that the funeral sermons fall not into abuse.

**What to preach, and from what book.** At the grave the subject is mortality, sin and death,
redemption, the resurrection and eternal life. The Oldenburg order points the pastor to the
printed funeral sermons of Johann Spangenberg and Johannes Mathesius. **Oldenburg,
*Kirchenordnung*, 1573** (Sehling 7/2.1, p. 1109):

<!-- doc 2151 -->
> sol der kirchendiener auftretten und von sterbhcheit und schwacheit der menschen, von ursachen
> der sünden und des tods, von erlösung durch Christum geschehen, von auferstehung der todten,
> von ewigen leben und dergleichen ein kurzen sermon und vermanung tun, wie er denn dazu aus den
> leichpredigten, so von alten herrn Johanne Spangenbergio, herrn Matthesio und andern […]

the minister shall step forth and of the mortality and weakness of men, of the causes of sin and
of death, of the redemption made through Christ, of the resurrection of the dead, of eternal
life and the like, do a short sermon and exhortation, as he [may] for this [draw] from the
funeral sermons which by old Master Johann Spangenberg, Master Mathesius and others […]

**Half an hour, or none at all.** Mecklenburg limits it: "if a funeral sermon is held, it shall
not be over half an hour" (*Conformitas ceremoniarum*, c. 1560; Sehling 5, p. 290). The
Hohenlohe visitors set the same limit (Sehling 15, p. 427). Some towns abolished funeral sermons
altogether, putting the old exhortation and a collect in their place, so that the people would
not be kept too long. **Aschersleben, *Kirchen-Agenda* (agreed by the council with the
ministers), 1575** (Sehling 2, p. 481):

<!-- doc 1260 -->
> Zum dritten, sollen die leichpredigten aus allerhand ursachen durchaus abgeschafft sein und an
> stat deroselben die alte verordente vermahnung, so ein generale funus, durch den hern pfarhern
> bei der hauswirt oder alten leichen, und die collecta, so man vor den heusern pflegt zulesen,
> bei der kinder und jungen leute begräbnus gelesen und die benedictio daruf gesprochen werden,
> damit die leute zulange nicht aufgehalten und die jugend in der schuelen verseumet werde

Thirdly, the funeral sermons shall for all manner of reasons be wholly abolished, and in their
stead the old appointed exhortation, [at] a general funeral, [be read] by the pastor at [the
burial of] householders or old people, and the collect which is wont to be read before the
houses be read at the burial of children and young people, and the blessing spoken thereupon,
that the people be not kept too long, and the youth in the school be not neglected.

Several orders refuse a funeral sermon for small children who had not yet come to the Lord's
Supper (Sehling 22, p. 480; Sehling 19/1, p. 430).

The burial rites in which the funeral sermon stood are treated in
[`EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md`](EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md), §9.

### 4.7 Wedding sermons

**Short and chaste.** The wedding sermon (*Hochzeitpredigt*, *Brautpredigt*) was customary in
many places and abolished in others. Anhalt allows it where custom has it or it is asked for,
but it must be "short, chaste and Christian". **Anhalt, *Kirchenordnung*, 1599** (Reformed;
Sehling 2, p. 582):

<!-- doc 1263 -->
> eine kurze, züchtige, christliche hochzeitpredigt (hindangesetzt alle ungeistliche,
> possirliche phrases und unzimliche sprichwörter, dadurch züchtige ohren der jugend leichtlich
> können verletzet werden) zur besserung gehalten, dazu denn die leut nüchtern und attent kommen
> sollen.

a short, chaste, Christian wedding sermon (all unspiritual, droll phrases and unseemly proverbs
set aside, whereby the chaste ears of the youth may lightly be hurt) [be] held for edification,
to which the people shall come sober and attentive.

Aschersleben abolished it in the same article as the funeral sermon. **Aschersleben,
*Kirchen-Agenda*, 1575** (Sehling 2, p. 481):

<!-- doc 1260 -->
> Es sollen forthin alle hochzeitpredigten abgeschafft sein, braut und breutgamb mit ihren
> geladenen gästen nur einmal des montags und nicht auch des sontags, wie bishero geschehen, in
> die kirchen zur trauung gehen, und also des montags die copulation und segen zugleich ohne
> alle predigt geschehen.

Henceforth all wedding sermons shall be abolished; bride and bridegroom with their bidden guests
[shall] go into the church to the marriage only once, on the Monday, and not also on the Sunday,
as hath been done hitherto; and so on the Monday the joining and the blessing [shall] be done
together, without any sermon.

At Grubenhagen, wedding guests were forbidden to lie "at the drinking, the early soup or the
brandy" during the wedding sermon (Sehling 6/2, p. 1065).

The marriage rites in which the wedding sermon stood are treated in
[`EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md`](EXTRAORDINARY_RITES_GUIDE.md), §7.

## 5. Texts: what was preached on

### 5.1 The Sunday gospels and epistles kept

**Why Luther kept the pericopes.** Luther kept the old series of Sunday epistles and gospels
(§3.2). He gave a practical reason: few preachers can handle a whole evangelist or another book
"mightily and profitably". **Luther, *Deutsche Messe und ordnung gottis diensts*, 1526**
(Sehling 1, p. 15):

<!-- doc 3 -->
> Denn auch das der ursachen eine ist, das wir die episteln und evangelia, wie sie in den
> postillen geordenet stehen, behalten, . das der geistreichen prediger wenig sind, die einen
> ganzen evangelisten oder ander buch gewaltiglich und nutzlich handeln mugen.

For this also is one of the causes why we keep the epistles and gospels as they stand ordered in
the postils: that there are few spiritual preachers who can handle a whole evangelist or other
book mightily and profitably.

The Lutheran orders follow him almost without exception. The visitation articles ask whether the
pastor preaches "the Sunday gospels" (*dominicalia evangelia*). The Hohenlohe visitors of 1578
charged a pastor with not keeping to the appointed gospels (Sehling's note, Sehling 15, p. 248).
The lessons themselves are treated in [`PROPERS_GUIDE.md`](PROPERS_GUIDE.md) §5.

### 5.2 Books in course on weekdays and at Vespers

**Lectio continua beside the pericopes.** Continuous preaching through a book of the Bible
(*lectio continua*) was not given up. It was moved to Vespers and to the weekday sermons. Luther
preached the Old Testament "in order" at Sunday Vespers (§3.2). The visitors of 1528 wanted
"useful and not hard books" for the weekday sermons (§3.3). In the towns the weekday preachers
worked through whole books. **Rötha, *Kirchen-Ordnung für Rötha*, 1574** (Sehling 1, p. 653):

<!-- doc 125 -->
> In der wochen wird am mittwoch früe gepredigt und gehandelt die bücher der heilichen schrift
> wie sie nach einander gehen, die vornembsten historien ein capitel nach dem andern, in der
> fasten den sontage zu mittage und an mittwoch wird die passion gepredigt.

In the week, on Wednesday early, there is preaching, and the books of holy Scripture are handled
as they go one after another, the chief histories, one chapter after another; in Lent, on Sunday
at midday and on Wednesday, the Passion is preached.

The Reformed Palatine order of 1563, repeated in 1601, goes further. Sermons are to be taken
from the canonical books, and those books expounded which are "most profitable to the common man
and most edifying to the church". In 1563 this applied "chiefly on the Sundays"
(Sehling 14, p. 557, with the variant noted there). The order thus allows a book in course on
Sunday as well as on weekdays.

### 5.3 The chief articles of doctrine through the year

**Loci communes.** Some orders asked the preacher to cover the chief heads of doctrine (*loci
communes*) in the course of a year's Sunday sermons. Pfalz-Veldenz 1574 has this rule (quoted in
§10.4). The catechism sermon did the same in a smaller compass. Where a sermon was on a feast,
the doctrine of the feast was to be taught: the Supper on Maundy Thursday (Mecklenburg c. 1560,
Sehling 5, p. 290), and the Passion on Good Friday (§4.4).

## 6. How to preach

### 6.1 The shape of a sermon

**Prayer, text, summary, doctrines, application, repetition.** The orders rarely give a
homiletic. Where they describe a sermon, they describe the same shape:
1. an exhortation to prayer, and a prayer or hymn;
2. the reading of the text;
3. a short summary (*summa*) of the text;
4. one, two or three doctrines drawn from it;
5. their application to the hearers;
6. a short repetition at the end;
7. the general prayer.

A village pastor in the county of Henneberg described his own practice in his report to the
visitors. **Ritschenhausen, *Gottesdienst-Ordnung*, 1562** (Sehling 2, p. 345):

<!-- doc 1249 -->
> idoch halte ich in meinen predigten gemeiniglich diesen proces und ordnung. 1. Vermane ich das
> volk zum gebet, darnach vertir ich aus einem buch den text des ordentlichen evangelii oder
> historien und zeige kurzlich den inhalt, desgleichen, wo es in die stück christlicher lehr
> gehorig, an; nehme nochmals heraus 1, 2 oder 3 lehr nach gelegenheit der zeit und zuhorer,
> darauf ich die dieselbe applicire. Entlichen widerhole ich kürzlich was gehandelt und beschlis
> also die predigt mit dem gemeinem gebet für alle stend und anligende not der ganzen
> christenheit und weret die ganze predigt uber 3 virtel einer stund nicht.

yet I keep in my sermons commonly this process and order. 1. I exhort the people to prayer;
thereafter I [read] out of a book the text of the appointed gospel or history, and show briefly
the content, likewise where it belongeth among the parts of Christian doctrine; then I take out
of it 1, 2 or 3 doctrines, according to the occasion of the time and the hearers, whereupon I
apply the same. Finally I repeat briefly what hath been handled, and so close the sermon with
the general prayer for all estates and the present need of the whole of Christendom; and the
whole sermon lasteth not over three quarters of an hour.

A Saxon town order describes the same outline, with the propositions repeated at the end "that
the hearers may know what they shall carry home with them". **Gottleuba, *Verzeichnus der
kirchenordnung zur Gotleuben*, 1567–1577** (Sehling 1, p. 567):

<!-- doc 87 -->
> Darauf folget die predigt, welche gemeiniglich über eine stunde sich nicht vorzeucht vom
> evangelio, da erstlich summa angezeicht, darnach propositiones oder themata, davon man handlen
> will, welche in fine repetirt, damit die auditores wissen, was sie mit sich anheim tragen
> sollen. Gemeingebet. Nach allen predigten bittet man für die not der ganze werde christenheit,
> vor alle drei regiment, geistlich, weltlich, und haushaltung, und auch für sondrer personen,
> die das gemeine gebet begeren und bedurfen.

Thereupon followeth the sermon on the gospel, which commonly is not drawn out over an hour;
wherein first the sum is shown, thereafter the propositions or themes which one will handle,
which are repeated at the end, that the hearers may know what they shall carry home with them.
The general prayer. After all sermons prayer is made for the need of the whole worthy
Christendom, for all three governments, spiritual, temporal and the household, and also for
particular persons who desire and need the common prayer.

**Doctrine, terror, comfort, exhortation.** The Corvey order of 1603, for the village of
Bruchhausen, sets out the same shape. It adds what the sermon is to do to each kind of hearer:
the ungodly are to be terrified, the troubled comforted, the careless warned. **Corvey,
*Kirchenordnung für das Corveyer Stiftsdorf Bruchhausen*, 1603** (Sehling 21, p. 244):

<!-- doc 1455 -->
> Folgents wirt die Predig angefangen, da dann, nach dem der Pfarher vom Predigstul ein kurtze
> vermahnung zum gebett gethan, die gantze Kirche eindrechtiglich singet das Vatter unser oder
> einen andern gewönlichen gesang nach gelegenheit der zeit. Nach diesem gesange wirdt der Text
> verlesen und folget darauff ein Christlich, dem Glauben ehnlich, auß Gottes Wort gezogen und
> mit demselben confirmirte und bestetigte erklerung, darin denn alle wege nach erforderung der
> fürgelesenen worte eins oder mehr Heuptstücke der Christlichen lehre fein ordentlich und
> unterscheidlich auffs aller kürtzeste, Also das es die einfeltigen vernehmen, begreiffen und
> behalten mügen, zur unterweisung fürtragen, die rohen, Gottlosen mit verkündigung Göttliches
> zorns geschrecket, die Gottseligen, ihrer Sünden halber bekümmerte Hertzen mit erinnerung
> Göttlicher gnaden getröstet, Die sichern und fahrlessigen ermahnet und einem jedern nach
> gelegenheit etwas zur besserung fürgehalten werden soll.

Next the sermon is begun; wherein, after the pastor hath made from the pulpit a short
exhortation to prayer, the whole church with one accord singeth the Our Father, or another
customary hymn according to the occasion of the time. After this hymn the text is read, and
thereupon followeth a Christian explanation, agreeable to the faith, drawn out of God's Word and
confirmed and established therewith; wherein always, as the words read out require, one or more
chief parts of Christian doctrine shall be set forth for instruction, finely, orderly and
distinctly, as briefly as may be, so that the simple may perceive, grasp and retain it; the rude
[and] ungodly shall be terrified with the proclamation of God's wrath; the godly, hearts
troubled because of their sins, comforted with the remembrance of God's grace; the secure and
careless exhorted; and to every one, as occasion serveth, something set before him for
amendment.

### 6.2 Law and gospel, repentance and faith

**The content.** The visitors of 1528 insisted that repentance be preached with forgiveness
(§3.3). The later orders put the same thing in the language of law and gospel. **Wurzen,
*Gemeine Artikel*, 1542** (Sehling 2, p. 96):

<!-- doc 1223 -->
> Alle pfarrer sollen auch vleissig vor allen dingen der lehre und ires pfar ampts und selsorg
> warten, und dasselbige durch die predige der buss, des gesetz und evangelii, glauben und
> liebe, gedult und auch die gute werk von got geboten mit allen treuen vleis treiben, one
> welchs kein selwartung oder recht christlich gemein bestehen kann.

All pastors shall also diligently, before all things, wait upon the doctrine and their pastoral
office and cure of souls, and drive the same with all faithful diligence by the preaching of
repentance, of the law and the gospel, faith and love, patience, and also the good works
commanded of God; without which no cure of souls nor right Christian congregation can stand.

**The uses of the sermon.** The Hessian order of 1566 (Moderate Reformed) lists the uses of a
sermon from 2 Timothy 3:16. It sets the limit of an hour, and it points to the short but weighty
sermons of the Fathers. **Hesse, *Kirchenordnung*, 1566**
(Moderate Reformed; Sehling 8, p. 242):

<!-- doc 2257 -->
> Es sollen aber die predigten nit uber ein stund weren und dahin gerichtet sein, daß sie leren,
> straffen, züchtigen, vermanen, bessern, trösten und sterken. Man find oft in der heiligen
> vetter bücher kurze predige, aber gar wichtig und welche den zuhörern das herz treffen und
> bewegen und ein merklich nachdenkens hinder sich lassen.

But the sermons shall not last over an hour, and shall be directed thereto, that they teach,
reprove, correct, exhort, amend, comfort and strengthen. One findeth oft in the books of the
holy Fathers short sermons, but very weighty, and such as smite and move the hearts of the
hearers and leave behind them a notable reflection.

### 6.3 Plain speech for the common man

**Clear words, no bitterness.** The orders want the sermon plain enough for the simple to
follow. Schleswig-Holstein 1542 puts the method, the length and the manner into one paragraph:
- pray first, read the text, then expound it;
- not more than an hour;
- speak the truth "with clean words, so that he may be understood";
- no abuse and no rebuking anyone by name from the pulpit (§9.1).

**Schleswig-Holstein, *Kirchenordnung*, 1542** (Sehling 23, p. 91):

<!-- doc 1576 -->
> De, de Predigen wil, schal thom ersten dat volck vormanen tho vlitiger anropinge der hulpe
> Gades, Darna den Text, den he duden wil, dem volcke vorlesen, Thom lesten ock uthleggen, nicht
> aver eine stunde lanck synen Sermon vorthen, ock syner bewechnisse nicht nageven, allene, wat
> de warheit ys, dat schal he mit reinen wörden uthsprecken, So dat he möge vorstan werden. Yn
> Sonderheit schal sick ein Prediger aller scheldwort unde bittericheit entholden, ock nemande
> up dem predigstole mit namen straffen, allene de laster ynt gemene hen, unde wat he vor
> gewisse gehört hefft, anfechten unde vordömen. Wes eme nicht bewust, darvan mach he swigen.

He that will preach shall first exhort the people to diligent calling upon the help of God;
thereafter read to the people the text which he will expound; lastly also lay it out, [and] not
draw out his sermon over an hour long, nor give way to his [own] emotion; only what is the
truth, that shall he speak out with clean words, so that he may be understood. In especial shall
a preacher refrain from all words of railing and bitterness, and rebuke no one from the pulpit
by name; only assail and condemn the vices in general, and what he hath heard for certain.
Whereof he hath no knowledge, thereof may he be silent.

**Fitted to the hearers.** At the end of the century the Reformed Upper Palatine government told
its preachers to suit the sermon to the hearers. They were to repeat the foundations "in the
simplest way", and to show the hearers how to apply them in repentance and prayer. **Kurpfalz
(Upper Palatinate), *Generalanweisung*, 1598** (Reformed; Sehling 13, p. 349):

<!-- doc 427 -->
> daß sie sich in ihren predigen nach der gelegenheit der zuhörer richten, denselben dasjenige,
> so zum rechten fundament der christlichen lehr und zu wahrer gottseeligkeit dienstlich, wie
> sie das sonsten in den predigten oft und viel uf das einfaltigste widerholen und wol
> inculciren, auch zeigen, wie sie dasselbige in exercitiis poenitentiae et invocationis ihnen
> selbst appliciren und gottseelich brauchen sollen.

that they direct themselves in their sermons according to the condition of the hearers, [and]
that which serveth to the right foundation of Christian doctrine and to true godliness, the same
they [shall] otherwise in the sermons oft and much repeat in the simplest way and well
inculcate, and also show how they shall apply the same to themselves in the exercises of
repentance and of prayer, and use it godly.

The Palatine class conventions of 1587 (Reformed) asked whether a preacher used "good German"
and not "Netherlandish phrases", and whether he spoke slowly enough (§11.3).

## 7. Length

### 7.1 An hour on Sunday morning, at most

**The common rule.** Almost every order that sets a length gives the chief Sunday sermon **one
hour at most**. Many give it three quarters. The reason given is always the hearer: the common
man cannot attend longer, nor carry more away. **Augsburg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1537**
(Moderate Reformed; Sehling 12, p. 58):

<!-- doc 338 -->
> Es sollen auch dise alle gemaine sontägspredigen nit uber ain stund weren; dann das gemain
> volk je nieht lenger ufmerklich bleiben und, was man da handlet zur besserung, behalten kan.

All these common Sunday sermons also shall not last over an hour; for the common people can in
no wise remain attentive longer, and retain what is there handled for [their] amendment.

The Württemberg order of 1536 compares the common man to a sick man, "to whom one must set food
often, but little at once". **Württemberg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1536** (Sehling 16, p. 104):

<!-- doc 651 -->
> Es sollen aber die predigen auch auff die Sontag unnd Feyrtag über ein stundt nit verzogen
> werden, damit die leut nit mit der vile unnd lengin überschütt und verdrützig werden, dieweil
> ye des gemeinen mans verstandt sich nit dermass auff einmal sovill mit lust zufassen auffthun
> mag, sonder mit ihme gleichsam einem krancken zuhandlen, dem man offt, aber wenig auff einmal
> fürstellen mus.

But the sermons also on Sundays and holy days shall not be drawn out over an hour, that the
people be not overwhelmed with the muchness and length, and grow weary; since the understanding
of the common man can in no wise open itself to grasp so much at once with delight, but [one
must] deal with him as it were with a sick man, to whom one must set [things] before him often,
but little at once.

The Hohenlohe order of 1578 repeats this sentence almost word for word, and sets the length at
"three quarters of an hour, and at the most not over an hour" (Sehling 15, p. 263).

Regensburg gives a reason from memory: long sermons do not edify; "they weaken the memory and
make the hearers weary". **Regensburg, *Kirchenregimentsordnung*, 1572** (Sehling 13, p. 510):

<!-- doc 452 -->
> Nachdeme lange predigten nicht erbaulich, sondern die gedächtnus schwächen und die zuhörer
> verdrossen machen, sollen die morgenpredigten an sonn- und feiertägen nicht mehr über eine
> stund verzogen, sondern aufs längst eine stunde gehalten werden. In den wochenpredigten aber
> sollen die ministri sich nicht weniger, sondern auch vielmehr der kürze befleißigen

Forasmuch as long sermons are not edifying, but weaken the memory and make the hearers weary,
the morning sermons on Sundays and holy days shall not be drawn out more than over an hour, but
be held an hour at the longest. In the weekday sermons, however, the ministers shall study
brevity no less, but rather much more

### 7.2 Half an hour on weekdays and for the catechism

**Shorter sermons.** The afternoon, weekday, catechism, funeral and wedding sermons are
generally held to **half an hour**. The Saxon visitors of 1580 were told to ask about all three
limits. **Albertine Saxony, *Ordnung* of Duke August, 1580** (Sehling 1, p. 393):

<!-- doc 44 -->
> Ob er auch lenger denn ein stund, morgens an sonn und feiertagen predige. Ob er die
> mittagspredigt also anstelle, das mit singen und allem lenger nicht denn ein stund das volk
> aufgehalten werde. Ob er am werktage uber ein halbe stund predige.

Whether he preach longer than an hour in the morning on Sundays and holy days. Whether he so
order the midday sermon that with singing and all the people be kept no longer than an hour.
Whether on the working day he preach over half an hour.

The same order limits the singing before the sermon, so that the hymns do not eat up the hour
(Sehling 1, p. 370). Nassau-Weilburg 1576 does the same: at most half an hour of singing before
the sermon on holy days, and a quarter on weekdays (Sehling 10, p. 229).

### 7.3 Weather and work

**Shorter in the cold.** Some orders shorten the sermon for the season. At Obereisenheim the
pastor is to start at eight sharp and cut the sermon shorter in winter, so that the poor and the
badly clothed young people have no cause to leave the church for the cold. **Castell,
*Dorfordnung für Obereisenheim*, 1579** (Sehling 11, p. 689):

<!-- doc 315 -->
> Es soll sich auch der pfarrer bevleißigen, wann man zuesammenleutet, daß er alsobalden in die
> kirchen gehe, die gesang nicht zu lang mache und gewißlich umb acht uhr anfahe zue predigen,
> die predigt über eine stund nicht verziehe und sonderlich winterzeit in der kälte noch mehr
> abkürze, hernacher auch das gebet und gesänge auch nit verlenger, damit der arme mann und die
> jugend, so etwas ubel bekleidet, nicht ursach haben, frost halben aus der kirche [zu laufen …]

The pastor shall also endeavour, when the bells ring together, that he go forthwith into the
church, make not the hymns too long, and surely begin to preach at eight o'clock, draw out the
sermon not over an hour, and especially in winter time, in the cold, cut it yet shorter;
afterwards also lengthen not the prayer and hymns, that the poor man and the youth, who are
somewhat ill clad, have no cause by reason of the frost [to run] out of the church […]

Troppau 1592 gives one hour in summer, three quarters in winter, and half an hour on weekdays
(Sehling 3, p. 478). The weekday sermon was set early, or held only in winter, so as not to take
the people from their work (§4.2).

### 7.4 Overrunning

**Complaints.** The limits were not always kept. In 1594 the Count of Hohenlohe complained that
his pastors preached far beyond the appointed time, "sometimes an hour and a half", to the
annoyance of the hearers. **Hohenlohe, *Befehl wegen des Kirchengesangs und der Predigtlänge*,
1594** (Sehling 15, p. 639):

<!-- doc 627 -->
> sondern auch, das die predigten uber hievor verordnete zeit weits lenger und manchmals uf
> anderhalben stunden

but also that the sermons [have been drawn out] far longer than the time appointed heretofore,
and sometimes to an hour and a half

The same command restores the limit of about three quarters of an hour (Sehling 15, p. 640).
Visitors were told to ask how long the pastor preached: in Saxony in 1580 (§7.2), and in
Hohenlohe in 1587, "how long, roughly, he letteth his sermon last" (Sehling 15, p. 532).

## 8. Postils and preparation

### 8.1 Reading a postil from the pulpit

**Luther's advice.** A postil was a printed collection of sermons on the Sunday epistles and
gospels. Luther thought it would be best to read his postil to the people. This was not only for
the sake of preachers who "could do no better". It was also a guard against enthusiasts and
sects, lest everyone preach what he likes, "of blue ducks" instead of the gospel. **Luther,
*Deutsche Messe und ordnung gottis diensts*, 1526** (Sehling 1, pp. 14–15):

<!-- doc 3 -->
> Und mich dunkt, wo man die deudsche postillen gar hette durchs jar, es were das beste, das man
> verordente, die postillen des tages ganz oder ein stucke aus dem buch dem volk fur zu lesen,
> nicht alleine umb der prediger willen, die es nicht besser kunden, sondern auch umb der
> schwermer und secten willen zuverhueten, wie man sihet und spuret an den homilien in der
> metten, das etwa eben auch solche weyse gewesen ist. Sonst, wo nicht geistlicher verstand und
> der geist selbst redet durch die prediger (wilchem ich nicht wil hiemit zil setzen; der geist
> leret wol bas reden, denn alle postillen und homilien), so komts doch endlich dahin, das ein
> iglicher predigen wird was er wil, und an stat des evangelii und seiner auslegunge widerumb
> von blau enten gepredigt wird.

And it seemeth to me, if one had the German postil complete through the year, it were the best
that it be ordained to read to the people the postil of the day, whole or a piece out of the
book; not only for the sake of the preachers who could do no better, but also to guard against
the enthusiasts and sects; as one seeth and perceiveth from the homilies at Matins, that such
also was once the manner. Otherwise, where spiritual understanding and the Spirit himself
speaketh not through the preachers (to whom I will not hereby set a bound; the Spirit teacheth
better to speak than all postils and homilies), it cometh at last to this, that every one will
preach what he will, and instead of the gospel and its exposition there will again be preaching
of blue ducks.

**Village pastors.** The Albertine orders of 1545 apply this to the poor village pastors. Until
they can preach "out of their own understanding", they are to read from the pulpit Corvinus's
postil or Luther's *Hauspostille*. **Albertine Saxony, *Die Cellischen Ordnungen*, 1545**
(Sehling 1, p. 297):

<!-- doc 33 -->
> Wo aber auf den dörfern arme einfeltige pfarern sölchs zuthun nicht vormöchten, damit sie
> nicht aus ihrem kopfe, eigenen tünkel oder sunst etwas irriges predigten, söllen sie für sich
> nehmen die postillam Corvini oder die hauspostil Lutheri und an dem sontage die auslegung des
> evangelii fur dem volke auf der canzel draus lesen, bis das sie aus langer ubung dermal einst
> auch möchten dahin kommen, das sie aus eigenem vorstande eine christliche predigt thun kunten.

But where in the villages poor simple pastors were not able to do this, lest they preach out of
their own head, their own conceit, or otherwise anything erroneous, they shall take before them
Corvinus's postil or Luther's house postil, and on the Sunday read out of it to the people from
the pulpit the exposition of the gospel, until through long practice they also might at length
come so far that they could make a Christian sermon out of their own understanding.

Many orders required every parish to own Luther's postils with the Bible and the catechisms. In
the Brandenburg villages the pastor had to own the Bible, the *Hauspostille*, the catechisms and
the church order (Sehling 3, pp. 153, 204).

### 8.2 Against preaching from postils

**Borrowed feathers.** By the second half of the century the complaint is the opposite. Young
men pass the examination by learning answers by heart, have read little of the Bible, and then
"betake themselves to postils, adorn themselves with strange feathers". **Albertine Saxony,
*Ordnung* of Duke August, 1580** (Sehling 1, p. 377):

<!-- doc 44 -->
> das die jungen studiosi, so sich auf das predigampt zubegeben fürhabens, solche examina von
> wort zu wort oftmals nicht allein mit grossem unverstand auswendig gelernet, und, da sie mit
> andern worten befraget, nicht allein nichts antworten können, sondern auch mehrmals im alten
> und neuen testament ganz und gar nichts gelesen, welche nachmals sich auf postillen begeben,
> mit frembden federn schmücken, und eigener kunst oder geschickligkeit, damit sie der kirchen
> dienen solten, wenig bei sich haben.

that the young students who purpose to betake themselves to the preaching office oftentimes have
not only learned such examinations word for word by heart with great want of understanding, and,
when they are asked in other words, can answer nothing at all, but also have many times read
nothing whatever in the Old and New Testament; who afterwards betake themselves to postils,
adorn themselves with strange feathers, and have little art or skill of their own wherewith they
should serve the church.

**Write your sermons.** Nassau-Dillenburg orders its pastors to study the Bible and to write all
their sermons out, not to take them "without understanding" from the printed postils.
**Nassau-Dillenburg, *Visitationsabschied*, 1570** (Sehling 10, p. 110):

<!-- doc 180 -->
> Es sollen auch die pastores durch die speciallsuperintendenten zu vleißigem lesen unnd
> studiren in der bibeln angehalten werden, das sie auch alle ire predigten schreiben unnd
> solche nicht ohne verstandt hin unnd wieder aus den ausgegangenen unnd gedrucktten postillen
> ziehen unnd nemen, sondern vielmehr aus Gottes wortt und heiliger schrifft der propheten unnd
> aposteln selber.

The pastors also shall be held by the special superintendents to diligent reading and studying
in the Bible, that they also write all their sermons, and draw and take them not without
understanding here and there out of the published and printed postils, but rather out of God's
word and the holy Scripture of the prophets and apostles itself.

**Use them, but do not lean on them.** The middle way is put most clearly in the lordship of
Wolfstein. The pastor is to use "pure postils", which are named, but not rely on them wholly:
they were published "not to make lazy pastors". **Wolfstein, *Christliche Instructio des Thomas
Stieber*, 1574** (Sehling 13, p. 569):

<!-- doc 463 -->
> und in solcher predig sich rainer postillen als Lutheri, Philippi, Brencii, Spangenbergii,
> Mathesii und anderer gottseligen lehrer gebrauchen. Doch sollen sich die pfarrherrn nicht ganz
> und gar allain auf die postillen verlassen, sondern sollen auch selbst studiren, wie denn
> solche postillen nicht darumb seind publicirt worden, daß sie solten faule pfarhern machen,
> sondern daß die prediger und pfarhern daraus lernen sollen, wie sie die lehre göttliches worts
> füren und bei ihren zuhörern gebrauchen sollen.

and in such sermon make use of pure postils, as Luther's, Philip's, Brenz's, Spangenberg's,
Mathesius's and [those] of other godly teachers. Yet shall the pastors not rely wholly and
altogether only on the postils, but shall also study themselves; as indeed such postils were not
published for this, that they should make lazy pastors, but that the preachers and pastors
should learn out of them how they shall set forth the doctrine of God's word and use it among
their hearers.

The Palatine visitation articles of 1594 (Reformed) ask "out of what writings and commentaries" the
ministers draw and gather their sermons (Sehling 19/2, p. 822).

### 8.3 Meditation and writing

**Not into the pulpit unprepared.** Late orders expect a written sermon, or at least a written
outline. In Sponheim an old pastor who could not write out every sermon might keep to a written
disposition of its parts. He was to show it at the ministers' conventions, to prove "that he
hath not gone up into the pulpit without foregoing meditation". **Sponheim (Hintere Grafschaft),
*Generalartikel*, 1608** (Sehling 18, p. 672):

<!-- doc 1021 -->
> Doch wird zu gemelter inspectorn discretion gestelt, ob ein prediger etwan hohen alters halben
> sein verhinderung hette, daß er nit alle predigt zu concipiren vermögt, das derselbe bei
> schriftlicher disposition partium gelassen würde, die er bei den conventibus ufzulegen und
> damit zu dociren hatt, daß er nit ohne vorgehende meditation uff die Canzel gestiegen.

Yet it is left to the discretion of the said inspectors, whether a preacher, perchance by reason
of great age, had his hindrance, that he could not compose all [his] sermons, [that] the same be
left at a written disposition of the parts, which he hath to lay before the conventions, and
therewith to prove that he hath not gone up into the pulpit without foregoing meditation.

**Assum's method.** Sehling's notes to the Hohenlohe questions of 1587 summarize the advice of
the court preacher Johann Assum. The pastor is to pray, read the text diligently, and make his
own thoughts on it: its argument, its division, parallels and examples from other parts of
Scripture. He is then to write it out "as if he were the very first writer on the Bible", and
only then read postils and amend his draft (editor's note, Sehling 15, p. 532).

## 9. Rebuke, polemic and the limits of the pulpit

### 9.1 Rebuke the vices, name no one

**The office of rebuke.** No order takes rebuke out of the sermon. The preacher is to rebuke sin
openly; the Saxon order of 1580 calls this his *Strafamt* (§9.2). But he is to rebuke vices "in
general" and leave persons unnamed. Private sinners are to be dealt with privately, following
Matthew 18. **Merseburg, *Was den pfarhern auf itztkünftigen synodo (1545) solle angezeigt
werden*** (Sehling 2, p. 26):

<!-- doc 1212 -->
> und dargegen zu christlichen guten werken und wandel vormahnen, auch die laster in genere
> strafen durch die heilige schrift und exempel derselben, aber nimandes specifice angreifen,
> ader in ban zuthun sich understehen, und lestern und unartiges scheltens auf der canzel und
> sünst sich enthalten.

and on the other hand exhort to Christian good works and conversation, and also rebuke the vices
in general by the holy Scripture and its examples, but attack no one specifically, nor take it
upon themselves to put [any] under the ban, and refrain from reviling and unseemly railing in
the pulpit and elsewhere.

An Augsburg preacher's contract of 1535 (Moderate Reformed) has him swear to rebuke "gently and
modestly", "but neither with naming nor with pointing at particular persons". **Augsburg,
*Bestallung eines Predigers*, 1535** (Moderate Reformed; Sehling 12, p. 46):

<!-- doc 336 -->
> daneben das ubel der notturft nach anregen, wie ainem getreuen christenlichen predicanten
> gezimpt, sanftmuetig und beschaidenlich strafen und von der canzel meniglich in der gemein,
> aber weder mit benamung noch andeutung sonderer personen vor allen falschen gotsdiensten,
> lastern und sunden zu der eer, auch warem dienst Gottes und allen loblichen tugenden, wie sich
> gepurt, ermanen

[and] besides touch upon the evil as need requireth, as becometh a faithful Christian preacher,
rebuke gently and modestly, and from the pulpit exhort every one in common, but neither with
naming nor with pointing at particular persons, away from all false worship, vices and sins, to
the honour and also the true service of God and all laudable virtues, as is meet

The Schleswig-Holstein order of 1542 says the same, and adds: "whereof he hath no knowledge,
thereof may he be silent" (§6.3).

**When names were spoken.** The pulpit was used for names, but by order and not by the
preacher's choice:
- **Excommunication.** Where the steps of Matthew 18 had failed, the consistory's sentence was
  read from the pulpit, with the name. **Mecklenburg, *Consistorialordnung*, 1570**
  (Sehling 5, p. 245):

<!-- doc 1926 -->
> so sollen sie in der kirchen oder kirchspiel, darin sie wohnen, durch eine öffentliche sentenz
> des consistorii von der canzel mit namen in den bann verkündiget und aus der gemeinschaft der
> heiligen verstossen werden.

they shall in the church or parish wherein they dwell, by a public sentence of the consistory,
be proclaimed by name from the pulpit into the ban, and be thrust out of the communion of
saints.

- **Banns of marriage.** Couples were "proclaimed" from the pulpit, with their parents and
  masters if they were strangers (§10.4).

### 9.2 No private grudges in the pulpit

**The visitors' questions.** The Saxon order of 1580 has the visitors ask how the pastor shows
himself "in his office of rebuke". Does he rebuke with Christian gentleness, or "out of private
affection and revenge" bring his own affairs into the pulpit and name people? **Albertine
Saxony, *Ordnung* of Duke August, 1580** (Sehling 1, p. 394):

<!-- doc 44 -->
> Wie sich der pfarrer (oder in stedten auch andere kirchendiener) in seinem strafampt erzeige,
> ob er mit christlicher sanftmut und guter bescheidenheit strafe, oder aus privat affection und
> rachgier, seine eigene sachen auf die canzel bringe, die leut namhaftig, oder sonst
> unvermeldet doch ausgemalet ubel ausmache, sich zu zorn bewegen lasse, scharfer,
> ungebürlicher, stachlichter, schmehelicher, grober wort und geberde in den predigten
> gebrauche.

How the pastor (or in towns also other ministers) showeth himself in his office of rebuke:
whether he rebuke with Christian gentleness and good discretion, or out of private affection and
revenge bring his own affairs into the pulpit, name the people, or otherwise, unnamed yet
painted out, make them out evil, let himself be moved to anger, [use] sharp, unseemly, stinging,
reviling, coarse words and gestures in the sermons.

Later in the same order the visitors are told that the rule against preaching against persons
concerns only these "offensive sermons". It does not forbid rebuke itself (Sehling 1, p. 422).

### 9.3 Disputations belong in the schools

**Controversy in the pulpit.** The doctrinal quarrels of the 1550s to 1580s produced a series of
mandates against "railing and condemning" from the pulpit. The Ansbach margrave told his clergy
that disputations belong "in the universities, and not in the churches in the pulpit".
**Brandenburg-Ansbach-Kulmbach, *Begleitschreiben zur Konkordie von 1570***
(Sehling 11, p. 377):

<!-- doc 290 -->
> das sie sich in betrachtung ires ambts und jetziger kirchen vorstehender gelegenheit und
> anfechtung uf der canzel alles ergerlichen disputirens genzlich enthalten. Dann die
> disputationes uf die hohen schulen und nicht in die kirchen auf die canzel (aldo allein, was
> bauet, gelehret, aber, was abbricht und ergert, vermitten bleiben soll) gehörig, wie dann auch
> ohne das nichts so einfeltig fürgebracht, das nit etwo von etzlichen in mißverstand gezogen
> wurdet.

that, in consideration of their office and of the present state and assault of the church, they
refrain wholly in the pulpit from all offensive disputing. For the disputations belong in the
universities and not in the churches in the pulpit (where only what buildeth up should be
taught, but what breaketh down and offendeth should be avoided); as indeed, even without that,
nothing is set forth so simply that it be not drawn by some into misunderstanding.

**Princes against condemnation.** The princes of the Lower Saxon Circle, meeting at Lüneburg in
1562, forbade "unseemly railing and reviling in the pulpit". They also forbade the condemnation
of others "who have not yet been heard nor ever convicted", on pain of banishment. **Lower Saxon
Circle, *Abschied des Lüneburger Kreistages*, 1562** (Sehling 23, p. 177):

<!-- doc 1582 -->
> Wann dann das ungebürliehe Schelten und Lestern auf der Cantzel, auch Condemnirung anderer
> Leute, die noch nicht gehöret noch jemals überwunden seyn, frommen und christlichen Predigern
> nicht gezimet, auch solch Vornement nicht allein wider Gottes Befehl und die christliche Liebe
> ist, sondern vielmehr dadurch grosse Widerwärtigkeit und Mißtrauen zwischen hohen und niedern
> Personen, auch Verachtung aller Religion entstehet

Whereas then the unseemly railing and reviling in the pulpit, and also the condemning of other
people who have not yet been heard nor ever convicted, becometh not godly and Christian
preachers, and such an undertaking is not only against God's command and Christian love, but
rather great hostility and mistrust between high and low persons, and also contempt of all
religion, ariseth thereby

The same year the Bremen council (Philippist) forbade railing in the pulpit and bound
preaching to Scripture and the Augsburg Confession (Sehling 7/2.2, p. 540). Johann Casimir's
Reformed government in the Palatinate issued a mandate "against condemnations" in 1584
(Sehling 14, p. 512). In 1609 Duke Johann Adolf of Gottorf forbade "unedifying school questions"
in sermons, on pain of loss of office (Sehling's introduction, Sehling 23, pp. 323–324). The
Upper Palatine *Generalanweisung* of 1598 (Reformed; §6.3) begins its rule on plain preaching
with the same complaint: preachers who angered themselves "in so holy a place" over matters of
the schools.

**No politics.** Some orders also kept the preacher out of the business of the magistrate. The
Colmar preachers' oaths of 1575, 1590 and 1615 forbade reviling anyone in the pulpit for their
views on disputed questions. They also bade the preachers abstain from "political matters and
administrations" and not disparage the council (Sehling's introduction, Sehling 20/2, p. 479).

## 10. Around the sermon: prayer, greeting, confession, intercession and notices

### 10.1 The medieval pulpit office

**What the reformers inherited.** Sehling's editor sets out the background in a note to an early
East Frisian order, Norden's of 1528 (Reformed). Long before the Reformation the preacher said a
group of vernacular pieces from the pulpit, mostly after the sermon:
- intercessions, or bidding to intercession;
- the Lord's Prayer and the Creed;
- in many places the open confession (*Offene Schuld*) with absolution;
- from the thirteenth century also the Ten Commandments and the *Ave Maria*;
- "interjections and announcements".

The orders of the Reformation kept this group around the sermon. They dropped the *Ave Maria*
and turned the rest into the prayer before the sermon and the confession, general prayer and
notices after it (editor's note, Sehling 7/1, p. 431).

### 10.2 The prayer before the sermon

**An exhortation to pray, and a hymn or the Our Father.** The preacher goes up, exhorts the
people to pray for the Holy Spirit and for the fruit of the Word, and a prayer follows. Often it
is the Lord's Prayer, said or sung, or the hymn "Nun bitten wir den heiligen Geist" (see
[`HYMN_PRACTICE_GUIDE.md`](HYMN_PRACTICE_GUIDE.md) §10.1). At Nördlingen the first stanza of
"Nun bitten" opened the sermon with the Lord's Prayer. **Nördlingen, *Ordnung der ceremonien in
der pfarkirchen zu Sant Georgen* (Caspar Löner), 1544**
(Sehling 12, p. 319; dated 1555 in the running head):

<!-- doc 373 -->
> Nun bitten wir den Hailigen Gaist, auf der canzel anzufahen, das erste gesetz mit dem Vater
> unser zum anfang der predig, welche aus des sontags evangelion oder des feiertages allzeit
> geschehen soll.

"Now pray we the Holy Ghost", the first stanza, to be begun in the pulpit, with the Our Father,
at the beginning of the sermon, which shall always be made out of the gospel of the Sunday or of
the holy day.

In Kurland the preacher might intone the Lord's Prayer from the pulpit and let the whole
congregation sing it, "as is customary in many evangelical churches of the German nation".
**Kurland, *Kurländische Kirchenordnung*, 1570** (Sehling 5, p. 92):

<!-- doc 1907 -->
> Dar man auch den text des vater unsers für der predige bisweiln auf der canzel wil intonirn,
> und der ganzen gemeine singen lassen, wie sonst in vielen evangelischen kirchen deutscher
> nation gewönlicher

Where one will also sometimes intone the text of the Our Father before the sermon in the pulpit,
and let the whole congregation sing it, as is otherwise customary in many evangelical churches
of the German nation

### 10.3 The greeting

**The apostolic greeting.** The Palatine order of 1601 (Reformed) prescribes a fixed opening for
every sermon: the apostolic greeting, in one of two forms. **Kurpfalz, *Kirchenordnung*, 1601**
(Reformed; Sehling 14, p. 558):

<!-- doc 515 -->
> Gemeiner eingang aller predigten. Gnad, fried und barmhertzigkeit von Gott, dem vater, und
> seinem geliebten sohn Jesu Christo, unserm herrn, sampt der gemeinschaft des heiligen geistes
> sey mit uns allen, Amen. Oder also: Die gnad unsers herrn Jesu Christi und die liebe Gottes
> und die gemeinschaft des heiligen geistes sey mit euch allen, Amen, 2.Corint. 13. V. 13.

The common entrance of all sermons. Grace, peace and mercy from God the Father and his beloved
Son Jesus Christ our Lord, together with the communion of the Holy Ghost, be with us all. Amen.
Or thus: The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, and the love of God, and the communion of the Holy
Ghost, be with you all. Amen. 2 Corinthians 13, verse 13.

### 10.4 After the sermon: confession, intercession and notices

**The order of the pulpit.** In many southern and western orders the sermon is followed, still
in the pulpit, by:
1. an exhortation to prayer;
2. the open confession of sins and the absolution or "comfort";
3. the general prayer, with particular intercessions for the sick, for those in trouble and for
   newly married couples;
4. the notices: banns, feasts of the coming week, alms.

**Pfalz-Veldenz, *Kirchenordnung*, 1574** (Sehling 18, p. 522):

<!-- doc 1011 -->
> Nach vollendtem gesang gehet der Pfarrer uff die Cantzel, facht mit dem gebett die predig an,
> liset dann den Text des Evangelii und erclerets nach seiner gaab und der zuhörer gelegenheit,
> doch nit uber ein stundt. Unnd sollen sich die Pfarrer befleissigen, die fürnembste Locos
> communes in den sontags predigen durch das jahr dem volckh aigentlich und verständtlich auß
> Göttlicher schrifft zu erklären. Nach der Predig (dieweil als dann die anzahl des volckhs am
> grösten) vermahnet er zum gemeinen gebett, thut die offentliche gemein bekhantnus der sünden
> sampt dem Trost und vergebung der sünden, wie hiebevor verzaichnet fol. Erzehlet darnach
> allerlay anligende noth, darfür nöttig zu pitten, dessgleichen wann newe Eheleuth verhannden
> oder für Kranckhe, betrüebte personen zu pitten

When the hymn is ended the pastor goeth up into the pulpit, beginneth the sermon with prayer,
then readeth the text of the gospel, and explaineth it according to his gift and the condition
of the hearers, yet not over an hour. And the pastors shall endeavour to explain to the people,
properly and understandably, out of the divine Scripture, the chief *loci communes* in the
Sunday sermons through the year. After the sermon (because then the number of the people is
greatest) he exhorteth to the common prayer, maketh the public common confession of sins
together with the comfort and forgiveness of sins, as is set down before, folio […]. Thereafter
he recounteth all manner of present need for which it is needful to pray, likewise when there
are newly married folk, or [when there is need] to pray for the sick [and] troubled persons

Note the reason given for placing the confession and prayer **after** the sermon: "because then
the number of the people is greatest". Many came late, after the opening hymns and lessons.

At Corvey the sermon is followed in the pulpit by the exhortation to the communicants, the
confession and absolution, and the general prayer. Banns and other notices "may be done in this
place". **Corvey, *Kirchenordnung für das Corveyer Stiftsdorf Bruchhausen*, 1603**
(Sehling 21, p. 244):

<!-- doc 1455 -->
> 9. Wann die Predigte geschlossen ist, geschicht eine kürtze erinnerung und vermahnung an die
> Communicanten. 10. Darauff volget die Confeßion, Beicht und bekendtnis der Sünden und die
> Absolution, loßkündigung oder ledigsprechung von Sünden, in massen die hernach verzeichnet
> seind. 11. Endlich wirt mit gewöhnlichem, für aller Stende notturfft oder gantzen Christenheit
> Gebett beschlossen, und werden diese stücke allesampt auff dem Predigstull verrichtet, und da
> etwa Personen, so sich Ehlich vertrawet, auffzuruffen oder sonst etwas in gemein anzuzeigen
> und zuverkündigen were, das möcht an diesem orth geschehen.

9. When the sermon is closed, a short reminder and exhortation to the communicants is made. 10.
   Thereupon followeth the confession, shrift and acknowledgement of sins, and the absolution,
   release or loosing from sins, in such manner as they are written hereafter. 11. Finally [it]
   is closed with the customary prayer for the need of all estates or of the whole of
   Christendom; and these pieces are all performed upon the pulpit; and if perchance persons who
   have betrothed themselves in marriage were to be called, or anything else to be shown and
   proclaimed in common, that might be done in this place.

**A prayer of repentance from the pulpit.** Schaumburg ordered a set prayer of repentance and
forgiveness to be recited from the pulpit after the main Sunday sermon. It was not meant to
replace private confession. **Schaumburg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1614** (Sehling 7/2.2, p. 117):

<!-- doc 2178 -->
> Unnd ob wir wol ein Christlich Gebet von Buß und Vergebung der Sünde, wie nachfolgen wird,
> welches unsere Pastores im gantzen Lande alle unnd jede Sontage nach gehaltener hohen
> Vormittags Predigt öffentlich von der Cantzel recitiren sollen, haben angeordnet, So sol
> dannoch dasselbe keines weges zu auffhebung itzermelter hochnützlichen privat Confession
> gemeint seyn

And although we have ordained a Christian prayer of repentance and forgiveness of sin, as shall
follow, which our pastors in the whole land shall recite publicly from the pulpit every Sunday
after the high forenoon sermon is held, yet shall the same in no wise be meant for the
abolishing of the aforesaid most profitable private confession

**Banns from the pulpit.** Prussia requires the pastor to name strangers' parents, masters and
homes in the banns "from the pulpit", so that any impediment may come to light. **Prussia,
*Kirchen ordnung und ceremonien*, 1568** (Sehling 4, p. 93):

<!-- doc 1833 -->
> wo die personen unbekannt weren, wie gemeiniglich die dienstboten sind, soll der pfarrherre
> nicht allein ihre namen und zunamen, sondern auch ire eltern, land- und hausherren, bei denen
> sie dienen, nennen und in der abkündigung eigentlich von der kanzel namhaft machen.

where the persons were unknown, as commonly the servants are, the pastor shall name not only
their names and surnames, but also their parents, country and the masters with whom they serve,
and make them known particularly in the announcement from the pulpit.

The texts of the general prayers said from the pulpit are collected in
[`GENERAL_PRAYERS_GUIDE.md`](../GENERAL_PRAYERS_GUIDE.md) and given family by family in
[`GENERAL_PRAYERS_TEXT_GUIDE.md`](GENERAL_PRAYERS_TEXT_GUIDE.md). The hymns sung after the
sermon ("Erhalt uns, Herr, bei deinem Wort" and others) are in
[`HYMN_PRACTICE_GUIDE.md`](HYMN_PRACTICE_GUIDE.md) §11.

## 11. Preachers: trial sermons, practice and censure

### 11.1 Who preached

**Pastor, deacons, chaplains.** In the towns the preaching was shared out by the order. The
pastor took the chief Sunday sermon; the deacons or chaplains took the early, afternoon, weekday
and hospital sermons (§4.1, §4.2). Funeral and wedding sermons often went by turn, or to
whichever minister the family asked for (Sehling 3, pp. 218, 290). The village pastor preached
everything himself. Schoolmasters and students did not preach in the orders' regular services.
The question of who might preach at all, and on whose call, belongs to ordination and is outside
this guide; see [`MINOR_ORDERS_DEACONS_ELDERS_GUIDE.md`](MINOR_ORDERS_DEACONS_ELDERS_GUIDE.md)
§5.1 for the deacons.

### 11.2 The trial sermon

**Examination, then a trial sermon.** From mid-century a candidate for a parish was examined in
doctrine and then had to preach a **trial sermon** (*Probpredigt*). It showed whether he could
teach, exhort and comfort out of Scripture, and how he spoke. **Kurpfalz, *Kirchenratsordnung*,
1564** (Reformed; Sehling 14, p. 412):

<!-- doc 504 -->
> Nach dem examen, wo der examinatus wol geantwort, soll im auch eine probpredig zu thun
> auferlegt werden, daraus unsere kirchenräthe zu sehen und abzunemen, wie er auß der schrift
> das gemeine volck leren, zur besserung vermanen und trösten könne, und auß allen umbstenden,
> dergleichen dem aussprechen und verstendtlicher deutscher sprach und action besser urtheilen
> mögen, welcher ort ime zu befelhen. So sollen auch die mengel so an im befunden, ime
> freuntlich undersagt und er, die zu verbessern, vermanet werden.

After the examination, if the examinee hath answered well, a trial sermon also shall be laid
upon him to do, from which our church councillors may see and gather how he can teach the common
people out of the Scripture, exhort [them] to amendment and comfort [them]; and from all the
circumstances, likewise from the utterance and the intelligible German speech and action, may
the better judge what place is to be committed to him. And the faults that are found in him
shall be told him in a friendly manner, and he be exhorted to amend them.

In Osnabrück the order of steps was: the testimonial of the call shown to the superintendent;
examination by the ministers; a trial sermon in St Mary's; and then ordination with the laying
on of hands "after the form which Doctor Martin Luther set" (*Agende* of 1588, as printed 1618;
Sehling 7/1, p. 287). The trial sermon appears in the same way in Saxony 1580
(Sehling 1, p. 380), Pfalz-Neuburg 1576 (Sehling 13, p. 172), Wolfstein 1574
(Sehling 13, p. 567) and the Calenberg-Göttingen visitation instruction of 1588
(Sehling 6/2, p. 883).

The examination and trial sermon before ordination are treated in
[`ECCLESIASTICAL_DUTIES_GUIDE.md`](ECCLESIASTICAL_DUTIES_GUIDE.md), §7.3.

### 11.3 Practice sermons and censure

**Practice for pastors already in office.** In Albertine Saxony the superintendents were to have
the town and country clergy preach in turn on weekdays in the towns. They set them texts from
the Psalms or Paul some days in advance, and repeated the exercise according to each man's
diligence. **Albertine Saxony, *Visitations-Instruktion von 1577*** (Sehling 1, p. 349):

<!-- doc 40 -->
> Das auch die pfarhern und diacon uf dem lande und in stedten zum studieren und fleiss erwegt
> werden, so sollen unsere superintendenten und adiuncten dieselbten nach der ordenung in den
> stedten in der wochen predigen lassen, ihnen etliche tage zuvor materias aus den psalmen,
> epistolis Pauli oder sonsten aus der bibel, davon zu predigen, geben und darnach eines jeden
> fleiss oder unfleis erfunden wird, solche ubung wiederholen.

That the pastors and deacons in the country and in the towns also be stirred to study and
diligence, our superintendents and adjuncts shall let them preach in order in the towns in the
week, give them some days before matters out of the Psalms, the Epistles of Paul or otherwise
out of the Bible, to preach upon; and thereafter, as each man's diligence or negligence is
found, repeat such exercise.

Ansbach made the same rule for its chapters in 1565 (Sehling 11, p. 350).

**A sermon criticized by colleagues.** The Palatine classical conventions of 1587 (Reformed)
made such criticism systematic. At each convention one minister preached. He then withdrew, and
the others were asked in turn what faults they had found and how they might be amended. The
order gives a checklist for the critics. **Kurpfalz, *Ordnung der Classicalconvente*, 1587**
(Reformed; Sehling 14, p. 530):

<!-- doc 510 -->
> Hierauf soll der kirchendiner, so die predig gehalten, zu einem abtritt angemanet und die
> andere befragt und nacheinander angehört werden, was sie an deß abgetretenen predig vor mengel
> und, wie eß zu verbessern. Alhie son alles, was zuo rechten und erbaulichen predigten gehöret,
> examinirt werden alß ungeverlich, ob er in demselbigen zu weitleufig und ob eß accomodatum, ob
> er recht den textum partirt, ob er scopum textus assequirt oder sonsten frembde questiones
> tractirt, so eygentlich auß dem text nicht genomen werden, ob er die partes, so er zu
> tractiren proponirt, recte persequirt, ob er nicht, in dem ein theil der predig sich so lang
> ufhalte, daß er die andere, wie verheissen, nicht außfüren möge, ob er auch langsam,
> bedächtlich und verstendiglich dem gemeinen mann zur erbauung die sachen vortrag und nicht die
> wordt eilents ubereinander werfe, ob er auch gut deutsch fürnemlich und nicht niderlendische
> phrases gebrauche, ob er alleß mit einem christlichen eyver proponir, auf das meniglich sein
> ernst verneme, er sichs und seine zuhörer excitiren konne

Hereupon the minister who hath held the sermon shall be bidden to withdraw, and the others asked
and heard one after another, what faults they [find] in the sermon of him that hath withdrawn,
and how it is to be amended. Here shall all be examined that belongeth to right and edifying
sermons, as for example: whether he be too long-winded therein, and whether it be fitted [to the
hearers]; whether he hath rightly divided the text; whether he attaineth the scope of the text,
or otherwise treateth strange questions which are not properly taken out of the text; whether he
rightly pursueth the parts which he proposed to treat; whether he, by staying so long on one
part of the sermon, may not carry out the others as promised; whether he also set forth the
matters slowly, deliberately and understandably to the common man for edification, and throw not
the words hastily one upon another; whether he also use good German chiefly, and not
Netherlandish phrases; whether he propound all with a Christian zeal, that every one may
perceive his earnestness [and] he may stir up himself and his hearers

The list continues with whether he brought private matters into the pulpit. The "Netherlandish
phrases" probably reflect the ministers from the Netherlands then serving in the Reformed
Palatinate. Hanau-Münzenberg (1600, Reformed) summoned the country preachers in turn, the
youngest first, every fortnight or month, to preach the weekday sermon. Afterwards the
consistory told each one what was lacking in his sermon "in formalibus et doctrinalibus"
(Sehling 10, p. 536).

## 12. Hearers

### 12.1 Attendance

**A duty, enforced.** The orders treat hearing the sermon as a duty of every household. It was
enforced by the parish officers and by fines. Late in the century the weekday sermons in
particular were poorly attended (§4.2). The Ysenburg counts required at least one person from
every house at every weekday sermon. The mayor (*Schultheiß*) and the church wardens counted the
people after the sermon and fined those absent. **Ysenburg-Birstein, *Kirchenzuchtordnung*,
1588** (Sehling 10, p. 638):

<!-- doc 229 -->
> Es soll auch bey allen Wochenpredigten auß jederm Hauß zum wenigsten eine Person erscheinen,
> darauff der Schultheis und die Kirchen Ruger gute achtung geben, nach gehaltener Predigt
> umbzehlen lassen unnd die außbleibenden zu gebührlicher obangedeuter Straffe bringen sollen

At all weekday sermons also at the least one person out of every house shall appear; whereto the
mayor and the church wardens shall give good heed, have [the people] counted after the sermon is
held, and bring those who stay away to the due penalty indicated above

Church attendance as a duty of the laity is treated in
[`ECCLESIASTICAL_DUTIES_GUIDE.md`](ECCLESIASTICAL_DUTIES_GUIDE.md), §8.1, and the enforcement of
rest on holy days in [`LITURGICAL_CALENDAR_GUIDE.md`](LITURGICAL_CALENDAR_GUIDE.md), §11.2.

### 12.2 Taverns, markets and games during the sermon

**The first visitations.** The first Saxon visitors already found people walking in the market
and round the church during the sermon, and going off to the beer houses and the brandy.
**Ernestine Saxony, *Verordnungen aus der Visitation von 1528*, 1529** (Sehling 1, p. 177):

<!-- doc 11 -->
> Weiter kombt seinen c. g. vor, das die underdanen das wort gottes zum teil unfleissig horen
> sollen, gehn ufm markte und umb die kirche, geben also andern auch ergernus und ursache darzu,
> auch gehen sie zum teil under der predigte in die bierheusser und zum gebranten weine, uf
> dieselbigen sal man achtung haben

Further it cometh before his Electoral Grace that the subjects in part hear the word of God
negligently, walk on the market and about the church, and so give offence and occasion thereto
to others also; also some of them go during the sermon into the beer houses and to the burnt
wine; on these heed shall be given

**Innkeepers forbidden to serve.** The remedy was to shut the inns for the length of the sermon.
**Ernestine Saxony, *Pollizei und landsordnung* of the dukes Johann Friedrich the Middle, Johann
Wilhelm and Johann Friedrich the Younger, 1556** (Sehling 1, p. 229):

<!-- doc 22 -->
> Uf das auch diejenigen, so die wein oder bier zechen unter den predigten pflegen zu besuchen,
> dazu desto weniger ursach haben mügen, so wöllen wir, das hinfürder ein jeder, so wein oder
> bier schenkt, unter der predigt, und solange dieselbige wehret, es seivor oder nachmittage,
> keine geste setzen noch setzen lasse.

That those also who are wont to frequent the wine or beer taverns during the sermons may have
the less occasion thereto, we will that henceforth every one that poureth wine or beer set no
guests, nor let [them] be set, during the sermon and so long as it lasteth, be it before or
after noon.

The same rules recur through the century: no shops open, no trading, no games, no dancing and no
labour service (*Fron*) during the sermon. The Albertine articles of 1557 tell the pastor to
rebuke such disorders from the pulpit, and the judges to fine the offenders (Sehling 1, p. 325).
A Palatine mandate of 1592 (Reformed) complains that labour service was called out on holy days
and prayer days and during the weekday sermon (Sehling 19/2, p. 785).

### 12.3 Chattering, sleeping, leaving early

**Fines in the church.** The Ysenburg-Ronneburg discipline order of 1591 (Reformed) fines those
who chatter or sleep during the sermon, leave before it ends, or come late. It sets the length
of the sermon in the same breath. **Ysenburg-Ronneburg, *Kirchenzuchtordnung*, 1591** (Reformed;
Sehling 10, p. 595):

<!-- doc 222 -->
> So jemandt under der predig schwetzet oder schläffet oder in der predig (so des sontags den
> morgen nicht uber ein stundt, nach mittag aber unnd zur wochenpredig ein halbe stundt wehren
> soll) nicht zu ende verharret, es sey dann leibs schwacheit oder andere erhebliche ursachen
> vorhanden, auch nicht zu rechter zeit nach vollendtem leuden erscheinet, solle des sontags 6 ß
> unnd die wochenpredig 4 ß erlegen.

If any one chatter or sleep during the sermon, or abide not to the end in the sermon (which on
Sunday in the morning shall last not over an hour, but in the afternoon and at the weekday
sermon half an hour), unless bodily weakness or other weighty causes be present, or appear not
at the right time when the ringing is ended, he shall pay six shillings on Sunday and four
shillings for the weekday sermon.

**Stay to the end.** Leaving after the sermon, before the Supper and the prayers, was a common
complaint. The orders tell the preachers to exhort the people often to stay until the whole
service is done. **Augsburg, *Kirchenordnung*, 1537** (Moderate Reformed; Sehling 12, p. 58):

<!-- doc 338 -->
> Von vermanung an das volk, das es bis zu end bei der gemaind beleibe. Und sollen die prediger
> das volk mit allem fleiß oft ermanen, das es bei ainander bleibe, bis also das gebet, gesang
> und die hailige sacrament gar verrichtet sein, mit getreuem erinnern, wie uncristenlich das
> seie, nicht in aller andacht mit den andern cristen und der ganzen kirchen den Herren umb
> gemaine notturft anruefen

Of exhortation to the people that they remain with the congregation to the end. And the
preachers shall with all diligence oft exhort the people that they remain together until the
prayer, the singing and the holy sacrament be wholly performed; with faithful reminder how
unchristian it is not to call upon the Lord, in all devotion, with the other Christians and the
whole church, for the common need

Corvey 1603 gives the same charge (Sehling 21, pp. 244–245). Pfalz-Veldenz moved the confession
and general prayer to after the sermon "because then the number of the people is greatest"
(§10.4). That shows the reverse problem: many people came only for the sermon.
