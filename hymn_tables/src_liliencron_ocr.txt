Translator's note
The body of these pages consists almost entirely of German hymn incipits followed by sigla
for the fifteen hymnals collated. I have rendered each entry as:

I German incipit — English title: sigla
The sigla are left exactly as printed (they are abbreviations of book titles, resolved in the
key on p. 61). English titles are matched to the established translation current in the
English-language Lutheran tradition (Winkworth, Massie, Reynolds, and the
TLH/LSB/ELH corpus) wherever one exists. Where no established English translation is in
current use, I supply a literal rendering marked with a degree sign (°). I have deliberately
not supplied hymnal numbers, since the same German incipit is sometimes carried under
different titles in different books and I cannot verify numbering from this text alone.
Points of genuine uncertainty are flagged in footnotes at the end. Two recurring
ambiguities are worth naming at the outset:

1. "Wo Gott der Herr nicht bei uns halt" (Justus Jonas) and "War Gott nicht mit uns
diese Zeit" (Luther) are both metrical settings of Psalm 124, and English hymnals
have used "If God Had Not Been on Our Side" for both at different times. I have
distinguished them below by giving Jonas's as "If God Had Not Been on Our Side" and
Luther's as "Were God Not with Us at This Time", but the reader should be aware that
the English titles are not stable.

2. "Jesus Christus unser Heiland" exists as two distinct hymns of Luther: the Easter
hymn (der den Tod tiberwand) and the Communion hymn (der von uns den
Gotteszorn wandt). Liliencron's Easter entry specifies der den Tod, so the Easter hymn
is meant.

Printer's slips in the sigla are noted where they occur.

These, then, are the hymns in whose appointment for a given Sunday all, or several, or at

least two of the fifteen hymnals agree, thereby attesting a connection with a general

tradition. The basic stock of these hymns is formed by the contents of the Babst hymnal: of

the 131 hymns contained in its later editions, 85 belong to the Lieder de tempore, among

them all of Luther's — for even the hymn on the Brussels martyrs, which does not appear

above, occurs here and there individually. To these are added five hymns from the hymnals

of the Bohemian Brethren of 1531, 1544, and 1566, together with a number of hymns that

are drawn in over the course of time: hymns by H. Sachs, Er. Alberus, Nic. Herman,

Selnecker, Schneegafd, Barth. Ringwaldt, P. Eber, Ph. Nicolai, L. Helmbold, and others; to

which there then come, from about 1630 onward, some more recent hymns, most of them

by P. Gerhardt and Rist, and besides these individual pieces by Joh. Heermann, J. Franck, E.

Chr. Homburg, and others.

The group of hymns which in this way constitutes the de tempore is, however, also to be

found assembled in other sources, namely in the ecclesiastical choir-hymnals. Of these, too,

a few examples may be given here.

Notes

1. [p.62]"G."in the entry for Vom Himmel hoch is almost certainly a misprint for "Go."
(Goslar); no siglum "G." is defined in the key.
2. [pp. 64, 65] Liliencron prints "Ach Gott, im Himmel sieh darein" at 5. after Epiphany,

Septuagesima, and Sexagesima, but "Ach Gott, vom Himmel sieh darein" elsewhere
(4. after Epiphany, 2., 8., 10., 20. after Trinity). Both readings circulate for Luther's
Psalm 12 hymn; I have used the same English title for both, but the variation in the
German is his, not mine.

3. [p.68] "G. LL." at Cantate is almost certainly a misprint for "Ge. LL."

4. [p.70] The Fraktur here is ambiguous between "SH." and a damaged "H.";"SH." is the
more likely reading.

5. Uncertain identifications. Titles marked ° are my own literal renderings where I
could not verify an established English translation. Several of these are hymns of
Michael Weisse and the Bohemian Brethren (Menschenkind, merk eben; Von Adam
her so lange Zeit; Als Jesus geboren war) and Erasmus Alber (Es wird schier der letzte
Tag; Ihr lieben Christen, freut euch nun), which have generally not passed into
English use. Others — notably Christus, der uns selig macht, Da Jesus an dem Kreuze
stund, Kommt her zu mir, spricht Gottes Sohn, and Danksagen wir alle (Grates nunc
omnes) — do exist in English versions, but under titles variable enough that I did not
want to assert one as standard without checking a hymnal directly.

6. "Herr Gott, dich loben alle wir" (Eber, on the angels) must be kept distinct from
"Herr Gott, dich loben wir" (the German Te Deum). Liliencron himself notes the
substitution in Go. at Michaelmas.
